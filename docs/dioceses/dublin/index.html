All Saint. ‘The ultimate object of veneration of the Saints is the glory of God and the sanctification of man by conforming one’s life fully to the divine will and by imitating the virtue of those who were pre-eminent disciples of the Lord.

Catechises and other forms of doctrinal instruction should therefore make known to the faithful that our relationship with the Saints must be seen in the light of the faith and should not obscure the “cultus latriae due to God the Father through Christ in the Holy Spirit, but intensify it ” NOVEMBER – WHY BOTHER HAVING A FUNERAL MASS? WHY NOT JUST GO STRAIGHT TO THE CEMETERY AND LEAVE OUT THE FUNERAL MASS? In times gone by every Catholic who died automatically came to the Church for their funeral.

In the recent times some families bypass the Church and do not bring their dead to the Church for the Requiem or Funeral Mass.

Instead they go straight to the cemetery or Crematorium.

In doing so they leave Jesus out. They forget that we are not merely individuals, we are part of a community of friends of Jesus called the Church.

It is sad to see people who loved the Mass and came to Mass regularly not being brought to the church and giving their friends and fellow Christians in the parish a chance to mark their passing with the greatest gift God has given us, the Mass.

WHY IS IT IMPORTANT TO HAVE A FUNERAL MASS? Every Catholic is baptized into God’s Holy Trinity, uniting themselves with Jesus and His Church.

We are baptized into the Death and Resurrection of Jesus from the dead because Jesus has conquered death and opened for us the way to eternal life.

So it does not make sense to leave Jesus out of the equation.

Jesus is the whole reason for Baptism, and especially for the Mass.

We expect to meet Jesus when we die. The funeral Mass is the heart of our funeral – Jesus gave us the Mass at the Last Supper just before He died on the Cross.

The Mass only makes sense because Jesus conquered death and evil by rising from the dead on Easter Sunday.

Remember the acclamation at the Solemn consecration of the Mass “Christ has died, Christ is risen, Christ will come again ”. In the funeral Mass we offer our dead to the Father as Jesus did for us on the Cross.

The funeral Mass asks God to admit the dead Catholic to the Paschal fullness of the table of the Kingdom.

Through Baptism we become part of God’s family – the parish community.

It is by the Mass and Holy Communion that the community of the faithful, especially the family of the deceased, learn to live in communion with the one who "has fallen asleep in the Lord," by communicating in the Body of Christ of which he is a living member and, then, by praying for him and with him.

For Christians to forget about the church funeral for the dead is to leave out the main player, Jesus Christ, who is our Saviour.

The greatest act of love you can do for your loved one who dies is to give them the gift of a Catholic Funeral with the complete rites of the Church in accord with God’s wish for each and every member of His Church when they die.

Pastors Desk Today, November 1st, the feast of All Saints is a distinctive day in our Catholic list of feast days.

The Church has a very precise process, called canonisation through which it feels confident to say that somebody has lived a life in tune with the will of God and has now reached the kingdom of heaven.

However the Church knows too that we can’t limit the number of people who have reached eternal happiness in continuation of their holy Christian lives.

They are people known to us – family, friends, colleagues and people like ourselves who have helped the thirsty, the hungry, those without clothes and shelter.

The love of neighbour that, after our death leads to God’s right hand.

These were the gestures that carried weight with Jesus.

The feast of All Saints is a day of great hope and inspiration for us.

As the Epistle to the Hebrews states “since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us rid ourselves of every burden and sin that clings to us and persevere in running that lies before us.”(Heb. 12:1) Mass attendance A holy elderly priest was once asked, “how many people attended the early morning Mass today”? He responded “ There were three old ladies, the janitor, several thousand archangels, a large number of seraphim, and several million of the triumphant saints of God”. But we always have room for one more!

We should always keep in mind the great cloud of witnesses that surround us at every Mass.

So on this feast we remember the many good people gone before us whom we’ve met in life and without embarrassment pray to them to help us on our journey.

May we join them one day in the communion of saints.

Monday 2nd November Mass at 10am in St Monica’s Church . Pastors Desk All Sain ts Today, November 1st, the feast of All Saints is a distinctive day in our Catholic list of feast days.

The Church has a very precise process, called canonisation through which it feels confident to say that somebody has lived a life in tune with the will of God and has now reached the kingdom of heaven.

However, the Church knows too that we can’t limit the number of people who have reached eternal happiness in continuation of their holy Christian lives.

They are people known to us – family, friends, colleagues and people like ourselves who have helped the thirsty, the hungry, those without clothes and shelter.

The love of neighbour that, after our death leads to God’s right hand.

These were the gestures that carried weight with Jesus.

The feast of All Saints is a day of great hope and inspiration for us.

As the Epistle to the Hebrews states “since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us rid ourselves of every burden and sin that clings to us and persevere in running the race that lies before us.”(Heb. 12:1) Mass attendance A holy elderly priest was once asked, “how many people attended the early morning Mass today”? He responded, “There were three old ladies, the janitor, several thousand archangels, a large number of seraphim, and several million of the triumphant saints of God”. But we always have room for one more!

We should always keep in mind the great cloud of witnesses that surround us at every Mass.

So, on this feast we remember the many good people gone before us whom we’ve met in life and without embarrassment pray to them to help us on our journey . May we join them one day in the communion of saints.

A time when we remember family and friends who have gone before us.

The Church sets aside this month, beginning with All Saints' and All Souls' Days, to remember all the

faithful departed

Our presence and our prayers, especially at Mass, assist souls on this journey towards the ultimate Christian vision of seeing God face to face where there is complete happiness and lasting peace.

May their souls and the souls of all the faithful departed, through the mercy of God, rest in peace.

The Solemnity of All Saints invites us to lift our eyes beyond the struggles of daily life and remember the countless men and women who have gone before us marked with the sign of faith.

In the vision of the Book of Revelation, John beholds a vast multitude from every nation, race and language standing before the throne of God.

They are ordinary people who remained faithful through trials and now share in the fullness of God's glory.

In the Gospel, Jesus presents the Beatitudes, a portrait of true holiness.

The saints are not remembered because they were powerful, wealthy or successful by worldly standards.

Rather, they were poor in spirit, merciful, peacemakers, and people who hungered for righteousness.

They allowed God's grace to shape their lives.

This feast reminds us that holiness is not reserved for a select few.
