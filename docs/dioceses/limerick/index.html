Sun 1st Nov 10 am 11.30am Eddie Lonergan RIP(Rec Dec) Sonny Larkin RIP (20th A) Mass Intentions Reflection In the verses in the gospel preceding today’s passage, the Sadducees - who deny the resurrection - approached Jesus and asked him a question about the afterlife not in order to seek an answer, not to learn, but to try to trip himup.

Yet again, Jesus gave a masterful answer ands ilenced them.

Then, not to be outdone, the Pharisees approached him and they put their question.

They weren’t asking in order to learn either but to disconcert Jesus.

In other translations, it is said they approached him with a question to test Jesus.

The only other place St. Matthew uses this verb is when he applied it to the devil who approached Jesus after his forty days’ fast in the desert, and the devil set out to test Jesus.

Clearly, St. Matthew is saying that what the Pharisees were up to was reprehen- sible, nothing short of diabolical.

Inspired by the devil they were treating Jesus - who is God - with utter contempt.

This then contrasts - like black contrasts with white - when Jesus tells them that the first law is to love God and not treat him with contempt as they had.

To get back to their question for a moment.

At first glance, it seems innocuous enough: “Master, which is the greatest commandment of the Law? ” We would happily admit that there is a hierarchy of importance in laws.

Some laws are more important than others.

It seems obvi ous enough. But the rabbis of Jesus’ day held that all the commandments - all six hundred and thirteen of them - being God’s laws were all of equal importance and ranking them in terms of importance would be human presumption and an affront to God.

Then using their own commandment from Deuteronomy which read: “Hear, O Israel: the Lord your God is one Lord: and you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might. ” Jesus instructed them that love of God is the first and most important law and then he went on to say that loving one’s neighbour as oneself is the second and that the whole law is summed up in these two commandments.

This is the key to interpreting all law.

If one truly loves God and neighbour one has in fact kept all of God’s laws.

Writing to the Romans, St. Paul put it this way: Love is the one thing that cannot hurt your neighbour; that is why it is the answer to every one of the commandments. ” (Rom. 13:10) Saints of the Week St Otteran

Sun 01 Nov. Rediscovering Catholic Customs Every generation inherits traditions from the past which shape our Catholic identity.

They have been woven into the daily of life, complimenting the liturgical year and enriching personal piety.

October and November are rich months for rediscovering Catholic customs that may have faded from everyday practice.

There are many graces to be received from praying for the dead.

We do this at Mass, at funerals, while visiting cemeteries.

Our dear departed are remembered in a special way on the Feast of All Saints (Nov 1) and the Day of All Souls (Nov 2).
