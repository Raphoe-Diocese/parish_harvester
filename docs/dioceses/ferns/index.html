Newsletter for the weekend of 10-11 October 2026. Reflection on the Gospel for the 28th Sunday in Ordinary Time.

In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells of a king who opens his wedding feast to everyone.

God offers us the same loving invitation —to receive His grace, for- giveness and hope.

Yet the demands of daily life can leave little room for Him.

We are invited not because we are perfect, but because we are loved.

Eradication of Poverty The International Day for the Eradication of Pov- erty, observed an- nually on 17 October, recognizes that ending pov- erty is inseparable from protecting human rights, human dignity and peace.

Under the 2026 –2028 theme “United for Peace and Hu-man Rights,” the 2026 observance focuses on “Safe Homes for All,” highlighting adequate hous- ing as a foundation for security, participation and a life of dignity.

Please give what you can —your support can make a real dif- ference.

October - Month of the Holy Rosary Significant Vatican appointment Pope Leo XIV has appointed Irish missionary Sr Mary Teresa Barron, a native of Donegal; to be president of the Pontifical Mission Societies and adjunct secretary of the Dicastery for Evangelization’s section for missionary territories.

She is the first woman to lead the socie- ties.

The Vati- can an- nounced the appointment on 8 October.

Sr Mary Teresa, 57, will take up the role on 4 January 2027. Her appoint- ment is the third major nomination of a woman to lead a major Vatican office by the pope.

He previously named former EWTN News president Maria Montserrat "Montse" Alvarado to lead the Vatican's communications department and Salesian Sr. Alessandra Smerilli to head its social justice office.

rocusonysunda a 4 Sik | a | ack Today is prisoner Sunday and the Church asks us to reflect , 4 on this most marginalised and often despised section of our population.

Many accept, without reflection, that our prisons are | of dangerous, violent criminals.

While there are certainly some dangerous people in prison, 80% of those convicted are sentenced to 12 months or less, suggesting that their crimes are less serious (accepting that even less serious crimes can be upsetting or even traumatic for the victim). 70% of prisoners have an addiction but our addiction services have long waiting lists.

The Government plan to provide 1,500 extra prison places to combat the overcrowding in our prisons, but it might actually be more effective to provide 1,500 extra drug treatment places. 70% have mental health issues and our mental health services are generally recognised as very inadequate.

Our prisons are overcrowded, full of drugs and increasingly violent places, making rehabilitation almost impossible.

Some are on remand with bail set at €50 or €100, but they haven't gotit, so they remain in prison simply because they are poor.

Others have bail, but because they cannot provide an address, they remain in prison because they are homeless.

Most people who commit crime grow out of it.

Many tell me that they gave up crime because they fell in love, or had a child, or found employment, and that now they have a reason, for the first time in their lives, not to go to jail.

Perhaps it is the system, just as much as prisoners, which needs rehabilitation!

This weekly ceremony was for the women of the parish at 7.30pm on Monday evenings and for the men on Tuesday.

Parishioners avoided appearing in public during Confraternity times if they were not attending.

The ceremony consisted of rosary and a short sermon followed by Benediction.

The congregation was divided into sections, more or less by street, and each section had a banner held in position by the rings.

Our section was St. Aidan Michael Street, and our leader was Pete Brothers, whose job it was to mark the attendance book which no doubt was examined by the clergy now and again to note non-attenders.

The final hymn was always “Happy we who thus united. ” which was sung with gusto.

The second half of the first line was “. joined in cheerful melody ”. Pete used to delight in singing “ .joined in fearful melody ”. Courtesy of Jim Sutton
