FROM THE EULOGY AT BETTY O’SHEA’S FUNERAL MASS Read by her son Brendan on 7/11/2026 Elizabeth (Betty) O’Shea (née) Doran was born to James & Margaret Doran of Moorefield, Rathaspeck, in 1946, the youngest of three children, her brother Teddy and sister Nuala.

Her father, James was a Labour Party county councillor and Betty followed in his steps by joining the Labour Party and remaining a lifelong member until her passing.

She also told us stories of her mother, who played camogie for Wexford.

A very proud moment for Betty on 13 Nov 2023, when she and other family members were presented with a jersey worn by her grandfather, Thomas (Skull) Hayes, when he captained the Wexford team to an All-Ireland Senior Football Final in 1893. Betty moved to Adamstown in 1969 after marrying Jimmy, having first met dancing to the tune of Slaney Valley.

She took to life in Adamstown like a house on fire, and during those early days of her marriage, neighbours would ramble into her home where they all had great fun with Betty in the middle of everyone laughing her heart out.

She loved the O’Shea family that she had married into.

Betty devoted herself to the community over the past 57 years.

She was a member of many organisations, including the Historical Society, Active Retirement, ICA, Community Games, Adamstown Development and & Adamstown Community Centre where she was Secretary, up until her passing.

She was always proactive and positive and if something needed to be done, she would get it done!rd thth th

Betty was secretary of the Parent’s Association in Coláiste Abbáin for many years, during the campaign for a new school, Betty would send a letter each week to the Minister of Education until shegot a response.

The new Secondary school in Adamstown is a testament to her determination and love for her community.

She fought many a battle and spoke her mind on improving services for young and old in the parish.

She loved to see people in the Parish doing well.

When she discovered Facebook, she shared good news stories over and over again.

She was a familiar face around the parish and welcomed everyone with a smile.

Once she discovered driving later in life, the car was never off the road!

Betty had a life long love of learning, particularly local history.

She was involved in the Historical Society.

One of her publications was Adamstown: A Place and Its People . Betty was so proud of her eight sons and daughter Eleanor and always had their back.

She had a unique and individual relationship with each of them.

In their times of need, she stood by them and gave them strength and courage to carry on.

She created a home where the door was always open and everyone was welcome.

She loved all of her 24 grandchildren. They in turn adored her, affectionately calling her Nanny Betty.

They will miss her terribly. Her son-in-law and daughters-in-law held a very special place in her heart.

Betty faced her illness with incredible dignity and courage.

Her faith never wavered, never once, and was with her right up to the end.

Betty was a daughter, sister, a wife, a mother, a grandmother, an aunt, a mother-in-law, a sister-in-law, a cousin, a scholar, an activist and campaigner, and a great neighbour and friend to all.

She is with her husband Jimmy now, who is saying to her. “What took you so long?” May her gentle soul rest in peace.

Hotspots of Persecution: The most dangerous places for Christians are typically countries where totalitarian or authoritarian governments restrict religious freedoms, where Islamist extremism is present, or where there are significant nationalist or tribal hostilities towards religious minorities.

Church of the Feast of Christ the King. - A Different Kind of Kingdom The Feast of Christ the King—celebrated on the final Sunday of the liturgical year—invites us to reimagine what true power looks like.

Unlike earthly rulers who hold authority through wealth, force, or status, Jesus reveals a kingship rooted in humility, truth, mercy, and self-giving love.

Below are a few points worth considering.

Service over status: Jesus leads as a shepherd who tends, protects, and seeks out the vulnerable rather than demanding to be served.

Love over coercion: His reign is built on forgiveness, peace, and justice, even when met with mockery or suffering.

A present call, not just a future hope: Praying "Your kingdom come" is a commitment to live out compassion, dignity, and solidarity right now.

The link of Jesus and ourselves is closer than any family or religious link; it is the link of our humanity and divinity.

Jesus turns some notions of our ways of life upside down.

It is the link of the friend, as in our Christmas song: The King of kings lay thus in lowly manger, In all our trials born to be our Friend!

The king is the friend. The king was expected in Jesus’ time to look after all the poor of this time.

He would be at gate where the poor gathered.

Caring for the poor in an immediate way: not from a place but being present where his people gathered.

What they had in common — and they are we — was their need for the essentials of life.

Kingdoms and kingship are not the best words of this mystery.

Perhaps ‘the reign of God’ is the better title for Christ's kingship. in some ways, as people who are responsible citizens, we can care for justice, peace, care for one another, care for the places where we live.

We are the carers for the essentials of life.

These would be signs of the reign of God.
