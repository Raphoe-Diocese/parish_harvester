Church of the A A thought for the weekend.

In the parable, the foolish bridesmaids ask the wise ones to share their oil when the groom is delayed but are told they must buy their own.

This illustrates personal responsibility.

Grace faith prayer and good works can't be borrowed from a spouse parent friend or priest. Each Christian is individually commissioned at Baptism to keep their own lamp burning and can’t rely on someone else's spiritual life.

Given this, we're asked to reflect on my own response

to Jesus’ call. How am | in terms of nurturing my faith

development, my personal prayer and my commitment to sharing Christ with others?

As a community of faith, we gather either daily or on weekends to celebrate Mass (Eucharist).

The Eucharist acts as the "source and summit" of your

faith development and an intimate, direct encounter with

Jesus Christ. Rather than a mere symbol or ritual, it is a living relationship that provides the ultimate spiritual

support for your journey.

Today, you're here to celebrate the Eucharist, find support from your community and to pray for the strength to be missionaries for Christ throughout the coming week.

Today's Gospel paints five bridesmaids in a rather unfortunate light.

In first- century Israel, wedding feasts were strictly guarded to prevent intruders and, once the wedding party entered, the door was locked.

The wedding party were identified by their roles as light-bearing ushers, and the word lampas used in the Gospel referred to outdoor torches, which were long sticks with oil-soaked rags wrapped tightly around the top.

They burned extremely quickly, needing large amounts of oil.

Therefore, it would have been culturally inexplicable to come to the wedding without extra oil.

The wise bridesmaids simply could not share their oil, as this risked all torches burning out, leaving the bridal walk to the groom’s house in pitch darkness and disarray.

Plus, without a lit lamp, one could not be identified as a member of the wedding party, resulting in the ultimate rebuttal in the words do not know you”. These words echo Peter's betrayal of Jesus.

Betrayal is based on lack of trust and a loss of confidence.

The five foolish bridesmaids were not prepared for the coming of the bridal party.

What does this tell us about their values?

What can it tell us about where we put our time and energy in our lives, and what we value?

In later centuries, and before the game was codiĮed and developed by the GAA, the game was popular as a summertime activity.

Before goalposts were thought of, a single willow (sally) post was used, with both ends sunk in the ground to form a hoop.

A goal was scored when the ball was passed through the hoop from either side, and the game was decided by the scoring of one or more goals, as agreed by the teams before the match.

Rules were fairly basic, and numbers of players per team was again a maƩer of agreement.

The sponsors of hurling were often the local landlord and each had a team of tenants.

Caesar Colclough of the Duīrey had a famous hurling team which he brought by invitation to Wales to play the team of a local landlord there.

The Duīrey team wore yellow sashes to distinguish them from the opposition, and an important Walsh lady at the game supporting the Duīrey men, shouted “Come on the Yellowbellies! ”. And our Wexford Yellowbellies hurl on to this day.
