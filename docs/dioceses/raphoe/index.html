Feast Day Prayer to St. Pope John Paul (22™ October): Oh, St. John Paul, from the window of heaven, grant us your blessing!

Bless the Church that you loved and served and guided, courageously leading her along the paths of the world in order to bring Jesus to everyone and everyone to Jesus.

Bless the young, who were your great passion.

Help them dream again, help them look up to the heavens again to find the light that illuminates the paths of life here on earth.

May you bless each and every family! You warned of Satan’s assault against this precious and indispensable divine spark that God lit on earth.

St. John Paul, with your prayer, may you protect the family and every life that blossoms from the family.

Pray for the whole world, which is still marked by tensions, wars and injustice.

You opposed war by invoking dialogue and planting the seeds of love: pray for us so that we may be tireless sowers of peace.

Oh St. John Paul, from heaven’s window, where we see you beside Mary, send God’s blessing down upon us all.

Amen.

Sunday 18th October 2026 Twenty-Ninth Sunday in Ordinary Time World Mission Sunday, is the one Sunday of the year when the entire global Church comes together in support of mission.

It is the Holy Father’s annual appeal for spiritual and financial support so that the life giving work of overseas mission and missionaries can continue.

It is a moment of universal solidarity when each member of the Church family, regardless of location or background, plays their part in supporting each other.

All offerings and donations made on World Mission Sunday become part of the Holy Father’s Universal Solidarity Fund.

This fund is a lifeline for struggling missionaries and the communities they serve across Africa, Asia and Latin America, where over 1,100 mission diocese are found.

These dioceses are often in remote areas devastated by war and natural disasters or where suppressed communities are just opening up to the life-saving message of Jesus Christ. As these dioceses form and grow, so do their needs.

The Mission Sunday collection offers young dioceses the financial and spiritual assistance they need to help their men, women and children survive and thrive.

The needs of overseas dioceses vary greatly.

These needs can include the purchase of a motorbike so a priest can celebrate Mass in remote villages, or the building of a church so that people can gather and celebrate in safety.

In essence, Mission Sunday: Provides Church-centred infrastructure by helping communities build churches, schools and clinics; Prepares the future leaders and carers of the Church by supporting the training of sisters, priests, religious brothers and catechists; Supports miss ionary programmes that protect and care for children’s well-being by offering safe shelter, healthcare, education and hope for the future.

Missio Ireland are the Holy Father’s official charity for overseas mission, his chosen instrument for sharing Gospel and building the Church throughout the world.

Through the generosity of parishioners and donors, we can reach out to offer aid to struggling communities , helping them to grow and thrive by bringing hope to places where there is of ten turmoil, poverty and uncertainty.

We can provide mission dioceses with the essential support they need to become self sufficient.

Working through the local bishops, churches and missionary congregations, we ensure resources are distributed equitably and justly, based on need.

We thank you for your great generosity and kindness.

World Mission Sunday, 18th October . What is World Mission Sunday?

World Mission Sunday is the Holy Father's annual appeal in support of overseas mission.

It takes place in very parish worldwide where the Church is present.

Who organises World Mission Sunday? Missio Ireland [the Irish branch of Pontifical Mission Societies] coordinates World Mission Sunday.

It is part of a global network, serving the Church in over 1,130 dioceses world wide.

How are funds received on World Mission Sunday dispersed?

All projects go through the local bishops and nuncios in the countries where funds are requested.

They are vetted and checked by the Pontifical Mission Societies in Rome.

Projects are allocated to Ireland to match the amount of funds that are available, once the audit is complete.

This year, €2.6m has been sent out to projects from Missio Ireland.

Why is World Mission Sunday so important?

All donations received on Mission Sunday become part of the Holy Father's Universal Solidarity Fund.

This Fund is essential, not only for missionaries, but for the future of the Church.

It supports missionary activities in over 1,130 mission dioceses, mostly found in Africa, Asia and Latin America.

WEEKLY NEWSLETTER 18th October 2026 – Twenty-Ninth Sunday of Ordinary Time Step by Step th rough the Mass The Liturgy of the Eucharist (Part 12) When the priest says “Pray Brothers and Sisters” he is not saying “Let us pray” as he does elsewhere.

Here the word “Pray” is a command. It is a call to attention of sorts.

And that is why the people respond by standing up at this point – an act which biblically and liturgically denotes taking a posture of readiness to do some great and godly task.

The task in hand is the uniting of our lives with the life and death of Jesus made present in this Mass.

Could there be a more important and godly task than this?

This great and godly task is not simply passively awaiting the priest, (who has sacred power the laity do not have), to bring about the change in the bread and wine which becomes the Body, Blood, Soul and Divinity of Jesus.

The lay faithful are encouraged, urged even, to unite themselves with self-offering of Jesus on Calvary, to make the pursuit of spiritual union with him an active endeavour in this prayer; and exercise their baptismally rooted Commo n Priesthood – a real power to offer right worship, praise and sacrifice to God the Father through Christ Jesus – as they will hear at every Mass: “Through Him, With Him, and In Him” – Him being the Lord Jesus.

Baptism unites us to Christ Jesus – it makes us Christian; makes us sons and daughters of the Father, and Co-Heirs with Christ. In a sense, it makes our souls capable of uniting our sacrifice of praise and the offering of our lives (and the many ways we serve) to the life and sacrificial service of Jesus on the Cross.

The Second Vatican Council taught on this aspect of the Common Priesthood of all the baptised: “The baptized, by regeneration and the anointing of the Holy Spirit, are consecrated as a spiritual house and a holy priesth ood, in order that through all those works which are those of the Christian they may offer spiritual sacrifices and proclaim the power of Him who has called them out of darkness into His marvellous light… The ministerial priest, by the sacred power he enjoys, teaches and rules the priestly people; acting in the person of Christ, he makes present the Eucharistic Sacrifice, and offers it to God in the name of all the people.

But the faithful, in virtue of their royal priesthood, join in the offering of the Eucharist…Taking part in the Eucharisti c Sacrifice, which is the fount and apex of the whole Christian life, they offer the Divine Victim to God, and offer themselves along with it.
