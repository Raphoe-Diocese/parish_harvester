It is concerned with properties of much greater value, properties that God has entrusted to all of us: life, love, freedom, beauty, truth, and other individual gifts.

These properties do not belong to us. They are entrusted to us by God, and we are accountable for them.

The word ‘entrusted’ is the key to understanding this parable.

God has confidence in us. He trusts us to look after the goods he has placed in our care.

We might say that God has dreams for us far greater than any earthly father or mother have for their children.

While the word talent, as used in today’s gospel, refers to a weight or unit of currency common in the world of Jesus’ time, most people today use it to refer to a person’s particular gifts or abilities of mind or body.

Interpreted in this sense, the parable may help us to reflect on the particular gifts or abilities God has given us, and which he is challenging us to use in the service of his reign.

We should ask ourselves how well we are using these gifts i n the service of our community, parish, and the wider society.

If I were to meet Jesus t oday and he were to ask me: How did you use the talents I gave you?

Who benefited and howdid they benefit from them?

Could I say, with confidence, that I have used them to the best of my ability to promote God’s reign in the world?

If I can honestly say that I have used them to serve others rather than myself, then I have every right to expect to hear the reply of the Master to the productive servants: ‘Well done, good and faithful servant; you have shown you can be faithful in small things; come and join in your master’s happiness’ (Mt 25:21).

Paróiste Chill Taobhóige Tithe Pobail Ghleann Fhinne agus Leitir Bric Thirty-Second Sunday Year A— November 8th 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel for the 32nd Sunday November 8th 2026 The approaching end of the liturgical year regularly brings the usual warnings about being prepared.

We know that in the very early Church the Christians be- lieved that Christ’s Second Coming, and therefore the end of the world, would come very soon, even in their lifetime.

However, by the time the Gospels were put in written form, this began to seem less likely.

Does that mean that the warnings about readiness can be put aside?

Because, for most of us, it is presumably purely academic to wonder when the end of our universe or of our planet will take place.

We are likely to be around when it happens.

What is much more practical, however, is the end of our own world —theend of our own time here on earth.

That is anything but an academic consid eration.

That is the one real- ity of our future that we can be absolutely certain about.

And we know very well —although we often prefer not to think about it —that it can happen at any time and in any place.

Today’s readings bring us face to face with this reality, this fact.

They ask us the question: Am I ready? A nd they imply consideration of a second question: How can I be ready?

Save The Date Milford GAA Health & Wellbeing Committee warmly invites everyone in our community to “Breaking the Silence” Information Night on Wednesday, 18th November at 7:30pm in Mulroy Woods.

The evening will explore challenges affecting individuals and families, including alcohol and drug misuse, recognising the signs of mental health difficulties, understanding the risks of gambling, and the positive role sport plays in supporting mental wellbeing.

Speakers with lived experience will share their personal stories and recovery journeys, while HSE and mental health professionals will provide practical advice, information on recognising warning signs and guidance on where to access help and support.

Brendan Devenney (DL Debate, Highland Radio) will MC the evening.

These are issues that affect every family and every community - we urge parents, coaches, volunteers and the wider community to come along, bring a friend and show your support.

Let’s work together to build a stronger, healthier and more supportive community.

Everyone is welcome!
