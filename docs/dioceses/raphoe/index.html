A Student’s Prayer at the Beginning of the New School Year: O God, we thank you for
everything that is new and beautiful, for everything which holds promise and brings us joy. Bless
all students & pupils as they start this new school year with their friends, teachers and school
community. Help them to make the most of every chance they have to start afresh. May the new
beginning of this school year remind them that you give us chances to start over again and again.
Help them to be considerate of others and to learn and to work together. As students and pupils
we thank you for our friends. Help us to be good friends this year. Help us to be patient with
ourselves and with others. Bless our school and keep us safe. Be with us as we travel each day.
Help us to be aware of your love shown to us in the people around us. Bless all those who care for
us in school and at home. Help us show our care for them and to say sorry when we need to. May
our Mother Mary, Réalt na Mara protect us and help us to live as well as we can, giving thanks
and glory to God as she did. Amen.

A PRAYER FOR OUR EARTH
(Tuesday 1st September ~ World Day of Prayer for the Care of Creation)
All-powerful God, you are present in the whole universe and in the smallest
of your creatures.
You embrace with your tenderness all that exists.
Pour out upon us the power of your love, that we may protect life and beauty.
Fill us with peace, that we may live as brothers and sisters, harming no one.
O God of the poor, help us to rescue the abandoned and forgotten of this
earth, so precious in your eyes.
Bring healing to our lives, that we may protect the world and not prey on it,
that we may sow beauty, not pollution and destruction.
Touch the hearts of those who look only for gain at the expense of the poor
and the earth.
Teach us to discover the worth of each thing, to be filled with awe and
contemplation, to recognise that we are profoundly united with every creature
as we journey towards your infinite light.
We thank you for being with us each day.
Encourage us, we pray, in our struggle for justice, love and peace.
Pope Francis, Laudato Si ’, 246

Reflection
Becoming disciples of Jesus means learning to think the way God thinks, rather than the
way we usually think. And that will be for us, as it was for Peter and his dis ciples, a
life-long process of ups and downs, successes and failures. There may be times when
we will feel a bit like Jeremiah in our first reading today. Chosen by God t o be his
‘prophet to the nations ’ (Jer 1:5), fidelity to his calling ‘made him a daily laughing -stock,
the butt of everyone ’s jokes, and brought him ‘insult and derision, all day long ’
(Jer 20:7 -8). Though tempted to stop being a spokesman for the Lord, he could not give
up because, as he states, ‘there seemed to be a fire burning in my heart, imprisoned in
my bones ’ (Jer 20:9). Jeremiah had been, as he states, ‘seduced ’ by God, and he had
let himself ‘be seduced ’ (cf. Jer 20: 7). In other words, he had fallen in love with God so that nothing could stop
him from doing God ’s will, no matter where this might lead him. The example of Jeremiah prom pts us to ask
ourselves the question: Have we let ourselves be seduced by Christ so that we persevere in fol lowing and serving
him, regardless of the consequences?

The Greatness of Eucharistic Adoration
“Those who stand before the Lord in Eucharistic Adoration are fulfilling an eminent service.
They are presenting to Christ all those who do not know him or are far f rom him; they keep
watch in his presence on their behalf," Pope St. John Paul II
The above quotation from Pope John Paul II speaks of the service we render to others by
presenting them to the Lord during our time of Eucharistic Adoration. The Holy Father
intended by these words to remind people of the immense good their prayers before the
Blessed Sacrament could do for those who right now have little or no knowledge of or
time for God and the things of God. In other words, we can and do win graces for others
who have need of them; graces of conversion, graces that will set them and confirm them
upon their path to God.
During our Holy Hour we sit or kneel in the presence of the Lord Jesus Christ truly present
in the Most Holy Eucharist. We gaze upon that little white host and by faith we declare
that behind the appearance of bread we encounter the face of the Lord of life. And we
bring to him ourselves and those we hold dear – living and deceased.
Only in heaven will we understand the value of our Holy Hour and the good it does our
souls and the souls of so many others. And so, we would encourage you to try some time
of Eucharistic Adoration for yourself. From the middle of September, across our 2
Parishes and 3 Churches we will have Adoration 3 days a week ( Wednesday in Newtown
11am to 8pm ; Thursday in St. Johnston 1pm to 6pm ; & Friday in Killea 2pm to 6pm ).
There is, therefore, ample opportunity to find time to be with Jesus’ face to face in the
Blessed Sacrament. However, these hours are contingent on there being at least 2 people
covering each hour. If you have already taken an hour but would like to change your hour,
or if you would like to be assigned a particular hour then please fill out and return the
leaflets you will find in the porch by next weekend. All hours are available to be taken
but we have particular need of adorers from 3pm to 6pm in St. Johnston, and from 7pm –
8pm in Newtown. New adorers would also be needed for the Killea hours on Friday
afternoon. Entire family groups might consider taking a particular hour – and then at least
two of the group would be present for the hour they have signed up for. Fr Philip Kemmy, PP Fr. Damien Nejad, CC
