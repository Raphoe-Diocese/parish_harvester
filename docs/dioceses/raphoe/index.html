Areweso tied upinall kinds of concerns and anxieties a bout things which do not really matter that we fail to recognise or simply ignore the invitation to God’s banquet? And, even ifwemaynot persecute God ’s prophets and messengers, how seriously do we take their words of wisdom? Following the refusal of the invited guests to accept the invitation, the King sends his servants out again – this time nottothe houses of the wealthy and respectable, buttothe crossroads in the town. These were places near the gates and markets of a typical Middle East town where large crowds of ordinary people would gather. Here you found the social and religious outcasts, tax collectors, prostitutes, all those in despised trades. This time no exceptions are made. Allare invited, good and bad alike, until the wedding hall is filled. What Jesus is saying is that everyone is invited to the Kingdom of God. All kinds of people are called to be part of God’snew world. It does not belong to an elite group of specially ch osen ones. It encompasses saints and sinners. As the sacrament of the Kingdom, the Church must never forget that, in God’s eyes, there are no first and second-class members. It must resist the temptation to create elite groups that are ‘different ’ and, by implication, ‘better ’ than the ordinary average members. While it might seem wonderful to have a Christian community consisting only of totally dedicated members, it would not reflect the love of God reaching outtoall. It would notbea catholic church, a church open toall. The only requirement is ‘the simple offering of your faith, green as a leaf ’.
