Paróiste Naomh Fionán

21ú Meitheamh 2026 - An Dara Domhnach Déag - A

Reflection

In March 2018, Fr Tony Coote, a 63 year old Dublin priest, was diagnosed with the dreaded Motor Neuron Disease, and died 18 months later. Before his death he wrote an inspiring memoir about his life entitled Live While you Can. It became an instant best-seller. The final words of Tony’s memoir were: ‘When the train stops, I will step onto that platform with hope and no fear.’ The words ‘Fear not’ are the most frequent words spoken by Jesus to his disciples. In today’s gospel they occur three times. But what is Jesus really saying when he tells men not to be afraid? Certainly, he is not giving them, or us, the false assurance that we have nothing to fear. Down through the centuries, all true disciples of Jesus have met with opposition from those who benefit from the unjust status quo. In recent decades, violent persecution of Christians has been on the rise. Speaking to diplomats on the 9th January this year, Pope Leo stated that ‘the persecution of Christians remains one of the most widespread human rights’ crises today, affecting over 380 million believers worldwide. They suffer high or extreme levels of discrimination, violence or oppression because of their faith.’ According to the 2026 World Watch List (WWL) report, the number of Christians suffering persecution rose by 8 million over the past year. In face of such opposition and persecution, Jesus urges his disciples not to allow their genuine fears to cripple them, but to continue to proclaim the truth of the gospel and to witness to him publicly. He assures them that the truth that the powerful elites try to cover up will eventually be revealed: ‘Nothing is covered up that will not be uncovered, and nothing secret that will not become known’ (Mt 10:26). He reassures them of the Father’s love and care for them: ‘Even the hairs of your head are all counted’ (Mt 10:30). And he ends by making them this promise: ‘Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I will also acknowledge before my Father in heaven’ (Mt 10:32).

So we pray: Lord, grant us the courage to stake our lives on the truth of your word. Amen.

STEP BY STEP THROUGH THE MASS

The Liturgy of the Word (Part 12) – The Prayers of the Faithful

Once we have made our Profession of faith with the recitation of the Creed, we move on to the final part of the Liturgy of the Word at Mass; namely: The Prayers of the Faithful – sometimes called the Bidding Prayers. These prayers are not inconsequential pious throw-aways – they are an expression of our union with Christ, praying as the Body of Christ in response to the Word of God – praying through him, with him and in him – which is why the prayers are ordinarily made (though not bindingly prescribed) as an addressed to God the Father.

The Church asks that this prayer be relatively short, as opposed to long winded paragraphs, and that they should ordinarily be prayers that gather the whole Church and World into the intentions of this congregation gathered for this Mass on this Sunday. These prayers, therefore, are not ordinarily a moment to be specific about a given private or very local issue. For example, while we might pray for good weather for the farming community to do its important business, we wouldn't be so specific as to pray that Farmer John gets his farming work done. Of course, anyone who knows our local Farmer John can include him in their participation in the prayer – the prayer spoken from the Ambo is usually more wide ranging rather than specific. Exceptions are permitted by the Church when the congregation is gathered for a specific ceremony or celebration: Funerals, Confirmations, Weddings etc.

For the Prayers of the Faithful the Church has guidelines to follow. The order of the prayers is usually: for the needs of the Church, for public authorities and the Salvation of the whole world; for grave difficulties that afflict people and even nations; then finally for the local community. At the end of each prayer the acclamation is spoken: “Lord hear us!” The congregation replies: “Lord, graciously hear us!” We call out to God, confident that he will hear us. We are encouraged by the Word of God which we have recounted to us something of God’s past interventions with the human race; especially in sending His Only Begotten Son, Jesus. In effect, in faith we are presenting him the difficulties that afflict our present world asking him to intervene with his grace, power, mercy and love we know he has done in the past, especially as we have heard from the Sacred Scriptures.

So, the Bidding Prayers are no mere formality. They are meant to be heartfelt pleas to God the Father, made IN the Holy Spirit, BY us, THROUGH Christ Jesus. In faith we call out: “Lord graciously hear us!”
