Richard Walsh fitz James, of Knockmoylan was one of the Constables of the Barony of Knocktopher in 1608. Robert Walsh, of Knockmopylan Castle, apparently son of Richard fitz James married Eleanor Blanchville of Blanchvillewstown and died before the Cromwellian confiscations.

Knockmoylan Castle fell to the ground around 1800. Its site is marked on the Ordnance Map, a little to the south of Knockmoylan village.

It also marks ‘St. Luke’s Well’ at the extreme southern point of Knoc kmoylan townland.

Locally the well is called Luke’s Well and tradition derives this name, not From St. Luke, but from a Luke Murphy who lived beside or owned the well about 1750. It is a holy well and a famous pattern was formerly held here on St. James’ Day, July 25th. Newsletter for the Parishes of Durrow and Rathdowney 24th / 25th October 2026 30th Sunday in Ordinary Time A Victory for the Generations What an extraordinary few days it has been for Durrow and Cullohill!

The celebrations following The Harps' historic victory last Sunday will live long in the memories of everyone associated with the club and our two communities.

And deservedly so. After forty-two years, the dream of winning a senior county championship has finally been realised.

But amidst all the excitement and celebrations, I found myself thinking about another group of people: those who would have loved to see this day but are no longer with us.

A few weeks ago, while working with priests Mayo, I was struck by how often people spoke about their county's great All-Ireland victory, not simply in terms of the present generation, but of parents, grandparents and generations of supporters who had gone before them.

People who followed their team through good times and bad, who dreamed of such a day but never lived to see it.

I suspect there were similar thoughts in many hearts and homes around Durrow and Cullohill last Sunday.

How many former players, parents, grandparents and lifelong supporters would have given anything to witness that final whistle?

Some now have sons, grandsons or even great-grandsons among this extraordinary group of young men.

Their contribution to Sunday's victory may not appear in any match report, but surely it is part of the story.

As we approach November, the month when we traditionally remember our dead, there is something particularly appropriate this year about remembering those who helped build and sustain the clubs down through the generations.

We inherit far more than a name or a place.

We inherit traditions, loyalties, friendships and dreams. Sometimes, without even realising it, we find ourselves completing something that others began long before us.

This Sunday's Gospel reminds us of the great commandment to love God and to love our neighbour.

Perhaps one of the loveliest expressions of that love is the sense of community, belonging and generosity that occasions like last Sunday bring to the surface.

Prayers for Pope Leo Many of us were moved last Sunday when Pope Leo shared, simply and calmly , that doctors had found a small growth on his lung.

On Tuesday he had surgery at Rome’s Gemelli Hospital, where part of his right lung was removed.

The Vatican has since confirmed it was an early-stage lung cancer, and that the operation went well, withou t complications.

He is expected to rest in hospital for about a week, his audiences have been cancelled until 20th October, and he still hopes to make his journey to South America in November.

Let us pray for our Holy Father this week, along with everyone in our own parish facing illness and treatment.

Lord Jesus, Divine Healer, we place our Holy Father, Pope Leo, in your loving care.

Guide the hands of his doctors and nurses, grant him rest, strength and peace, and restore him to full health, that he may continue to shepherd your people with joy.

Mary, Health of the Sick, pray for him. Amen.
