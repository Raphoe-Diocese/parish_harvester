The first big moment of vocation is baptism.

The anointing of chrism at baptism might be

called the anointing for vocation.

The baptismal vocation is for witness, love and

service. This is expressed in ways in which

people live out their baptism in married life,

single life - and within the single life, maybe

religious life or priesthood.

Our active witness is to the life and the values of Jesus in our lives.

Teaching is not itself a vocation, for example, but the way we teach is a

way of living out our vocation. It is the same with many of the helping

professions and employments. Being a good neighbour can be a living

out of our vocation.

We witness to love in marriage, in family, extended family, and in

friendship. Any love is a sharing in the love of God. In the moments of

unselfish love in any relationship, we are living out our vocation. When

we love, we are doing God's will!

We witness to service in the wider world in our care for the poor and in

welcoming the stranger: the widow, the orphan, the immigrant - in fact

anybody needy.

Diocesan Vocations Prayer

God, our Father, at the very beginning of your Church your Son Jesus

chose twelve apostles to continue the work he had begun. By the power

of your Holy Spirit, they continued to bring the Good News of your love to

our world. Send that same Holy Spirit into the hearts of those you are

calling to serve as priests for your people in our diocese. Fill them with

your gifts of wisdom, understanding, piety and knowledge as they

discern your will. Give them the gifts of right judgement and courage in

following their calling. Through the outpouring of your grace may many

come to the joy of knowing and responding to the invitation of Jesus to

“Come, Follow Me! ”

Amen

A count of attendance takes place at all Masses this weekend, 25 & 26

April 2026 . The annual parish census captures valuable information

about the numbers, ages, and gender of people attending Mass. This

information helps the Diocese and parishes to identify pastoral priorities,

make plans to address them, and provide support to our faith

communities. As you arrive at Mass, you are given a small counter for

yourself and for each person in your family group and are asked to place

the counters in baskets labelled according to age. Red counters for

bands and the number of counters for each age band are recorded after

each Mass. Thank you for your patience and co -operation.

Liturgical ‘Sign of Peace ’ The Sign of Peace remains an integral part of

the celebration of the Eucharist and has been reintroduced as a normal

practice across the Diocese. The customary expression of the Sign of Peace

in Ireland is a handshake. However, other non -contact forms of the sign of

peace such as a nod, a bow or a smile are also legitimate expressions of the

Sign of Peace. No offence should be taken if someone prefers to express a

non-contact Sign of Peace. The Diocese encourages all to continue to

exercise good hygienic practices, and if you exhibit flu -like symptoms, you

are encouraged to remain at home.

24 HOURS OF PRAYER FOR VOCATIONS

FEAST OF ST JOHN VIANNEY

Date: 3rd –4th August Venue: St Colmcille ’s Church

On the Feast of St John Vianney, the patron saint of

parish priests, we warmly invite the faithful from across

the Diocese to join us for 24 Hours of Prayer for

Vocations to the Diocesan Priesthood.

The vigil will begin with Holy Mass at 7pm on

Monday, 3rd August, and conclude with Holy Mass at

7pm on Tuesday, 4th August. Throughout the 24 hours,

the Blessed Sacrament will be exposed for Eucharistic

Adoration. Priests will be available at various times

during the night to celebrate the Sacrament of

Reconci liation, offering an opportunity to encounter the

Lord’s mercy as we pray together.

We especially invite our parishes, prayer groups,

schools, religious communities, youth groups, families,

and individuals to come, whether for a few minutes or a

longer pe riod, to pray that the Lord of the harvest will

continue to raise up generous men to serve His people

as diocesan priests.

St John Vianney once said, “The priesthood is the love

of the Heart of Jesus.” As we celebrate his feast, let us

unite in asking the Lord to inspire new vocations and to

strengthen those already called to serve.

Whether you can stay for an hour, join us for Mass,

or simply drop in to pray, your presence and your

prayers will make a real difference.

POPE LEO’S JULY PRAYER INTENTION -

“FOR RESPECT FOR HUMAN LIFE”

Pope Leo XIV has urged Catholics across the globe to

join him this July in praying for respect for human life.

In his monthly “Pray with the Pope” video , prepared

by the Pope’s Worldwide Prayer Network and released

by the Vatican on 2nd July, the Pope prayed for “the

grace to recognise and protect the unique and

unrepeatabl e value of every human being.”

“Each person is a sacred gift that reflects your face from

the instant of existence to the final breath of their

journey on earth” he said in the video.

Pope Leo expressed his wish for everyone to learn to

“welcome life uncon ditionally , to tenderly care for

fragility , to accompany each stage with respect ,

to bravely defend those who have no voice”.

WEEK OF IGNATIAN SPIRITUALITY

Knock Shrine 26th July to 1st August 2026

The Week of Ignatian Spirituality 2026 takes place

from Sunday, July 26th to Saturday, August 1st. The

theme for this year's retreat is 'Jesus, if people only

knew You'.

There are moments when something in us wonders if

we have missed the heart of it all, when life feels

fragmented, and we long for a deeper meaning that can

hold everything together. This week is an invitation to

pause, to encounter Jesus of Nazareth not as an idea, but

as a living presence, one who still calls, still gathers,

still heals and transforms.

Guided by the wisdom of Ignatius , we will explore the

life, mission, and prayer of Jesus, and discover how

God’s quiet work is already unfolding within our own

lives. In a changing and uncertain world, this is a space

to listen again for the deeper story of a God who is

drawing all thing s into love.

You are welcome for the full week or for individual

days. It can serve as a retreat, with spiritual direction

available daily

There is no charge for this event but donations are

welcome. All main inputs will be live -streamed, with

opportuniti es for prayer and shared reflection online

PORTAFERRY AND BALLYGALGET BAPTISM MINISTRY

Our newly commissioned Parish Baptism Team is a voluntary ministry that supports

parents, families and Priests as they prepare to celebrate the Sacrament of Baptism.

Central to their role is ensuring that every family experiences their Parish as a place of genuine

welcome and encouraging faith within the home beyond the day of their Baptism. Our Baptism

Ministers will help parents understand the meaning of the Rite and what it involves by:

• Welcoming parents and children into the Parish community.

• Providing a preparation session for parents (and Godparents if they wish to attend).

• Offering guidance on the symbols, prayers and commitments involved in the Sacrament.

• Helping parents participate more fully in the celebration on the day of Baptism.

May the time of preparation for Baptism be filled with peace, gratitude, and the loving presence of

God in our families.
