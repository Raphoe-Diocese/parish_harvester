Today marks the twenty-seventh Sunday of Ordinary Time. Wearenowatthe beginning of October. Where does the time go? Interestingly, the name ‘October’ comes from the Latin word ‘octō’, which means eight. Itwasthe eighth month in the old Roman calendar, but it kept its name even after January and February were added. October is a month dedicated to the Rosary. Mass of Our Lady of the Rosary will be celebrated on the seventh of October. This feast was established by Pope St. Pius Vto thank the Lord for the naval victory at Lepanto on 7 October 1571, which stopped the Ottoman Turks from invading Europe. Pope St. Pius Vnamed it the Feast of Our Lady of Victory, anditwas originally celebrated on the first Sunday of October. Later, Pope Gregory XIII renamed it the Feast of the Holy Rosary. The Rosary is also known as the ‘Breviary of the Common People’ (because it has 150 Hail Marys, just like the Breviary has 150 Psalms ) and it is also named ‘Psalms of the Illiterate’. The prayers wesayare from the Bible, so they’re ‘inspired’, and the mysteries we think about come from the lives of Jesus and Mary. The ‘Our Father’ is a prayer that Jesus himself taught us, andthe ‘Hail Mary’ is also based on the Scriptures. The first part of the ‘Hail Mary’ is what the Archangel Gabriel and Elizabeth said to Mary. And the third prayer, the ‘Glory betothe Father’, is a long- standing prayer of adoration and praise, just like what we find in the Book of Revelation. The Rosary invites us to reflect on the events in Jesus and Mary’s lives, which are like chapters in the Paschal Mystery—the Life, Death and Resurrecti on of Jesus. The Rosary’s goal is to help us ponder the big mysteries of our salvation. This weekend, the Gospel (Matthew 21:33-43) tells us about the Vineyard. It’s a carefully planned place, surrounded by hedges or fences, where God has intentionally set aside a space for his people to grow and mature in his ways, producing the fruits of love and peace. This Gospel reveals three important things about God: 1. God trusts us. He doesn’t hover over the vineyard’s cultivators like a police officer. 2. God is patient with us. The master sent messengers repeatedly to the vineyard, not rushing in with harsh punishment when one messenger had been mistreated. He gives the cultivators many chances to respond and repent. God is patient with usinour sins and doesn’t abandon us. 3. God will come to judge us. There will beaday when we will all be held accountable forhowwe have carried out the tasks God has given us. However in today’s second reading from Paul to the Philippians (4:6-9), he gently reminds us: ‘There is no need to worry; but if there is anything you need, pray forit, asking Godforit with prayer and thanksgiving, and that peace of God which isso much greater than we can understand will guard your thoughts and hearts in Christ Jesus’. RELECTING ON MERCY & FORGIVENESS In our Catholic understanding of marriage, mercy and forgiveness are not simply helpful qualities; they are part of the sacrament’s spiritual life. Marriage brings two imperfect people into a lifelong covenant in which each is called to reveal something o f Christ’s faithful and merciful love. Mercy sees the person before the fault. A husband or wife may act selfishly, speak carelessly or disappoint the other. Mercy does not pretend the wrong did not happen, but refuses to reduce the person to that failure. Forgiveness is a decision as well as a feeling. A spouse may choose to forgive before the hurt has completely disappeared. Healing can take time, but forgiveness can prevent resentment from taking root and can open the way to reconciliation and peace. THE MONTH OF THE HOLY ROSARY The celebration of the Month of the Rosary goes back toam oment in history when prayer changed everything. In 1571, during the Battle of Lepanto, Christians across Europe prayed the Rosary for peace a nd protection. Against overwhelming odds, victory came, and the Church recognized this as the intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary. Soon after, the Church established October as a month dedicated to the Rosary, inviting the faithful to turn to Our Blessed Mother in times of need. Today, that invitation is still opentoall! The Rosary: A Global Prayer That Unites the Church The Rosary isn’t just a personal devotion, it’s a global mission. Right now, over 1.3 billion Catholics worldwide are united in praye r; the Rosary is prayed daily on every continent and missionaries oft en rely on the Rosary as a primary spiritual tool in remote communiti es. From villages in Africa to urban centers in Latin America, the Rosary crosses language barriers and cultural differences. It’s simple. It’s accessible. And it’s powerful! Why the Rosary Still Matters in Today’s World We live in a world filled with anxiety, division, and uncertainty.
