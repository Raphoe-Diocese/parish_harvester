PARISH NEWSLETTER 25th October 2026 THIRTIETH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME We must Love God and neighbour

The Jewish people had often wondered which is the greatest commandment. In addition to

the Ten Commandments, the Rabbis had counted 613 other precepts of the Mosaic Law.

But no Rabbi had been able to answer irrefutably the question about the greatest

commandment of the Law. Our Lord Jesus Christ was the only one in Jewish life and

history to satisfactorily answer the question: ‘which is the greatest commandment of the

Law?’

When the Pharisees asked Jesus in our Gospel passage, ‘which is the greatest

commandment of the Law?’ they did so in order to embarrass him. In his answer, Jesus

makes it abundantly clear that the greatest commandment is the love of God. Jesus quotes

Deuteronomy 6:5. “You must love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your

soul and with all your might.” Jesus goes further to add the second greatest

commandment, namely, the love of neighbour. Jesus cites Leviticus 19:18: “You shall

love your neighbour as yourself.”

The First Reading warns against oppressing or doing harm to the poor, widow, orphan,

and stranger. To love God and our neighbour requires showing practical concern for

others, especially the most vulnerable, and those who are forced to live on the margins of

society. Jesus teaches us in today’s Gospel that the love of God and love of neighbour are

inseparable.

As Christians, we need to ask ourselves: Deep within my heart, do I really love God? Do I

really love my neighbour? If I do, it will show in my actions. Love is something practical.

If we truly love God and others, it will show by our dedication to God and our concern for

the needy in our society. The Christian life can be rightly summed up as the love of God

and neighbour. Jesus commands us: “Love one another as I have loved you” (John 15:12).

Let us strive and pray for the grace to continually grow in the love of God and neighbour. - Fr. Callistus

TODAY - Thirtieth Sunday in Ordinary Time YOU SHALL NOT WRONG A SOUJOURNER Immigration has become a fraught political subject.

The global wealth inequality, particularly between the northern hemisphere compared to the southern hemisphere, has led to mass migration of peoples seeking a better life for themselves and their children.

The Catechism of the Catholic Church does recognise the right of states to restrict their borders: “Political authorities, for the sake of the common good for which they are responsible, may make the exercise of the right to immigrate subject to various juridical conditions, especially with regarc to the immigrants’ duties toward their country of adoption.

Immigrants are obliged to respect with gratitude the material and spiritual heritage of the country that receives them, to obey its laws, and to assist in carrying civic burdens.” (Catechism of the Catholic Church §2241).

Those who migrate into another country must be treated with dignity and respect.

Pope Leo XVI, in his homily at the Mass 4th July 2026 on the Italian island of Lampedusa said of the challenges of migration: "Europe is capable of addressing the crisis in this region in a comprehensive manner, integrating immediate relief efforts into a long-term strategic plan capable of receiving, protecting, supporting and integrating migrants" while "assisting developing countries so that no one is forced to emigrate.

We live in a country with a history where millions were forced to immigrate to other lands due to economic deprivation at home.

Pope John XXIII wrote in Pacem in Terris (§25, 1963): “Every human being has the right to the freedom of movement and of residence within the confines of their country; and, when there are just reasons for it, the right to emigrate and take up residence elsewhere.” Pope Leo XVI reminds us that human dignity does not have a passport and does not lose value when a border is crossed.

On World Refugee Day 11" June 2026 on the island of Gran Canaria he said: “Human dignity demands legal and safe pathways, rescue and assistance, real cooperation against traffickers, effective protection for victims, serious processes of reception and integration, and policies that allow every person to live with dignity in their own land.” Catholic communities must play their part in assisting and helping to integrate those who immigrate into our country. 30'" Week in Ordinary Time,
