Thought for the Week

Living each day as if it was your last is a powerful concept that can help us to live more fully and with greater purpose.

It reminds us that our time on earth is limited and that we should make the most of every moment.

It also encourages us to focus on what is truly important in life, such as our relationships with others and our relationship with God.

THE WISDOM OF VIGILANCE THROUGH VIRTUE! As we draw closer to the end of the church’s calendar year, the of the readings especially the gospel will sound more eschatological (of final judgement), pr esenting different aspects of God’s judgement thereby exhorting us towards r eadiness for God’s kingdom.

Of what use is our spiritual journey if it is not geared towards a final end? – the need to be ready.

The Readings : First reading (Wis 6:12-16) from book of wisdom presents w isdom as a maiden radiant and unfading willing to encounter those who desire to know her.

Wisdom as a virtue, we must remember, is one of the greatest values in the ancient I srael, valued much than wealth and influence.

It’s believed to come from God and understood primarily as the art of living a good, virtuous life.

That’s why the five virgins in the Gospel (Mt.25:1-13) who were vigilant and did what they were expected were refer red to as “wise” virgins.

In the second reading (1 Thes 4:13-18), St Paul exhorts the Thessalonians who were expecting the imminent return of the Lord and worried about their dead relatives, to remain vigilant in their hope and not loose faith because at the coming of the Lord there wouldn’t be any difference between those dead in Christ a nd those still living and believing.

All will be taken up with to eternal life.

Take Home : We notice vigilance as a theme running through the readings.

It is w isdom in the eyes of the Lord to be ready.

To be ready in grace, in v irtue; not ready with fear of the final end/judgement. but more especially for the love of the peace and joy and serenity that comes with the presence life of God in you.

There is a joy, serenity and confidence that accompanies living a life in the Holy spirit.

We need to long for and trust in the guidance of God’s wisdom to make good choices.

Like the Psalmist today: “For you my soul is thirsting, O Lord, my God”. Prayer : Lord that we may know your wisdom to be vigilant to our Chr istian calling.

Amen. - Fr. John PARISH NEWSLET TER 8th November 2026 THIRTY SECOND SUNDAY IN ORDINARY T IME ROSARY Will be recited on Sunday evenings at 4 p.m. during the month of November in St. Coman's Cemetery.

Dd Parable of the Wise and Foolish Virgins Ger McDonnell from Kilkornan, Co. Limerick, was the first Irishman to scale K2 on the 1st of August 2008. K2 is the second highest mountain in the world and most believe more difficult to summit than Mount Everest. Due to an avalanche Ger chose to turn back and assist in rescuing climbers who were trapped and injured.

Unfortunately, despite his bravery and heroism, he was killed a day after he summited due to a second massive ice avalanche, killing ten people, in the deadliest single accident in K2 history.

Many climbers do not help other climbers in the “kill zone ” due to the extremely harsh and dangerous conditions.

In the parable of the wise and foolish virgins, the wise virgins realise that if they assist the foolish virgins (Matthew 25:1-13) no one will make it into the wedding feast. The unconditional love of Christ does not mean that we have no personal responsibility to love in return.

The faith of others cannot pray us into heaven.

You cannot loan out your relationship to God to someone else.

We must be prepared and ready as the day of judgment is unknown at all of us and it will happen at a time we do not expect.

As Pope Francis posed the thought: “It would be good to consider for a moment: one day will be the last. If it were today, how prepared am I? ” Picture on Front: Parable of the Wise and Foolish Virgins, The Gospel of Rossano, Rossano Cathedral (6th Century) TODAY - Thirty-Second Sunday in Ordinary Time 32nd Week in Ordinary Time,
