Reflection: The Saints as Stained Glass Windows I think of the saints as stained- glass windows.

An ordinary window lets in the sunlight but the stained-glass window gives colour, shape and story to the light.

The saints in their various ways show us how to live the Christian life.

The saints manifest the holy side of the Church.

They give shape, story and colour to the light of God.

They inspire us by their holy lives, instruct us by the ir teaching and give us their protection in answer to our prayers.

I know a lot of saints, don’t you? ( by Christy Kenneally) One of the most striking aspects of All Saints’ Day is its universality.

Unli ke feasts dedicated to specific saints, this day commemorates all the holy men and women who have faithfully followed Christ, even if their names and deeds are lost to history.

At its heart, All Saints’ Day is an expression of hope.

It points to the transformative power of God’s grace, which turns ordinary people into extraordinary witnesses of love, charity, and faith.

The saints, in all their diversity, provide us with a glimpse of what it means to in communion with God.

It reminds us that sainthood is not limited to a select few who achieved great or accomplished extraordinary feats; rather, it encompasses countless individuals whose lives, while perhaps quiet or humble, were nonetheless deeply rooted in fait h.

These are the mothers and fathers, the workers and neighbours, the ordinary people who lived out thei r discipleship in simple yet profound ways.

This day calls us to remembe r that holiness is not reserved for the few but is open to all of us.

In the Beatitudes which ar read on All Saints’ Day Jesus lays out the path to holiness in a way that is both universal and personal.

Blessed are the poor in spirit, the meek, the peacemakers, and those who hunger and thirst for righteousness.

These words reveal that sainthood is less about perfection & more about a life oriented toward God & others.

The saints are models for us, not because they were sinless, but because they allowed God’s grace to work in and through them, even in their weakness Prayer on Lighting a Candle during November: As you hold this candle in your hand, recall the memory of your loved one.

A NOTE FROM FR.

As we celebrate this weekend the Feast of All Saints, I'd like to share a powerful sermon by the American preacher John Piper, entitled Don't Waste Your Life.

He began by saying, “The really sad thing is that there are hundreds of you in this crowd who don't want your lives to make a difference.

He spoke about two elderly women in their eighties, Ruby Eliason and Laura Edwards, who had devoted their lives to serving Jesus Christ among the poor and sick in Cameroon.

Both were tragically killed in an accident while carrying out their mission.

Piper asked a very interesting question: “Was this a tragedy?

His answer was no. These women had given their lives to something that would last for eternity.

He contrasted that with an article he had read in Reader's Digest, entitled Start Now, Retire Early. “Bob and Penny took early retirement five years ago from their jobs in the Northeast. He was 59; she was 51. They now live in Punta Gorda, Florida, where they cruise on their 30-foot trawler, play softball and collect shells!" "Now that's a tragedy!

Imagine in the last chapter of your life, standing before the Creator of the universe to give an account of what you did with your life: “Here it is, Lord, my shell collection!

And look at my boat!" It reminded me of the quote attributed to Joan of Arc: “I know this now.

Every man gives his life for what he believes.

Every woman gives her life for what she believes.

Sometimes people believe in little or nothing, and yet they give their lives to that little or nothing.

One life is all we have, and we live it as we believe in living it, and then it's gone.

But to surrender what you are and to live without belief is more terrible than dying—even more terrible than dying young.

On this Feast of All Saints, let us realise the truth of the words of St. Irenaeus: “The only real tragedy in life is not to become a saint."
