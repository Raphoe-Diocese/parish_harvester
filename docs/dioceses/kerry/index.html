(Matha 25:12-15) Chugaibh an fear nuaphósta!

Amach Tá teachtaireacht soiléir ag an parabal sa léacht seo – bheith réidh bualadh leis an Tiarna nuair a thagann sé. Tá an léacht roghnaithe go cuí agus sinn ag druidim le deir eadh bhliain an liotúirge, agus an glaoch a ghabhann leis sin bheith ullamh le teagmháil leis an Tiarna ag deireadh ár saoil, a bheith ag faire de bhrí nach bhfuil a fhios againn an láná an uair.

Naomh Mártan (11 Samhain) Rugadh Naomh Mártan san Úngáir sa bhliain 316. In aois a cúig déag bhí sé ina shaighdiúir in arm na Róimhe.

Cuireadh a chathlán go dtí Amiens na Fraince (Gaul) agus is ansúd a thárla míorúilt na fallainge.

Lá amháin agus é gar do gheataí na cathrach, chonaic sé bochtán caite ar thaobh na sráid agus é leata leis an bhfuacht . Free event. Reflection: In the framing of today’s parable, Jesus is referring to early Palestinian Jewish marriage customs, which tell us that the waiting maidens are likely young family members of the bridegroom.

In other words, they know him. They may have grown up with him.

They have a relationship with him. Furthermore, a delay in his arrival is to be expected because the final marriage contract needs to be negotiated before the celebration can take place.

The maidens who love the bridegroom dearly —the wise ones —are excited enough in their anticipation of this event to prepare.

Their focus is on their love for him and the celebration (the party) that will commence upon his arrival.

The “foolish” virgins on the other hand —and notice the word is “foolish,” not forgetful —don’t bother to prepare.

They, like the wise, also know the groom, but their focus is not on him or his important day.

The celebration these maidens know will be coming isn’t enough of a priority to pull them away from their foolish pursuits to prepare themselves.

The foolish virgins love for him is not the same as that of the wise.

We know this because what we love always guides our actions and choices, always guides how we spend our time.

A Candle of Hope! November is a month when we remember those who have died, especially our family members, friends and neighbours who are no longer with us.

One of the simple ways we express that remembran ce is by lighting a candle.

A candle may be small, but it carries a beautiful message.

Its light reminds us that love does not end with death, that memories remain precious, and that the light of Christ continues to shine even in the darkness of loss.

When we light a candle for someone we love, it is also a prayer.

We place that person in God’s loving care and ask that they enjoy the peace and happiness of heaven.

During November, I invite you to light a candle in the church or at home for someone speci al who has died.

As a little flame burns, may it bring comfort to our hearts and remind us that those we love are never forgotten.

This Sunday’s Gospel leaves us with a very simple but important question: If Jesus came into our lives today, would He find our lamps burning?

Would He find us living with faith? Would He find us forgiving those who have hurt us?

REFLECTION A peacocks feathers are very colourful; they are worn more for show than for warmth.

The Pharisees wore their virtue for show; they wanted to be seen and praised by others.

They were more concerned with appearing good than with really being good.

Today there is great emphasis on appearances.

The image is more important than the reality.

We may deceive others but we cannot deceive God.

But then it would be foolish to even try.

We don’t have to put on an outward show, or pretend to be what we are not.

All we have to do is try to be true to what we are - God’s sons and daughters.

Lord, help us to shun all falsity and pretence, and to live a life of genuine goodness.

Then our deeds will flow from what we are, as naturally as good fruit from a good tree.

Thank you. A NOTE FROM FR. You’ll notice that all our daily and Sunday Gospels at this time of year are very apocalyptic.

The theme is “Be Ready!” This weekend’s Gospel usesa very familiar analogy for a first-century Jew: bridesmaids awaiting the coming of the bridegroom to accom pany him and his bride to a hall to celebrate the wedding feast. Of course, Jesus is the Bridegroom who, as Scripture tells us , wants to marry His people.

The bridesmaids represent all of us. We too can be either wise or foolish.

All the bridesmaids look v ery similar. All are invited, all have lamps, all are waiting, and indeed, all fall asleep!

This is a reminde r that we’re all similar in the sense that we all have the capacity to be saints and si nners.

We can be strong and we can be weak. But what makes or foolish depends on whether or not we are prepared for our eternal union with God and have done what we can to accept His invitation to Heaven when the time comes.

Some of us have an eternal perspective; others have a here-and-now perspective.

Some have oil in their flasks; others don’t.

And this oil represents our spiritual readine ss to meet Christ — a living faith, nourished by daily prayer, God’s grace, good works and a personal relationship with Him.

I’ve always wondered, especially from a Chr istian perspective, why didn’t the wise bridesmaids share their oil with the foolish ones?

The answer is that there are certain things we cannot share, c ertain things we cannot borrow from another person.

You cannot borrow someone else’s character.

You cann ot borrow someone else’s relationship with God.

You cannot borrow someone else’s spiritual preparation.

And finally, then, come some of the most frightening words in the whole Bible.

When the foolish bridesmaids eve ntually arrive, they find the door closed . They cryout, “Lord, Lord, open to us.” But the bridegroom replies, “Truly, I say to you, I do not know you.” Their failure not a lack of intention, but a lack of preparation.

There is a great difference between intending to live a Christian life and actually living one.

We can spend years saying: “When I have more time, I’ll pray.” “When things settle down, return to Mass.” “One of these days, I’ll go to Confession.” “Someday, I’ll make peace with that person.” But the Gospel reminds us that som eday is not a date on the calendar So, ask yourself the question: God forbid, if we died today, would you have oil in your lamp?
