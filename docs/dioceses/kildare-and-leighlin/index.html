To be completed and returned to Bishop’s House by 12th March 2026 . Thanks too to their parents, the group leaders and parish priests who supported the facilitation of these groups.

National Board for Safeguarding Children in the Catholic Church in Ireland – Biannual conference The held their biannual conference from 3rd – 5th September 2025 titled: Towards a Restorative and Transitional Justice model of engaging with victims and survivors in the Catholic Church in Ireland.

The event aimed to enhance the understanding we all have of how we must engage in a just and restorative way with those who have suffered harm in the Catholic Church in Ireland.

Our diocesan director of safeguarding and DLP attended the event and shared valuable feedback with our diocesan safeguarding committee.

The learning from the conference will help guide our practice going forward.

Special thanks to the National Board for organising and hosting the event.

ARLES DOONANE PARISH NEWSLETTER First Reading A reading from the book of Wisdom is bright and does not grow dim.

By those who love her she is readily seen and found by those who look for her.

Quick to anticipate those who desire her, she makes herself known to them.

Watch for her early and you will have no trouble; you will find her sitting at your gates.

Even to think about her is understanding fully grown; be on the alert for her and anxiety will quickly leave you.

She herself walks about looking for those who are worthy of her and graciously shows herself to them as they go, in every thought of theirs coming to meet them.

The Word of the Lord Responsorial Psalm Response: For you my soul is thirsting, O God, my God.

Second Reading A reading from the first letter of St. Paul to the Thessalonians We want you to be quite certain, brothers, about those who have died, to make sure that you do not grieve about them, like the other people who have no hope.

We believe that Jesus died and rose again, and that it will be the same for those who have died in Jesus: God will bring them with him.

We can tell you this from the Lord’s own teaching, that any of us who are left alive until the Lord’s coming will not have any advantage over those who have died.

At the trumpet of God, the voice of the archangel will call out the command and the Lord himself will come down from heaven; those who have died in Christ will be the first to rise, and then those of us who are still alive will be taken up in the clouds, together with them; to meet the Lord in the air.

So we shall stay with the Lord for ever.

With such thoughts as these you should comfort one another.

The Word of the Lord. Gospel Acclamation Alleluia, alleluia!

Stay awake and stand ready, because you do not know the hour when the Son of Man is coming.

Alleluia! Gospel A reading from the holy Gospel according to Matthew Jesus told this parable to his disciples: ‘The kingdom of heaven will be like this: Ten bridesmaids took their lamps and went to meet the bridegroom.

Five of them were foolish and five were sensible: the foolish ones did take their lamps, but they brought no oil, whereas the sensible ones took flasks of oil as well as their lamps.

The bridegroom was late, and they all grew drowsy and fell asleep.

But at midnight there was a cry,“The bridegroom is here!

Go out and meet him.” At this, all those bridesmaids woke up and trimmed their lamps, and the foolish ones said to the sensible ones, “Give us some of your oil: our lamps are going out.” But they replied, “There may not be enough for us and for you; you had better go to those who sell it and buy some for yourselves.” They had gone off to buy it when the bridegroom arrived.

Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding hall and the door was closed.

The other bridesmaids arrived later. “Lord, Lord,” they said “open the door for us.” But he replied, “I tell you solemnly, I do not know you.” So stay awake, because you do not know either the day or the hour.

All support greatly appreciated. The Five Pathways of the Irish Synodal Pathway: Over the past five years the Irish Church has asked the question of itself: “What is God asking of the Church in Ireland at this time?” The National Synodal Assembly in October was the culmination of this discernment.

Delegates made their recommendations on concrete proposals for action.

These proposals were housed under five pathways that emerged in the final phase of discernment.

The pathways now give us a vision for the Church in Ireland that have their root in local conversations in parishes across the country.

The five pathways describe a vision of the Church that proclaims the gospel; the Church as a community of belonging; the Church as a place of healing; the Church that accompanies; the Church that empowers for ministry and co-responsibility.

See Kandle.ie/synod/ or synod.ie to view the working document of the assembly where you can read more about the five pathways and the concrete proposals.

Message from Bishop Denis Nulty Sunday, February 22, is Diocesan Safeguarding Sunday.

It is the first Sunday of Lent and appropriately so, as we enter the Penitential Season and seek forgiveness for past failings.

In his address at the January Consistory Pope Leo XIV reminded all that “abuse itself causes a deep wound, which may last a lifetime; but often the scandal in the Church is that the door was closed and victims were not welcomed or accompanied with the closeness of authentic pastors”. This year we have intentionally linked the ‘Day of Prayer for Survivors and Victims of Abuse’ on Friday, February 20 with ‘Safeguarding Sunday’ two days later.

The National Child Safeguarding Policy for our Church is called ‘A Safe and Welcoming Church’ and that is what our diocese is and our parishes are, a safe and welcoming Church.

The fact that ‘Safeguarding Sunday’ has become such a regular part of our Diocesan and Parish life, demonstrates how the safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults has been so well embedded into every area of the life of our diocese, its 56 parishes and 117 faith communities.

Safeguarding Sunday is once again an opportunity to acknowledge and recognise the years of dedication and work by so many to ensure that Kildare & Leighlin Diocese is a safe place for children and all who work with them.

It’s an opportunity to thank all who today ensure safeguarding is so well embedded in our diocesan culture, Ailish Higgins, our Director of Safeguarding, currently on maternity leave, Michele Hughes, our Acting Designated Liaison Person, the Diocesan Safeguarding Committee, our Parish Safeguarding Representatives, our priests, our deacons and our people, particularly the many who volunteer at parish level, ensuring the life and vitality of their faith community.

As ever, on my parish visits, I encourage ministry with children and young people, this is an essential part of the mission of the Church.

Where altar serving, youth choirs, Sunday School have returned I welcome them warmly; where they haven’t for whatever reason, once again I encourage their early return.

The life of a parish is often defined by the active engagement of young people in that parish.

Safeguarding Sunday is a reassurance to all that being involved in the parish is governed by best practices in safeguarding.

Message from Bishop Denis Nulty Sunday, February 22, is Diocesan Safeguarding Sunday.

It is the first Sunday of Lent and appropriately so, as we enter the Penitential Season and seek forgiveness for past failings.

In his address at the January Consistory Pope Leo XIV reminded all that “abuse itself causes a deep wound, which may last a lifetime; but often the scandal in the Church is that the door was closed and victims were not welcomed or accompanied with the closeness of authentic pastors”. This year we have intentionally linked the ‘Day of Prayer for Survivors and Victims of Abuse’ on Friday, February 20 with ‘Safeguarding Sunday’ two days later.

The National Child Safeguarding Policy for our Church is called ‘A Safe and Welcoming Church’ and that is what our diocese is and our parishes are, a safe and welcoming Church.

The fact that ‘Safeguarding Sunday’ has become such a regular part of our Diocesan and Parish life, demonstrates how the safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults has been so well embedded into every area of the life of our diocese, its 56 parishes and 117 faith communities.

Safeguarding Sunday is once again an opportunity to acknowledge and recognise the years of dedication and work by so many to ensure that Kildare & Leighlin Diocese is a safe place for children and all who work with them.

It’s an opportunity to thank all who today ensure safeguarding is so well embedded in our diocesan culture, Ailish Higgins, our Director of Safeguarding, currently on maternity leave, Michele Hughes, our Acting Designated Liaison Person, the Diocesan Safeguarding Committee, our Parish Safeguarding Representatives, our priests, our deacons and our people, particularly the many who volunteer at parish level, ensuring the life and vitality of their faith community.

As ever, on my parish visits, I encourage ministry with children and young people, this is an essential part of the mission of the Church.

Where altar serving, youth choirs, Sunday School have returned I welcome them warmly; where they haven’t for whatever reason, once again I encourage their early return.

The life of a parish is often defined by the active engagement of young people in that parish.

Safeguarding Sunday is a reassurance to all that being involved in the parish is governed by best practices in safeguarding.

The Second (Offertory) Collection covers the day-to-day costs of running the parish, e.g. energy costs, staff wages, running costs for the parish offices and shops, etc.

Fundraising

An increase in the total for the Repairs and Maintenance category was due to the improvements to the lighting both inside and outside Ballycane Church and some initial expenditure relating to the building work for the new Parish Centre on the Sallins Road.

The ‘talent’ was an unreal sum of money to be responsible for as one ‘talent’ was equivalent to fifteen year’s wages.

The slaves are not all ex pected to have the same outcomes, but their master does expect some progress.

While the first two sl aves make great returns, the third slave buries his talent.

This was one way of keeping money safe in the ancient world (13:44), but it also removed liability from the person who buried it.

This poor slave is afraid of failure and appears a bit lazy; he does nothing with his talent and so loses everything.

While the word ‘talent’ comes from this very parable, it is about more than our gifts.

It points tow ards a bigger picture: how will Jesus’ followers be in this world following his death?

We have no idea when the ‘end-times’ will come, but we do know that each of us has a limited amount of time at our disposal; we will all, ev entually, run out of time!

The parable asks Matthew’s community, and us, how we might live responsibly, using the opportunities that are given to us for the good of the world and t he glory of God.

Digging a hole and hiding our gifts, and in doing so hiding ourselves, is certainly not the way to go.

Everything is moving towards fulfilment, our very lives and all of creation.

So the challenge put before us here is, what will we do with the time that has been given to us?

The parable encourages us to use our time and gifts creatively, embracing adventure, being curious about what possibilities might come if we take a chance.

Jesus calls us to the fullness of life so that we can enter into joy.
