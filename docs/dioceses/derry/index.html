Season of Creation: The Season of Creation takes place from 1st September to 4th October, bringing Christians throughout the world together in prayer, reflection and action for the care of our common home. We all love a good story especially with a happy ending, but today's story or parable doesn't seem to have such a happy ending, or does it? Inallor most of the stories about Jesus, we hear about trees, vineyards a corn field and more. This was the world in which Jesus lived, moved and taught. So, it’snot surprising that his stories or parables were on themes his listeners would recognize and, in this case, in this case the vineyard. But this story is also for us. What can we make of it? What or where is the vineyard today? Maybe it's a bigger place, or what Pope Francis called ‘our common home’, or the home we share with all creation or nature. Having experienced a summer of contrasts, rain and extreme heat, we realize increasingly that our well-being as the human family, is interwoven with the well-being ofallof God’s creation. Right now, weareinthe Season of Creation, that time set aside to celebrate God's gift of creation and our responsibility to care for it. Maybe this is our vineyard! We are being, as it were, sent into it - to do what? First, togetto know it, to love it, to give thanks for it, which then leads us to care for it. Each Season of Creation has a theme, and the theme this year is ‘Living Water. As we think of a vineyard, we can presume it needed water, the right amount at the right time. Our Common Home, all of nature including the human family needs living water, the right amount at the right time. Our symbol for 2026 - Immersion in Living Water - is a symbol of renewal and birth: water flowing from the Temple as a sign of God’s caring and healing forus. Weare called to step into the water, until weareso deep that we must depend on God’s grace to sustain us. Those who receive this water are i nvited to become sources of life for others, allowing God’s love to flow through them into the world. And thus, the water of God will become inus“a spring of water welling up to eternal life”, as Jesus promised (John 4:14). A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 21:28-32 In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells the story of two sons. One says “no”tohis father but later changes his mind and goes to work; the other says “yes” but does not go. Jesus reminds us that what matters is not simply what wesay, buthowwe live our response to God’s call. This Gospel speaks beautifully tousasa parish community at this time, following the installation last week of Bishop Michael Router asthenew Bishop of Derry. At his installation, Bishop Michael spoke of the importance of listening, humility and walking together as a Church. He acknowledged that he has much to learn and said that perhaps the best place foranew bishop to begin is“notby talking too much, but by listening.” The Gospel challenges each ofustoask: Howam I responding to God’s invitation? It is easy tosay“yes”to faith with our words, but the Lord calls us to make that “yes” visible through kindness, forgiveness, service and care for others. As Bishop Michael begins his ministry among us, maywe pray that he will be guided by the Holy Spirit, and that together wemaybea Church that does not merely speak about faith, but lives it. Mayour words and our actions increasingly say the same thing: “Yes, Lord, I will go.” Lord, bless Bishop Michael as he begins his service among the people of the Diocese of Derry.
