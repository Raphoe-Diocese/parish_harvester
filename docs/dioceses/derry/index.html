Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 18:15– 20
In today’s Gospel, Jesus teaches that reconciliation is a sacred responsibility , rooted
in love, humility and a desire for restored relationship.
When conflict or hurt arises, the first response should not be anger, gossip, avoidance
or revenge, but a quiet and honest conversation with the person involved. This private
dialogue gives space for listening, understanding and healing .
If the conflict cannot be resolved alone, Jesus encourages the involvement of trusted
witnesses — not to shame or accuse, but to help guide the conversation with wisdom,
fairness and care. When difficulties continue, the wider community has a role in
encouraging accountability, unity and peace.
Remember LOVE
L — Listen with humility
Hear one another deeply and seek to understand.
O — Offer reconciliation
Choose restoration and forgiveness over pride.
V — Value community
Protect the unity, peace and wellbeing of the community.
E — Embrace divine presence
Trust that God is present wherever people gather in faith, prayer, forgiveness and
harmony.
Jesus reminds us that love is more than a feeling; it is a way of living . Through
patience, courage, humility and faith, conflict can become an opportunity for healing,
forgiveness and renewed fellowship.
May the peace of Christ guide our hearts, strengthen our unity, and fill our community
with love, forgiveness and grace. Amen.

Feast Days this Week
Tuesday 8th September: Nativity of Mary. We celebrate the Birth of Mary, who
brought Jesus Christ the dawn of hope and salvation into the world. She is the Ark of
the Covenant, the temple of God, because her body r eceived the one Mediator.
Wednesday 9th September: St. Ciaran was born in Roscommon around 512. He
came to Clonmacnoise in January 545 where he founde d a monastery which was to
become one of the most renowned in Europe. He died at the age of thirty-three while
the monastery was still being built.
Thursday 10th September: St. Peter Claver was born in Verdu, Catalonia, in 1580
and studied at the University of Barcelona. He bec ame a Jesuit; and while he was
studying philosophy in Mallorca, the doorkeeper of the college, Alfonso Rodríguez, saw
that his true vocation was to evangelize the New Wo rld, and encouraged him to fulfil
that vocation. (Rodríguez was later canonized on t he same day as Peter Claver himself).
He arrived in Cartagena, in what is now the country of Colombia, in 1610, and after his
ordination six years later he became a servant to t he native people, labouring on their
behalf for 33 years, attending to both their spirit ual and material needs. The slave trade
around at the time was repeatedly condemned by the Popes, but it was too profitable
to be stopped and on the whole the local church hie rarchy kept quiet about it, much as
they did in North America in the 19th century. He brought fresh food to the slave-ships
as they arrived, instructed the slaves and baptized them in the faith, followed their
progress and kept track of them even when they were sent to the mines and plantations,
defending them as best he could from oppressive sla ve-owners. At the end of his life
he fell ill with a degenerative disease and for fou r years he was treated neglectfully and
brutally by the servant whose task it was to look a fter him. He did not complain but
accepted his sufferings as a penance for his sins.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 18:15 -20 )
In today’s gospel, Jesus teaches that reconciliation is a sacred responsibility r ooted in
love, humility and a desire for restored relationship. When conflict or hurt ar ises, the
first response should not be anger, gossip, avoidance or revenge, but a quiet and
honest conversation with the person involved. This private dialogue gives space f or
listening, understanding and healing. If the conflict cannot be resolved alone, J esus
encourages the involvement of trusted witnesses, not to shame or accuse, but to help
guide the conversation with wisdom, fairness and care. When difficulties continue, the
wider community has a role in encouraging accountability, unity and peace.
This message can be remembered through the word LOVE :
Listen with humility,
Offer reconciliation,
Value community, and
Embrace divine presence.
Love calls us to hear one another deeply, choose restoration over pride, protect unity,
and trust that God is present wherever people gather in faith, prayer, forgiveness, and
harmony. Overall, Jesus reminds us that love is more than a feeling; it is a w ay of living.
Through patience, courage, humility, and faith, conflict can become an opportunity for
healing, forgiveness, and renewed fellowship.
May the peace of Christ guide our hearts, strengthen our unity, and
fill our community with love, forgiveness and grace. Amen.

granted.

our hearts" and to pray that the Lord will renew our

part in World Youth Day in Seoul, South Korea, next
year, that they will take courage, and become streams of
Living Water, bringing faith, hope, love, and care for
creation into the life of the Church and the world.
Let us pray together.
Let us act together.
Let us make space for conversion.
Let us make Living Water flow.

The Famine Bell
We have recently acquired, through the
good auspices of the Late Father Liam
Donnelly, a very old church bell. This bell
was cast during the famine years.
The bell has been placed in the old
cemetery near the old church ruin in
memory of the victims of the Great
Famine, many of whom are buried here. It is also a memorial to
all victims of conflict in the years since the buil ding of the
Church in 1803.
The bell also reminds us that the law of the land forbade Church
bells when the old church was built. This was one of the Penal
Laws.
Going further back, it also links us to the Great St. Patricks Bell,
which was for many years concealed in Mayogall for safety.
Since the bell was erected last week it has become a focal
point of great interest. It marks a site or remembr ance and
prayer.
The design and construction of the bell was the great work of
Packie Duggan, Andreus and Mark. It is an outstandi ng
addition to the parish along with the great work al ready
carried out in the old cemetery.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 18:15 -20 )
In today’s gospel, Jesus teaches that reconciliation is a sacred responsibility r ooted in
love, humility and a desire for restored relationship. When conflict or hurt ar ises, the
first response should not be anger, gossip, avoidance or revenge, but a quiet and
honest conversation with the person involved. This private dialogue gives space f or
listening, understanding and healing. If the conflict cannot be resolved alone, J esus
encourages the involvement of trusted witnesses, not to shame or accuse, but to help
guide the conversation with wisdom, fairness and care. When difficulties continue, the
wider community has a role in encouraging accountability, unity and peace.
This message can be remembered through the word LOVE :
Listen with humility,
Offer reconciliation,
Value community, and
Embrace divine presence.
Love calls us to hear one another deeply, choose restoration over pride, protect unity,
and trust that God is present wherever people gather in faith, prayer, forgiveness, and
harmony. Overall, Jesus reminds us that love is more than a feeling; it is a w ay of living.
Through patience, courage, humility, and faith, conflict can become an opportunity for
healing, forgiveness, and renewed fellowship.
May the peace of Christ guide our hearts, strengthen our unity, and
fill our community with love, forgiveness and grace. Amen.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 8:15-20
In today’s Gospel, Jesus reminds us that Christian community is built on honesty,
reconciliation and love. When someone hurts us, makes a mistake, or goes astray, we are
not called to condemn or abandon them. Instead, we are invited to approach them with
patience, compassion and a genuine desire to restore the relationship. This is not always
easy. It can take courage to speak honestly, humility to admit when we are wrong, and
generosity of heart to forgive. Yet this willingness to reach out, rather than turn away, is at
the heart of how Jesus calls us to live together as his disciples.
Jesus also gives us the beautiful promise: “Where two or three are gathered in my name, I
am there among them.” Our faith is never meant to be lived alone. We need one another,
especially in moments when life is difficult or when our faith feels weak. Christ is present
when we come together in prayer, when we support one another through difficult times,
when we forgive, and when we patiently work to bring peace and understanding. Even the
smallest acts of kindness and encouragement can become signs of Chris t’s presence among
us.
As a parish family, may we have the courage to speak with kindness, the humility to listen,
and the grace to forgive. May we be willing to look beyond our differences and remember
that each person we meet is valued and loved by God. May we never be afraid to take the
first step towards reconciliation, even when it is difficult or uncomfortable. And may our
communities always be places where Christ’s presence can be recognised - not only in our
words and prayers, but especially in the way we welcome, support, forgive and care for one
another. In doing so, may we become a true reflection of the love and mercy that Jesus asks
us to share with the world.

the Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary - the birthday of Mary, the Mother of Jesus.
Although Scripture does not tell us the details of Mary's birth, this feast has been celeb rated
by the Church for many centuries. It invites us to rejoice in the gift of Mary, chosen by God
from the beginning to play a unique part in the story of our salvation.
Mary's life reminds us that God often works quietly and patiently, preparing the way for
great things. Her humble “yes” to God would one day bring Christ into the world. In
celebrating her birth, we give thanks for the young woman who became the Mother of God
and a mother to us all.
