Ardmore Parish | 25 October 2026 ARDMORE PARISH Newsletter THIRTIETH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME 25 OCTOBER 2026 YEAR A Gospel Reflection | Matthew 22:34 –40 In today’s Gospel, Jesus teaches that the greatest commandment is to love God who leheartedly and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

These two responsibilities are inseparable and form the foundation of Christian Loving God involves more than religious practices such as prayer, worship, and Scripture r eading.

It means trusting Him in difficult times, remaining faithful through challenges, choosing what is right, and allowing Him to guide every aspect of our lives.

Genuine love for God is shown not only in words but also in daily actions.

Our love for God becomes visible in how we treat others.

We cannot truly love God while harming, judging, or refusing to forgive those around us.

Instead, we are called to show compassion, kindness, patience, and mercy.

Even simple acts such as listening, encouraging, forgiving, and helping those in need can become powerful expressions of God’s love.

Jesus also calls us to recognise the dignity and worth of every person, incl uding those who differ from us or disappoint us.

True love does not ignore wrongdoing, but it speaks the truth with respect ands ets boundaries without hatred.

The central message of this Gospel is that love must be at the heart of both our rel ationship with God and our relationship with others.

Faith is not measured by religious knowledge or activities alone, but by whet her it transforms us into more loving people.

When love guides our thoughts, words, and actions, our lives become a living witness to God’s presence in the world.

Prayer for the Week May the Lord bless us with hearts open to love.

May He help us to see Christ in one another and to bring kindness, hope, and compassion wherever we go.

These two responsibilities are inseparable and form the foundation of Christian life.

Loving God involves more than religious practices suc h as prayer, worship, and Scripture reading.

It means trusting Him in difficult times, remaining faithful through challenges, choosing what is right, and allowing Him to guide every aspect of our lives.

Genuine love for God is shown not only in words but also in daily actions.

Our love for God becomes visible in how we treat others.

We cannot truly love God while harming, judging, or refusing to forgive those around us.

Instead, we are called to show compassion, kindness, patience, and mercy.

Even simple acts such as listening, encouraging, forgiving, and helping those in need can become powerful expressions of God's love.

Jesus also calls us to recognize the dignity and wort h of every person, including those who differ from us or disappoint us.

True love does not ignore wrongdoing, but it speaks the truth with respect and sets boundaries wit hout hatred.

The central message of this Gospel is that love must be at the heart of both our relationship with God and our relationship with othe rs.

Faith is not measured by religious knowledge or activities alone, but by wheth er it transforms us into more loving people.

When love guides our thoughts, words , and actions, our lives become a living witness to God's presence in the world.

May the Lord bless us with hearts open to love.

May us to see Christ in one another and to bring kindness, hope, and compassion wherever we go.

A common assumption is that love must just come, that it grows organically, and that it cannot be forced.

That’s infatuation, not true love. Any kind of love – for a parent, spouse, child, or friend – will reach a point during which it is no longer easy.

For love to be true, it must be a free choice and commitment. ‘All your heart, soul, and mind’ sounds rather extreme, even rather dangerous.

The world says that loving anyone totally is a risky proposition – what i f the one we love rejects us or lets us down, leaving us with nothing?

And the world is even more terrified to love God totally, who cannot even be seen.

This makes sense; after all, to love totally is to trust that we will find fulfillment precisely by not looking for it ourselves, but by looking to fulfill someone else.

True love takes a leap of faith, which is what frightens nonbelievers.

Many people are okay with going to church every now and then or volunteering a charity from time to time (and posting it on Instagram, of course).

Loving like that does not require us to make any kind of leap, any kind of definitive change, any commitment.

But loving God with everything means that all we do is for love of him.

Ardmore Parish | 25 October 2026 CULMORE PARISH Newsletter THIRTIETH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME 25 OCTOBER 2026 YEAR A Gospel Reflection | Matthew 22:34–40 In today’s Gospel, Jesus teaches that the greatest c ommandment is to love God wholeheartedly and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

These two responsibilities are inseparable and form the foundation of Christian life.

Loving God involves more than religious practices suc h as prayer, worship, and Scripture reading.

It means trusting Him in difficult times, remaining faithful through challeng es, choosing what is right, and allowing Him to guide every aspect of our lives.

Genuine love for God is shown not only in words but also in daily actions.

Our love for God becomes visible in how we treat othe rs.

We cannot truly love God while harming, judging , or refusing to forgive those around us.

Instead, we are called to show compa ssion, kindness, patience, and mercy.

Even simple acts such as listening, encouraging, forgiving, and helping those in need become powerful expressions of God’s love.

Jesus also calls us to recognise the dignity and worth of every person, including those who differ from us or disappoint us.

True love does not ignore wrongdoing, but it speaks the truth with respect and sets boundaries without hatred.

The central message of this Gospel is that love must be at the heart of both our relationship with God and our relationship with others.

Faith is not measured by religious knowledge or activities alone, but by whether it transforms us into more loving people.

When love guides our thoughts, words, and actions, our lives become a living witness to God’s presence in the world.

May He help us to see Christ in one a nother and to bring kindness, hope, and compassion wherever we go.

LOVE GOD AND YOUR NEIGHBOUR Matthew 22:34–40 Jesus brings us to the heart of faith: love God with our whole being and love our neighbour as ourselves.

These commandments belong together. Our prayer should shape how we treat others, especially those who inconvenience us, misunderstand us or cannot repay our kindness.

To love God wholeheartedly is to bring him our fears, disappointments and weaknesses, trusting that we are loved even in our poverty.

Loving our neighbour means seeking their good through patience, forgiveness and honesty.

And “as yourself” reminds us that we too are people God loves.

Humility acknowledges our faults while allowing us to receive his mercy.

This Gospel invites us to ask: are our prayers, plans and activities helping us become more loving?

Lord, let me receive your love today, and let that love shape how I treat the next person I meet.

These two responsibilities are inseparable and form the foundation of Christian life.

Loving God involves more than religious practices suc h as prayer, worship, and Scripture reading.

It means trusting Him in difficult times, remaining faithful through challenges, choosing what is right, and allowing Him to guide every aspect of our lives.

Genuine love for God is shown not only in words but also in daily actions.

Our love for God becomes visible in how we treat others.

We cannot truly love God while harming, judging, or refusing to forgive those around us.

Instead, we are called to show compassion, kindness, patience, and mercy.

Even simple acts such as listening, encouraging, forgiving, and helping those in need can become powerful expressions of God's love.

Jesus also calls us to recognize the dignity and wort h of every person, including those who differ from us or disappoint us.

True love does not ignore wrongdoing, but it speaks the truth with respect and sets boundaries wit hout hatred.

The central message of this Gospel is that love must be at the heart of both our relationship with God and our relationship with othe rs.

Faith is not measured by religious knowledge or activities alone, but by wheth er it transforms us into more loving people.

When love guides our thoughts, words , and actions, our lives become a living witness to God's presence in the world.

May the Lord bless us with hearts open to love.

May us to see Christ in one another and to bring kindness, hope, and compassion wherever we go.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 21:15-21 In today’s Gospel Jesus tells us to “repay to Caesar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God.” His words invite us to reflect on our priorities, our responsibilities and the place God holds in our lives.

While we have duties to our families, communities and society, our relationship with God must always remain at the centre.

Giving to God what belongs to God means more than attending Mass or saying our prayers.

It means living our faith through love, kindness, forgiveness and compassion.

When we care for the vulnerable, support those in need and reach out to the lonely, we make God's love visible through our actions.

In the busyness of everyday life, we can sometimes forget to make time for God.

Today's Gospel reminds us that our possessions and achievements should never take his place in our hearts.

What matters most is how we use our gifts and blessings to serve God and others.

As we reflect on today's Gospel, let us ask ourselves: Am I putting God first?

Am I using my gifts for the good of others?

Do my words and actions reflect the love of Christ?
