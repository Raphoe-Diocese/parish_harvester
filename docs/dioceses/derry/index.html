ARDMORE PARISH NEWSLETTER Sunday, 1st November 2026 FEAST OF ALL SAINTS Remembering the Saints, Praying for the Faithful Departed FEAST OF ALL SAINTS

SUNDAY 1ST NOVEMBER Today, 1st November, is the Feast of All Saints . On this day, the Church celebrates all the saints: canonised or beatified, and the multitude of those who are in heaven enjoying the beatific vision that are only known to God.

Prayer for All Saints God our Father, source of all holiness, the work of your hands is manifest in your saints, the beauty of your truth is reflected in their faith.

May we who aspire to have part in their joy be filled with the Spirit that blessed their lives, that having shared their faith on earth we may also know their peace in your kingdom.

Grant this through Christ, our Lord. FEAST OF ALL SOULS —

MONDAY 2ND NOVEMBER Tomorrow, Monday, on the Feast of All Souls , we are encouraged to remember and pray for the faithful departed, those members of our families and dearly beloved who have gone before us marked with the sign of faith.

Indulgences for the Holy Souls 1. Visiting a Church or Oratory From 12 noon on 1st November until midnight on 2nd November , all who have confessed, received Holy Communion, and prayed for the Pope’s intentions (one Our Father and Hail Mary, or any other prayer of one’s choice) can gain one plenary indulgence by visiting a church or oratory, and there reciting one Our Father and the Apostles’ Creed.

This indulgence is applicable only to the souls of the departed.

Confession may be made at any time within the week preceding or the week following 1st November.

Holy Communion may be received on any day from 1st to 8th November. 2. Visiting a Cemetery The faithful who visit a cemetery and pray for the dead may gain a plenary indulgence applicable only to the Holy Souls on the usual conditions, once per day from 1st to 8th November . The conditions mentioned above apply also for this.

Prayer for All the Deceased God our Father, Your power brings us to birth, Your providence guides our lives, and by Your command we return to dust. Lord, those who die still live in Your presence, their lives change but do not end.

I pray in hope for my family, relatives and friends, and for all the dead known to You alone.

In company with Christ, who died and now lives, may they rejoice in Your kingdom, where all our tears are wiped away.

Unite us together again in one family to sing Your praise forever and ever.

ALL SAINTS DAY AND ALL SOULS DAY – Everyone is invited to be a saint.

The Beatitudes Gospel of All Saints Day (1st November) is a portrait of Christ but also a portrait of a saint.

The saints are those who are in heaven, already enjoying the Beatific Vision.

Those who have died and are on their way to heaven but have not yet entered it are the Holy Souls, whose Feast is the following day (2nd November).

ALL SOULS – OUR DAY OF PRAYER FOR THE FAITHFUL DEPARTED Holy Souls and Saints: The Catechism (CCC) teaches that immediately after death comes the Particular Judgement (CCC 1021).

The soul has three possible paths: immediate entrance to Heaven immediate entrance to Purgatory to prepare for Heaven immediate condemnation to Hell (CCC 1022).

Those who are in Purgatory are on their way to Heaven, but they are not ready.

These are the Holy Souls. They are perpetual friends of God because they died in the state of grace, but they must spend time in Purgatory to achieve the holiness needed for heaven (CCC 1030).

For this purification from sin to take place, the Holy Souls need the prayers of the living (CCC 1032).

Prayer, especially the Mass, hastens their entry to heaven.

We are making Saints, by getting deceased people across the finish line, so to speak.

This is our prayer intention for November.

It’s our love for the dead. Indulgences: We can name a specific soul and ask God to grant the person a shortening (partial indulgence) or a complete remission of punishment for sin (plenary indulgence).

For the indulgence prayers and conditions, see the leaflets at the church door and on FB. CATECHISM – SEVEN DEADLY SINS – Pride, avarice, envy, wrath (anger), lust, gluttony and sloth (lack of interest in the spiritual).

Also called “capital” sins because they generate other sins/vices (CCC 1866).

Feasts during the Week: Sunday - All Saints: ‘The ultimate object of veneration of the Saints is the glory of God and the sanctification of humanity by conforming one’s life fully to the divine will and by imitating the virtue of those who were pre-existent disciples of the Lord.

Tuesday - All Souls: ‘Indeed the church in its pilgrim members, from the very earliest days of the Christian faith, has honoured with great respect the memory of the dead; and ‘because it is a holy and a wholesome thought to pray for the dead that they may be loosed from thei r sins.’ (2 Mac:12-46), she offers her suffrages for them.

These consist, primarily, in

the celebration of the Eucharist, and prayers for the dead, alms deeds, works of mercy, and the application of indulgences to the souls of the faithful departed’. (Directory of Popular Piety and the liturgy, 251) Tuesday - St. Malachy: Was born near Armagh in 1094. He became Vicar of Ceallach, the reforming Bishop of Armagh., and he continued his work to reform as Bishop of Connor.

He founded the monastery of Mellifont and established the Canons Regular into Ireland.

In 1148 Malachy set out from Ireland to meet the Pope but died on his way at Clairvaux, where he caught fever and died in the arms of Bernard and is buried in the abbey church.

Wednesday - St. Charles Borromeo (1538 - 84): Cardinal Archbishop of Milan at the age of twenty-one, worked for reform in his diocese, drafting the Catechism of the Council of Trent.

Patron saint of catechists and seminarians.

Thursday - St. Martin de Porres (1579 – 1639): Became a Dominican lay-brother, spending his nights in prayer and penance, his days in the monastery, caring for the sick and the poor.

He is renowned for his devotion to the Blessed Sacrament.

Friday - All the Saints of Ireland: ‘In the Communion of Saints, many and varied spiritualities have been developed throughout the history of the Churches … The different schools of Christian Spirituality share in the living tradition of prayer and are essential guides for the faithful.

In their rich diversity the are refractions of the one pure light of the Holy Spirit.’ (CCC,2684) Saturday - St. Willibrord: Was born in Northumbria in 658. He entered the Benedictine Order and was sent to study, including spending twelve years, 678 - 690, at the monastery of Rath Melsigi, near Milford, Co Carlow before going, with eleven companions, to evangelise Frisia.

He established a session of Ulrecht and in 695 was ordained Archbishop of Ulrecht by Pope Serguis I. He founded a monastery of Ech ternach in Luxemburg in 700, where he died in 739. Providing compassionate care at the end of life - To address the sensitive issue of compassionate care at the end of life, and in response to the need to inform the faithful of Catholic teaching on this important life issue that affects so many families, the Bishops of Ireland have published the pastoral letter: Freedom to Live Fully, Until Death Comes.

You are invited to read this letter and view the accompanying video Through the Valley, which offers personal testimonies by carers and people with terminal illness.

The key message of both letter and video asserts that every person possesses inherent, infinite dignity that remains intact regardless of their circumstances.

In this context, bishops strongly oppose the Oireachtas Committee’s proposal to Government to legislate for assisted suicide and call on support for healthcare workers who uphold the sanctity of life.

To read Freedom to Live Fully, Until Death Comes and to watch Through the Valley, visit the following link: https://www.catholicbishops.ie/2024/06/24/bishops-statement-on-end-of-life-carefreedom-to-live-fully-until-death-comes/

NOVEMBER – A COMMUNION OF LOVE AND HOPE As November begins, the Church celebrates All Saints and All Souls , reminding us that our relationship with Christ and one another is stronger t han death.

On All Saints’ Day, we celebrate not only the great saints whose na mes we know, but countless ordinary people who allowed God’s grace to transform their lives.

Holiness is not about being perfect or self-sufficient, but about trusting Christ and allowing His love to work through our weakness.

On All Souls’ Day, we remember our deceased relatives, friends and parishioners.

We pray for them because we believe in the Communion of Saints.

Those who die in God’s friendship but still require purification are prepared for the fullness of heaven.

Our prayers, especially the Holy Mass , express our continuing love for them.

From 1–8 November, the Church offers a plenary indulgence for the Holy Souls in Purgatory.

This is the remission of temporal punishment remaining after sins have been forgiven, through the merits of Christ. To obtain this indulgence, we are invited to: 1. Visit a cemetery and pray for the deceased. 2. Receive the Sacrament of Confession. 3. Receive Holy Communion. 4. Pray for the Pope’s intentions. 5. Be sincerely detached from all sin, even venial sin.

The usual conditions also apply to a plenary indulgence available throug h visiting a church on All Souls’ Day and praying the Our Father and Creed.

An indulgence is not something we earn, but a gift of Christ’s mercy.

It reminds us that we belong to one another in Him.

In a world that often values success, wealth and recognition, Jesus reminds us that God blesses the humble, the merciful, the peacemakers and those who hunger for justice.
