SUNDAY 8 NOVEMBER 2026 32nd Sunday in Ordinary Time Gospel Reflection | Matthew 25:1 –13 In today’s gospel, Jesus tells the parable of ten virgins who were waiting for the b ridegroom.

Five were wise because they br ought extra oil for their lamps, while the other five were foolish because they failed to prepare . When the bridegroom finally arrived, those who were ready entered the wedding celebration, while the others were lef t behind.

Through this parable, Jesus reminds us of the importance of being spiritually prepared because we do not know when He will return.

The lamp is a symbol of our lives, our faith, and our relationship with God.

Just as a lamp needs oil to continue burning, our spiritual lives need to be constantly nourished through prayer, love, acts of kindness, and unwavering trust in the Lord.

We are called to remain vigilant and faithful, keeping our hearts close to God so that o ur faith never grows weak or runs dry.

By living according to His will each day, we stay prepared for the return of Jesus.

Rathe r than fearing His coming, we should look forward to it with joyful anticipation, confidence, and hope, knowing that a life root ed in faith and obedience allows us to welcome Him with hearts full of peace and devotion.

Loving God, thank You for always being with me.

Help me keep the lamp of my faith burning brightly each day.

May I always be ready to welcome You with joy, hope, and a faithful heart.

Prayer for All the Deceased God our Father, Your power brings us to birth, Your providence guides our lives, and by Your comma nd we return to dust. Lord, those who die still live in Your presence, their lives change but do not end.

I pray in hope for my family, relatives and friends, and for all the dead known to You alone.

In company with Christ, who died and now lives, may they rejoice in Your kingdom, where all our tears are wiped away.

Unite us together again in one family to sing Your praise forever and ever.

May the Divine Assistance remain always with us, and may the souls of the faithful departed, through the mercy of God, r est in peace.

Amen. Many of us will be familiar with the panic of seeing the red fuel light appear on the dashboard when we are miles from a petrol station, or had to deal with a flat tyre or a dead car battery.

Thankfully these days we have mobile phones and roadside rescue to bail us out.

But even with all the warning signals available to us, we can still run out once in a while.

In this wedding story , the bridesmaids who run out of oil for their lamps are condemned as ‘foolish’. Among the wedding customs in Jesus’ time was a torch-lit procession at night time from the bride’s home to the groom’s.

Bridesmaids were required to be in a constant state of preparedness, since the groom could appear at any time.

In this parable, the fact that the bridesmaids fell asleep is not the issue; all 10 bridesmaids grow drowsy and fell asleep.

The issue is that some of them – the foolish ones – were not sensible or watchful enough to ensure they had sufficient oil, because of this they miss out on the wedding feast. There was no one to bail them out; the other bridesmaids had to carry on, for fear that sharing their oil would mean no one would have enough to continue.

They must have got quite a shock when they eventually arrived at the bridegroom’s house, only to be denied and turned away.

This parable must have served as a dramatic warning to the early Christian community, who were hoping for Jesus’ speedy return.

It let them know that they could be in for a long wait – ‘you do not know either the day or the hour’ – and that they must be prepared.

As well as having the light of faith, they must possess the fuel – the motivation and the actions – to keep it lit.

READINESS AND WEARINESS Matthew 25:1–1 Readiness is compatible with weariness.

In the parable of t he ten bridesmaids, all ten fall asleep, including the wise.

Jesus does not condemn their tiredness. The difference lies in the oil they have brought.

We might understand that oil as our willingness to open our hearts to Christ: to receive his love, acknowledge our need, and allow him to sustain us.

We cannot manufacture grace, but we can make room to receive it.

Sometimes we imagine that being ready for God means having everything accomplished, our problems resolved, and our weaknesses overcome.

Yet Christ can meet us amid unfinished tasks, anxious thoughts, and tired bodies.

Our weariness need not close our hearts.

Even a simple prayer can keep the lamp of faith burning: “Lord, I am tired, but I am here.

Help me welcome you.” The Bridegroom comes to gather us into his joy.

Readiness means remaining willing to receive the love he offers, even when we have little strength.

clonleighparish@derrydiocese.org ; Website : www.clonleighparish.com Gospel Reflection (Matthew 25:1- 13) In today’s gospel, Jesus tells the parable of ten virgins who were waiting for the bridegroom.

Five were wise because they brought extra oil for their lamps, while the other five were foolish because they failed to prepare.

When the bridegroom finally arrived, those who were ready entered the wedding celebration, while the others were left behind.

Through this parable, Jesus reminds us of the importance of being spiritually prepared because we do not know when He will return.

The lamp is a symbol of our lives, our faith, and our relationship with God.

Just as a lamp needs oil to continue burning, our spiritual lives need to be constan tly nourished through prayer, love, acts of kindness, and unwavering trust in the Lord.

We are called to remain vigilant and faithful, keeping our hearts close to God so that our faith never grows weak or runs dry.

By living according to His will each day, we stay prep ared for the return of Jesus.

Rather than fearing His coming, we should look forward to it with joyful anticipation, confidence, and hope, knowing that a life rooted in faith and obedience allows us to welcome Him with hearts full of peace and devotion.

Loving God, thank You for always being with me.

Help me keep the lamp of my faith burning brightly each day.

May I always be ready to welcome You with joy, hope, and a faithful heart.

Thirty-Second Sunday in Ordinary Time 8 November 2026 Oil You Cannot Borrow Nobody wastes oil these days.

Ask anyone who has just filled their car or home heating oil tank.

In the 1970s people recall f illing the tank without a second thought.

Now we watch the gauge and treat every drop as precious.

Keep that in mind, because this Sunday’s Gospel is about lamps and oil.

Last Sunday we looked at the finished picture, the saints.

This Sunday asks how you get there. Ten bridesmaids wait for the bridegroom.

He is late . All ten fall asleep, the wise as well as the foolish.

Sleep is not the sin. When the shout goes up at midnight, the difference is who has oil.

Five do not, and by the time they buy some, the door is shut.

The oil is the point. It is the one thing in the story that cannot be borrowed.

You cannot borrow another person’s prayer life, or their habit of forgiving, or their years of coming to Mass when no body made them.

The oil is a life filled a little at a time, long before it is needed.

The first reading calls that oil wisdom and says so mething consoling.

Wisdom is bright, does not fade, and is found by those who look for it.

It even comes looking for us. We seek and the Holy Spirit finds and plants those sevenfold gifts in us in Baptism.

November keeps the second reading close.

Saint Paul tells people grieving their dead not to grieve as those who have no hope.

Jesus died and rose, and it will be the same for those who have died in him.

It’s not known when the bridegroom is returning, but he is c oming.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 25: 1-13 In today’s Gospel, Jesus reminds us of the importance of being ready for His coming.

While five of the bridesmaids are prepared with enough oil for their lamps, the other five are caught unprepared when the bridegroom arrives.

The message is simple but profound: we are called to keep our faith alive and our hearts ready for the Lord.

This readiness is not about fear or worrying about the future, but about living each day with faith, love and trust in God.

The oil in our lamps can be seen as the everyday acts of kindness, prayer, forgiveness and generosity that nourish our relationship with Christ. These are the things that help our faith shine, especially when life becomes difficult or uncertain.

As members of our parish community, let us encourage one another to keep our lamps burning brightly.

May we never become so distracted by the busyness of life that we forget to make room for God.

Lord, help us to remain watchful, faithful and ready to welcome You, keeping the light of Your love shining in our hearts and in the lives of those around us.

Sunday 8th November 2026 Thirty-Second Sunday in Ordinary Time A frequent biblical image for God’s homecoming is that of a wedding feast. This is an image that recurs in many of the parables of Jesus, and we come across it in today’s gospel reading from Matthew, which recounts the parable of the wise and the foolish bridesmaids.

According to Jewish custom, the role of bridesmaids was to accompany and help the bride to prepare for the wedding.

Bridesmaids would often wait with the bride in her home until the groom arrived and then joyfully escort the bridal couple with lighted lamps to the bridegroom’s home, where the marriage ceremony and subsequent feast would take place.

In the parable of Jesus, there are ten bridesmaids.

Five of them are foolish and five are wise.

Because of a lengthy delay in the bridegroom’s arrival, the five foolish bridesmaids run out of oil and rush to town to purchase more.

In their absence, the bridegroom arrives and the wise bridesmaids, who had brought extra oil with them, are ready to accompany the groom and his bride to the wedding feast. When the foolish bridesmaids eventually arrive at the wedding hall and beg to be admitted, they are turned away with the words, ‘Truly I tell you, I do not know you’(Mt 25:12).

What is this oil that we need to keep topped up?

Our first reading today provides us with an answer.

It highlights the importance of a commodity that seems in short supply these days, namely wisdom.

In the reading, wisdom is personified as a bright, attractive lady who makes herself known to those who desire and seek her:‘’She is readily perceived by those who love her and found by those who seek her’(Wis 6:12).

Unlike knowledge, which we may attain by our own efforts, and, in a sense, possess, wisdom cannot be possessed.

It can only be received as a gift from God.

It is this gift that keeps us open to the One ‘for whom our hearts were made,’ to quote St Augustine, the one who alone can fulfil our deepest longing, as we sing in the words of the responsorial psalm: ‘For you my soul is thirsting, O God, my God.
