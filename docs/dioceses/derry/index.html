Season of Creation 2026: 1 September to 4 October
With the theme of ‘ Living Water ’ for this year’s Season of Creation, we pray:
Holy God, whose Spirit hovered over the waters at the beginning,
we gather as part of your community of all creation, thirsty for your grace.
We give thanks for the gift of water, the veins of our common home,
that sustains the cedar and the whale, the pasture and the city.
We are grateful for the local waters that nourish our homelands and our fellow creatures.
We give thanks that in your well-watered garden, every drop is a sacred testament to your l ove.
Yet, we confess that we have treated this gift as a mere commodity.
We have choked the seas with our waste and watched the warming tides rise in judgment.
Melt our frozen hearts, O God, and let the waters of repentance flow.
Lord of all Life, you promised that wherever the river flows, everything shall live.
Stir in us a calling to care for the waters and to honour the dignity of the thirsty.
May your Word be a spring within us, gushing for the healing of the nations.
May Christ our Lord who is Living Water refresh us for the work of restoration.
And may the Holy Spirit carry us on currents of grace until justice rolls down like wate rs
and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream. Amen.

✝ Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 18:21- 35
The gospel today tells of Peter asking Jesus how many times he should forgive someone
who wrongs him. Jesus responds with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt
refuses to forgive a fellow servant of a small debt.
The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend mercy.
Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have
received. It is not weakness, but strength rooted in grace. Humility opens our hearts to
God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others.
Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, we
can respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour the
forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace.
Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us
more like the One who forgives endlessly.
The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; it is an invitation. It calls us
to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, accountability, release, and
transformation.
Forgiveness is not easy, but it is essential. When we forgive, we reflect the very heart of
God. And in doing so, we discover freedom, peace, and the joy of a life lived in mercy.
Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 18:21 -35) The gospel today tells of Peter asking
Jesus how many times he should forgive someone who wrongs him. Jesus responds
with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow
servant of a small debt. The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend
mercy.
Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have
received. It is not weakness, but strength rooted in grace. Humility opens our hearts
to God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others.
Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, we
can respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour
the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately,
forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like
the One who forgives endlessly.
The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; it is an i nvitation. It calls
us to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, accountability, release, and
transformation. Forgiveness is not easy, but it is essential. When we forgive, we reflect
the very heart of God. And in doing so, we discover freedom, peace, and the joy of a
life lived in mercy.
Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen.

The gospel today tells of Peter asking Jesus how many times he should forgive
someone who wrongs him. Jesus responds with a parable: a serv ant forgiven of an
immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow servant of a small de bt. The lesson is
clear: those who receive mercy must extend mercy.
Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frai lty and the mercy we
have received. It is not weakness, but strength rooted in gra ce. Humility opens our
hearts to Godʼs mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others.
Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity o f those who hurt us,
we can respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing . In forgiving, we
honour the forgiveness we ourselves have received and becom e stewards of grace.
Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationshi ps, and communities,
making us more like the One who forgives endlessly.
The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; it is an invitation. It
calls us to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, acc ountability, release,
and transformation. Forgiveness is not easy, but it is essen tial. When we forgive, we
reflect the very heart of God. And in doing so, we discover fre edom, peace, and the

Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen.

the great philosophies and religions of the world. Why?
Because you can’t be happy, or have any sort of inn er peace,
far less inner joy, if you refuse to forgive. Dwell ing on past
hurts, nurturing resentment, indulging simmering an ger:
these things can blight or embitter a person’s whol e life.
Unfortunately though, as all of us know: sometimes
forgiveness can seem very difficult indeed, even im possible.
Perhaps this is so nowadays more than ever, because
contemporary secular culture encourages us to think of
ourselves as victims, who have a right to demand
compensation and redress.
What though if we find it too hard? What if my brot her
really hurts or upsets me, and ever continues to do so? What if
I have been so deeply wounded by someone that my li fe is
effectively ruined? In that case, let us simply adm it the
impossibility of our task, and turn to God for help . O God
come to my aid, we cry. ‘Lord, help me to let go of this. If I
can’t forgive, then do you forgive in my place. Bles s us both,
Lord, so that each of us is able to receive the gift of your
forgiveness.’
We pray, we go to confession, and we come to Mass. Here
the love and mercy of Jesus that flows from his sac rificial
death are made present, and received in sacramental form.
Here supremely we find the grace and strength we ne ed, in
order to let go of the debt we are owed: we, who ha ve been
redeemed from our own incomparably greater debt, th rough
the blood of Jesus Christ our Lord.

With the theme of ‘ Living Water ’ for this year’s Season of Creation, we pray:
Holy God, whose Spirit hovered over the waters at the beginning,
we gather as part of your community of all creation, thirsty for your grace.
We give thanks for the gift of water, the veins of our common home,
that sustains the cedar and the whale, the pasture and the city.
We are grateful for the local waters that nourish our homelands and our fellow creatures.
We give thanks that in your well-watered garden, every drop is a sacred testament to your l ove.
Yet, we confess that we have treated this gift as a mere commodity.
We have choked the seas with our waste and watched the warming tides rise in judgment.
Melt our frozen hearts, O God, and let the waters of repentance flow.
Lord of all Life, you promised that wherever the river flows, everything shall live.
Stir in us a calling to care for the waters and to honour the dignity of the thirsty.
May your Word be a spring within us, gushing for the healing of the nations.
May Christ our Lord who is Living Water refresh us for the work of restoration.
And may the Holy Spirit carry us on currents of grace until justice rolls down like wate rs
and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream. Amen.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 18:21 -35) The gospel today tells of Peter asking
Jesus how many times he should forgive someone who wrongs him. Jesus responds
with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow
servant of a small debt. The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend
mercy.
Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have
received. It is not weakness, but strength rooted in grace. Humility opens our hearts
to God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others.
Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, we
can respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour
the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately,
forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like
the One who forgives endlessly.
The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; it is an i nvitation. It calls
us to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, accountability, release, and
transformation. Forgiveness is not easy, but it is essential. When we forgive, we reflect
the very heart of God. And in doing so, we discover freedom, peace, and the joy of a
life lived in mercy.
Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen.

Let It Go!
Peter thinks forgiving seven times is generous. Jesus says seventy-
seven. Which means: stop counting!
Then Jesus tells a story about a servant who owed a fortune he could
never repay. He begged, and the master wiped the whole thing out.
Minutes later that same servant found a man who owed him a few
months' wages and took him by the throat.
Look at him closely. He has just been handed the gift of a lifetime and he
cannot see it. He is so buried in his own materialism, his own rights, his
own small world, that the master's mercy has not touched him at all. He
walked out of that room exactly the man he walked in. Forgiven, but not
free.
That is the danger for every one of us. We can receive God's mercy
again and again, in confession, at the altar, and still walk out clutching the
old grudge. The neighbour. The sibling. The house. The land. The job
promotion. The one who let us down. The First Reading puts it bluntly:
resentment is a thing we nurse, and it eats the one holding it. It narrows
our vision until, like that servant, we cannot see past the debt.
Jesus ends the parable with one condition: we must forgive from the
heart. Not through gritted teeth. Not a cold truce that keeps the old score
sheet handy in a drawer. Forgiveness is not pretending the wrong did not
matter, but it is a real letting go, deep down from where the grudge lives.
And the most powerful thing is that the moment we let it go, we are the
one set free. The weight lifts. We can breathe.
So this week, as you receive mercy from God, go and be mercy in action.
Name it. Let it go from the heart. Then you will enjoy the freedom God's
love has won for us, now and forever.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 18:21-35
In today’s Gospel, Peter asks Jesus how often he should forgive someone who sins against
him. Peter suggests seven times, but Jesus goes beyond counting: forgiveness is not
something we measure; it is a way of life.
Jesus tells the parable of the unforgiving servant. A man owes his master an enormous debt,
far beyond anything he could repay. Yet when he asks for mercy, the master forgives the
whole debt. This reminds us of the generosity of God’s mercy towards us. But the servant
then refuses to forgive someone who owes him a much smaller amount. Having received
mercy, he fails to show mercy himself.
The message is challenging. We can receive God’s forgiveness while still holding on to
anger, resentment or hurt. Jesus reminds us that the mercy we receive is meant to be shared.
Forgiveness does not mean ignoring injustice or pretending that hurt does not matter.
Serious wounds may take time, prayer, wise counsel and appropriate boundaries. But we are
invited to place our hurts in God’s hands and not allow bitterness to have the f inal word.
At every Mass, we encounter God’s mercy. Christ gives himself for us and calls us to extend
that same love to others.
This week, perhaps we can bring one difficult relationship before the Lord and ask for the
grace to take one step towards forgiveness. Forgiveness is not simply something God asks
of us - it is something God first gives to us.
Lord Jesus, you have forgiven us more than we can ever repay. Teach us to receive your
mercy with grateful hearts and to share it with those who have hurt us. Give us the grace to
forgive, the wisdom to seek justice where needed, and the humility to place every wound
into your loving hands. Amen.
