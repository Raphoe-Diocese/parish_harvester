READINGS FOR 17TH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME
First Reading 1 Kg 3:5, 7 -12. The Lord was pleased
that King Solomon should have prayed for wisdom,
with which he might govern his people well, rather than
for either wealth or the lives of his enemies. God was
delighted with this choice and bestowed on him "a heart
wise and shrewd as none before you and none will have
after you.”
Second Reading Rom 8:28 -30 If you want to know
what a lively faith in Jesus looks like this passage from
St Paul, along with next Sunday’s great hymn to God’s
love (Rom 8), are good examples. Paul is certain that
the Holy Spirit works unceasingly to weave the events
of his life into making him into a true image of the Son.
Gospel Mt 13:4 Jesus uses three parables to explain the
wisdom and the urgency of grasping the offer of ‘the
kingdom of God’ The Jewish teacher or scribe who
becomes a disciple has all the revelation given in the
Old Testament as well its completion in the New Testa-
ment. Some commentators think that Matthew here
refers to himself.

Seeds of God ’s Promise
The 15th Sunday in Ordinary Time invites us to think about how God ’s word works
in our lives. Each reading uses simple images from the world around us. Rain, seeds,
soil, and harvest all show how God ’s word brings life and hope.
These readings remind us that God always works with purpose.
In Isaiah, we hear how God ’s word is like rain that makes the earth fruitful. Paul tells
us in Romans that creation itself longs for freedom and shares in our hope.
In the Gospel, Jesus uses the parable of the Sower to teach us that our hearts must be
ready to receive God ’s word.
On the 15th Sunday in Ordinary Time Year A, these passages come together to remind
us that God ’s plan is certain. His word nourishes us, even when life feels dry or hard.
With open hearts, we can grow strong in faith and bear much fruit. Drumragh

The costly parrot trained to sow the word of God: Four brothers left home for
college and became successful doctors and lawyers. Some years later, they had a
reunion. They chatted after having dinner together. They discussed the gifts they had
been able to give their elderly mother who lived in a faraway city and decided to open
their mother ’s thank you letter to each. The first said, “I had a big house built for
Mama.” The second said , “I had a hundred -thousand-dollar theatre built in the
house.” The third said, “I had a Mercedes dealer deliver an SL600 to her. ” The fourth
said, “You know how Mama loved reading the Bible, and you know she can ’t read
anymore because she can ’t see very well? Well, I met a preacher who told me about a
parrot that can recite the entire Bible. It took twenty preachers 12 years to teach him. I
had to pledge to contribute $100,000 to the church, but it was worth it. Mama just has
to name the chapter and verse, and the parrot will recite it. ” The other brothers were
impressed. Then they solemnly opened the thank -you letters sent to them by their
mom. Mama wrote: “Milton, the house you built is so huge. I live in only one room, but
I have to clean the whole house. Thanks anyway. ” “Michael, you gave me an expensive
theatre with Dolby sound, it could hold 50 people, but all my friends are dead, I ’ve lost
my hearing and I ’m nearly blind. I ’ll never use it. Thank you for the gesture just the
same.” “Marvin, I am too old to travel. I stay at home and I have my groceries
delivered, so I never use the Mercedes. The thought was good. Thanks. ”
“Dearest Gerald ”, she wrote to her fourth son. “You have the good sense to know what
your mother likes. I cooked the chicken you sent. It was absolutely delicious! ”

6th World Day for Grandparents and the Elderly The theme chosen by Pope Leo XIV for
the 6th World Day for Grandparents and the Elderly – “I will never forget you ” (Is 49:15)
– reminds us that God ’s unwavering love is the ultimate answer to the isolation and neglect
so often experienced by the elderly. It brings comfort and hope, assuring us that the Lord
knows each of us personally, forgets no one, and loves us unconditionally, even through the
frailties of old age. Since its creation in 2021, the World Day for Grandparents and the
Elderly has grown into a cherished tradition that teaches our communities to truly value
the elderly, not through occasional gestures, but as a permanent, central part of our lives. This celebration helps us ap-
preciate older generations as vital keepers of our history, witnesses to our faith, and teachers for our lives. This year, we
want to place a special emphasis on closeness to others. We ask parishes and diocesan communities to reach out directly
to those who live alone, are staying in care homes, or have no visitors. Let this Day be a tangible opportunity for everyone
— especially young people —to spend time with their grandparents, the elderly in their families, and most importantly,
those who are completely isolated. Inspired by the wonderful initiatives of past years, we encourage similar creativity for

Fourth Sunday of Advent Sunday 21 December
Christmas Message 2025
This week we will celebrate Christmas Day. The birth of Jesus
Christ, over two thousand years ago, was a pivotal moment in
the lives of many people at that time, and ever since. In a
sense, we join the Angels, the Shepherds, and the Wise Men
who rejoiced with Mary and Joseph at the birth of the long
awaited Saviour of the world.
We join the apostles and the disciples - men and women - who
knew Jesus personally, who walked with Him and worked with
Him in those early days of spreading the Good News of God's
love for us in sending His Son among us.
We join all those who, down the years and right up to our own
time, have lived up to their baptismal promises to be God's
people, living and handing on the faith to the generations
coming behind them.
As we gather with family, friends, neighbours, and fellow
parishioners for Christmas Mass and other celebrations, let
us pledge ourselves to be a people of Faith, Hope, and Love
in a world which needs the presence of Jesus Christ - through
our care, our words, and our actions - now more than ever
before.
Merry Christmas and a very Happy New Year.
Nollaig shona daoibh, agus Athbhliain faoi mhaise.
Fr. Pat

WHOM OR WHAT —AM I GIVING MY TIME TO?
What would you do if you discovered a hidden treasure worth more than you
could ever imagine? Jesus tells us that the Kingdom of Heaven is exactly like
that. The man who finds the treasure and the merchant who discovers the
pearl of great price both sell everything —not reluctantly, but joyfully —
because they know that what they have found surpasses everything they are
leaving behind.
The Christian life is often seen as a list of sacrifices, but today's Gospel offers
a different perspective. Following Christ is not first about what we lose; it is
about what we gain. When we truly encounter the love, mercy and friendship
of Jesus, everything else begins to find its proper place.
One of the clearest signs of what we truly treasure is the time we willingly
spend in its presence. We make time for the people we love.
If Christ is truly the Pearl beyond all price, then we will naturally desire to
spend time with Him —in prayer, in the Eucharist, in His Word, and in the
quiet moments of each day. The amount of time we spend with someone is
often the clearest indication of how much we value that relationship.
This week, let us ask ourselves: What —or whom—am I giving my time to?
May we have the wisdom to recognise Christ as the Pearl beyond all price and
the courage to place Him above everything else.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 13: 24-43
In today's Gospel, Jesus reminds us that God's kingdom grows quietly and patiently.
Through the parables of the weeds among the wheat, the mustard seed, and the yeast, He
teaches that good and evil often exist side by side in our world and even within our own
hearts. Rather than acting with haste, God shows remarkable patience, giving every person
the opportunity to repent and grow in holiness.
The tiny mustard seed becomes a great tree, and a small amount of yeast transforms the
whole batch of dough. In the same way, even the smallest acts of faith, kindness,
forgiveness, and prayer can have a profound impact when entrusted to God. We may not
always see the fruits immediately, but the Lord is always at work.
As we continue our journey of faith, may we trust in God's timing, persevere in doing good,
and allow His grace to transform our lives from within. Then, as Jesus promises, "the
righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father."
