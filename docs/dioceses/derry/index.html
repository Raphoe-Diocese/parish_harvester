Sunday, 13th September 2026 Twenty-Fourth Sunday in Ordinary Time ✝ Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 18:21- 35 The gospel today tells of Peter asking Jesus how many times he should forgive someone who wrongs him. Jesus responds with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow servant of a small debt. The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend mercy. Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have received. Itisnot weakness, but strength rooted in grace. Humility opens our hearts to God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others. Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, wecan respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like the Onewho forgives endlessly. The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; itisan invitation. It calls us to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, accountability, release, and transformation. Forgiveness is not easy, butitis essential. When we forgive, we reflect the very heart of God. Andin doing so, we discover freedom, peace, andthejoyofa life lived in mercy. Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen. Jesus responds with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow servant of a small debt. The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend mercy. Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have received. Itisnot weakness, but strength rooted in grace. In forgiving, we honour the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like the Onewho forgives endlessly. Forgiveness is not easy, butitis essential. When we forgive, we reflect the very heart of God. Andin doing so, we discover freedom, peace, andthejoyofa life lived in mercy. Humility opens our hearts to God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others. Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, wecan respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like the Onewho forgives endlessly. A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 18:21-35 In today’s Gospel, Peter asks Jesus how often he should forgive someone who sins against him. Peter suggests seven times, but Jesus goes beyond counting: forgiveness is not something we measure; itisawayof life. Jesus tells the parable of the unforgiving servant. Butthe servant then refuses to forgive someone who owes him a much smaller am ount. Having received mercy, he fails to show mercy himself. The message is challenging. We can receive God’s forgiveness while still holding on to anger, resentment or hurt. Jesus reminds us that the mercy we receive is meant to be shared.
