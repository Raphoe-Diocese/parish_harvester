Thoughts on the Gospel
“You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church.” — Matthew 16:18
In today’s Gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience.
Peter answers with confidence, recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son
of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We may
learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but each of
us must eventually make our own response to Jesus.
🪨 Remember the word ROCK
R — RECOGNISE Jesus as Lord and Saviour.
O — OWN our faith through our daily choices.
C — COMMIT to the calling God gives us.
K — KEEP standing firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same way, God
can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His mission.
When we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope grows deeper,
and our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response should not be seen only
in our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve and remain faithful.
Christ is the true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s storms may shake us,
but they cannot destroy the hope He gives.
Prayer
Lord Jesus, you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Strengthen our faith to
confess You with courage. May nothing overcome the hope we have in You.

Feast Days Monday 29th June: St. Peter and St. Paul were important apostles of Jesus Christ, with distinct backgrounds and roles. St. Peter, an ordinary man, one of the original twelve disciples, was chosen by Jesus and considered the ‘rock’ upon which the Church was to be built. St. Peter was martyred in Rome, likely during Nero’s persecution. St. Paul, initially a persecutor of Christians, underwent a dramatic conversion and became ‘Apostle to the Gentiles’, a zealous missionary spreading Christianity and a prolific writer whose scripts and letters are foundational to Christian theology. St. Paul was martyred in Rome, likely around the same time as St. Peter. Their joint feast signifies their remarkable commitment to spreading the Gospel; their grace and courage to bear witness and the wisdom to shape the early Church. Tuesday 30th June: The First Martyrs of the See of Rome When the city of Rome had been devastated by fire in the year 64, the Emperor Nero launched a persecution against the Christians, who were thrown to the wild beasts in the arena or soaked in tar and used as living torches. Their deaths are documented in the writings of the Roman historian Tacitus and in Pope St. Clement’s letter to the Corinthians. Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 14:13 -21)
In today’s gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience. Peter answers with confidence,
recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We may
learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but each of
us must eventually make our own response to Jesus. The word ROC K helps us
remember the message. We are called to RECOGNIS E Jesus as Lord and Saviour, OWN
our faith through daily choices, COMMIT to the calling God gives us, and KEEP standing
firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same way,
God can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His mission. When
we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope grows deeper, and
our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response should not be seen only in
our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve, and remain faithful. Christ is the
true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s storms may shake us, but they
cannot destroy the hope He gives.
Lord Jesus, you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Strengthen our faith to
confess You with courage. May nothing overcome the hope we have in You.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 14:13-21)
In today’s gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience. Peter answers with
confidence, recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We
may learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but
each of us must eventually make our own response to Jesus. The word ROCK helps
us remember the message. We are called to RECOGNISE Jesus as Lord and Saviour,
OWN our faith through daily choices, COMMIT to the calling God gives us, and KEEP
standing firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same
way, God can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His
mission. When we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope
grows deeper, and our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response
should not be seen only in our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve, and
remain faithful. Christ is the true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s
storms may shake us, but they cannot destroy the hope He gives.

Thoughts on the Gospel
“You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church.” — Matthew 16:18
In today’s Gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience.
Peter answers with confidence, recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son
of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We may
learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but each of
us must eventually make our own response to Jesus.
🪨 Remember the word ROCK
R — RECOGNISE Jesus as Lord and Saviour.
O — OWN our faith through our daily choices.
C — COMMIT to the calling God gives us.
K — KEEP standing firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same way, God
can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His mission.
When we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope grows deeper,
and our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response should not be seen only
in our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve and remain faithful.
Christ is the true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s storms may shake us,
but they cannot destroy the hope He gives.
Prayer
Lord Jesus, you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Strengthen our faith to
confess You with courage. May nothing overcome the hope we have in You.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 14:13 -21)
In today’s gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience. Peter answers with confidence,
recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We may
learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but each of
us must eventually make our own response to Jesus. The word ROC K helps us
remember the message. We are called to RECOGNIS E Jesus as Lord and Saviour, OWN
our faith through daily choices, COMMIT to the calling God gives us, and KEEP standing
firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same way,
God can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His mission. When
we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope grows deeper, and
our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response should not be seen only in
our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve, and remain faithful. Christ is the
true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s storms may shake us, but they
cannot destroy the hope He gives.
Lord Jesus, you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Strengthen our faith to
confess You with courage. May nothing overcome the hope we have in You.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 6:13-20
In today’s Gospel, Jesus asks his disciples a question that goes to the heart of our faith:
“Who do you say that I am?” Peter answers with courage and conviction: “You are the
Christ, the Son of the living God.”
It is a question Jesus asks each one of us too. We may know about Jesus through Scripture,
prayer and the life of the Church, but Jesus invites us to something deeper than simply
knowing about him. He asks us to recognise him personally as the Son of God a nd to place
our lives in his hands.
Peter’s answer is a moment of great faith, yet Jesus also reminds him that this faith is a gift
from God. The Church itself is built upon this faith, and Christ promises that the powers of
evil will not overcome it. These words are a source of hope for us, especially when our own
faith feels weak or when the Church faces difficulties.
Our response to Jesus cannot remain only words. To call him “the Christ” means allowing
his presence to shape the way we live: how we treat others, how readily we forgive, how we
respond to those in need, and how faithfully we carry our own crosses.
Perhaps then, this Gospel invites us to pause and answer Jesus’ question for ourselves: Who
is Jesus for me? May we have the grace to recognise him more clearly, trust him more
deeply, and follow him more faithfully each day.

Thoughts on the Gospel
“You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church.” — Matthew 16:18
In today’s Gospel, Jesus asks His disciples a very personal question about who they
believe He is. Many people had different opinions about Him, but Jesus wanted His
followers to speak from their own faith and experience.
Peter answers with confidence, recognising Jesus as the promised Saviour and the Son
of God.
This passage reminds us that faith cannot depend only on what others believe. We may
learn about God from our parents, teachers, friends or church community, but each of
us must eventually make our own response to Jesus.
🪨 Remember the word ROCK
R — RECOGNISE Jesus as Lord and Saviour.
O — OWN our faith through our daily choices.
C — COMMIT to the calling God gives us.
K — KEEP standing firm when life becomes difficult.
Peter was not perfect, yet Jesus still used him for a great purpose. In the same way, God
can work through our weaknesses and help us become part of His mission.
When we build our lives on Christ, our faith becomes stronger, our hope grows deeper,
and our lives show the answer to Jesus’ question. Our response should not be seen only
in our words, but also in how we love, forgive, serve and remain faithful.
Christ is the true foundation, and when we stand on Him, life’s storms may shake us,
but they cannot destroy the hope He gives.
Prayer
Lord Jesus, you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Strengthen our faith to
confess You with courage. May nothing overcome the hope we have in You.

Christian: “Who do you say I am?” The disciples can easily repeat what others are saying, but Jesus invites them to

rely solely on what others believe or what we learned long ago. It grows through our own experience of Christ's presence
in our lives, espe cially in moments of joy, struggle, loss and hope. As we reflect on the Gospel this week, may we
recognise anew the Lord who walks with us and find in him the firm foundation on which to build our lives .

keys of the kingdom of heaven. Keys are small things, but they carry great
meaning. they give access and they open doors. They also show trust,
because a key is not usually given to someone unless they are being
entrusted with responsibility.
Many doors in life can feel closed: the door of hope after failure, the door
of belonging after loneliness, the door of faith after disappointment, the
door of peace after hurt. The Gospel invites us to ask what doors Christ
wants to open through us for the good of others. A patient conversation, a
sincere apology, a generous welcome, or a quiet prayer may become a key
in God’s hands.
Jesus does not give Peter keys so that he can keep people out. He gives
them so that Peter, and the Church built on his faith, can open the way to
God’s mercy, truth and life. The keys remind us that faith is never only
private. When we recognise who Jesus is, we are drawn into his mission
and we too are trusted with keys: words that can encourage and build up,
forgiveness that can release, kindness that can welcome, and courage that
can open a path for someone who feels shut out.
Peter’s strength was not that he understood everything or was always
successful. His strength began with recognising Jesus and that is where our
mission begins too. Before we can open doors for others, we must let Christ
open the locked places in our own hearts. He asks us again today, “Who do
you say I am?” If we answer with faith, he will place keys in our hands: not
for power, but for service; not for control, but for welcome; not to close
people off, but to help open the door to the kingdom.
