Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 16:21– 27
The Gospel today teaches us that following Jesus is not always a journey toward
comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of surrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love .
Jesus showed His disciples that His mission would involve suffering and death before
victory could come. Peter resisted this difficult truth because he wanted a safer path.
Like Peter, we sometimes want God’s blessings without life’s burdens, a crown without
a cross, and victory without sacrifice.
Yet Jesus teaches us that the greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in
becoming more like Him.
Let the word PROFIT guide our reflection
True PROFIT is not having everything while losing ourselves. It is:
P — Pursuing God’s purpose
R — Rejecting empty paths
O — Offering our lives
F — Following faithfully
I — Investing in what lasts
T — Treasuring our souls
When we choose Christ above everything else, even our sacrifices become seeds of
renewal. We may lose temporary comforts, but we gain a life filled with meaning, peace,
and eternal hope.
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ enlighten the eyes of our mind, so that we can
see what hope his call holds for us.

We thank you Lord, for all that was good during the summer. For the love of family and
friends, and for the sunshine and warm days.
As we begin a new school year we ask for your help and pray your blessing on all our
school communities. Show us how to work together for the good of all.
May we share our talents and abilities to the full and grow in knowledge, wisdom and
happiness. Give us the grace to become more like Jesus in the ways we think and
behave.
During this school year, let us become builders of your ways and instruments of your
peace. Be with us all as we embark on this new year.
We ask this prayer through Jesus Christ, Our Lord. Amen.

Feast Days this Week
Thursday 3rd September: St. Gregory the Great , also known as Pope Gregory I, was
born in 540 AD into a noble Roman family. Gifted w ith intelligence and deep faith, he
first pursued a successful career in government, be coming Prefect of Rome, yet he longed
for a life of prayer and simplicity. He converted his family’s villa into a monastery and
lived as a monk, dedicating himself to study, servi ce and contemplation. In 590, during
a time of famine and plague, Gregory was chosen as Pope. He became one of the most
influential leaders in Church history. He reformed Church administration, organized relief
for the poor, promoted missionary activity and rest ructured the liturgy, giving rise to the
singing style we now call ‘Gregorian Chant’. Known for his humility, pastoral care and
tireless work for both the spiritual and material w elfare of his flock, Gregory earned the
title ‘The Great’. He died in 604 and was declared a Doctor (Teacher) of the Church.
Saturday 5th September: St. Teresa of Calcutta (1910 – 1997) Agnes Gonxha
Bojaxhiu was born into an Albanian family on 26th August 1910 at Skopje in Macedonia.
She left home at the age of 18 to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland, where she received
the name Sister Mary Teresa, after St. Thérèse of L isieux. In 1931 she was assigned to
the order’s Calcutta house in Indi and taught at th eir school there, where she eventually
became headmistress. She later received a new voca tion to help the poor and destitute,
and in 1948, obeying this call, she left the conven t and took up a new life caring for
them wherever they might be, lying sick in the stre et or even dying in dustbins. Mother
Teresa radiated light for those in need. Some of h er former pupils joined her, one by
one, and the new congregation of the Missionaries o f Charity was established in the
Diocese of Calcutta in 1950, spreading across India and eventually onto every continent.
Mother Teresa of Calcutta died on 5th September 1997. She was beatified by Pope John
Paul II on 19th October 2003 and canonized St. Teresa of Calcutta by Pope Francis on

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 16:21-27)
The Gospel today teaches us that following Jesus is not always a journey toward
comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of surrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love. Jesus showed His disciples that His mission would involve suff ering and
death before victory could come. Peter resisted this difficult truth because he want ed
a safer path. Like Peter, we sometimes want God’s blessings without life’s burdens, a
crown without a cross, and victory without sacrifice. Yet Jesus teaches us that the
greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in becoming more like Him.
Let the word PROFIT guide our reflection.
True PROFIT is not having everything while losing ourselves. It is Pursuing God’s
purpose, Rejecting empty paths, Offering our lives, Following faithfully, Investing in
what lasts, and Treasuring our souls. When we choose Christ above everything else,
even our sacrifices become seeds of renewal. We may lose temporary comforts, but
we gain a life filled with meaning, peace, and eternal hope.
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ enlighten the eyes of our mind,
so that we can see what hope his call holds for us.

A Prayer for our Earth (Pope Francis, Laudato Si, 246)
(Tuesday 1st September - World Day of Prayer for the Care of Creation)
All-powerful God, you are present in the whole universe and in the smallest of
your creatures. You embrace with your tenderness all that exists. Pour out upon
us the power of your love, that we may protect life and beauty. Fill us with peace,
that we may live as brothers and sisters, harming no one. O God of the poor, help
us to rescue the abandoned and forgotten of this earth, so precious in your eyes.
Bring healing to our lives, that we may protect the world and not prey on it,
that we may sow beauty, not pollution and destruction. Touch the hearts of
those who look only for gain at the expense of the poor and the earth.
Teach us to discover the worth of each thing, to be filled with awe and
contemplation, to recognise that we are profoundly united with every creature as
we journey towards your infinite light. We thank you for being with us each day.
Encourage us, we pray, in our struggle for justice, love, and peace.

comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of s urrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love. Jesus showed His disciples that His mission w ould involve suering
and death before victory could come. Peter resisted this di icult truth because he
wanted a safer path. Like Peter, we sometimes want Godʼs bles sings without lifeʼs
burdens, a crown without a cross, and victory without sacrif ice. Yet Jesus teaches us
that the greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in be coming more like
Him.

what lasts, and Treasuring our souls. When we choose Christ above everything else,
even our sacrifices become seeds of renewal. We may lose temp orary comforts, but

Poor Peter. He never seems to get it quite right.

Why? Because the Kingdom that Jesus

are built on the principles of solidarity, mutuality,
respect and love. And not just any love, but
AGAPE love, i.e. love of people we may not
even like. Why is this so hard?
Jesus’ love changes things. It is a radical love,
one with no boundaries, to achieve a world
where this Kingdom is a reality will most
definitely involve going against the grain and
therein lie the crosses. Jesus is trying to explain
to Peter the path that lies ahead.

Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 16:21– 27
The Gospel today teaches us that following Jesus is not always a journey toward
comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of surrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love .
Jesus showed His disciples that His mission would involve suffering and death before
victory could come. Peter resisted this difficult truth because he wanted a safer path.
Like Peter, we sometimes want God’s blessings without life’s burdens, a crown without
a cross, and victory without sacrifice.
Yet Jesus teaches us that the greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in
becoming more like Him.
Let the word PROFIT guide our reflection
True PROFIT is not having everything while losing ourselves. It is:
P — Pursuing God’s purpose
R — Rejecting empty paths
O — Offering our lives
F — Following faithfully
I — Investing in what lasts
T — Treasuring our souls
When we choose Christ above everything else, even our sacrifices become seeds of
renewal. We may lose temporary comforts, but we gain a life filled with meaning, peace,
and eternal hope.
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ enlighten the eyes of our mind, so that we can
see what hope his call holds for us.

Feasts during the Week: Monday - St. Aidan of Lindisfarne: Was of Irish descent and
was a monk of Iona. When Oswald, the exiled King of Northumbria who had fled for refuge
to Iona, returned to his throne in 634, he invited Aidan to come to reconvert his people. Aidan
made his headquarters at Lindisfarne. With the aid of the king as interpreter, he was very
successful in his mission. He died in 651. Thursday - St. Gregory the Great (540 - 604): A

England. Patron of musicians. Friday - St. Mac Nissi: Oengus Mac Nissi took his name from
his mother Cnes or Ness. It is claimed that Patrick baptised him and taught him the psalms.

early in the sixth century. Saturday - St. Teresa of Calcutta: Anjeze Gonxhe Bojoxhiu was
born 26th August 1910 in Skopje, Albania (now north Macedonia) and at eighteen years of
age, came to Dublin to become a sister of Loreto with the singular desire to be a missionary
in India. She founded in 1950 the Missionaries of Charity to serve the hungry, the unwanted
and the dying. Often her faith was tested, her words and actions criticised, yet she was always
certain in remaining a servant of Christ who reaches out to the poor. The Missionaries of
Christ, once a small number in Calcutta, now number 5000 sisters in 130 countries.

Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 16:21-27)
The Gospel today teaches us that following Jesus is not always a journey toward
comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of surrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love. Jesus showed His disciples that His mission would involve suff ering and
death before victory could come. Peter resisted this difficult truth because he want ed
a safer path. Like Peter, we sometimes want God’s blessings without life’s burdens, a
crown without a cross, and victory without sacrifice. Yet Jesus teaches us that the
greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in becoming more like Him.
Let the word PROFIT guide our reflection.
True PROFIT is not having everything while losing ourselves. It is Pursuing God’s
purpose, Rejecting empty paths, Offering our lives, Following faithfully, Investing in
what lasts, and Treasuring our souls. When we choose Christ above everything else,
even our sacrifices become seeds of renewal. We may lose temporary comforts, but
we gain a life filled with meaning, peace, and eternal hope.
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ enlighten the eyes of our mind,
so that we can see what hope his call holds for us.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 6:21-27
In today’s Gospel, Jesus reminds us that following him is not always the easy path. He
speaks honestly about suffering, sacrifice and the cross, and calls his disciples to take up
their own cross and follow him with courage and trust.
These words can challenge us. We naturally want a life free from difficulty, yet Jesus shows
us that true love often involves self-giving and putting others before ourselves. Taking up
our cross does not mean seeking out suffering, but faithfully responding to the demands of
love, forgiveness, service and faithfulness in the circumstances of our daily lives.
Jesus also asks a profound question: What will it profit us to gain the whole world but lose
our true selves? In a world that can place great value on success, possessions, comfort and
recognition, the Gospel calls us to step back and remember what truly matters. Our
relationship with God and the way we love others are worth far more than anything the
world can offer.
May we have the courage to follow Christ faithfully, especially when the path is difficult,
trusting that the way of the cross ultimately leads to life. And may our everyday acts of love,
kindness and service become a quiet witness to the hope we have in him.

with courage and hope as they hear Jesus calling them to follow Him by renouncing
themselves, taking up their cross every day, and finding life in Him and in the service of His

St Teresa of Calcutta: A Life of Love. On 5th September, the Church celebrates the
memorial of St Teresa of Calcutta, a woman whose life was a remarkable witness to Christ’s
love for the poor, the sick and the forgotten.
Born in Skopje in 1910, Mother Teresa dedicated her life to serving those most in need.
Through the Missionaries of Charity, she sought to see Christ in every person she
encountered, especially those who were suffering or abandoned. Her simple message was
powerful: we are called to do small things with great love.
Her life reminds us that holiness is not always found in extraordinary deeds, but in the
faithful, loving actions of everyday life. A kind word, a visit to someone who is lonely, an
act of forgiveness, or practical help offered to someone in need can all become expressions
of God's love.

Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 16:21– 27
The Gospel today teaches us that following Jesus is not always a journey toward
comfort, popularity, or worldly success. It is a journey of surrender, sacrifice, and
faithful love .
Jesus showed His disciples that His mission would involve suffering and death before
victory could come. Peter resisted this difficult truth because he wanted a safer path.
Like Peter, we sometimes want God’s blessings without life’s burdens, a crown without
a cross, and victory without sacrifice.
Yet Jesus teaches us that the greatest profit is not found in gaining more but in
becoming more like Him.
Let the word PROFIT guide our reflection
True PROFIT is not having everything while losing ourselves. It is:
P — Pursuing God’s purpose
R — Rejecting empty paths
O — Offering our lives
F — Following faithfully
I — Investing in what lasts
T — Treasuring our souls
When we choose Christ above everything else, even our sacrifices become seeds of
renewal. We may lose temporary comforts, but we gain a life filled with meaning, peace,
and eternal hope.
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ enlighten the eyes of our mind, so that we can
see what hope his call holds for us.

Are your ways God’s ways?
In a major shift from last week, Peter learns how difficult it can be to accept
God’s way. The previous few lines on St. Matthew’s Gospel show Peter
recognising Jesus as the Christ, while now he cannot bear to hear that the
Lord must suffer, die, and rise again. To us as modern-day listeners, Jesus’
reply is abrupt: Peter is thinking “not as God does, but as human beings
do.” It is a reminder that faith is not simply agreeing with pleasant truths;
it is learning to trust God even when the road ahead includes sacrifice. Even
Peter took time to realise this.
Jeremiah understands this struggle. God’s word burns within him like fire,
yet speaking it brings mockery and rejection and many of us know
something of that tension. We want to follow Christ, but understandably
we also want comfort, approval, and an easier path. We may be tempted
to keep quiet, avoid difficult choices, or shape our lives around what the
world expects.
Paul’s letter to the Romans offers us a practical answer: “Do not conform
yourselves to this age but be transformed by the renewal of your mind.”
Christian discipleship begins in the daily offering of ourselves to God: our
time, work, relationships, worries, and hopes. This is not dramatic for most
of us. It is found in forgiving someone, serving without being noticed,
choosing honesty, praying when tired, or standing beside someone in our
lives who is struggling.
Jesus does not ask us to take up the cross because suffering is good in itself.
He asks it because love is costly, and true life is found when we give
ourselves away as anyone who is married could testify. This week, let us
ask for the courage to see with God’s eyes and to follow Christ in one
concrete choice. The cross we carry with him is never the end of the story;
it is the path that leads to resurrection and life.
