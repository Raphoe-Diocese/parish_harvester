God’s Ways are Not Our Ways… This Sunday’s Gospel reading shows us what it means to be truly foolish.

We believe in Christ – pray – good! We put time into our Christian faith growth and we value o pportunities for more faith formation – very wise!

A nd we say that we trust God – wonderful!

But what happens when God doesn’t a nswer our prayers fast enough?

What if we need Chri st’s loving embrace or his protection from evil and yet we’re s uffering with no end in sight?

Where is Jesus then? Usually, God’s timing is not ours.

We think we have the right idea about how soon God should intervene when we ask for his help . Right now is a good time for it, right?

Of course! But perhaps not from God’s perspective.

When we despair over unanswered prayer, the reality behind it is that we’re emotionally unprepared for any timing that’s different from what we think it s hould be.

In today’s gospel, the foolish bridesmaids thought the groom would arrive before their lamps burned out.

Like us, they tho ught they understood the situation.

Do we? Furthermore, if God’s plans ar illogical – if they don’t make sense to us – we dis trust them.

And then we get ourselves into the mess of trying to handle things apart and separate from God.

However, a lot of t he time and especially in difficult times, we cannot trust our own logic or understanding of the situations we’re in.

Therefo re if we can fully trust in what it represents to be a Christian, the sooner we will find out that God’s ways are always awesomely much, much better than anything we can ever imagine!

From this our trust i n God is strengthened more and more!

The heart framing the composition represents an invitation to open one’s heart to God, to one’s brothers and sisters, and to the future with hope.

Inside the heart is a map of Peru, an emblem of the entire nation in all its cultural, human and spiritual richness.

The dove of the Holy Spirit, placed above the national territory, recalls the presence of God who guides the Church, inspires reconciliation and renews the hope of the Peruvian people.

In the centre, Pope Leo XIV is depicted in the act of greeting and with the smile of the Good Shepherd, as a sign of his closeness and of his mission to confirm God’s holy people in the faith.

Surrounding him are some of the principal saints of the Peruvian tradition: St Rose of Lima, St Turibius, St Martin de Porres, St Francis Solano and St John Macias.

Taken as a whole, the logo conveys the image of a Church sustained by the Holy Spirit, enriched by the witness of her saints, and guided by the Successor of Peter — a Church called, through the Holy Father’s visit, to renew hope and strengthen unity in Peru.
