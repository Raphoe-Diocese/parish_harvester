This Sunday is World Mission Sunday: The theme for this year’s celebration of Mission Sunday in Christ, United in Mission”. World Mission Sunday is the Holy Father’s annual appeal for spiritual and financial support so that the life-giving work of overseas mission and missionaries can continue.

Mission Sund ay collections are held in every single parish where the Church is present.

It is a moment of universal solidarity when each member of t he Church plays their part in supporting each other.

All offerings and donations made for World Mission Sunday become part of the Holy Father’s Universal Solidarity Fund.

This fund is a lifeline for struggling missionaries and the communities they serve across Africa, Asia and Latin America, where over 1,100 mission dioceses are found.

As these dioceses form and grow, so do their needs.

On top of this, mission dioceses are often in remote areas devastated by war and natural disasters, or wheres uppressed communities are just opening up to the life-sav ing message of Jesus Christ. This is why World Mission Sunday is so important.

It offers young dioceses the financial and spiritual assistance they need to help their men, women, and children to survive and t hrive.

To donate, use the envelope in your offertory collection box, the special envelopes available in church or donate through t he missio.ie website.

Our policy regarding children in our parish churches?

If your kids are loud, please still come to church.

If you're running late because getting everyone out the door was a battle, please still come to church.

If your toddler won't sit still, please still come to church.

If your baby needs to eat, please still come to church.

If your child asks questions at the least convenient time, please still come to church.

If someone cries, drops a toy, needs a snack, or has to make their third trip to the bathroom, please still come to church.

If your child doesn't know all the words, all the prayers, or exactly when to sit and stand, please still come to church If you feel like you spent the entire mass managing your children and heard approximately 12% of the sermon, please still come to church.

If Sunday morning feels messy instead of peaceful, please still come to church If you're worried about, if you're worried your children are distracting the people around you, please still come to church.

Because children do not learn how to be part of the church by staying home they can sit quietly.

They learn by being here. They learn the singing by hearing us sing.

They learn the prayers by hearing us pray.

They learn to worship by watching us worship.

They learn that church is home because we make room for them here.

The wiggles, whispers, dropped crayons, crying babies, and little voices are not interruptions to the life of the church.

They are evidence that the church is alive.

So bring the babies, bring the toddlers, bring the big kids, come late, sit at the back, sit in the front, step out where you need to. and come right back in.

Just come.

Children are not simply the future of the church, they are part of the church right now.

If we don't make room for the next generation in the church, we risk becoming a generation that leaves behind empty churches.

The children in our pews today are the church leaders, worshippers, and servants of tomorrow.

And in our parish churches, there is a place for everyone.
