Love God and Love Your Neighbour In today’s Gospel (Matthew 22:34 –40), Jesus reminds us that the heart of our faith is sim-ple: to love God with all our heart, soul and mind, and to love our nei ghbour as ourselves.

These two commandments are closely connected.

We cannot truly love God w ithout al- lowing that love to shape how we treat others.

A kind word, a listening ear, a helping hand, or a willingness to forgive can all become expressions of God’s love in our daily lives.

Sometimes we may think that living our faith requires great gestures or extraordi nary deeds.

Yet Jesus reminds us that holiness is often found in the ordinary moments of everyday life, in the patience we show, the compassion we offer, and the care we extend to those around us.

As we go about the week ahead, may we ask ourselves: Can others recognise God’s love through the way I treat them?

May we strive to make love the guiding principle of our words, our actions and our relation- ships, so that our homes and parish community may become places where God’s love is truly felt and shared.

Points to Ponder To love the Lord with all our heart – is this even possible?

A common assumption is that love must just come, that it grows organically, and that it cannot be forced.

That’s infatuation, not true love. Any kind of love – for a parent, spouse, child, or friend – will reach a point during which it is no longer easy.

For love to be true, it must be a free choice and commitment. ‘All your heart, soul, and mind’ sounds rather extreme, even rather dangerous.

The world says that loving anyone totally is a risky proposition – what if the one we love rejects us or lets us down, leaving us with nothing?

And the world is even more terrićed to love God totally, who cannot even be seen.

This makes sense; after all, to love totally is to trust that we will ćnd fulćlment precisely by not looking for it ourselves, but by looking to fulćl someone else.

That old, almost clichéd phrase, ‘love grows when it is given away,’ is actually spot-on in this regard.

True love takes a leap of faith, which is what frightens nonbelievers.

Many people are okay with going to church every now and then or volunteering for a charity from time to time (and posting it on Instagram, of course).

Loving like that does not require us to make any kind of leap, any kind of dećnitive change, any commitment.

But loving God with everything means that all we do is for love of him.

We trust that he will take care of us. That is heroic; that is what it means to be a saint.

The indulgence is applicable only to the souls of the departed.

Confession may be made at any time within the week preceding or the week following November.

Holy Communion may be received on any day from 1* of November to the 8°November.

The faithful who visit a cemetery and pray for the dead may gain a plenary indulgence applicable to the Holy Souls on the usual conditions once per day from 1* to the 8° November.

Reflection: What Is Our Life For? Much of life today is spent managing responsibilities, meeting demands, going to appointments, and trying to find a little time for ourselves.

Amid all this activity, we can gradually become less available to one another, often without noticing.

Today's Gospel invites us to reconsider what our lives are for.

Jesus places love of God and love of neighbour at the heart of everything.

Our lives are not simply our own to manage.

They are gifts received from God. And the same is true of everybody else.

Each person matters to God, independently of what they may mean to us.

Christian faith invites us to grow in our awareness of these relationships and to become more capable of living them.

We do not become fully ourselves on our own.

Our journey towards maturity, and ultimately towards heaven, is a journey into deeper communion with God and with one another.

That is the life for which we have been created.
