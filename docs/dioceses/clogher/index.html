Weekly Reflection.
The grace of forgiveness.
Forgiveness is not easy. There is no more difficult form of giving than forgiving. For some people,
forgiveness seems an impossible requirement, because of what they have suffered and the harm
that has been done to them. How do you forgive someone who has done something terrible to you
or to a member of your family? There are times in our lives when we are not able to forgive
someone, especially when the offending person shows no sign of remorse or of wanting to make
restitution for the harm done.
Jesus, in today's parable of the unforgiving servant, is suggesting that God's tender, limitless mercy
towards us is with a view to our own transformation. To really open our lives to God's limitless
mercy is to be transformed by God's love into God's likeness. We all have someone to forgive and
we all need to be forgiven by someone. Offering forgiveness to another will never come easy to us;
it sometimes comes very slowly. Today's Gospel suggests that the more we appreciate and open
ourselves up to the gift of God's limitless forgiveness of us, the freer we will be to pass on that
forgiveness to others.
Fr. Owen Joe.

Reflection on Todays Gospel
In today ’s Gospel, Peter asks Jesus, “How often must I forgive? ” Jesus ’ answer— “not seven times but
seventy -seven times ”—reminds us that forgiveness is not about keeping count. It is a way of l iving.
The parable of the unforgiving servant shows us the depth of God ’s mercy. We have received forgiveness
far beyond what we could ever earn, yet we can sometimes struggle to extend that same mercy to oth-
ers. Jesus challenges us to remember that the forgiveness we receive from God sh ould overflow into our
relationships.
Forgiving does not mean pretending that hurt never happened. It means c hoosing not to let resentment
have the final word. As we pray the Lord ’s Prayer, “forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who
trespass against us, ” may we ask for the grace to forgive generously, patiently, and from the heart .

Reflection: What Are We Living From?
We cannot go through life without being hurt. People disappoint us, misunderstand us, sometimes treat us
badly. Forgiveness does not mean pretending that these things do not matter, nor does it mean that every
damaged relationship can simply be restored. But there is another question worth asking: what do w e allow
our hurts to do to us? What happens within us eventually reaches other people. Hurt can travel through us.
Resentment can travel through us. Someone else’s bitterness can slowly become our bitte rness too. But
mercy can travel through us as well. None of us has made our life entirely by ourselves. We have rec eived
love we did not earn, patience we did not deserve, opportunities we could not have created, fau lts that others
chose to overlook. Above all, we live within the mercy of God. The challenge is to rem ember not only what
has been done to us, but also what has been done for us. Sooner or later, one or the other will pass through
us into the lives of others.
