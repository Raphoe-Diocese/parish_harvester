I was given a book recently about former Taoiseach Brian Cowen who was in office when the world economy crashed in the 2008/09 period.

He and his Minister for finance, the late Brian Lenihan have been condemned for a lack of wisdom, for proclaiming that the Irish economy was on a sound footing, that the fundamentals were secure, just as the entire banking system collapsed and the Troika were coming in.

The two men were condemned for not knowing what to do when all the strategies of the world economy were about to be turned upside down.

They were condemned for a lack of wisdom.

The Bridesmaids in the Gospel today are presented as either foolish or wise.

The foolish were safe only if everything went to plan but were lost completely if anything unexpected turned up.

The wise were safe regardless of what circumstance prevailed, be they unexpected or not, because they were prepared.

In life we face many challenges where we often find ourselves challenged by the unexpected.

Wisdom consists in doing the ordinary things with sensitivity, doing the next thing you have to do sensibly, and putting your whole heart into enjoying what you do.

Faith is about knowing what to do next. Fr Shane.

Points to Ponder In today’s Gospel, Jesus draws from this revelation about the future the practical moral that a Christian needs to be on the watch, living each day as if it were his last. The important thing is not to speculate about when these events will happen and what form they will take, but to live in such a way that they ćnd us in the state of grace.

Living each day as if it were your last is a powerful concept that can help us to live more fully and with greater purpose.

It reminds us that our time on this earth is limited and that we should make the most of every moment.

It also encourages us to focus on what is truly important in life, such as our relationships with others and our relationship with God.

Keeping watch is another important aspect of this passage.

It reminds us to be vigilant and to stay alert to the signs of the times.

We should be aware of the world around us and the events that are unfolding, so that we can respond appropriately.

This means being informed about what is happening in our communities, our nation, and the world, and taking action when necessary.

Today’s Gospel, then, is a powerful reminder to Christians to live each day as if it were their last and to keep watch for the signs of the times.

By doing so, we can live more fully and with greater purpose, and we can be prepared for whatever the future may hold for us.

The current heating arrangement is only temporary and does not always provide sufficient warmth.

In the meantime, please dress warmly f or Mass and, where possible, sit near the temporary heaters.

Given the size of the cathedral, we want to be sure that any system we install will be effective, af fordable to operate and sustainable into the future.

With no comparable large building in Ireland, we have identified two large churches i n England which have installed similar systems and we are arranging visits to learn from their experience, particularly regar ding effectiveness and running costs.

Our objective remains to find a heating system that is effective, effic ient, environmentally friendly and sustainable for generations to come.

We thank you sincerely for your patience, understanding and continued sup port.

Reflection: What Do We Take With Us? We often hear it said that we cannot take anything with us when we die.

Certainly, everything we possess will be left behind.

But what about everything we have become?

In today's Gospel, the wise bridesmaids have prepared for the arrival of the bridegroom.

Their oil reminds us that some capacities cannot be borrowed at the last minute.

Patience, honesty, generosity and forgiveness grow through being practised.

But developing character is not an end in itself.

These qualities enlarge our capacity to love and to enter into relationships with others.

And where life's wounds have diminished that capacity, God's grace can heal and restore it.

The destination in Jesus' parable is a wedding feast — a celebration of love and belonging.

If love can bring us such happiness in this life, how much greater will our joy be when, through God's grace, we can love freely and fully, with all that we are and all that we have become?
