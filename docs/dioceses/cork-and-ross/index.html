Mon - Wed. 9.00am - 12. 30pm The Scripture Readings today challenge us to re flect on our response to God’s commandment of love.

In the first Reading we hear of the loving relationship the people of Israel were expected to have towards the under privileged and vulnerable.

Widows and orphans; people forced to le ave their Countries due to circumstances such as wars, plagues or famines; as well as the poor who had to borrow to survive.

The Israelite people had been treated compassionately by a God of love in their times of vulnerability and now were to treat others as they themselves were treated.

In the second reading, Paul praises the Thessalo nians for remaining faithful to the Gospel in spite of obstacles.

The life that they lived by the power of the Holy Spirit enabled them to spread their loving faith in God to surrounding places; b ecoming a model for others by the witness of their lives.

We too are called to do the same. May we witness to others the joy of the Gosp el both in word and deed.

In the Gospel, the Pharisees put a question to Jesus asking ‘which is the greatest commandment of the law’. In response, Jesus says'You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, a nd with all your soul, and with all your mind.

This is the greatest and first commandment.

And the second is like it:'You shall love your neighbour as yourself’. To love God with our whole being means having God as the centre of our life.

If God is at the centre of our lives then loving our neighbour ought to flow from that.

It sounds simple but it is very challenging!

I’m sure we all face situations where we find it difficult to love our neighbour as ourselves - our neighbour may be someone we work with, live with - yet Jesus also created th at person out of love and Jesus died for that perso n.

The love we know God has for ourselves is also the love God has for the person beside me, for the people I may not particularly like, and the people who may not particularly like me.

If we really think about God loving everyone as much as he loves us, we’d see everyone in a new way!

In the first reading from Exodus, God reminds His people that genuine faith is expressed not only in prayer and worship but also in the way we treat others, especially those who are vulnerable.

The Israelites are instructed not to oppress the stranger, exploit the poor, or neglect those in need.

Having experienced hardship themselves, they are called to show compassion, justice and mercy to others.

In the Gospel, Jesus is asked which commandment is the greatest. His answer unites the whole of God's law into two inseparable commands: to love God with all our heart, soul and mind, and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

Love of God and love of neighbour are not separate duties but two dimensions of the one Christian calling.

These readings challenge us to examine how our faith is lived in everyday life.

It is easy to profess belief in God; it is often more demanding to show patience, kindness and understanding to those around us.

Jesus reminds us that the measure of our love for God is reflected in the care, respect and compassion we show to others.

In loving our neighbour, we reveal the presence of God's love in our world.

Reflection on Today’s Gospel: What does the term ‘apostle’ mean?

It is someone who is sent. Jesus himself was sent by the Father to reveal God to the world and to redeem humanity.

Jesus chose twelve of his followers and they acted as his core group of collaborators.

Their closeness to Jesus over the time of his public ministry was a preparation for their mission in life.

Just as Jesus had been sent by the Father, Jesus in turn sent out his twelve disciples to make him known, initially to the Jews and eventually to the whole world.

In today’s gospel he tells them: ‘And as you go, proclaim that the kingdom of heaven is close at hand’ (Matthew 10:7).

Later in that gospel we read t he Great Commission, Jesus’ final sending out of his apost les: All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything that I have commanded you.

And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age(Mt 28:18-20) This commission is now the responsibility of the whole Church , not just the successors of the apostles.

Pope Francis proposed this extension of the apostolic mission to all Christians as synodality.

Pope Leo has continued this emphasis on synodality from the beginning of his papacy.

Sunday 25th October 2026 30th Sunday in Ordinary Time ~ Year A Gospel Reflection The non-negotiables … Have you noticed how often the term “non- negotiables” comes up in discussions lately?

Each side seems to set out its non- negotiables before any conversation can begin.

In today’s Gospel, Jesus is asked which commandment matters most. His answer offers two essentials for anyone who calls themselves Christian: love God and love your neighbour.

These commandments belong together. They shape all that we give and receive —in our relationship with God and in our relationships with one another.

Simply put, life begins and ends with love.

We see these two movements in the cross.

Through Jesus, we reach upward towards God, offering our love.

And with Jesus, we reach outward towards the people around us, loving them as he does.

These are the foundations of our lives as his disciples.

Take a moment this week to reflect on these movements: How do you show your love for God?How do you show your love for others?
