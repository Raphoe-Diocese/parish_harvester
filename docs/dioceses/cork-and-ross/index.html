Mon - Wed. 9.00am - 12. 30pm The Society for the Propagation of the Faith was founded in 1822 by a French laywoman, Pauline Jaricot.

She wished to help not only French missions and missionaries but all missions and missionaries especially the poorest. In 1922 The Society for the Propagation of the Faith became the primary organisation of the Church for supporting the activity of the missionaries.

Pope Pius X1 instituted Mission Sunday in 1926 and ordered that it be observed in a ll churches and oratories throughout the world as a day of prayer and material support for missionary activity.

It was Pauline who drew up the basic plan of the Society.

It is one of prayer, offering up of suffering and making a financial contribution for the spread of the Faith.

This year the theme chosen by Pope Leo is "One in Christ, united in mission" “Holiness and mission are inescapable aspects of the vocation of every Baptised person.

Sick Calls & Synodality - A Missionary Resource: Synodality is not an end in itself.

It exists so that the Church may proclaim the Gospel more effectively and serve the men and women of our time more faithfully.

We live in a world marked by profound transformations.

Wars and violence continue to wound entire peoples.

Social inequalities are increasing. Migration is reshaping the face of our societies.

New technologies are changing the way we communicate, learn and even understand ourselves.

Many people are searching for meaning, hope and authentic relationships.

Many look to the church for a credible witness to the Gospel.

Faced with these challenges, synodality appears ever more clearly as a missionary resource.

It helps the church to listen more attentively to the questions of humanity, to recognize the signs of the times, to value the gifts of all and to discern together the steps to be taken.

In this way, the implementation phase becomes a new stage in the reception of the Second Vatican Council and in the missionary renewal of the Church within the concrete realities of ecclesial life.

This is the responsibility that the Holy Father, Pope Leo XIV, encourages us to embrace together: not merely to preserve an inheritance we have received, but to make it bear fruit in the life of the churches and in the mission entrusted to the church in our time. (Cardinal Mario Grech, Secretary General of the Synod of Bishops, at the extraordinary consistory of bishops, convened at the Vatican 26-27 June 2026). If we do not worship God – we worship ourselves In the scriptures the heavy thread of fidelity versus idolatry runs through it like blood in the veins - from the Fall to the Golden Calf, from Solomon’s slide to Daniel’s stand, from Peter’s denial to Peter’s restoration… “The history of Israel shows us the temptation of unbelief to which the people yielded more than once,” wrote Pope Francis in Lumen Fidei , his encyclical on faith. “Here the opposite of faith is shown to be idolatry.” And fidelity ( fidelitas ) is faithful devotion.

Fidelity, it can be said, is the living out of faith in a spirit of worship and self-gift, demonstrated by inward disposition and outward moral uprightness.

Technology and modernity have not done away with idols; they may, in fact, have spawned countless new variations on the ancient themes.

Idolatry “remains a constant temptation to faith” (CCC 2113), precisely because we are made to worship - that is, to declare, profess, and proclaim that God alone is worthy of our praise and adoration - and we will either worship God or something else.

If there is no God, then you are a ‘god’ with your ownlaw, your own rules, and, essentially, your own religion.

And what do these numerous, autonomous little gods do?

They use, they abuse, they lie, they indulge, they corrupt, and they destroy.

This deviant dynamic is captured well in Lumen Fidei : “Once man has lost the fundamental orientation which unifies his existence, he breaks down into the multiplici ty of his desires; in refusing to await the time of promise, his life-story disintegrates into a myriad of unconnected instants.

Idolatry, then, is always polytheism, an aimless passing From one lord to another.” (par 13).

WORLD MISSION SUNDAY – 18TH OCTOBER 2026 World Mission Sunday, which is celebrated on the 2nd last Sunday of October each year, is the one Sunday of the year when thee ntire global Church comes together in support of mission.

It is the Holy Father’s annual appeal for spiritual and financial support so that the life giving work of overseas mission and missionaries can continue.

Mission Sund ay collections will take place in every single parish where the Church is pr esent.

It is a moment of universal solidarity when each member of the Church family, regardless of location or background, plays their part in supporting each other.

All offerings and donations made on World Mission Sunday become part of the Holy Father’s Universal Solidarity Fund.

This fund is a lifeline for struggling missionaries and the communities they serve across Africa, Asia and Latin America, where over 1,100 mission dioceses a re found.

These dioceses are often in remote areas devastated by and natural disasters or where suppressed communities are just opening up to the life-saving message of Jesus Christ. As these dioce ses form and grow, so do their needs.

The Mission Sunday collection offers young dioceses the financial and spiritual assistance they need to hel p their men, women and children survive and thrive.

The needs of overseas dioceses vary greatly.

These needs can include the purchase of a motorbike so a priest can celebrate Mass in remote villages, or the building of a church so that people can gath er and celebrate in safety.

In essence, Mission Sunday: provides Church-centred infrastructure by helping c ommunities build churches, schools and clinics; prepares future leaders and carers of the Church by supporting the training of sisters, priests, religious brothers and catechists; supports missionary programmes that protect and care for children’s well-being by offering safe shelter, healthcare, educ ation and hope for the future.

Remembering Bishop Michael Murphy 30th Anniv ersary The Cork Cathedral Family of Parishes remembers Bishop Michael Murphy, former Bishop of Cork and Ross, on the 30th anniversary of his death on 7 October 1996. Bishop Murphy served as Bishop of Cork and Ross from 1980 until his passing in 1996, dedicating many years to the Church and the communities of the Diocese.

One of his lasting contributions was overseeing the major renovation and restoration of the Cathedral of St Mary and St Anne, commonly known as the North Cathedral, between 1994 and 1996. The Cathedral was rededicated on 29 September 1996, with Bishop Murphy presiding over what would be his final public function, just eight days before his passing.

Thirty years on, we remember Bishop Murphy with gratitude for his years of service, his contribution to the Diocese of Cork and Ross, and his lasting connection to our Cathedral.

Bishop Murphy also laid the foundation stone of St Oliver’s Church in Ballyvolane in December 1991. May he rest in peace.

Reflection on Today’s Gospel: What does the term ‘apostle’ mean?

It is someone who is sent. Jesus himself was sent by the Father to reveal God to the world and to redeem humanity.

Jesus chose twelve of his followers and they acted as his core group of collaborators.

Their closeness to Jesus over the time of his public ministry was a preparation for their mission in life.

Just as Jesus had been sent by the Father, Jesus in turn sent out his twelve disciples to make him known, initially to the Jews and eventually to the whole world.

In today’s gospel he tells them: ‘And as you go, proclaim that the kingdom of heaven is close at hand’ (Matthew 10:7).

Later in that gospel we read t he Great Commission, Jesus’ final sending out of his apost les: All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything that I have commanded you.

And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age(Mt 28:18-20) This commission is now the responsibility of the whole Church , not just the successors of the apostles.

Pope Francis proposed this extension of the apostolic mission to all Christians as synodality.

Pope Leo has continued this emphasis on synodality from the beginning of his papacy.

What is Synodality?

The term ‘synodality’ owes its origin to two Greek words sun (with) and hé hodos (the road/journey): we journey on the one road together.

The basis of synodality is the Incarnation: the Son of God became one of us and joined us on the journey of life with its joys and sorrows, until he met with a most brutal death.

And he continues to journey with us:‘And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age’(Mt 28:20).

We too need to journey together. Though we have different functio ns in the Church and different vocations, nonetheless, we are all travelling together.

Sunday 18th October 2026 29th Sunday in Ordinary Time ~ Year A Gospel Reflection In the image of God … Today Jesus meets the Pharisees who question him, essentially to catch himout.

However, Jesus rises to their challenge placing himself above the controversy they are creating.

He acknowledges that taxes must be paid, by law.

He then reminds them that each person carries another image with them, the image of God, in whose likeness they are made.

Each of us is called to abide by the laws of the state but also to put first in our minds that we owe our existence to God.

This statement makes a clear distinction between the political and religious spheres of life.

So, on this Mission Sunday we are reminded in the Gospel of our own responsibility for the mission of Christ: to speak of God and to bear witness to Him to all whom we encounter.

Every one of us, by Baptism, is called to be a living presence in society, inspiring it with the Gospel and with the lifeblood of the Holy Spirit.

It is a question of committing ourselves with humility and courage, making our own contribution to the building of God’s kingdom in our time and place.

World Mission Sunday 1926-2026 World Mission Sunday is a powerful expression of our shared Catholic identity.

Celebrated each year on the second-to-last Sunday of October, it unites Catholics around the world in supporting the Church where it faces the greatest challenges.

Instituted by Pope Pius XI in 1926, this collection is unique: it directly supports 1,124 mission territories where the Church is young, vulnerable, or facing persecution.

On World Mission Sunday, Catholics everywhere are invited to support communities that cannot yet sustain themselves.

Our prayers and donations help fund church construction, pastoral care, education, and healthcare.

When your parish takes part, you join Catholics worldwide in supporting missionaries as they build churches, train seminarians, form catechists, and serve vulnerable communities.

Together, we answer our baptismal call to share the Gospel and bring hope to all peoples as one Church.
