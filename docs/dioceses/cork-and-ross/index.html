SAINT OF THE WEEK One of the interesting things that happens in life is making friends with people from different cultures.

We learn from other people, especially those different from us.

Malachy did that. This native of Armagh travelled through Europe, and became friends with St Bernard in Clairvaux.

When travelling to Rome and back, he always stopped off in Bernard’s monastery, and what a chat these two reforming saints must have had.

It was in Clairvaux that Malachy fell ill and died, and there with Bernard he was buried.

Does Purgatory exist? Facebook occasionally presents me with an album of photos, tracing my years as a subscriber.

It’s a limited look back, but always interesting.

When I die, I expect to see a similar review of my life, from God’s perspective — an album of memories.

Some things I may have conveniently forgotten, and some of the good I did also.

When I’ve made a review with God’s loving eyes on me, I can see myself saying I’m not ready to enter heaven — because in that moment of judgement, there can be no lies, only Supreme Truth.

I’ll need time to get my head around divine forgiveness, as I remember times I failed to mirror that.

I hope my friends will pray for me, to keep my hope alive until I am ready to pass through to eternal Beauty — a destiny promised to all who are faithful.

The Church calls this a time of purification, which is what I’ve described above.

As the Catechism puts it:“All who die in God’s grace …. are assured of their eternal salvation, but after death they undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven.” What do you think?

Ballinhassig Parish Newsletter for Ballygarvan, Ballyheada and Goggins Hill Vol. 35 No. 8 Sunday: November 1st, 2026 (All Saints) Remembrance and Thanksgiving for our Loved Ones Mary O’Hwrites…. Many people find November a difficult month, those who have been recently bereaved and those who carry the scars from a past painful bereavement.

Most of November we concentrate on remembering our deceased loved ones: - The Holy Souls Novena, the visits to the graveyard, while these are spiritually good for us they make it impossible for people to avoid being revisited by the pain and loss of a loved one.

We hold dear the memory of those who have been a truly significant part of each of us.

The gift of memory and its place within our hearts brings us consolation . Our hearts are warmed by the comfort of others and slowly over time we begin to heal and embrace life once again as our loved ones would wish us to live.

We ask God to heal our pain and sadness as the month of November is a time of Remembrance and Thanksgiving for the lives of our loved ones , no matter how long or short that earthly life was.

Once again with all our loved ones All our sorrow it will end In God’s home we’ll be re united Parents, family and friends.

Vision because our Gospel today describes the path we must take to make the same journey home to God.

These are the ones who have passed through trials and washed their robes clean in the blood of Christ. Second Reading (1 John 3:1–3) St. John reminds believers of their present identity as children of God, noting that while what we shall be has not yet been fully revealed, we will be like Him when we see Him as He is.

Gospel (Matthew 5:1–12a) The Beatitudes from the Sermon on the Mount.

Jesus proclaims true blessing not in worldly power, but in spiritual poverty, meekness, mourning, hunger for righteousness, mercy, purity of heart, peacemaking, and endurance of persecution.

The blessings in the Beatitudes are primarily future blessings, but there can be an anticipation of the blessings in the present.

At /f_irst reading some Beatitudes may seem to describe circumstances that you would like to avoid at all costs.

Read them slowly. Stay with each one for a while.

Let yourself get a sense of the paradox involved in each one.

Perhaps you have had an experience of a deeper and more authentic life, a blessing, when… 1. You were poor - you knew your need of God 2. You mourned – could feel for others 3. You were meek – not emotionally out of control 4. You hungered and thirsted for some cause 5. You were merciful rather than vengeful 6. You were pure in heart – a person of integrity, whose actions and intentions correspond 7. You were a peacemaker 8. You were persecuted because you stood for something PRAYER All-holy God, you call your people to holiness.

As we keep the festival of your saints, give us their meekness and poverty of spirit, a thirst for righteousness, and purity of heart.

May we share with them the richness of your kingdom and be clothed in the glory you bestow.

We call this the ‘Communion of Saints’ - the ninth art icle of faith in the Apostles’ Creed.

A spiritual union exists among the saints in Heaven, the holy souls in Purgatory and the people of God living on Earth.

The Second Vatican Council describ ed our loved ones who have died as ‘our outstanding benefactors’ . They can pray for us and inspire us.

Sunday 1st November we celebrate ‘All Saints’ Day’. It is the feast of all the holy women and men of every time and place, praising God in Heaven. On

Monday 2nd November we celebrate ‘All Souls’ Day’. We remember all the fai thful departed.

Remembering our dead these days reminds me that we usually only see death from one side, pain, loss, t ears, grief, a difficult and sad time.

Yet there is another side w hich tells us that death is a friend that takes us to the ‘Father’s house’ where all ‘tears are wiped away’. The darkness of death is no more.

Remembering our dead and reflecting on the story of their lives can help us look at our values and what really matters at the end.

The Solemnity of All Saints invites us to lift our eyes beyond the struggles of daily life and remember the countless men and women who have gone before us marked with the sign of faith.

In the vision of the Book of Revelation, John beholds a vast multitude from every nation, race and language standing before the throne of God.

They are ordinary people who remained faithful through trials and now share in the fullness of God's glory.

In the Gospel, Jesus presents the Beatitudes, a portrait of true holiness.

The saints are not remembered because they were powerful, wealthy or successful by worldly standards.

Rather, they were poor in spirit, merciful, peacemakers, and people who hungered for righteousness.

They allowed God's grace to shape their lives.

This feast reminds us that holiness is not reserved for a select few.

It is the calling of every Christian. As we honour the saints, we are encouraged to follow their example, trusting that God continues to lead us along the path that ends in the joy of heaven.

Family of Parishes Newsletter Our Lady Crowned Church St Joseph’s Church & Holy Family Church Gospel Reflection: Be persistent… and patient… Our Gospel today is about persistence… just as the widow in the story pesters the judge until he delivers a just decision we too are called to be persistent.

Persistence in prayer demonstrates that we are not afraid to show our dependence on God even though all around us society says we need be self-sufficient, to look after ourselves.

To keep praying is equally looking after ourselves but also the needs of those around us and the needs of our world.

The widow in the story is concerned about the delay in getting a response, however we must not only must persevere in prayer we must be patient, awaiting God’s time for an answer and accepting the answer even though it may not be one we wanted!

God will always give the right answer to our prayers, even if in the immediate moment it may not seem that way.

Take time to reflect on what have been the fruits of your own persistent prayer?

Sunday 1st November 2026 All Saints’ Day 2026 ~ Year A Gospel Reflection Saints… who are they?

I’m sure you can think of many names in answer to that question: Mary, Joseph, Anthony, Carlos, Anne, Teresa, and many more.

These are people whom the Church has declared saints: people whose example we can aspire to follow and through whose intercession we can pray to God.

But is there more to today’s feast? I believe there is.

Saints are more than those whose names we know and whose feast days we celebrate each year.

We are all called to be saints. Yes, we are sinners, but, more importantly, we are sinners called to become saints.

How, you might ask? The answer is in today’s Gospel.

Jesus tells the people of his time that they are blessed in their poverty, their pain and grief, and their quest for peace.

He calls them to offer that same generosity of spirit to those around them.

Living by these words is what leads us towards sainthood.

We all know people who lived these ideals of Christian life every day and who now rest with God.

They are saints who touched our lives, even without an official title.

So, as we celebrate all those who let the light of God shine through their lives into our world, let us pray that we, too, may become saints.
