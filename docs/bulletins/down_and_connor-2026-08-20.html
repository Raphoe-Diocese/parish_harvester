Why Our Lady of Knock is celebrated on two different days in Ireland‘The anniversary of the apparition at Knock is on August 21, but most of Ireland honours Our Lady on August 17 in the liturgy.Our Lady appeared in Knock on the night of August 21, 1879. Though she never said a word, her appearance comforted many and gave a renewed vigour to the faith of the people of Ireland, and encouraged many to turn to the Virgin Mary.The anniversary of this apparition is remembered each year at the Knock Shrine on August 21 with a special liturgy offered at the site of Mary's appearance.However, the rest of Ireland celebrates Our Lady of Knock on August 17. It is classiﬁed as a "memorial," and parishes around Ireland may celebrate Our Lady's apparition on that day.Currently this celebration is only on select calendars, primarily in Ireland, and not on the universal Roman calendar.Knock Shrine social media explains that, "Although the Anniversary of the Apparition takes place on the 21st August, the 17th August was chosen as the Feast day of our Lady of Knock, as the 21st August is already assigned to St. Pius X, the Pope of the Eucharist.”For the rest of Ireland (and most of the Roman Catholic world), August 21 remains dedicated to St. Pius X. The rationale behind the switching of liturgical days is to give more focus to a particular feast day, instead of supplanting one that is already being widely celebrated.’ (By Philip Kosloski)Our Lady of Knock, pray for us! Liturgical ‘Sign of Peace ’ The Sign of Peace remains an integral part of the celebration of the Eucharist and has been reintroduced as a normal practice across the Diocese. The customary expression of the Sign of Peace in Ireland is a handshake. However, other non -contact forms of the sign of peace such as a nod, a bow or a smile are also legitimate expressions of the Sign of Peace. No offence should be taken if someone prefers to express a non-contact Sign of Peace. The Diocese encourages all to continue to exercise good hygienic practices, and if you exhibit flu -like symptoms, you are encouraged to remain at home. This Sunday 9th August will mark the last Sunday Mass celebrated by me in the parish of St Anthony, as its parish priest. I have acknowledged the various previous parish priests who have supported the faith community. I have seen the pride with which parishioners have supported this parish and church remembering its difficult years during the Troubles. I have acknowledged the deep faith of the remnant of those parishioners, and the Polish community who has also found it a home, fostering their language, customs and traditions which are witnesses to their deep faith as well. I have witnessed the many other nationals who have come to find it their home, from Eastern European nationals, Philippino, Indian, Goan, African, Chinese, Portugese, and many others besides. We have witnessed together the 80th Anniversary of the Opening of St Anthony’s in 1918 by Bishop Noel Treanor and the commissioning of the then Pastoral Council. We have coped with the recent Covid-19 Pandemic, which has led to the broadcasting of mass on Facebook Live. I have found the support of parishioners and visitors to the parish as something alive and faith-filled, encouraging my ministry and vocation. And hopefully in the guidance of parishioners and visitors to St Anthony’s they have found in me a loyal and faithful shepherd after the heart of Jesus the Good Shepherd. I continue to be the Chaplain to the Ulster Hospital, Dundonald, as I move to the parish of St Malachy, in the city centre, on the weekend of 15th August, but I hope not to be stranger, as we as clergy continue to work in the family of Parishes of St Anthony’s, St Malachy’s, St Matthew’s, St Colmcille’s (Ballyhackamore) and St Colmcille’s (Holywood). A Brief history of St Malachy’s Church, Kilclief The first Church dedicated to St Malachy was erected in 1785 when the Parish Priest was Fr John Fitzsimons. It replaced several small Chapels in the area. Mass was also held in houses and fields. It cost £200 and was paid for by subscription. Over the alta r there was a painting of the Crucifixion. The Church was capable of seating 280 people and there was a sundial on the walls. A school was held in the Chapel then. A gallery was added to increase its capacity in 1830. The ruins of the original Church are still accessible through the Cemetery. In 1925 a porch was built but was later replaced by a geometrical tower which housed the bell which was blessed by Fr Crossin on 15th August 1952 and was rung for the first time. Fr Crossin christened the new bell “Joseph”. Unfortunately the bell cracked and a new bell was fitted and blessed on 10th May 1956. In 1968/69 as assessment was carried out on the Church which stated the roof and walls were in an advanced state of decay and it was decided the Church should be replaced. The site for the new Church was given to the Parish by Mrs Cultra of Ballywooden. Th e last Mass was celebrated in the old Church on 5th January 1969. Mass was first celebrated in the new Church on 4th April 1971 . The Church was dedicated to St Malachy , blessed and officially opened by the Most Rev Dr Philbin on 16th May 1971.
