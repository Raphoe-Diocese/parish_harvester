This is the landlord’s Heir: Come, let us kill Him. William Shakespeare had a remarkable ability to cap ture eternal truths in just a few words. One line From his play, Julius Cæsar, captures almost exactly what Jesus is saying to the Pharisees in the parable of today’s gospel. In the play Brutus says of Cæsar: “The abuse of greatness is when it disjoins remorse from power.” In ordinary language it means, ‘when those with power and authority stop feeling empathy and pity, and become blind to the effects of their Laws on people, then their greatness is abused and lost ‘. This is exactly the accusation Jesus is levelling at the Pharisees in today’s parable. In their pride and arrogance, and because of the power and authority they have as guardians ofthe Lawof Moses, the Pharisees have cometo imagine themselves as its creators and owners and have lost sight of how the Law affects ordinary people; they have become harsh judges, insisting on laws and rituals that only dama ge and deny tenderness and friendship, often times i nsisting that others keep these Laws, while ignoring them themselves. So then, far from being the ‘landowner’ , as the Pharisees thought they were, they are in fact only the ‘tenants’ – those employed to guard or be custodians ofthev ineyard, to look after and care forthe Law. But like the tenants in the parable, they have become cr uel, harsh, and pitiless, abusing their power and grea tness and becoming hypocrites. Jesus’ conclusion is scathing and unamb iguous. Amid all of the questions, thoughts and mean ings evoked by this wonderful parable, the last eleven words contain the greatest challenge and the greatest invitation tousall. “Therefore I tell you, the kingdom of God will be taken away from youandgiventoa people that produces the fruits ofthekin gdom.” Collections 26th/27th September – Development Fund: €1,070 ; Plate: €680 ; October Offerings: €20 . THE PLIGHT OF CHRISTIANS IN THE HOLY LAND: many parishioners continue to care deeply about th e plight of their Christian brothers and sisters in the Holy Land. Attacks by I sraeli settlers on Palestinian Christian communities in the West Bank, especially in the historic village of Taybeh, have escalated si gnificantly, placing increasing pressure on families to leave. Earlier this year, Pope Leo assented to the creation of ‘Friends of the Order’ within the Equestrian Order of the Holy Sepulchre of Jerusalem. The purpose is to allow those who share our concern for the Holy Land to be associated with aspects of the Order’s mission, without requiring full investiture into the Order. By exten ding this outreach, it creates a broader opportunity for parishioners to express and bear witness to their faith through charity and solidarity with Christians in the Holy Land.
