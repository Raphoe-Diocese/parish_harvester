Thirtieth Sunday in Ordinary Time To love the Lord with all our heart – is this even possible?

A common assumption is that love must just come, that it grows organically, and that it cannot be forced . That’s infatuation, not true love . Any kind of love – for a parent, spouse, child, or friend – will reach a point during which it is no longer easy.

For love to be true, it must be a free choice and commitment. ‘All your heart, soul, and mind’ sounds rather extreme, even rather dangerous.

The world says that loving anyone totally is a risky proposition – what if the one we love rejects us or lets us down, leaving us with nothing?

And the world is even more terrified to love God totally, who cannot even be seen.

This makes sense; after all, to love totally is to trust That we will find fulfilment precisely by not looking for it ourselves, but by looking to fulfil someone else.

That old, al most clichéd phrase, ‘love grows when it is given away ,’ is actually spot-on in this regard.

True love takes a leap of faith, which is what frightens nonbelievers . Many people are okay with going to church every now and then or volunteering for a charity from time to t ime(and posting it on Instagram, of course).

Loving like that does not require us to make an y kind of leap, any kind of definitive change, any commitment.

But loving God with everything means that all do is for love of him.

We trust that he will take care of us. That is heroic; that is what it means to bea saint.

Louisburgh Church Restoration and Repair works Since 2015 the south wall of the church and the bel l tower have suffered from significant water penetration (a problem first noted in the 1950s).

Internal render is breaking down, and paintwork is peeling off the walls.

Humidity levels in the building are high and water saturation of walls is reducing thermal performance making the building difficult to heat and maintain.

Investigative repo rts were carried out to identify and remedy the sources of moisture penetration.

These included a measurement survey and Structural , Stained Glass, Services, Asbestos, and Building Condition reports.

Temporary screens were erected to test the performance of the wall structure.

A specialist conservation contractor carried out drill testing o f the walls which confirmed significant water-logging of wall cores.

A Conservation Management Plan was commissioned to guide the remedial works and to aid in making applications for grant funding.

In February 2026 an application was made to Mayo County Council for permission to carry out repair works to remedy wall damp initially on 2 bays of the west wall, replace the bell tower roof and resurface the gravel path connecting the two entrances.
