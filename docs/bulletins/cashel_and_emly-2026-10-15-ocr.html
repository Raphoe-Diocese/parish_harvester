LITURGY OF THE EUCHARIST

AFTER THE CONSECRATION

The mystery of faith. We proclaim your Death, O Lord, and profess your Resurrection until you come again. Today is prisoner Sunday and the Church asks us to reflect on this most marginalised and often despised section of our population. Many accept, without reflection, that our prisons are full of dangerous, violent criminals. While there are certainly some dangerous people in prison, 80% of those convicted are sentenced to 12 months or less, suggesting that their crimes are less serious (accepting that even less serious crimes can be upsetting or even traumatic for the victim). 70% of prisoners have an addiction but our addiction services have long waiting lists. The Government plan to provide 1,500 extra prison places to combat the overcrowding in our prisons, but it might actually be more effective to provide 1,500 extra drug treatment places. 70% have mental health issues and our mental health services are generally recognised as very inadequate. Our prisons are overcrowded, full of drugs and increasingly violent places, making rehabilitation almost impossible. Some are on remand with bail set at €50 or €100, but they haven't gotit, so they remain in prison simply because they are poor. Others have bail, but because they cannot provide an address, they remain in prison because they are homeless. Most people who commit crime grow out of it. Many tell me that they gave up crime because they fell in love, or had a child, or found employment, and that now they have a reason, for the first time in their lives, not to go to jail. Perhaps it is the system, just as much as prisoners, which needs rehabilitation! Fr. Peter McVerry SJ
