NORMS FOR INDULGENCES AT THE COMMEMORATION OF ALL SOULS 1. From twelve o’clock noon on 1st November until midnight on 2nd November, all who have confessed, received Holy Communion & prayed for the pope’s intentions (one Our Father & Hail Mary, or any other prayer of one’s choice) can gain one plenary indulgence by visiting a church or oratory, & there reciting one Our Father & the Apostle’s Creed.

This indulgence is applicable only to the souls of the departed.

Confession may be made at any time within the week preceding or the week following the 1st of November.

Holy Communion may be received on any day from 1st to 8th November. 2. The faithful who visit a cemetery & pray for the dead may gain a plenary indulgence applicable only to the Holy Souls on the usual conditions once per day from 1st to 8th November.

Feast of All Saints Sometimes the Beatitudes seem like ideals which are just too hard , or we may think we know better.

But today’s feast calls to mind men and women – some who are known to us perhaps – who really embody these ideals, and who are taught by the Spirit to learn from the Wisdom of Christ. This great multitude of Christians are strengthened each day to seek the face of Christ; strengthened by the Holy Spirit.

Some do so despite persecution, and great tribulation.

Many of our brothers and sisters in various parts of the world are still being killed and unjustly tre ated for their faith.

And the witness of these saints gives us hope and courage.

But there are also the litt le sacrifices offered each day for Christ; for truth, goodness, and love.

Think, for example, of how often you might have had to be a peacemaker, or be merciful at home, or at work.

The path to holiness begins with an acknowledgement that we are sinners , and a longing for God’s mercy and salvation.

But the path winds through our kitchens and gardens, across our cars and schools, by our offices and homes, and into our hearts.

For it is ordinarily in these places that we hunger and thirst for righteousness… that we can seek God with a pure and undivided heart.

And God’s response is always to fill us with his blessing, giving us a share in the very life of the Blessed One, so that – if we persevere in friendship with God – we might ourselves become Blessed, and join the company of his saints.

Everyone welcome Louisburgh Church Restoration and Repair works Since 2015 the south wall of the church and the bel l tower have suffered from significant water penetration (a problem first noted in the 1950s).

Internal render is breaking down, and paintwork is peeling off the walls.

Humidity levels in the building are high and water saturation of walls is reducing thermal performance making the building difficult to heat and maintain.

Investigative repo rts were carried out to identify and remedy the sources of moisture penetration.

These included a measurement survey and Structural , Stained Glass, Services, Asbestos, and Building Condition reports.

Temporary screens were erected to test the performance of the wall structure.

A specialist conservation contractor carried out drill testing o f the walls which confirmed significant water-logging of wall cores.

A Conservation Management Plan was commissioned to guide the remedial works and to aid in making applications for grant funding.

In February 2026 an application was made to Mayo County Council for permission to carry out repair works to remedy wall damp initially on 2 bays of the west wall, replace the bell tower roof and resurface the gravel path connecting the two entrances.

Prayer The former Archbishop of Tuam, Joseph Cunnane wasn’t too fond of driving and so during Confirmation season he had the habit of getting one of the curates in Tuam to drive him around the diocese.

At one point however it happened that a certain curate wasn’t a particularly good driver – he had no sense of direction and seemed to have a total lack peripheral vision.

When it was suggested to the Archbishop that particular curate might be left off the rota the Archbishop declined declaring that the curate in question was doing wonders for his (the Archbishop’s) prayer life!

I was on duty in Clare Island last weekend.

There is now Mass on the island on every second weekend and duty entails that a priest takes the ferry out from Roonagh on the Friday evening to say the 8pm Mass on the Island.

He then stays over in the priest’s house and after saying an early morning Mass leaves again for Roonagh.

It’s always a nice experience and the islanders will always have a fire down and some food left for the visiting priest. I was aware that the forecast for last weekend was iffy at best but by Friday morning it was clear that the weather would play a factor in whether there would be Mass on the Island or not.

As it happened there was a Month’s Mind Mass scheduled for Friday evening so with family home it wasn’t easy to cancel.

Before I left Newport I confirmed that there was a boat from Roonagh at 6pm but I hadn’t reached Westport when the Ferry Company rang me to say that there would be only one boat off the island on Saturday morning and that would be to Cloughmore in Achill.

After a few quick phonecalls I had organised that I’d leave my car in Westport, get the bus out to Roonagh and that the priest in Achill would bring me from Cloughmore back up to Newport on Saturday morning.

The sea was pretty choppy on the way out but by Saturday morning it was a whole different affair.

When I arrived down at the pier the swell was high as I spotted the boat appearing around the headland being pounded by the waves as it rose and then disappeared in the heavy seas.

Heading to Cloughmore it was a case of holding on for dear life.

I have a good sea stomach but still prefer to stay outside on deck meaning that with the rain and the spray there was no possibility of keeping dry.

Halfway across, with the boat being heavily buffeted by wind and sea visibility became so bad that we couldn’t see land in any direction.

As I prayed earnestly to see Mulranny just one more time the truth of Archbishop Cunnane’s declaration dawned on me.

My prayers were answered when gradually land reappeared on the horizon and we arrived safe and wet in Cloughmore.

It is certainly very easy to pray when your life is in God’s hands!

But aren’t our lives always in God’s hands?
