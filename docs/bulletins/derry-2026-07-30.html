What would you do if you discovered a hidden treasure worth more than you could ever imagine? Jesus tells us that the Kingdom of Heaven is exactly like that. The man who finds the treasure and the merchant who discovers the pearl of great price both sell everything —not reluctantly, but joyfully — because they know that what they have found surpasses everything they are leaving behind. The Christian life is often seen as a list of sacrifices, but today's Gospel offers a different perspective. Following Christ is not first about what we lose; it is about what we gain. When we truly encounter the love, mercy and friendship of Jesus, everything else begins to find its proper place. One of the clearest signs of what we truly treasure is the time we willingly spend in its presence. We make time for the people we love. If Christ is truly the Pearl beyond all price, then we will naturally desire to spend time with Him —in prayer, in the Eucharist, in His Word, and in the quiet moments of each day. The amount of time we spend with someone is often the clearest indication of how much we value that relationship. This week, let us ask ourselves: What —or whom—am I giving my time to? May we have the wisdom to recognise Christ as the Pearl beyond all price and the courage to place Him above everything else. Please note: There will be NO 10AM Mass on Wednesday 15th or 2nd July Seeds of God ’s Promise The 15th Sunday in Ordinary Time invites us to think about how God ’s word works in our lives. Each reading uses simple images from the world around us. Rain, seeds, soil, and harvest all show how God ’s word brings life and hope. These readings remind us that God always works with purpose. In Isaiah, we hear how God ’s word is like rain that makes the earth fruitful. Paul tells us in Romans that creation itself longs for freedom and shares in our hope. In the Gospel, Jesus uses the parable of the Sower to teach us that our hearts must be ready to receive God ’s word. On the 15th Sunday in Ordinary Time Year A, these passages come together to remind us that God ’s plan is certain. His word nourishes us, even when life feels dry or hard. With open hearts, we can grow strong in faith and bear much fruit. Well, I met a preacher who told me about a parrot that can recite the entire Bible. It took twenty preachers 12 years to teach him. I had to pledge to contribute $100,000 to the church, but it was worth it. Mama just has to name the chapter and verse, and the parrot will recite it. ” The other brothers were impressed. Then they solemnly opened the thank -you letters sent to them by their mom. Mama wrote: “Milton, the house you built is so huge. I live in only one room, but I have to clean the whole house. Thanks anyway. ” “Michael, you gave me an expensive theatre with Dolby sound, it could hold 50 people, but all my friends are dead, I ’ve lost my hearing and I ’m nearly blind. I ’ll never use it. Thank you for the gesture just the same.” “Marvin, I am too old to travel. I stay at home and I have my groceries delivered, so I never use the Mercedes. The thought was good. Thanks. ” “Dearest Gerald ”, she wrote to her fourth son. “You have the good sense to know what your mother likes. I cooked the chicken you sent. It was absolutely delicious! ” Fr. Each image reminds us that life with God is the greatest gift we can receive—worth far more than anything this world can offer. The Kingdom of God is not just a future hope but a reality that begins whenever we choose to follow Christ with faith and trust. The treasure and the pearl invite us to reflect on what we truly value. The people in these parables willingly give up everything because they have found something of incomparable worth. In the same way, following Christ sometimes requires sacrifice, but whatever we surrender is insignificant compared to the joy, peace, and hope that come from knowing Him and sharing in His Kingdom. When Jesus becomes the centre of our lives, everything else finds its proper place. The parable of the net reminds us that God is patient and merciful, inviting everyone to receive His grace. At the same time, it encourages us to live faithfully each day, striving to grow in holiness and trusting in God's perfect justice. Every day is an opportunity to renew our commitment to Him through prayer, acts of charity, and faithful living. Jesus concludes by speaking of the scribe who brings out "what is new and what is old." As disciples, we are called to treasure the rich tradition of our Catholic faith while allowing the Gospel to renew our hearts each day. May we recognise the priceless treasure of Christ, choose Him above all else, and joyfully share His love and hope with everyone we meet.
