Jesus is not a king who seeks power, wealth or status.

He is a king who serves, loves and gives his life for others.

In today's Gospel, Jesus reminds us that whatever we do for the least of our brothers and sis- ters, we do for Him.

When we feed the hungry, welcome the stranger, visit the sick, comfort the lonely or care for those in need, we are welcoming Christ Himself into our lives.

This feast challenges us to ask ourselves: Can we recognise Jesus in the people we meet each day, especially those who are struggling, forgoƩen or in need of kindness?

As we bring the Church's liturgical year to a close, may Christ truly reign in our hearts, our homes and our community.

May we not only call Him King with our words, but show that He is King through our actio ns, by living lives of compassion, generosity, love and prayer.

Points to Ponder What is the Kingdom of God?

What is the Kingdom of Christ the King? The Kingdom of God is a space.

It exists in every home where parents and children love each other.

It exists in every region and country that cares for its weak and vulnerable.

It exists in every parish that reaches out to the needy.

The Kingdom of God is a time. It happens whenever someone feeds a hungry person, or shelters a homeless person, or shows care to a neglected person.

It happens whenever we overturn an unjust law, or correct an injustice, or avert a war.

It happens whenever people join in the struggle to overcome poverty, to erase ignorance, to pass on the Faith.

The Kingdom of God is in the past (in the life and work of Jesus of Nazareth); it is in the present (in the work of the Church and in the eĄorts of many others to create a world of goodness and justice); it is in the future (reaching its completion in the age to come).

The Kingdom of God is a condition. Its symptoms are love, justice, and peace.

Jesus Christ is king! We pray today that God may free all the world to rejoice in his peace, to glory in his justice, to live in his love.

Reflection: The King Who Never Overlooks Us Most of us want our lives to amount to something.

We work hard, care for our families, and try to build a secure future.

These are good things. But life can become so demanding that we fail to notice what we are not noticing.

People close to us may be struggling. Someone may be lonely, ill, or simply in need of a little attention.

Not noticing is rarely deliberate, but it can gradually become a way of life.

On the Feast of Christ the King, Jesus reminds us that what matters ultimately is how we treat one another.

He identifies himself with those most easily overlooked: the hungry, the stranger, the sick and the prisoner.

And he assures us that we ourselves are never overlooked by God.

His kingdom begins to take shape whenever we recognise another person's worth, make room for them in our lives, and help them to flourish.

The King is not far away. He is present in the person beside us.
