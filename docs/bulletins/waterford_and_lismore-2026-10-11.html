Bible school Why Are There Four Gospels? When we open the New Testament, we find not one Gospel, butfour: Matthew, Mark, Luke and John. At first, we might ask: Whydid God give us four Gospels instead of one? Would it not have bee n simpler to have a single account of the life of Jesus? The four Gospe lsarenot four different stories about four different Jesuses. They are fo ur witnesses to the one Jesus Christ, written from different pers pectives and for different communities. Each evangelist helps ustosee something unique about who Jesus is and what He came to accomplish. Matthew presents Jesus especially as the promised Messiah and King. He frequently shows how Jesus fulfils the promises ofthe Old Testament. Matthew helps us understand that Jesus is the One whom Israel was waiting for and that His Kingdom is now present among us. Mark is the shortest and most direct Gospel. His account moves quickly from one event to another and presents Jesus asthe Sonof God andthe suffering Servant. Mark reminds us that following Chris t is not simply about admiring Him; it means being willing to f ollow Him onthewayofthe Cross. Luke emphasises the mercy and compassion of Jesus. He gives particular attention to the poor, sinners, women, foreigners and those who were often forgotten by society. Through Luke, weseea Saviour who came to seek and save the lost. His Gospel reveals the extraordinary depth of God's mercy. John is different in style and language. He invites ustogo more de eply into the mystery of Jesus. From the very beginning, Jesus is presented as the eternal Word who was with God andwho became flesh. John's Gospel continually calls us to believe in Jesus and to discover that He is the source of eternal life. The four Gospels therefore complement one another. Each gives us a different window through which we can encounter the same Lord. Together they give us a richer and fuller picture of Jesus: the promised Messiah, the Sonof God, the compassionate Saviour and the eternal Word made flesh. That iswhythe Church treasures all four Gospels. Four voices, one Good News; four witnesses, one Lord; four Gospels, one Jesus Christ. From the Catechism of the Catholic Church What Is the Liturgy? The word “liturgy ” can sometimes sound like something reserved for priests, churches, or formal ceremonies. But the Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches us that the liturgy is much more profound: itisthe participation of the People of Godinthe work of Christ (CCC 1069). The liturgy is first and foremost the work of Jesus Christ. Through the liturgy, Christ continues to make present among us the mystery of His Passion, Death, Resurrection, and Ascension. The Church does not simply remember these events as something that happened in the past; through the sacraments, especially the Eucharist, Christ makes His saving work present to His people. The Catechism teaches that the liturgy is also a participation in the prayer of Christ to the Father. When we gather for Mass, wearenot merely attending a religious service. We are joining ourselves to Christ, who offers Himself to the Father for the salvation of the world. The liturgy is therefore the source and summit of Christian life (CCC 1074). Everything wedoas Christians should lead us towards the liturgy, and from the liturgy we receive the grace and strength to live our Christian vocation in the world. The Eucharist isatthe heart of this life because in it Christ gives u s Himself and unites us more deeply with God and with one another. The Church celebrates the liturgy through the sacraments, the Liturgy of the Hours, and other forms of liturgical prayer. The sacraments are visible signs through which Christ communicates His grace. Through Baptism we become children of God; through Confirmation we are strengthened by the Holy Spirit; through Reconciliation we receive God's mercy; and in the Eucharist we receive Christ Himself. The liturgy also involves our active participation. This does not simply mean saying prayers, singing hymns, or performing a particular role. True participation means offering our hearts and our lives to God together with Christ. We come to Mass carrying our joys, struggles, families, work, suffering, and hopes, and we place everything before the Lord. Finally, the liturgy does not end when we leave the church. Having encountered Christ, we are sent into the world to live what we have celebrated. The grace received at the altar should transform our daily lives, making us more loving, forgiving, generous, faithful, and attentive to those around us. The liturgy, therefore, is not merely something wedofor God. It is first the work of Christ, who invites us to share in His life and His saving mission. Through the liturgy, God sanctifies us, andweare sent forth to become witnesses of Christ in the world. Family Corner The Family as a Witness of Hope In a world where many people experience fear, uncertainty and l oneliness, the Christian family has a beautiful mission: tobea witness of hope. Hope is not simply thinking that everything will be fine. Christian hope comes from trusting that God is with us, that He loves us, and that He never abandons us. A family becomes a witness of hope when children experience, thro ugh their parents, that faith makes a real difference in everyday life. Children learn hope not only from what we teach them, but especially from what they see in us. When parents pray, forgive, encoura ge one another and remain faithful during difÏculties, children discover that God can be trusted even when life is not easy. The family can witness to hope through small and simple things. A prayer together before going to bed, Sunday Mass, saying “I am sorry ” after an argument, helping someone in need, visiting a lonely pe rson, sharing what we have, or simply listening to a child who is worried—all these moments can become signs of God's presence. Parents have a particularly important role. Children need to see that Chris tianity is not merely a collection of rules, but a relationship with Jesus Christ who gives meaning and direction to our lives. Whe n parents speak about God with trust and gratitude, children gradually learn to bring their own joys, fears and questions to Him. Christian hope is especially important when a family faces difÏculties. There may be illness, financial worries, disagreements, disappointment or uncertainty about the future. The Christian family does not pretend that these things do not exist. Instead, it faces them together, remembering the words of Christ: “Donotlet your hearts be troubled. Believe in God, believe also in me ” (John 14:1). Children who grow upina home where faith, love and forgiveness are lived are given a precious gift. They learn that failure isnotthe end, that forgiveness is always possible, that difÏculties can be overcome together, and that God's love is greater than our fears. Every Christian family, therefore, can become a small light of hope in the world. Wedonot need tobea perfect family. We simply need to allow Christ tobeatthe centre of our home. When a family lives with faith, love, prayer and trust in God, it quietly proclaims to the world: there is always hope, because God is with us. GOSPEL REFLECTION MATTHEW 22:1-14 The landowner had prepared for a large crowd. When he sent his servants out to personally remind the guests to come, some of the servants were mistreated, beaten and even killed by his prospective guests! Quite naturally, the king was enraged. What does this Sunday ’s Gospel saytous?I doubt that we have had the experience this story speaks of. However, we all have been disappointed and upset at various times in our lives. We may have been deeply hurt by someone or betrayed by another. Our natural human response istobe angry, hurt and disappointed – and rightly so. In these situations we need to take time to calm down and deal with the hurt, anger and disappointment. We need to acknowledge and feel the emotions. If we repress them, most likely the emotions will come back with a vengeance. Butwecan’t stop there. We also need to honestly acknowledge what we are experiencing but then with time to gradually ‘let those feelings go ’ and forgive. Most often this is the last thing that we want todo!It will take time butitisan important step to take. If we nurse our anger and hurt, wearenot hurting the individuals who disappointed us, weare hurting ourselves and those we love! Ifwearenot able to forgive, we simply need toask Godto help us. Maywe have the grace toask Godto heal the anger and hurt – and to free us!
