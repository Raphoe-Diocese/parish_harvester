Naomh Columbán (Féile 23 Samhain) Deir Naomh Columbán ina Instructiones XI (Treoracha Spioradálta) : "Má dhéanann an duine úsáid cheart as na buanna sin a thug Di a dá anam, déanfar cosúil le Dia é. Cuimhnímis go gcaithfimid na bronntanais sin go léir a chuir sé ionainn a thabhairt ar aisdó. Mhúin sé an bealach dúinn lena Aitheanta.

Deir an chéad cheann díobh linn grá a thabhairt dond Tiarna Dia lenár gcroí go léir, mar ghráigh sé ar dtús sinn, ó thús thánamar iste ach i solas an tsaoil seofiú". Week beginning 22nd November 2026 Feast of Christ the King

Today is the Feast of Christ the King and the end of the Church's year.

Jesus is the one True King and His Kingdom is a Kingdom of righteousness and peace.

Unlike worldly kings who stand for power, wealth, war, Jesus' kingdom is based on the law of love; loving God and loving our neighbour in a spirit of giving and self-sacrifice.

In the first reading from Ezekiel, we see that God is a shepherding king taking care of the lost and the injured sheep in his flock.

The Gospel is taking up the same message of God caring for his people.

Jesus has taken darkness and evil upon himself and conquered sin and death so that we could have eternal life with God.

Our King has died for each one of us; His mercy knows no bounds if we seek His forgiveness.

He has shown us by example how to live our lives in His greatness of service to the poor, the lonely the marginalized.

He has identified Himself with the poor and needy saying, 'if you give to the least of my brethren, you did it to me ; he leaves us in no uncertain terms how important this message of love and self-sacrifice means to our acceptance in being part of the Kingdom of God.

At the end of the age Jesus will come to judge the living and the dead, will we be part of the right kingdom?

Will we have lived our lives according to His Law?

Jesus has a burning desire to save all people; He continually invites us to follow him and be part of His Church to further the Kingdom of God, it’s up to us to accept His call to love and forgiveness and self - sacrifice.

Do you know the purpose of Holy Mass?

The primary purpose of the Holy Mass is to worship God through participation in the re- presentation of Jesus’ passion, death and Resurrection.

The Secondary purpose is to receive Jesus in Holy Communion and thirdly to unite as God’s Church sharing Faith and Thanksgiving.

According to a New Testament vision, Jesus our king rules paradoxically by loving service, humility and the gift of himself.

This example and teaching of Jesus have lost none of their power.

Why do we celebrate Holy Week? The purpose of Holy Week is to re-enact, relive, and participate in the passion of Jesus Christ. In Holy Week we relive with Jesus his road the Cross: the triumphant entry into Jerusalem; the institution of the Eucharist and the ministerial priesthood; his loneliness and agony in the garden as he is betrayed by the disciple Judas; his trial before the Romans and the Jews; his once and forever sacrifice upon the Cross; the emptiness of a world where Christ appears lost; and his glorious Resurrection on Easter.

Christians have been observing Holy Week since the earliest days of the Church.

Early Christians would travel to Jerusalem at Passover to re-enact the events of the week leading up to the Resurrection.

The earliest accounts of Holy Week were recorded by Egeria in the 380s A. D. From her accounts we know that on Palm Sunday, crowds of Christians waved palm fronds as they processed from the Mount of Olives into the City.

She also details how the community gathered on Good Friday at Golgotha to honour the wood of the True Cross and to remember the Crucifixion.

Customs spread from Jerusalem to around the Christian world.

And we do it this year in pray and participate in the Lord’s suffering, death and resurrection.
