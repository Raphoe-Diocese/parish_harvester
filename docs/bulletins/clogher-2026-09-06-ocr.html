Weekly Reflection.
‘If only it was so easy!’
Occasionally the Gospel presents us with challenges that can appear outlandish. Today’s passage
from Matthew’s Gospel is one such example.
Jesus suggests that each one of us has an obligation to point out faults to another, in the hope of
achieving a reform of the alleged failing. This can be a dicey enterprise. We are not good at
receiving reprimands. The garda telling us to slow down when he has pulled us for speeding is
acceptable and indeed such a warning could indeed be preferable to points on the licence. But for
others to give me a reprimand it could appear as an unwarranted act of self-righteousness on their
behalf.
However, it can often be a tremendous act of true friendship when one’s errors are pointed out.
Indeed, it can be a kindness and a blessing and when well-received can be constructive. A priest
friend occasionally asks me for constructive criticism. It requires great maturity to ask, and even
greater sensitivity to respond to the request.
Let us pray today for such sensitive wisdom in all our relationships. Fr Shane.

Reflection on todays Gospel
In today ’s Gospel, Jesus reminds us that our faith is lived in relationship
with others. When disagreements arise, He encourages us not to ignore
them or speak about one another behind their backs, but to approach
each other with honesty, patience and love.
Jesus also gives us the beautiful promise: “Where two or three are gath-
ered in my name, I am there among them. ” Our parish community may
be small or imperfect, but whenever we gather with faith, prayer and a
sincere desire to follow Christ, He is truly present.
May we seek to build communities where forgiveness is stronger than
resentment, reconciliation stronger than division, and Christ ’s love is at
the heart of all we do.

In Loving Memory of Father Jim Moore:
It is with profound sadness and a deep sense of loss t hat our Parish community learns
of the passing of Father Jim Moore at the age of 59 . Though his time ministering
directly in our Parish was brief – serving us for tw o years immediately following his
Ordination on 18th June 1995 – the impact of his ministry has spanned decades.
As a newly ordained priest, Fr. Jim brought a vibra nt faith, energy, and a
generous heart to our Parish. He was quickly loved in this place, and he remains
very fondly remembered by all who had the privilege of crossing his path during
those early years of his priesthood. He taught us, comforted us, and celebrated
with us, leaving behind great memories and many stor ies to be told.
Fr. Jim’s early days here were just the beginning of a lifetime defined by service.
His deep care for both Parish life and the wider di ocese was well known. He walked
alongside countless families through their moments of greatest joy and deepest trial,
always carrying the light of the Gospel with humility a nd grace. Leaving us far too young,
his absence will be felt acutely across the entire Diocese of Clogher, especially in Irvinestown, Enniski llen, Fintona
and Clones where he ministered.
We extend our heartfelt prayers and deepest condolen ces to his loving family who mourn this immense loss. We
hold in our hearts his mother, Winnie, and his sibl ings, Ethena, Marina, Niamh, Barry and Christopher. May they
find comfort in their treasured memories and in the knowledge of how deeply Fr. Jim was cherished by the people he
served.
Grant unto him eternal rest, O Lord, and let perpet ual light shine upon him. May his soul rest in peace .

Reflection: We Need Each Other
We sometimes imagine that maturity means becoming completely independent: making our own decisions,
standing on our own feet and taking responsibility for our own lives. But none of us becomes fully human
alone. There are times when other people see us more clearly than we see ourselves. A husband or wife, a
family member or a good friend may notice when something is going wrong long before we are ready to
recognise it. That is why we need people who will walk beside us, encourage us and sometimes care enough
to tell us something we would rather not hear. It works both ways. Loving another person sometimes means
having the courage to speak when remaining silent would be easier. And maturity sometimes means having
the humility to listen. We become responsible for our own lives partly by learning that we cannot live them
entirely by ourselves. We belong to one another's journey. Learning to live that way is already to begin
living the life of heaven.
