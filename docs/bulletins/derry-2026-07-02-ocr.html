At first glance, Jesus’ words about loving Him more than family can sound harsh. However, this is not an endorsement of neglecting family. Rather, it is a spiritual principle: when God is the ultimate priority, our love for others becomes purer and more selfless. Discipleship requires a willingness to carry one’s cross—enduring hardship and prioritizing God’s will above worldly comforts. You don't need to do something monumental to serve God. Jesus promises that even giving "a cup of cold water" a simple, small act of kindness done in His name—will be deeply noticed and rewarded by the Father. St Eugene's Cathedral (Templemore)

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 10: 37-42 ‘Anyone who finds his life will lose it; anyone who loses his life for my sake will find it’. These words spoken by Jesus in today’s Gospel, spoken with such force, can initially seem enigmatic, paradoxical, even. How can we find ourselves by losing ourselves? And then it dawns on us. If the life we find ourselves living and cherishing is a life wedded exclusively to the material world, a life which embraces the power-hunger, greed and corrupt practices of that world, then that life, when it ends, is lost. But anyone who lives life with Jesus as its centre, a life which embraces Jesus fully – his teaching, his guidance, the cross he carried out of a love so profound it made the cold world reel in shocked incredulity, that person has found eternal life. To be a true and faithful disciple of Jesus is not easy, and Jesus knows this. He knows it is not easy for us to carry the cross of our suffering with the same courage, fortitude and humility with which he carried his own heavy cross to Calvary. He knows our predilection to attach ourselves to the wrong things, the wrong people, and the shiny and superficial as opposed to the deep and worthwhile. He assures us over and over that he will never desert us: he will never abandon us to our fears. He assures us that true faith overcomes all fears: if we put his spirit before all else, our reward will be great indeed, in this life as well as in eternal life. We can experience in the here and now the peace and joy which is the reward of casting aside the ego-centred life and embracing a life which is gloriously Jesus-centred. Jesus, may we never forget that faith can overcome all fear. Jesus, may we always remember to put you before all else if we are to gain a life of peace and joy for all eternity.
