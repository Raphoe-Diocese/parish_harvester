Reflection

In March 2018, Fr Tony Coote, a 53 year old Dublin priest, was diagnosed with the dreaded Motor Neuron

Disease, and died 18 months later. Before his death he wrote an inspiring memoir about his life entitled Live

While you Can. It became an instant best -seller. The final words of Tony ’s memoir were: ‘When the train stops, I

will step onto that platform with hope and no fear. ’ The words ‘Fear not ’ are the most frequent words spoken by

Jesus to his disciples. In today ’s gospel they occur three times. But what is Jesus really saying when he tells

them not to be afraid? Certainly, he is not giving them, or us, the false assurance that we have nothing to fear.

Down through the centuries, all true disciples of Jesus have met with opposition from those who benefit from the

unjust status quo. In recent decades, violent persecution of Christians has been on rise. Speaking to diplomats

on the 9th January this year, Pope Leo stated that ‘the persecution of Christians remains one of the most

widespread human rights ’ crises today, affecting over 380 million believers worldwide. They suffer high or

extreme levels of discrimination, violence or oppression because of their faith ’. According to the 2026 World

Watch List (WWL) report, the number of Christians suffering persecution rose by 8 million over the past year.

In face of such opposition and persecution, Jesus urges his disciples not to allow their genuine fears to cripple them, but t o continue to proclaim the truth of

the gospel and to witness to him publicly. He assures them that the truth that the powerful elites try to cover up will event ually be revealed: ‘Nothing is

covered up that will not be uncovered, and nothing secret that will not become known ’ (Mt 10:26). He reassures them of the Father ’s love and care for them:

‘Even the hairs of your head are all counted ’ (Mt 10:30). And he ends by making them this promise: ‘Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before

others, I will also acknowledge before my Father in heaven ’ (Mt 10:32).

So we pray: Lord, grant us the courage to stake our lives on the truth of your word. Amen.

Why Sunday Matters

Our invitation to reflect on Sunday Mass is presented through the lens of Synodality - Communion, Participation,

and Mission - reminding us that the Eucharist is not iust a personal encounter with Christ but also a communal

experience that unites us all as the People of God... Mass is not just a personal act of devotion; it is a communal

act of worship. We go to God together. The Sunday Mass is where the Church is most visibly alive, 'and where

every member of the community plays an essential role. Each per son's participation is needed for the Church

to truly live.

'We are deeply thankful to priests, ministers and all those who gather to celebrate Mass. Special gratitude goes

to those involved in liturgical ministries, such as Ministers of the Word, of Holy Communion, Music Ministers,

Sacristans, and Altar Servers. E ncouraging the return of young people as Altar Servers is especially important

and should be actively supported by their families and parish.

(Irish Catholic Bishops' Conference, Why Sunday Matters, 2025)

QUASQUICENTENNIAL [125TH ANNIVERSARY ], CATHEDRAL OF SAINTS EUNAN & COLUMBA , LETTERKENNY

To mark the 125th Anniversary of the consecration of the Cathedral of Saints Eunan and Columba, Letterkenny,

the Holy See has granted a Plenary Indulgence under the usual conditions. It may be obtained as follows:

The Plenary Indulgence may be obtained under the usual conditions by any of the faithful who, rightly disposed,

participate in the liturgical life of the Cathedral with the intention of gaining the Indulgence, beginning with the

Mass of Chrism on Holy Thur sday of this year and concluding with the feast of the Epiphany, 6th January 2027.

The same Indulgence may be obtained by the sick and others who, unable to leave their homes, participate

spiritually in the same liturgical services by electronic means.

The Indulgence may be gained under the usual conditions by all who piously come on pilgrimage to the

Cathedral in the aforementioned period with that purpose in mind.

The same Indulgence is extended to those who wish to obtain it by spending at least one hour in Eucharistic

Adoration at the St. Pius X Chapel of Perpetual Adoration in the grounds of the Cathedral.

To obtain an indulgence , the usual conditions of detachment from all sin, Sacramental Confession, Holy

Communion, and prayer for the intentions of the Pope must be met within 7 days.

Step by Step through the Mass

The Liturgy of the Word (Part 12) – The Pr ayers of the Faithful

Once we have made our Profession of faith with the recitation of the Creed, we move on

to the final part of the Liturgy of the Word at Mass; namely: The Prayers of the Faithful –

sometimes called the Bidding Prayers. These prayers are not inconsequential pious throw -

aways – they are an expression of our union with Christ, praying as the Body of Christ in

response to the Word of God – praying through him, with him and in him – which is why

the prayers are ordinarily made (though not bindingly prescribed) as an addressed to God

the Father.

The Church asks that this prayer be relatively short, as opposed to long winded paragraphs ,

and that they should ordinarily be prayers that gather the whole Church and World into

the intentions of this congregation gathered for this Mass on this Sunday. These prayers,

therefore, are not ordinarily a moment to be specific about a given private or very local

issue. For example, while we might pray for good weather for the farming community to

do its important business, we wouldn’t be so specific as to pray that Farmer John gets his

farming work done. Of course, anyone who knows our local Farmer John can include him

in their participation in the prayer – the prayer spoken from the Ambo is usually more

wide ranging rather than specific. Exceptions are permit ted by the Church when the

congregation is gathered for a specific ceremony or celebration: Funerals, Confirmations,

Weddings etc.

For the Prayers of the Faithful the Church has guidelines to follow. The order of the

prayers is usually: for the needs of the Church, for public authorities and the Salvation of

the whole world; for grave difficulties that afflict people and even nations ; then finally for

the local community. At the end of each prayer the acclamation is spoken: “Lord hear

us!” The congregation replies: “Lord, graciously hear us!” We call out to God, confident

that he will hear us. We are encouraged by the Word of God which will have recounted

to us something of God’s past interventions with the human race ; especially in sending his

Only Begotten Son, Jesus. In effect, in faith we are presenting him the difficulties that

afflict our present world asking him to intervene with his grace, power, mercy and love as

we know he has done in the past, especially as we have heard from the Sacred Scriptures .

So, the Bidding Prayers are no mere formality. They are meant to be heartfelt pleas to

God the Fath er, made IN the Holy Spirit, BY us, THROUGH Christ Jesus. In faith we

call out: “Lord graciously hear us!”

Fr Philip Kemmy, PP Fr. Damien Nejad, CC
