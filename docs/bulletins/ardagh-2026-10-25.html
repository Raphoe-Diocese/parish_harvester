30th Sunday in Ordinary Time: To love the Lord with all your heart, is this even possible?

A common assumption is that love must just come, that it grows organically, and cannot be forced.

Any kind of love; for a parent, spouse, child, or friend will reach a point during which it is no longer easy.

For love to be true, it must be a free choice and commitment.

All your heart, soul, and mind, sounds rather extreme, even rather dangerous.

The world says that loving anyone totally is a risky propo sition, what if the one we love rejects us or lets us down, leaving us with nothing.

And the world is even more terrified to love God totally, who cannot be seen.

After all, to love totally is to trus t that we will find fulfilment by not looking for it ourselves, but by looking to fulfill someone else.

True love takes a leap of faith, which frightens non-believers.

Many people are okay with going to church every now and then or volunteering for a charity from time to time.

But loving God with everything means that all we do is for love of him.

We trust that will take care of us. November - Month of the Holy Souls The month of November is almost here again, it is that special month when we pray for the Holy Souls and for all those who passed away in the last year.

To love God, to love our neighbour, and yes, to love ourselves, is the core of the Old Testament covenant and also the call of Jesus.

Compassion, generosity, and forgiveness are the nature of this love.

Love is not simply an emotion, it is action.

Love is walking the extra mile, giving from our poverty, listening to the tedious, caring for the helpless, welcoming the stranger.

Love calls us to live as if the Kingdom of God were already here and, by doing so, to make it a reality.

Love demands us to go beyond our basic instinct of self-preservation to serve the needs of others.

‘‘Love the Lord your God… Love your neighbour as yourself… ’ I met a community organiser at a number of meetings locally.

I liked her style, so one day I asked her howshe had gotten into her profession.

She spoke of her idealism in college - but that it was mostly talk and little action.

A trip to Malawi in Africa radicalised her as she experienced the sheer enormity of the poverty that she found all around her.

So she decided whenshe came home that she would try to make a difference with her life by working for justice for the homeless, the elderly, and the poor.

What made her different from the others with whom she worked was her ability to listen to people and to help them feel they were all that was important to her at that moment.

She never spoke to me of her religious beliefs, but she clearly exhibited a genuine faith, a gospel faith put into action.

The world in which we live is political, like it or not.

The politics she practiced was based on a re spect for the dignity of each person.

We are called as Christians to share that same perspective, to see t he dignity in everyone.

But we are also called to take it a step further.

We are called to love all others as our neighbours and in that way show our love for God.

That is our political action. Justice flows from that love like a river flows th rough its bed.
