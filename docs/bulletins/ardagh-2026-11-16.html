Thirty-Third Sunday in Ordinary Time – World Day of the Poor: Today’s readings mark the beginning of the Week of Witness dedicated to praying for Christians persecuted for their faith, and speak of trials and endurance.

As we reflect on the readings, we focus on Christians persecuted for their faith, trusting in God’s promise of ultimate victory.

St . Paul today speaks directly to those who may have to pay a price for staying faithf ul.

He says that the day of the Lord will come like a thief.

The world around them talks of peace and security.

The c harity “Aid to the Church in Need ” tells us that some 413 million Christians live in countries where religious freedom is severely violated.

That’s 18% or about one in five Christians.

Let’s keep them in our prayers in a special way this weekend.

Clonony Castle: Sir Mathew De Renzy Versus Sir John MacCoghlan, a play by Eddie Alford, is a Historical Drama about life in West Offaly during the plantations of thes eventeenth century.

This is a fast moving tale o f ambition, reinvention, rivalry and resilience.

The story and text are inspired by the writings of Sir Mathew De Renzy, (1577-1635) who came to West Offaly in 1613 and took up residen ce in Clonony Castle.

Much of the information came from knowledge gleaned by the forensic study of Dr Renzy's manuscripts by Banagher historian Kieran Keenagh an who died earlier this year leaving a great treasure trove of essays on De Renzy's correspondence and land surve ys.

Please come and support The Harbour Theatre Company as we tour this new original play across West Offaly, Luker's Tues Nov 24th McIntyre's Wed Nov 25th Hough's Thurs Nov 26th and Birr Theatre Sat Nov 28th all at 8 p.m.

Thirty-Third

Sunday 15th November This Sunday’s first reading from The Book of Proverbs speaks about the perfect wife nurturing her household.

It is an image of what God has entrusted to us.

In the Gospel the talents refer to our share if the life of Grace– our share in the work of creation, our sharing in the building up of the kingdom of God, and our responsibility for nurturing all those around us.

St Thomas Aquinas writes: “ creation is none other than the relation of creature to the creator. ” 2a.

We depend on God for everything-life, faith the world, beauty and each other.

GOSPEL READING : Matthew 25:14-30 Jesus spoke this parable to his disciples: “The kingdom of heaven is like a man on his way abroad who summoned his servants and entrusted his property to them.

To one he gave five talents, to another two, to a third one; each in proportion to his ability.

Then he setout. The man who had received the five talents promptly went and traded with them and made five more.

The man who had received two made two more in the same way.

But the man who had received one went off and dug a hole in the ground and hid his master’s money.

Now a long time after, the master of those servants came back and went through his accounts with them.

The man who had received the five talents came forward bringing five more. “Sir, ” he said “you entrusted me with five talents; here are five more that I have made. “ His master said to him, “Well done, good and faithful servant; you have shown you can be faithful in small things, I will trust you with greater; come and join in your master’s happiness ”. Next the man with the two talents came forward. “Sir, ” he said “you entrusted me with two talents; here are two more that I have made. ” His master said to him, “Well done, good and faithful servant; you have shown you can be faithful in small things, I will trust you with greater; come and join in your master’s happiness ”. Last came forward the man who had the one talent. “Sir, ” said he “I had heard you were a hard man, reaping where you have not sown and gathering where you have not scattered; so I was afraid, and I went off and hid your talent in the ground.

Here it is; it was yours, you have it back. ” But his master answered him , “You wicked and lazy servant!

So you knew that I reap where I have not sown and gather where I have not scattered?

Well then, you should have deposited my money with the bankers, and on my return I would have recovered my capital with interest. So now, take the talent from him and give it to the man who has the five talents.

For to everyone who has will be given more, and he will have more than enough; but from the man who has not, even what he has will be taken away.

As for this good-for-nothing servant, throw him out into the dark, where there will be weeping and grinding of teeth. ” Making a Case for the ‘Lazy’ Servant We most often read the Parable of the Talents as a lesson a bout using our gifts wisely, taking risks and making the most of what God has entrusted to us . But, can a case be made for the lazy servant?

Among others, Americ an preacher Barbara Brown Taylor thinks so: ‘I know Jesus told it as a parable of the kingdom.

But how does that turn the little master in the story into the capital Master of the world?

Read the parable from the view of someone living on a lake in Nica ragua or one of the'little ones' who followed Jesus around, or someone camping out and it c an be hard to see the master as anything but one more tycoon sitting on a pile of money so high that he cannot see the bottom of it.

Look again at the third servant. He refuses to participate in a system that rewards the rich and demands ever-increasing returns.

He buries the money rather than using it to make an already wealthy man wealthier.

And, most importa ntly, he dares to speak the truth: the master reaps where he has not sown and gathers where he has not scattered.

Perhaps this servant is not simply lazy, fearful or unfaithful.

Perhaps he is the only one brave enough to challenge an unjust system.

His refusal to play the game comes at a cost: he is condemned and thrown out.

The master ca nnot tolerate someone who exposes the truth about how his wealth is accumulated.

Could Jesus be inviting us to look beyond the obvious interpretation?

Might this story challenge us to question systems built on greed, exploitation and the relentless pursuit of profit?

Perhaps the real question is not whether we are multiplying our ta lents, but whose interests we are serving.

Are we helping to build a kingdom of compassion and generosity, or simply making the powerful more powerful?

Sometimes, what looks like laziness to the world may actually be an act of courage and resistance . Longford (Templemichael) https://longfordparish.com/pdf/151126.pdf
