BIC: AIBKIE2D OCTOBER—MONTH OF THE ROSARY The Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary is celebrated on 7 October every year. World Rosary Day is also held on 7 October, specifically to coincide with that feast. We encourage the rosary to be recited throughout the month of October and especially on World Rosary Day at 7:00pm. Pope Leo’s Message on the Rosary “I would like to make an appeal that is particularly close to my heart. I invite all the faithful & all Christian communities to pray the holy rosary throughout the coming month of October, with the particular intention that wars may cease & that peace may be established among peoples in conflict. Let us accompany this prayer with concrete acts of penance & mercy, committing ourselves always to fostering dialogue & reconciliation. May our most holy Mother intercede forus!”(In Lourdes recently). When back in Rome Pope Leo was joined by approx. 700 children in the Vatican Gardens. Encouraging them in their Rosary prayer, he reminded the children that they were asking Jesus and Mary for “a special gift: peace forallthe children of the world, especially those who live in places where there is war, inso many countries around the world.” Pope Leo also highlighted that the children gathered in the Vatican were part of something much larger. During the week of October 1–7, around one million people around the world are joining in the Rosary. He described this as “Fifty million ‘Hail Marys’ rising to Heaven from every part of the world & in every language, asking for peace, joining together like one enormous Rosary, embracing all the continents, peoples, & nations of the world.” Pope Leo concluded by stressing that prayer must be lived out through action: “Prayers must also be accompanied by good deeds & acts of love. St Francis of Assisi, whose feast day we celebrate on 4th Oct, was known by the many inhisowndayasthe Poverello - the little poor man. Over the course ofthe last 800 years the ideal of poverty of St Francis has been much discussed and often misunderstood. The early friars didnotgo without – going without wasnotthe understanding of poverty that St Francis held – what he wanted for his friars was to live without anything of their own, and what they needed they grew or t ended themselves, or they worked, not for money, butforthe actual things they needed. He had recognised that with ownership often came conflict, and that itwasa strange idea that we human beings could own that which was created in relationship with us. Wearein fact tenants, and our rent is love, trust, and a life lived together. We need to trust that God will provide for us both materially and spiritually, he will give us the grace we require to tend and keep His Garden, and howdowe know this? Because He sent His Son. Just like the Landlord of the parable the Sonwas sent. Jesus was sent tousto remind usofthe true nature of our life and the real relationship we have with God and Earth, and to fulfil the covenant God has made with the whole of Creation. Yes, itis true that human fear ledtothe Passion and Death of Jesus, but the Resurrection promises us that God is not going to take the Vineyard away from us and give it to someone else, but Heis going to continue to offer his Gifts tousso that what we have harmed can be healed. We will be, ifweare open, brought back into the Covenant between God – the Human Family – Earth, and live together in the fulness of life. - Br. God Entrusts Us with His Blessings: Just as the landowner provided everything for the vineyard to thrive, God has given usallwe need – His Word, His Spirit, and His Grace. God is Patient, but there are Consequences for Rejection: God’s patience is great, butitisnot limitless. When people continuously turn away from Him, they face the cons equences of their choices. Jesus warned that the kingdom would be taken from those who rejec ted Him and given to those who bear its fruit. Jesus, the Cornerstone we must build our lives on: We must ask ourselves: Is Jesus the cornerstone of our lives? Do we build our decisions, actions, and purpose on Him?Orarewe trying to build on our own understanding? Only when Christ is our foundation can we truly bear fruit for God’s kingdom. A Call to Repentance and Fruitfulness: The religious leaders understood that Jesus was speaking about them, but instead of repenting, they sought to kill Him. God desires that we bear fruit, that is, to live lives filled with faith, love, and obedience. He calls us to humility and repentance, turning from self-centeredness to Him. May we embrace Christ as our foundation, live faithfully, and bear the fruit that glorifies God.
