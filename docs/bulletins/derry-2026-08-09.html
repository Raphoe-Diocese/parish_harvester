Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand
The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the
crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those
who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope.
In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely,
discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear,
or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle.
The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry
people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what
they already possessed.
God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our
time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these
gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine.
Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds
us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all
carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place
them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough.
Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His
blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive
generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace
continues to multiply in countless lives.
Prayer
Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great
things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our
limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom
we meet. Amen.

First Reading: 1 K. 19:9, 11 -13. In the time of Solo-
mon (from 970 BC) and after him Israel had become
prosperous but corrupt. The prophet Elijah (active 870 -
850 BC) was threatened by Queen Jezebel and had fled
to Mount Horeb where Moses had received the 10 com-
mandments. There God revealed himself to him in a
'gentle breeze' (and gives him firm instructions, as he
had done for Moses.)
Second Reading: Rm 9:1 -5. St Paul is heart -broken
that his Jewish brothers and sisters, who from the time
of Abraham had been prepared so carefully by God to
receive the world’s Redeemer, had rejected Him. They
had not known ‘the time of their visitation.’ (Lk 19:44).
Gospel: Mt. 14:22 -33. The ‘fourth watch’ was between
3 and 6 am. A scary time in a storm. St Matthew’s mes-
sage is that in troubled times, when we find ourselves
caught between doubt and belief, we must venture forth
in faith, relying confidently upon God’s grace. It is
always there for us through Jesus, to make up our short-
fall in courage.

REST A WHILE
In our day -to-day life’s journey, oftentimes we are overwhelmed with
our daily routines, individual tasks as well as duties and responsibilities.
It seems that the 24 hours of the day are not enough because we are too
busy with so many things. We do not have time to “stop and smell the
roses.” We do not have time to relax and to take time out of our busy
schedules to appreciate and enjoy God ’s gift of the beauty of life. We
need time to rest, to recognises our human limitations that we need essential respite to
prevent us from being exhausted and to avoid the possible danger of breakdown.
According to the mechanical engineers, even machines need cooling -off time to prevent
overheating. When we approach our “hallowed space ” like spending time praying in front
of the Blessed Sacrament or lighting candles inside the church, we are refreshed and
strengthened. We will be inspired to go on despite all the burdens, challenges, and
obstacles in life. That is why after the tiresome and successful mission of the Apostles the
Lord told them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while. ”
Following Jesus ’ command, these first followers were spiritually and physically recharged
to do the next job. We also badly need to have some “refreshment ” of the Lord and to
spend time with Him in silent prayer to find meaning in every life ’s situation. In this way,
we are far from being “worn out” and preventing ourselves from “falling apart. ” And so,

Christmas Message 2025
This week we will celebrate Christmas Day. The birth of Jesus
Christ, over two thousand years ago, was a pivotal moment in
the lives of many people at that time, and ever since. In a
sense, we join the Angels, the Shepherds, and the Wise Men
who rejoiced with Mary and Joseph at the birth of the long
awaited Saviour of the world.
We join the apostles and the disciples - men and women - who
knew Jesus personally, who walked with Him and worked with
Him in those early days of spreading the Good News of God's
love for us in sending His Son among us.
We join all those who, down the years and right up to our own
time, have lived up to their baptismal promises to be God's
people, living and handing on the faith to the generations
coming behind them.
As we gather with family, friends, neighbours, and fellow
parishioners for Christmas Mass and other celebrations, let
us pledge ourselves to be a people of Faith, Hope, and Love
in a world which needs the presence of Jesus Christ - through
our care, our words, and our actions - now more than ever
before.
Merry Christmas and a very Happy New Year.

JESUS IS WITH US IN THE STORMS OF LIFE
Every Christian experiences storms. We naturally ask the Lord to remove our
difficulties, yet the Gospel reveals that Jesus often offers something even
greater: His presence. Rather than simply calming the storm around us, He
seeks to deepen our trust in Him within it.
The greatest miracle in this Gospel is not that Jesus walked on water, nor even
that Peter briefly did the same. The true miracle is that frightened, faltering
disciples became worshippers who recognised Jesus as the Son of God.
Perhaps our prayer should be less, “Lord, make my life easier, ” and more,
“Lord, whatever storm I must face, keep my eyes fixed on You. ” When we do
stumble, as Peter did, we can be confident that Christ ’s saving hand is already
reaching out to lift us up

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 4:22-33
In today’s Gospel, the disciples find themselves struggling against strong winds while Jesus
comes to them, walking on the water. At first, they are filled with fear, but Jesus reassures
them with the simple yet powerful words: "Take courage! It is I. Do not be afraid."
Like the disciples, we all encounter storms in our lives - times of uncertainty, anxiety,
illness, or loss. Peter's experience reminds us that faith is not about never doubting, but about
keeping our eyes fixed on Christ. When Peter focuses on Jesus, he walks on the water; when
he focuses on the wind and waves, he begins t o sink. Yet even then, Jesus immediately
reaches out His hand to save him.
This Gospel is a beautiful reminder that the Lord is always present, even when He seems
distant. He does not promise a life free from storms, but He does promise that we will never
face them alone. As we gather around the Lord's table this Sunday, may we h ear His
reassuring voice, trust in His presence, and reach out to Him in faith, knowing that His hand
is always extended towards us.
