https://www.parishofaghyaran.com Ardmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen. READINGS FOR 19TH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME First Reading: 1 K. 19:9, 11 -13. In the time of Solo- mon (from 970 BC) and after him Israel had become prosperous but corrupt. The prophet Elijah (active 870 - 850 BC) was threatened by Queen Jezebel and had fled to Mount Horeb where Moses had received the 10 com- mandments. There God revealed himself to him in a 'gentle breeze' (and gives him firm instructions, as he had done for Moses.) Second Reading: Rm 9:1 -5. St Paul is heart -broken that his Jewish brothers and sisters, who from the time of Abraham had been prepared so carefully by God to receive the world’s Redeemer, had rejected Him. They had not known ‘the time of their visitation.’ (Lk 19:44). Gospel: Mt. 14:22 -33. The ‘fourth watch’ was between 3 and 6 am. A scary time in a storm. St Matthew’s mes- sage is that in troubled times, when we find ourselves caught between doubt and belief, we must venture forth in faith, relying confidently upon God’s grace. It is always there for us through Jesus, to make up our short- fall in courage. Christ the King, Strathroy: Saturday 6.15pm -6.45pm REST A WHILE In our day -to-day life’s journey, oftentimes we are overwhelmed with our daily routines, individual tasks as well as duties and responsibilities. It seems that the 24 hours of the day are not enough because we are too busy with so many things. We do not have time to “stop and smell the roses.” We do not have time to relax and to take time out of our busy schedules to appreciate and enjoy God ’s gift of the beauty of life. We need time to rest, to recognises our human limitations that we need essential respite to prevent us from being exhausted and to avoid the possible danger of breakdown. According to the mechanical engineers, even machines need cooling -off time to prevent overheating. When we approach our “hallowed space ” like spending time praying in front of the Blessed Sacrament or lighting candles inside the church, we are refreshed and strengthened. We will be inspired to go on despite all the burdens, challenges, and obstacles in life. That is why after the tiresome and successful mission of the Apostles the Lord told them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while. ” Following Jesus ’ command, these first followers were spiritually and physically recharged to do the next job. We also badly need to have some “refreshment ” of the Lord and to spend time with Him in silent prayer to find meaning in every life ’s situation. In this way, we are far from being “worn out” and preventing ourselves from “falling apart. ” And so, let us all find time to relax and rest for a while with Him. JESUS IS WITH US IN THE STORMS OF LIFE Every Christian experiences storms. We naturally ask the Lord to remove our difficulties, yet the Gospel reveals that Jesus often offers something even greater: His presence. Rather than simply calming the storm around us, He seeks to deepen our trust in Him within it. The greatest miracle in this Gospel is not that Jesus walked on water, nor even that Peter briefly did the same. The true miracle is that frightened, faltering disciples became worshippers who recognised Jesus as the Son of God. At first, they are filled with fear, but Jesus reassures them with the simple yet powerful words: "Take courage! It is I. Do not be afraid." Like the disciples, we all encounter storms in our lives - times of uncertainty, anxiety, illness, or loss. Peter's experience reminds us that faith is not about never doubting, but about keeping our eyes fixed on Christ. This Gospel is a beautiful reminder that the Lord is always present, even when He seems distant. He does not promise a life free from storms, but He does promise that we will never face them alone.
