In Irish, the idea numi nous with a sense of final adventure, the adventure of our immortality.

Grounded in Story John Moriarty Saturday evening the news that Ciara McKeenan had come to the end of her earthly sojourn, after bravely living with a cancer diagnosis since the Spring of 2025. Her memoir My Greatest Race , was available in bookshops from June 18, 2026. The book is an amazing read mindful of the fact that at the time of writing, she was living with cancer; She was living with the affliction, but she was doing so with a style, a faith, a determination, an attitude that was inspiring.

I o ften wonder, when a person is confronted with such a horrific healt h diagnosis, how does a person cope?

Is it a time to ‘stop living’; drown in self-remorse, allow despair to eat into the soul of a pers on?

Finding oneself in such a prediction, it seems natural to wrap oneself up in self-pity, ‘whyme?’ ‘it’s not fair’ . I’m forever amazed at people who are shouldered with impossible health issues; so often they are inspirational.

It seems to me, that people journeying along this form of ‘road less travelled’ , a path that no one wants to tread, there is no doubt that such individuals show us a way of ‘seeing and being in the darkness’. Ciara was at the pinnacle of her sporting career, she had won a European Gold medal, and could have been a contender for Olympic gold if it wasn’t for injury, which is an ever-present dread for an athlete.

Because of her prowess as an athlete, her application, grit, and attitude so deeply rooted, it seemed very likely that she would bring to followers of the sport for many a year to come.

Then, as I mentioned, to her surprise and horror; at such a she was told of her prognosis.

Coming to grips with the news, she obviously had to try to come to deal with the shock, and respond somehow.

Her make up, her faith, her phil osophy steered her on an awe-inspiring path.

She stated “I am determined to fit as much living into the years I have left . I don't know when the end of the journey will come, but I know that sure as hell I'm going to enjoy every path along the way". She has expressed a deep determination to “fit as much living into the years I have”.

Fitting as much living into her life, did not turn towards indul ging in the best foods, drinking the finest wines, travelling to exotic places across the world.

I was am azed to learn that, at one stage, she had volunteered to participate in the RTÉ series Uncharted , screened last May 2026. Ciara, teaming up with the Dubl in GAA star Michael Darragh MacAuley, under the stewardship of Ray Gogginse xposed herself to the rigours of a massive, six-day, 25 0km coast-to-coast trek across the rugged terrain of Costa Rica, after she had already completed 12 rounds of chemotherapy!

A few years ago, at his invitation, I reme mber walking behind Denis Crosby along a lesser well know mud path flanking the cliffs spanning part of the West Clare coast. I’m not good on heights, after a scare I had when I was a teenager.

So, after placing my trust in Crosby for 50 yards or so, conscious that there was an unhindered 100 ft drop all the way down to the Atlantic Ocean, I notified leader who seemed oblivious to the danger, that I was turning back post- hast to base.

So, when I spotted this woman climbing a cliff faced mountain, not only was I awestruck by her determination and resilience, but was actually feeling panic take hold on my own body in the viewing.

As with all the programmes in this gruelling series, the t hree participants spent ‘time out’ moments sharing aspects of their own lives, and Ciara was not wanting in speaking of the recent happenings concerning her health, that has changed her life with a bang.

I think, that part of her motivation for living and speaking in such a public way through the various media outlets, was with a view of helping, supporting, others who were confronted with similar health related challenges.

Ciara married Thomas Moran, her partner and her ‘steadfast rock’, on Oct . 13th last. She stated at one stage “I'm not scared to die.

I just don't want it to happen anytime soon.

It is a very strange thing to be 34 and face the fact of your mor tality.” Last Saturday, her spirit left her broken body, and has now joined with so many others ‘atá‘ar Shlí na Fhirinne . Her story, for years to come, be it as a camogie player , a top-class track athlete, an incredibly resilient brave woman, will be a source of strength, and comfort to so many.

In last Sunday’s Duilleog, I had included a beautiful definition of sainthood. ‘For centuries the church has confronted the human c ommunity with role models of greatness.

We call the m saints when what we really often mean to say is'icon,' 'star,' 'hero,' ones so possessed by an internal vision of divine goodness that they give us a glimpse of the face of God in the centre of the human.

To my mind, Ciara has ‘certainly given us a glimpse of the face of God, in the centre of the human.

May she rest in peace… Resting in Peace…Suaimhneas Shioraí This weekend, we especially remember members of the co mmunity who have departed this earthly life over the past 12 months.

It is a time, as a community, to grieve their great loss as we are also mindful of their families who in their grief are trying to go on living in their absence.

It's important to bring to mind once again the many and varied ways these special people graced family and community with their gift and their goodness over the years . The quality of life in every community is coloured by t he personalities who dwell in it.

In our own unique way, every person adds to the texture and colour of the community.

We don’t have to be spectacular or loud; just doing our bes t to honour ourselves and our God, by being true to the gifts the Good Lord has given us.

In saying that, I believe there can be a sadness, that resi des in the hearts of those who die as well.

Very fewpeople choose death; it comes to us, visits us in a myriad of ways, be that sickness of mind or body, accide nts.

Most people, I would prefer to ‘ go on living’ with family and friend.

So, to be denied that ongoing opportunity has to be felt by them as well.

I do think, therefore, that the souls of the virtuous also need to be comforted by those left behind.

It seems unnatural, to me , that a person who loved being in the presence of family, can of a sudden, switch over to enjoying eternal bliss, and not feel regret, remorse in having to leave loved ones behind.

I believe that they forever try to reach out to us, to comfort us, to reassure us as well.

In the same way, it is incumbent on us, to reassure them as well that we are managing in their absence.

We entrust them to the Lord’s Divine Embrace . Let us comfort them, in the remembering. “Hoping against hope, we affirm that they [our loved ones who have died] have fallen not into nothingness but into the embrace of the living God.

And that is where we can find them again; when we open our hearts to the silent calmness of God’s own life in which we dwell, not by calling them back to where we are, but by descending into the depth of our own hearts where God also abides.

This week's reading a fe i follows a series of warnings and predictions by Jesus about the jou know neithe eich | coming of the Son of Man.

He teaches the disciples that they must remain vigilant so that they will not be caught unprepared.

When thinking about the parable of the wise and foolish virgins, it is important to consider the first-century wedding traditions of Palestine.

Scholars tell us that it was the custom of the day for young maidens— friends and family members of the bride—to meet the bridegroom when he came to bring his bride to her new home.

As with many of Jesus’ parables, several levels of interpretation are possible.

In last week's Gospel, we heard Jesus warn against following the example of the Pharisees and scribes.

If read in the context of early Christianity’s struggle to define itself against Pharisaic Judaism, this parable is a continuing critique of Judaism.

It suggests that the Jewish leaders were like the foolish virgins, unprepared to meet Jesus, the bridegroom of Israel In the chapter preceding this parable, however, Jesus warns about the destruction of Jerusalem, the tribulation of the end times, and the coming of the Son of Man.

When read in this context, today’s parable is a waming to the Christian community to remain vigilant and prepared to receive Jesus, the Son of Man who will return at the end of time.

This interpretation is supported by the reference to the delay of the bridegroom.

The Christian community for whom Matthew wrote this Gospel was coming to terms with the realization that the promise of Jesus’ return would not be fulfilled within their lifetimes.

The question remains for us to ask ourselves, Are we ready to receive Jesus?

Will we be prepared to receive him?
