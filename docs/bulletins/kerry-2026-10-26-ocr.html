Each week invites us to reflect on a different aspect of the journey.

This week, our theme is “Setting Sail” — a special invitation to those who may be discerning God's call to the priesthood.

Our thoughts turn naturally to St Brendan the Navigator, our great Kerry saint, who reminds us that every journey begins with a willingness to leave the familiar behind.

Brendan did not remain on the shore, waiting until every uncertainty had been resolved.

He untied the rope, set sail and entrusted himself to God.

There is something deeply meaningful in that image for anyone discerning a vocation.

It can be tempting to wait until we have all the answers, until the whole journey is mapped out, or until we feel completely ready.

Yet God rarely reveals the entire voyage at once.

He invites us to trust Him enough to take the next faithful step.

Is God Calling You? An invitation to men in our parish and throughout South Kerry If you have ever wondered whether God might be calling you to the Diocesan Priesthood, please do not be afraid to take that first step.

Reach out to Fr Sean or your local priest. You will be met with understanding, encouragement and every support as you explore what God may be asking of you.

There is no need to have everything figured out before beginning the conversation.

Discerning is a journey, and you do not have to make it alone.

You may be surprised by where that first conversation leads.

All that is asked at the beginning is a willing heart and the courage to listen.

Fáilte roimh cách. A NOTE FROM FR. In this Sunday’s Gospel (Matthew 22:34– 40), Jesus gives us the two greatest commandments: love God with all your heart, soul and mind, and love your neighbour as yourself.

Jesus brings together two commandments fromd ifferent parts of the Old Testament and makes them inseparable.

But not ice the order. Love of God comes first. Why is that so important?

St Pope John Paul lived through the horrors of Nazism and Communism.

He witnessed what happens when human beings are stripped of their dignity and treated as objects rather than persons.

In his encyclical Eva ngelium Vitae, he warned that when we lose our sense of God, we risk losing our sense of the human person.

When we forget that every human being is created in God’s image and likeness, we begin to measure people by their usefulness or co nvenience rather than their inherent dignity.

I heard something recently that really struck me: “There was a time when we used things and loved people.

Today, unfortunately, we seem to love things and use people.” This is a real danger in our increasingly secular society, where the unborn, the elderly and the vulnerable c an be regarded as burdens rather than precious human beings.

Human dignity is not bestowed by the State or earned through achievement.

It comes from God. This is why Jesus places love of God first. When we recognise God as our Father, we begin to recognise every human being as our brother or sister.

Whe n we forget God, we risk forgetting what it means to be human.

May we as a nation realise the consequences of removing God from our lives.

We just simply don’t remove God but we remove the innate dignity of every human person.
