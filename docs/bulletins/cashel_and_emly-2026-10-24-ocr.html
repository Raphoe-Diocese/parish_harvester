MISSION SUNDAY: TWENTY-NINTH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME 18th October 2026 Year A

On this World Mission Sunday, we reach out and offer our love, our support, prayers and joy to all the Christian communities throughout the world. "Then repay to Caesar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God". The Pharisees are looking to Jesus for advice on their taxes.

They try to flatter him first, ironically by speaking the truth about him, but the tone is still disrespectful.

This is a dangerous situation because in an unlikely alliance, the Pharisees have brought along the Herodians with them.

The Herodians were great supporters of the Roman Empire, so tensions here would have been high.

If Jesus answers "no" to their question about paying taxes to Caesar he risks being reported to the Roman authorities as a revolutionary; if he says "yes" then he may be accused of colluding with the occupying Empire and abandoning the people.

Jesus sees through this "malice" and speaks truth to power.

He invites us to explore another way, a third way, one that is not a revolt but neither does it lie down and submit to oppression.

Yes, do your civic duty, but do not forget that what belongs to God; this overrides any political power.

Sometimes kingdom values come into conflict with the status quo.

We only have to glance at our newsfeeds to see this play out across our world right now.

The Gospel calls us to walk a different path.

A path that is inclusive, that builds peace, not walls; that welcomes the stranger, that calls out injustice and speaks up for those who have no voice.

World Mission Sunday, 2026 marks a global effort to support missionary work across the world.

The 2026 theme for the day is "One in Christ, united in mission," a theme which reflects Pope Leo's motto for his pontificate, although we are many, we are One in Christ. As we support Mission Sunday, how might each of us respond to this call to unity in our own communities?
