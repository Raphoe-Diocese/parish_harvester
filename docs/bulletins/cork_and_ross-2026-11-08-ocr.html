Ballinhassig Parish Newsletter for Ballygarvan, Ballyheada and Goggins Hill Vol. 35 No. 8 Sunday: November 1st, 2026 (All Saints) Remembrance and Thanksgiving for our Loved Ones Mary O’Hwrites…. Many people find November a difficult month, those who have been recently bereaved and those who carry the scars from a past painful bereavement.

Most of November we concentrate on remembering our deceased loved ones: - The Holy Souls Novena, the visits to the graveyard, while these are spiritually good for us they make it impossible for people to avoid being revisited by the pain and loss of a loved one.

We hold dear the memory of those who have been a truly significant part of each of us.

The gift of memory and its place within our hearts brings us consolation . Our hearts are warmed by the comfort of others and slowly over time we begin to heal and embrace life once again as our loved ones would wish us to live.

We ask God to heal our pain and sadness as the month of November is a time of Remembrance and Thanksgiving for the lives of our loved ones , no matter how long or short that earthly life was.

Reflection:- Holy Souls are special people Whom from us God did call We are left here broken hearted For we love them one and all.

All we have left is just our memories Of the times when they were near Longing for those precious moments Wishing they were here.

But death is not the end for us It is just an open door where some day we will be with them With more happiness that before.

Once again with all our loved ones All our sorrow it will end In God’s home we’ll be re united Parents, family and friends.

So in this special holy month Remember them and pray Those holy souls our own dear loved ones to God are on their way.

The sky is still as blue, the grass is still as green, the birds sing as sweetly, the flowers have not lost their fragrance, it may not seem so at the moment but slowly it will.

Plenary Indulgences in November By long tradition, plenary indulgences for the souls of t he departed may be gained in November as Follows: - On All Souls Day A Plenary indulgence for the holy souls may be gained once between noon on November 1st and midnight on November 2nd, by visiting a church or oratory and there reciting one Our Father and the Apostles’ Creed.

The person making this prayer must: Have recently received the Sacrament of Confession (any time within the week preceding or the week following November 1st) Have received Holy Comunion (any day from November 1st to 8th).

Have prayed for the Pope’s intentions :- one Our Father and one Hail Mary, or any other prayer of one’s choice.

On any day in November A plenary indulgence for the holy souls may be gained once each day between November 1st and 8th, by visiting a cemetery and praying for t he dead.

The same conditions as above (confession, communion and prayer f or the pope’s intentions) are also required.

An Invitation to our family of Parishes Why not join us on Thursdays 5th 12th 19th and 26th November in the Parish room in Holy Cross Church, Mahon from 7.30 pm to 9.00 pm. An opportunity to come together and spend some time reading and discussing the Scriptures, followed by prayer.

Everyone is most welcome and we even have a cuppa and chat afterwards as well!Today we celebrate the community of faith, being in communion with all the saints, a day of both thanksgiving and vision.

Thanksgiving because of the example and inspiration of the saints, who have come to maturity, to the measure of the full stature of Christ. (Eph 4:13).

Vision because our Gospel today describes the path we must take to make the same journey home to God.

If we wish to join the saints both now and into eternity, we must make our own the “mission statement” of Jesus, which is the Sermon on the Mount and especially the opening invitation to true happiness.

Prayer Jesus you are our teacher within and today we ask your help.

Just as you have brought our brothers and sisters to full maturity in you, so also guide us and be with us as we walk your Way.

By your grace, may we too be counted among saints in the present moment and in the life to come.• First Reading (Revelation 7:2–4, 9–14) John sees a vast, uncountable multitude from every nation, race, and tongue, robed in white and standing before the throne and the Lamb.

These are the ones who have passed through trials and washed their robes clean in the blood of Christ. Second Reading (1 John 3:1–3) St. John reminds believers of their present identity as children of God, noting that while what we shall be has not yet been fully revealed, we will be like Him when we see Him as He is.

Gospel (Matthew 5:1–12a) The Beatitudes from the Sermon on the Mount.

Jesus proclaims true blessing not in worldly power, but in spiritual poverty, meekness, mourning, hunger for righteousness, mercy, purity of heart, peacemaking, and endurance of persecution.

The blessings in the Beatitudes are primarily future blessings, but there can be an anticipation of the blessings in the present.

At /f_irst reading some Beatitudes may seem to describe circumstances that you would like to avoid at all costs.

Read them slowly. Stay with each one for a while.

Let yourself get a sense of the paradox involved in each one.

Perhaps you have had an experience of a deeper and more authentic life, a blessing, when… 1. You were poor - you knew your need of God 2. You mourned – could feel for others 3. You were meek – not emotionally out of control 4. You hungered and thirsted for some cause 5. You were merciful rather than vengeful 6. You were pure in heart – a person of integrity, whose actions and intentions correspond 7. You were a peacemaker 8. You were persecuted because you stood for something PRAYER All-holy God, you call your people to holiness.

As we keep the festival of your saints, give us their meekness and poverty of spirit, a thirst for righteousness, and purity of heart.

May we share with them the richness of your kingdom and be clothed in the glory you bestow.

Remembering – As we move into the month of November, we will lovingly remember our loved ones who have died. 107 funeral Masses have been celebrated across St. Michael’s and Holy Cross Churches since this time last year. 70 funeral Masses have been celebrated in St. Michael’s and 37 in Holy Cross Church since the start of November last year.

These details give us a sense of the strength of both parishes to prayerfully support one another at the most difficult of times.

At each of these Masses, there is a deep sense of the consoling presence of God and trust in the ultimate power of the Risen Jesus who ‘defeats death forever’. This sense runs very deep and is much more than just sharing our sympathies.

It’s our prayer to accompany those who have died into the eternal presence of God and back into arms of their own deceased family members and loved ones.

So, when we gather during this coming week for the Remembrance Mass, we will be praying, consoling and strengthening the heartbroken.

While this is a sad time, it’s also a beautiful time and that is how we approach November, in hope and new life.

May all those who have died now rest in the peace of the Lord and may we remember them lovingly.
