Thirty-Third Sunday in Ordinary Time The Gospel tells us clearly that God distributes his gifts among us as he wills and entrusts each of us with a mission.

He gives us what we need to produce fruits for his kingdom and expects us to responsibly and diligently.

No two people are exactly alike, a nd God treats each one individually as a unique person made in his image.

We need to live before God and respond to him sincerely by using the talents he has given us to the maximum.

The servants who invest their talents and make a return on them have understood the purpose of their lives and the time they have at their disposal.

These servants were generous with everything their master had given them, making it bear fruit, and they received from him the reward of grea ter intimacy and more responsibility.

How often are we paralysed by fear or false prudence into doing nothing, into trying to preserve ourselves!

Sometimes, we don’t take God’s gifts seriously or think we have been given very little , and we use that as a rationalisation for making no effort or producing little for God.

We blame circumstances or others, but the fact is we are neglecting to produce the fruits God wants.

The master didn’t expect a return of five talents from the servant to whom he gave only one.

He would have been happy with a return of one more, but the lazy servant closed in on his egoism, se lf-love, and laziness.

We must resolve to use our God-given talents wisely to nethim a significant return.

Bringing Communion to the Elderly, Sick and Housebound From the earliest celebrations of the Mass, one of its important dimensions has been bringing Holy Communion to those who are unable to attend because of illness or infirmity.

This tradition continues in parishes throughout the world today.

Here in our parish, we are deeply grateful to the Ministers of the Eucharist and family members who bring Holy Communion to parishioners who, in earlier years, were faithful in their attendance at Mass.

This ministry remains an important extension of our celebration of the Eucharist. During the COVID pandemic, certain practices emerged in response to the challenges of the time.

Now that those restrictions have passed, however, some of these practices are no longer appropriate.

To ensure best practice and to account properly for the Blessed Sacrament, we ask all who bring Holy Communion to the housebound to observe the following guidelines.

Please use a valid and blessed pyx when carrying Holy Communion.

To ensure that we know where the Blessed Sacrament is being brought, those bringing Communion to the housebound are asked to approach the priest during the distribution of Holy Communion.

The priest will place the hosts directly into the pyx.

Please do not ask a Minister of the Eucharist to place Communion in the pyx.

Holy Communion should be brought to the person as soon as possible after Mass, and only the number of hosts required should be taken.

The recommended prayers should be used when giving Communion in the home.

After Communion has been received, the pyx should be purified.

If the person is unavailable to receive Communion, the minister should consume the Blessed Sacrament.

Under no circumstances should Holy Communion be kept overnight or left in the pyx.

Our parishioners greatly value the opportunity to receive Holy Communion in their homes.

To continue this important ministry well, it is essential that we follow these practices and ensure that the Blessed Sacrament is treated with the reverence and respect it deserves at all times.
