Ardmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen. Local Safeguarding Representatives are Mary McGranaghan and Brídín Hannaway A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 4:13-21 In today's Gospel, Jesus sees a hungry crowd and responds with compassion. Rather than sending the people away, he invites the disciples to bring him the little they have: five loaves and two fish. In his hands, this humble offering becomes more than enough for everyone. Not only are the people fed, but there are even baskets of leftovers, revealing the abundance of God's love and care for his people. This miracle reminds us that God often works through ordinary gifts offered with faith. We may feel that our time, talents, or resources are too limited to make a difference, yet Christ asks only that we place them in his hands. He is the one who multiplie s what is given in love, bringing hope where there is discouragement, healing where there is pain, and generosity where there seems to be scarcity. As we gather around the Lord's table each Sunday, we too come with our needs, our hopes, and our small offerings. Here we are nourished by the Bread of Life, strengthened by God's grace, and reminded that no gift offered in love is ever wasted. Having been fed by Christ, we are sent out to feed others through acts of charity, kindness, forgiveness, and hope. May we trust in God's abundant generosity, share freely with those in need, and become instruments of his compassion in our families, our parish, and o ur wider community. Christ the King, Strathroy: Saturday 6.15pm -6.45pm REST A WHILE In our day -to-day life’s journey, oftentimes we are overwhelmed with our daily routines, individual tasks as well as duties and responsibilities. It seems that the 24 hours of the day are not enough because we are too busy with so many things. We do not have time to “stop and smell the roses.” We do not have time to relax and to take time out of our busy schedules to appreciate and enjoy God ’s gift of the beauty of life. We need time to rest, to recognises our human limitations that we need essential respite to prevent us from being exhausted and to avoid the possible danger of breakdown. According to the mechanical engineers, even machines need cooling -off time to prevent overheating. When we approach our “hallowed space ” like spending time praying in front of the Blessed Sacrament or lighting candles inside the church, we are refreshed and strengthened. We will be inspired to go on despite all the burdens, challenges, and obstacles in life. That is why after the tiresome and successful mission of the Apostles the Lord told them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while. ” Following Jesus ’ command, these first followers were spiritually and physically recharged to do the next job. We also badly need to have some “refreshment ” of the Lord and to spend time with Him in silent prayer to find meaning in every life ’s situation. In this way, we are far from being “worn out” and preventing ourselves from “falling apart. ” And so, let us all find time to relax and rest for a while with Him. What would you do if you discovered a hidden treasure worth more than you could ever imagine? Jesus tells us that the Kingdom of Heaven is exactly like that. The man who finds the treasure and the merchant who discovers the pearl of great price both sell everything —not reluctantly, but joyfully — because they know that what they have found surpasses everything they are leaving behind. The Christian life is often seen as a list of sacrifices, but today's Gospel offers a different perspective. Following Christ is not first about what we lose; it is about what we gain. When we truly encounter the love, mercy and friendship of Jesus, everything else begins to find its proper place. One of the clearest signs of what we truly treasure is the time we willingly spend in its presence. We make time for the people we love. If Christ is truly the Pearl beyond all price, then we will naturally desire to spend time with Him —in prayer, in the Eucharist, in His Word, and in the quiet moments of each day. The amount of time we spend with someone is often the clearest indication of how much we value that relationship. This week, let us ask ourselves: What —or whom—am I giving my time to? May we have the wisdom to recognise Christ as the Pearl beyond all price and the courage to place Him above everything else.
