Ardmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen. Do the disciples not realise by now that if they leave the shore without Jesus in the boat, the waters become very choppy indeed? In fact, they had gone so far, that they didn’t even recognise him when he did catchup to them. Those of you sitting in the nave of a church and reading this may like to look up above you [notice to clergy: parishioners looking at the ceiling may not be listening]. The word nave comes from the Latin ,navis,literally meaning ship andsometimes used to refer to the ship of St Peter, i.e. the Church. So perhaps bearingthat in mindthis story can take on an even deeper significance as we read of Peter’sbriefwalk on the water. It is important to remember one thing – at least he got out of the boat. The situation was very serious, the disciples as fishermen knew how quickly conditions could change and soon they’d bein ,well….in over their heads! There was no great surfing culture in Galilee at the time so one has to appreciate Peter’s courage as he steps out onto the stormy water in a real leap of faith and cries ‘Lord, save me’. The reply is not the bureaucratic response above, but ‘Jesus immediately reachedout his hand….’ POINTS TO PONDER The storm at sea is Jesus’ response to the problem of storms in our lives. First, Jesus shows that he is present even where we least expect him. When the disciples saw Jesus walking on the sea he identified himself with the words, It is I, ego eimi in Greek, the same words God used to identified himself to Moses on Mt Sinai. Next, the story teaches us a powerful prayer: ‘Lord, save me.’ St Peter’s cry occurs only once in the whole bible, but it goes to the heart of our helplessness. True to his name, Peter ‘the rock’, he begins to sink like a stone. Peter cries out: ‘Lord, save me.’ At Mass we repeat the litany, ‘Lord, have mercy.’ In our private prayers we might say with the publican: ’Lord, be merciful to me a sinner.’ But, Peter’s short pray includes them all: ‘Lord, save me.’ Finally, the story teaches how Jesus helps us. He brings us back to the boat. That’s the climax of Matthew’s story: Jesus saves by bringing us into the praying community. Separated from the boat, from the people of God, we are exposed to being overwhelmed by the wind and the waves. The Christian assembly gathered in church at the Eucharist is the boat that carries us safely to the other shore. To this day the long central part of our Church buildings, where the community gathers to pray, is called a nave, from the Latin word navis, which means a boat. Culmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen. Christ the King, Strathroy: Saturday 6.15pm -6.45pm REST A WHILE In our day -to-day life’s journey, oftentimes we are overwhelmed with our daily routines, individual tasks as well as duties and responsibilities. It seems that the 24 hours of the day are not enough because we are too busy with so many things. We do not have time to “stop and smell the roses.” We do not have time to relax and to take time out of our busy schedules to appreciate and enjoy God ’s gift of the beauty of life. We need time to rest, to recognises our human limitations that we need essential respite to prevent us from being exhausted and to avoid the possible danger of breakdown. According to the mechanical engineers, even machines need cooling -off time to prevent overheating. When we approach our “hallowed space ” like spending time praying in front of the Blessed Sacrament or lighting candles inside the church, we are refreshed and strengthened. We will be inspired to go on despite all the burdens, challenges, and obstacles in life. That is why after the tiresome and successful mission of the Apostles the Lord told them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while. ” Following Jesus ’ command, these first followers were spiritually and physically recharged to do the next job. We also badly need to have some “refreshment ” of the Lord and to spend time with Him in silent prayer to find meaning in every life ’s situation. In this way, we are far from being “worn out” and preventing ourselves from “falling apart. ” And so, let us all find time to relax and rest for a while with Him. JESUS IS WITH US IN THE STORMS OF LIFE Every Christian experiences storms. We naturally ask the Lord to remove our difficulties, yet the Gospel reveals that Jesus often offers something even greater: His presence. Rather than simply calming the storm around us, He seeks to deepen our trust in Him within it. The greatest miracle in this Gospel is not that Jesus walked on water, nor even that Peter briefly did the same. The true miracle is that frightened, faltering disciples became worshippers who recognised Jesus as the Son of God. 15 Sunday in Ordinary Time (Year A) 12 July 2026 The Soil of Our Lives This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower. It is one of the most familiar stories in the Gospels. A farmer scatters seed. Some falls on the path, some on rocky ground, some among thorns, and some on rich soil. Only the rich soil produces a harvest. Anyone who has ever grown anything in soil knows that you can have the best seed in the world, but if the ground isn't ready, nothing will come of it. The work is in the preparation. The digging, the clearing, the weeding. It is slow and unspectacular, and nobody sees it. But it makes all the difference. Isaiah reminds us this weekend that God's Word never returns to Him empty. Like the rain and the snow that water the earth, His word always accomplishes what it was sent to do. The seed is never the problem. God is endlessly generous. He scatters His grace everywhere, on all of us, without measure and without favourites. The question the parable puts to each of us is a simple one: what kind of ground is He landing on? Every one of us has been planted with gifts. Gifts of faith, of kindness, of patience, of prayer. Some of them are lying dormant because the soil of our lives has become hardened, or crowded, or shallow. The worries of the week, the busyness, the old hurts we haven't let go of — these are the thorns and the stones. They don't destroy the seed. They just stop it growing. The good news is that soil can always be worked: A little time in prayer each day. An honest look at what is choking the Word in us. A return to the Sacraments. 15 Sunday in Ordinary Time (Year A) 12 July 2026 The Soil of Our Lives This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower . It is one of the most familiar stories in the Gospels. A farmer scatters seed. Some falls on the path, some on rocky ground, some among thorns, and some on rich soil. Only the rich soil produces a harvest. Anyone who has ever grown anything in soil knows that you can have the best seed in the world, but if the ground isn't ready , nothing will come of it. The work is in the preparation. The digging, the clearing, the weeding. It is slow and unspectacular , and nobody sees it. But it makes all the difference. Isaiah reminds us this weekend that God's W ord never returns to Him empty . Like the rain and the snow that water the earth, His word always accomplishes what it was sent to do. The seed is never the problem. God is endlessly generous. He scatters His grace everywhere, on all of us, without measure and without favourites. The question the parable puts to each of us is a simple one: what kind of ground is He landing on? Every one of us has been planted with gifts. Gifts of faith, of kindness, of patience, of prayer . Some of them are lying dormant because the soil of our lives has become hardened, or crowded, or shallow . The worries of the week, the busyness, the old hurts we haven't let go of — these are the thorns and the stones. They don't destroy the seed. They just stop it growing. The good news is that soil can always be worked: A little time in prayer each day . An honest look at what is choking the W ord in us. A return to the Sacraments. Small, quiet acts of clearing the ground! When we do that, our roots go down deeper into God, and we find a steadiness that the heat of life cannot wither . St Paul tells us the whole of creation is groaning, waiting to be set free. A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 4:22-33 In today’s Gospel, the disciples find themselves struggling against strong winds while Jesus comes to them, walking on the water. At first, they are filled with fear, but Jesus reassures them with the simple yet powerful words: "Take courage! It is I. Do not be afraid." Like the disciples, we all encounter storms in our lives - times of uncertainty, anxiety, illness, or loss. Peter's experience reminds us that faith is not about never doubting, but about keeping our eyes fixed on Christ. When Peter focuses on Jesus, he walks on the water; when he focuses on the wind and waves, he begins t o sink. Yet even then, Jesus immediately reaches out His hand to save him. This Gospel is a beautiful reminder that the Lord is always present, even when He seems distant. He does not promise a life free from storms, but He does promise that we will never face them alone. As we gather around the Lord's table this Sunday, may we h ear His reassuring voice, trust in His presence, and reach out to Him in faith, knowing that His hand is always extended towards us. Steelstown Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen.
