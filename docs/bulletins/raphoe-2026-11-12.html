This is an image that recurs in many of the parables of Jesus, and we come across it in today’s gospel reading from Matthew, which recounts the parable of the wise and the foolish bridesmaids.

A bit of background may help us to understand this parable.

According to Jewish custom, the role of bridesmaids was to accompany and help the bride to prepare for the wedding.

Bridesmaids would often wait with the bride in her home until the groom arrived and then joyfully escort the bridal couple with lighted lamps to the bridegroom’s home, where the marriage ceremony and subsequent feast would take place.

And the feast would last for several days.

In the parable of Jesus, there are ten bridesmaids.

Five of them are foolish and five are wise.

Because of a lengthy delay in the bridegroom’s arrival, the five foolish bridesmaids run out of oil and rush to town to purchase more.

In their absence, the bridegroom arrives and the wise bridesmaids, who had brought extra oil with them, are ready to accompany the groom and his bride to the wedding feast. When the foolish bridesmaids eventually arrive at the wedding hall and beg to be admitted, they are turned away with the words, ‘Truly I tells you, I do not know you’(Mt 25:12).

The meaning of the parable seems fairly clear.

It is telling us to be always ready to welcome the Lord into our lives whenever he comes.

Jesus ends the parable with the warning, ‘Keep awake, therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour’ (Mt 25:13).

We should always be ready to meet the Lord when he calls us.

Paróiste Chill Taobhóige Tithe Pobail Ghleann Fhinne agus Leitir Bric Thirty-Second Sunday Year A— November 8th 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel for the 32nd Sunday November 8th 2026 The approaching end of the liturgical year regularly brings the usual warnings about being prepared.

We know that in the very early Church the Christians be- lieved that Christ’s Second Coming, and therefore the end of the world, would come very soon, even in their lifetime.

However, by the time the Gospels were put in written form, this began to seem less likely.

Does that mean that the warnings about readiness can be put aside?

Because, for most of us, it is presumably purely academic to wonder when the end of our universe or of our planet will take place.

We are likely to be around when it happens.

What is much more practical, however, is the end of our own world —theend of our own time here on earth.

That is anything but an academic consid eration.

That is the one real- ity of our future that we can be absolutely certain about.

And we know very well —although we often prefer not to think about it —that it can happen at any time and in any place.

Today’s readings bring us face to face with this reality, this fact.

They ask us the question: Am I ready? A nd they imply consideration of a second question: How can I be ready?

This tires the mind and saddens the heart.

Heart speaks to heart. Go in, love and adore.
