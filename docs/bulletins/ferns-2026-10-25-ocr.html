He is known as Simon the Zealot, but nothing else is known about him.

His other name of “Simon Cananaeus” simply adapts another Hebrew word for “zeal” and has nothing to do with the town of Cana.

St Jude, Apostle Jude, also called Thaddaeus, is the apostle who at the Last Supper asked the Lord why he showed himself only to the disciples and not to the world.

For many centuries he was scarcely venerated because people confused him with Judas Iscariot.

He is the patron saint of lost and desperate causes.

Wexford General Hospital Annual Service of Remembrance Wexford General Hospital is holding their annual Service of Remembrance at Clo nard Church, Wexford on

Thursday 12th November 2026 at 7.30pm – this is an annual event.

The Service of Remembrance is particularly intended for families and friends of those who died at Wexford General Hospital over the past year or who have a strong connection with the hospital.

However, the service is also open to anyone who wishes to attend.

The purpose of the service is to provide an opportunity to remember, as a community, the people we loved and who have died - to reminisce and celebrate their lives and all they meant to us.

This event is open to people of all faiths and of none.

Tea and coffee will be served aft erwards.

The Service is organised on an annual basis by the End-of-Life Care Committee at Wexford General Hospital.

This committee is comprised of staff from all areas of the hospital.

Their work includes organising the Service of Remembrance, implementing initiatives such as the writing and sending out of sympathy cards, refurbishment of the mortuary and family rooms, providing wri tten bereavement information for relatives and developing education programmes for staff.

Wexford General Hospital would also like to inform people about the Bereavem ent Support Line which is available from 10am to 1pm, Monday to Friday on 1800 80 70 77. This is a national Freeph one service that has been set up by the Irish Hospice Foundation, in partnership With the HSE, to provide connection, comfort and support for those grieving in these exceptional times.

Any adult who has been impacted by bereavement may contact the support line.

For more information visit www.hospicefoundation.ie . We look forward to seeing you on

This new nested* oral storytelling performance features traditional and original stories, interwoven with atmospheric music from harpist Ruth Lydon.

A gentle reflection on grief, Butterfly Soul follows the journey of a man so consumed by loss that his identity has become simply ‘The Widower’. A recurring dream prompts The Widower to go in search of his beloved.

As he travels he confronts his fears and denial, empowering him to give expression to his grief and to f ind his way home.

Blessed Sacrament Chapel: Emy Rawson, The Bosheen - Remembrance History Notes - Part 146: THE MALT STORES The main locations of the town’s malt stores were the Quay and John Street in times past. These buildings were in effect huge bulk stores with timber floors supported by strong beams to bear the weight of drying grain.

The grain was barley for which south Wexford and Kilkenny were famous.

This grain was purchased by the local malting family, Roche & Sons for conversion to malt via a drying and baking process.

The finished malt was shipped by schooner from the quays of town to Guinness brewery in Dublin for manufacture of its famous stout.

Some names of these schooners spring to mind, including The De Wadden and the Harvest King.

The loading of the malt into these schooners was by dockers who carried the open bags on their shoulders down the bouncing gang-plank to tip the contents into the ship’s hold.

This was a skilled job as a missed step on the gang-plank could send bag and carrier into the tide.
