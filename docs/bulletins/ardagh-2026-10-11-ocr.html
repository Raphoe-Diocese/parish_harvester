In the final years of his life, Francis’s deep immersion in Christ’s passion culminated in a remarkable mystical experience. In 1224, he received the stigmata —the physical wounds of the crucified Christ —becoming the first recorded individual in Christian history to bear these marks. Yet, ashishealth deteriorated his inner joy never wavered. His name was deliberately chosen by Pope Francis, who has utiliz ed the saint's legacy to guide the global Church toward a renewed focus on marginalised populations and environmental protection, most notably through the encyclical, Laudato si'. Ultimately, the 800th anniversary of Saint Francis of Assisi is not merely a his torical retrospective, but a living invitation. His life demonstrates that true wealth is disc overed in self-emptying love, authentic peace is forged through humble dialogue, and human survival is deeply intertwined with a reverent care for creation. Eight centuries l ater, the gentle friar of Assisi still invites the world to rebuild our lives and move towards God. The year 2026 marks a profound milestone in Christian history and global spirituality: the 800th anniversary of the death of St Francis. On the evening of October 3, 1226, this great saint passed away attheage of forty-four, stripped of his earthly possessions and lying humbly on the bare earth at the Porziuncola chapel. Eight cen turies later, the legacy of the beloved "Little Poor Man" —remains an enduring, powerful beacon of peace, radical love, and environmental stewardship. The global commemoration, anchored by a Vatican proclaimed Franciscan Jubil ee Year, offers a timeless opportunity for modern believers to reflect on a life that continues to challenge us today. Born into luxury asthesonofa wealthy cloth merchant in Assisi. The decisive turning point in his life arrived at the crumbling chapel of San Damiano, where Francis heard a crucifix speak tohim:"Goand repair my house, which is falling int o ruin." Initially taking the command literally, Francis sold his fath er’s cloth to fund repairs, and declared that from that day forward he would recognise only "Our Father who art in heaven." This radical renunciation marked the birth of a spiritual revolution. Francis embraced a life of absolute poverty, choosing to become an iti nerant preacher and beggar. His magnetic joy and uncompromising adherence to the Gospel quic kly attracted companions, which allowed him to start the Order of Friars Minor in 1209. Francis and his brothers lived directly among the public, ministering to outcasts, lepers, and the destitute. Their simple lifestyle, provided a stunning, prophetic alternative to the institutional wealth of the medieval Church at that time. His vision expanded further through the co-founding of the Poor Clare’s with Saint Clare of Assisi, and the establishment of the Secular Franciscan Order, allowing laypeople to live out Franciscan charisms within their secular callings. Fra ncis sought fraternity rather than conquest, exemplifying the peace prayer traditionally attributed to his herit age. Equally revolutionary was his mystical intimacy with creation, beautifully captured in his masterpiece, the "Canticle ofthe Sun." Long before the advent of modern ecology, Francis recognised all living things as interconnected expressions of divine love. Continues on back page/.
