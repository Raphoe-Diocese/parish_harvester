WHAT BELONGS TO CEASAR AND WHAT BELONGS TO GOD If the earth and everything in it belongs to rulership of Gods.24:1), where is the place of the rulers of this earth?

Does believing in God negate us from b eing a believer in the rulership of earth?

This friction is part of what the readings of today seem to reflect upon.

The Readings : First reading, Cyrus, a pagan king, ruler of the earth, conquered Babylon in 539BC without any bloodshed, the prophet Isaiah calls him “Shep herd, anointed, beloved…” etc, titles reserved for kings of Israel, sees him as part of God’s plan prepared by the Lord.

Why? Despite being a pagan king, God can still of this earth to further his kingdom, believer or non believer.

In the Gospel Jesus sa ys: “ Render to Ceasar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God” . Should this suggest a divide between the government of heaven and earth?

I doubt. God has not created us into communities of people and at the same time united us by one faith throu gh baptism, one love and one hope by mistake (like Paul commended the Thessalonians for in the second reading).

Take Home : John Michael Talbolt in his song titled “ St Theresa’s Prayer ” said Christ has no body now but yours, no hands, no feet on earth yours, yours are the eyes through which he looks. yours are the feet with which walks to do good. .” As Christians, we are called to be exemplars of obedience to earthly authorities and a litm us test of our obedience to God.

The first law in heaven is orderliness. Order is w here there is peace, love joy, righteousness and the scripture says where there are all these, there is the kingdom of God (Rom.14:17).

God can use any person anyone to h is glory whether Caesar or Jesus.

In the end what should matter more for us is to render to God what belongs to God which is our hearts through faith, hope and charity . Prayer : For the grace of Obedience to the will of God both in the church and in the society.

Dd WISHING YOU GRACE AND PEACE Paul made several missionary journeys to modern day Greece and Turkey (Asia Minor) establishing new Christian communities.

Although he had to l eave the places after a relatively short time he never forgot the people and was constantly keeping up with their progress through the use of letters which we have today.

He could admonish them if he heard the churches were deviating from what he taught or were getting caught up in petty politics (or worse).

Most of the time it was to encourage them.

He complimented them on how they were progressing and he praised them in their hard work.

Encouragement is so important in lives. We all know what we should be doing but it is easier when people give us uplifting words.

St Teresa of Kolkata said: “Kind words can be short and easy to speak, but their echoes are truly endless. ” It can be easy to find fault in others and sometimes correction is necessary.

But it is far more important to uplift people with words of encouragement!
