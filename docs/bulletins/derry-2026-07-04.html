GRAVEYARD SUNDAYS 2026 Sunday 26 July at 3pm in St Mary’s, Cockhill Sunday 2 August at 3pm in Star of the Sea, Desertegney READINGS FOR 14TH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME First Reading: Zec 9:9 -10. Zechariah addresses the Chosen People, shortly after the catastrophic destruc- tion of Jerusalem c. 586 BC, and their subsequent re- turn from Exile. He envisions the future time of the Messiah when the terror of war will have been replaced by the comfort of salvation. ‘All who are in anxiety, in sorrows, in sin ……. I will place you in all secu- rity.’(Homily of St John Chrysostom). Second Reading: Rm 8:9, 11 -13. ‘In St Paul, ‘to live un-spiritually’ means to confine oneself to human strength and values; this is a way to death. ‘To live spiritually’ is to rely on Christ and belong to him. God, who raised Jesus from the dead, will lead to eternal life folk who produce the fruits of the Spirit.’ (Glenstal Missal) Gospel: Mt 11:25 -30 ‘Yes, my yoke is easy and my burden light.’ Perhaps it has to be experienced to be believed, when - with the help of divine grace - the law of God becomes written in one’s heart, and the burden of keeping it becomes as light as breathing. While the learned and the powerful of the world have difficulty understanding it, ‘the humble welcome the light burden of the law of love.’ (Glenstal Missal). Christ the King, Strathroy: Saturday 6.15pm -6.45pm The Blessings of Hospitality The 13th Sunday in Ordinary Time Year A highlights the importance of welcoming others with kindness. The woman in the first reading recognizes Elisha as a man of God and offers him a place to stay. She does not ask for anything in return, but her generosity brings an unexpected blessing. Elisha promises that she will have a son, something she had not even dared to hope for. This story shows that acts of kindness often lead to blessings. The woman did not give to receive; she gave because she saw a need. In return, God granted her a gift beyond her expectations. This reminds us that when we open our hearts and homes to others, we allow God to work in unexpected ways. Hospitality is more than just sharing food or shelter. It is about welcoming others with love, respect, and an open heart. Jesus also speaks about this in the Gospel when He says that those who welcome His messengers welcome Him. Even small acts of kindness matter in God ’s eyes, encouraging us to think about how we treat others. Are we open to welcoming people into our lives? Do we recognize the presence of God in those we meet? When we show kindness without expecting anything in return, we reflect God ’s love in the world. These words spoken by Jesus in today’s Gospel, spoken with such force, can initially seem enigmatic, paradoxical, even. How can we find ourselves by losing oursel ves? And then it dawns on us. If the life we find ourselves living and cherishing is a life wedded exclusively to the material world, a life which embraces the power -hunger, greed and corrupt practices of that world, then that life, when it ends, is lost . But anyone who lives life with Jesus as its centre, a life which embraces Jesus fully – his teaching, his guidance, the cross he carried out of a love so profound it made the cold world reel in shocked incredulity, that person has found eternal life. To be a true and faithful disciple of Jesus is not easy, and Jesus knows this. He knows it is not easy for us to carry the cross of our suffering with the same courage, fortitude and humility with which he carried his own heavy cross to Calvary. He knows our predilection to attach ourselves to the wrong things, the wrong people, the shiny and superficial as opposed to the deep and worthwhile. He assures us over and over that he will never desert us: he will never abandon us to our fears. He assures us t hat true faith overcomes all fears: if we put his spirit before all else, our reward will be great indeed, in this life as well as in eternal life. We can experience in the here and now the peace and joy which is the reward of casting aside the ego -centre d life and embracing a life which is gloriously Jesus -centred. Jesus, may we never forget that faith can overcome all fear. Jesus, may we always remember to put you before all else if we are to gain a life of peace and joy for all eternity. Whether we are in school, college, working, or juggling family life with all sorts of other commitments, there is sometimes little time to pause and take stock of things. It can be hard to find time to reflect on how our faith is faring. When we do get a minute to assess things, do we find ourselves wanting? Many of us have the idea that we would like to live better lives, to be more caring, to help those in need. We would like to live up to what Jesus asks of us, if only we had time to do more. This desire to do better is an important element of our Christian journey. But often we imagine that this will require some sort of grand gesture or dramatic change of direction. If we are paralysed or overwhelmed by what we can ’t do, Jesus has some good news for us today. A cup of water isn ’t much, and offering hospitality to a stranger might not seem like a big deal, but that is where Jesus starts. Instructing his twelve disciples, he tells them that anyone who welcomes them or offers them a cup of cold water will be rewarded. What ’s more, he adds that anyone who welcomes them in fact welcomes Jesus, the one who sent them. Small acts of kindness and hospitality have significance beyond what we might expect. No matter how busy we are, there are opportunities every day to offer a cup of water here, or a helping hand there. All these small things play their part in building up God ’s kingdom.
