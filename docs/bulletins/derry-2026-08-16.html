Ardmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom we meet. Amen. READINGS FOR 20TH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME First Reading: Is 56:1, 6 -7. This passage from Isaiah serves to introduce today’s much -loved gospel passage. The time of writing was probably after 500 BC. The Lord will welcome those Gentiles who come to ‘observe and who cling to my (Mosaic) Covenant.’ God’s house will become for all people a house of heartfelt prayer. Second Reading: Rom. 11:13 -15, 29 -32. It had been the definitive rejection of Christ by the Jews that had driven the Apostles out of Jerusalem to bring salvation to the Gentiles, i.e., people like the Romans and the Greeks. Yet Paul continues to long that God’s mercy may somehow triumph in achieving the conversion of his countrymen. Their choice by God as the Chosen People was permanent and will never be revoked. Gospel: Mt. 15:21 -28. Leaving the argumentative Pharisees and scribes behind, Jesus had moved to southern Lebanon. There he encounters a pagan woman who addresses him with an ardent plea. His initially harsh attitude towards her alters mid -stream. Jesus first noticed the crowd’s pain before providing for their hunger. In our busy lives, God invites us to see those who are lonely, discouraged, or forgotten. Even small acts of kindness can become someone’s miracle. The disciples focused on what they lacked, yet Jesus asked them to offer what they did have. God rarely asks for what we do not possess. When we offer our time, talents, and resources — however small — He multiplies them in ways we cannot imagine. Before the food was shared, Jesus surrendered everything to the Father. We too carry burdens that feel heavy, but surrender is not giving up; it is trusting God with our limitations. What seems insufficient to us becomes more than enough in His hands. The abundance left over reminds us that God’s blessings are meant to flow through us. When we share generously, encourage others, and forgive freely, His grace continues to multiply. Lord, fill us with compassion, use what little we offer, and multiply it for Your glory. Teach us to trust You and to share Your blessings generously. Amen. Culmore Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear, or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle. The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what they already possessed. God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine. Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough. Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace continues to multiply in countless lives. Prayer Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great things for Your Kingdom. EVEN A CRUMB OF MERC Y IS ENOUGH Matthew 15:21–28 tells of the Canaanite woman who begs Jesus to heal her daughter. At first, Jesus seems silent and distant. Even the disciples want her sent away. Yet she refuses to leave. Her prayer is simple and urgent: “Lord, help me.” When Jesus speaks about the children ’s bread not being thrown to the house - dogs, she answers with remarkable humility and trust: “Even house -dogs can eat the scraps that fall from their master ’s table.” She does not insist that she deserves anything. She simply believes that even the smallest crumb of God ’s mercy is enough to transform her daughter ’s life. Jesus responds: “Woman, you have great faith. Let your wish be granted. ” This Gospel teaches us that God ’s silence is not necessarily God ’s absence. Sometimes prayer appears unanswered, but faith continues to call upon him. The woman moves through silence, apparent rejection and humiliation without surrendering her trust. She also crosses every boundary: she is a woman, a foreigner and an outsider. Yet Jesus reveals that faith —not race, status or belonging —opens the heart to God’s saving mercy. A prayer for the week “Lord, help me. 15 Sunday in Ordinary Time (Year A) 12 July 2026 The Soil of Our Lives This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower. It is one of the most familiar stories in the Gospels. Steelstown Parish Newsletter Eighteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time Sunday 2nd August 2026 Thoughts on the Gospel Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope. In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely, discouraged, forgotten, or struggling.
