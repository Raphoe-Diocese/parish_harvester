Baptism ‘enables all believers to possess an instinct for the truth of the Gospel’ - The views and opinions of many different people andof interest groups are important to the Church in discerning ‘the signs of the times’, sothatitcan determine how best to proclaim the Gospel . For instance, scientists, many of whom are non-Chri stians and perhaps even atheists, are often consult ed on church policy documents when the subject matter relates to areas in which they have expertise. Discerning the sensus fidei , however, it is quite different. It doesn’t involve seeking out experts, at least notifby this wemean people with extensive knowledge, outstanding qualifications, or exception al experience. Rather, it involves seeking out those who, in their daily lives, try as faithfully as they can to live as disciples of Jesus within the community of the Church, nourished in this by the Word of God, perso nal prayer, regular celebration of the sacraments, and selfless acts of service. It makes sense to understand the sensus fidei as residing with such people because it is the fait hful living of one’s faith within the church community and keeps alive the gift of being able to appreciate the Church’s faith given at baptism. A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 21:33-43 In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells the parable of the vineyard, reminding us that God lovingly entrusts gifts, responsibilities and opportunities to his people. The vineyard is fruitful, carefully prepared and full of promise, yet the tenants forget that it does not truly belong to them. They begin to act as though they are the owners rather than those who have been entrusted with its care. Wetoocanbe tempted to hold tightly to what God has placed in our hands - our time, talents, possessions, abilities and even our relationships. We can easily forget that these are gifts to be received with gratitude and used for the good of others. The Gospel invites us instead to become faithful stewards, recognising that everything we have is ultimately a gift from God and that we are called to help it flourish. At the heart of the parable is God’s patient and enduring love. Even when his messengers are rejected and his people turn away, God does not abandon them. He continues to call them back to himself, offering opportunities to change, to return and to begin again.
