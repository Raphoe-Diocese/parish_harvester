THIRTY SECOND SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME YEAR A 8TH November, 2026 Rituals and how they call us to attention.

Context and reference point Jesus comparing events at a wedding celebration to the “Kingdom of Heaven” is far from rare and yet here this parable has it’sown reference point to a particular moment of traditional ritual of the day …. Here Jesus introduces us to one of the roles the bridesmaids perform by way of the welcoming procession of the couple from the brides home to her new home with her husbands family, as they would draw near the ten bridesmaids represent the ladies who would be there to welcome the couple on their final steps with lighted torches …. Here Jesus draws the picture for us … for reasons not shared there had been a delay … the five and prepared bridesmaids had planned for this eventuality while the other five seem to have been somewhat complacent and got caught off guard.

Having been sent away to get some extra oil the couples arrival sees celebrations get underway and their return finds them left outside not recognised by the groom … a little harsh it would appear … here there is room for allegory … the Bridegroom represe nts Jesus and the procession, our waiting for HIS RETURN … the bridesmaids surely relayed to US as we wait for Jesus’ return at a time we do not know.

The message is relatively simple but carries in it’s simplicity the reality of living in a world that for us is our lifetime and how we as Christians chose to live it … wise and prepared or foolish and complacent.

The time referred to is that time in theological terms between Resurrection and the return of the Son of Man which is not meant to be an “empty time” but a time to remain alert with “our lamps lit” SO . Sr. Maureen Haugh, St. Xavier’s Convent of Mercy Born 28th August, 1933 Died 27th October, 2026 Maureen was born and grew up in Shragh, Doonbeg attending the local national school and St. Xavier’s Secondary School, Kilrush.

She entered the Sisters of Mercy in Nenagh on 26th April, 1954 and made her final profession on 29th October, 1959. She qualified as a Secondary School Teacher with qualifications from both UCD and UCC. Her teaching life began in Birr and Borrisokane before her appointment as Principal of St. Joseph’s Secondary School, Tulla in 1980. Six years later, she transferred to Shannon where she taught in St. Caimin’s Community School.

In her retirement from teaching Maureen was appointed by the late Bishop Willie Walsh as our Diocesan Delegate for the Care of Asylum Seekers and Refugees following the example of her foundress, the Venerable Catherine McAuley.

She welcomed the stranger. Shesaw the face of Jesus in the migrant, the refugee, the displaced, those who arrived with noth- ing and needed help and support.

Maureen put mercy into action. She opened doors, made room, helped fill out forms, helped navigate bureaucracy.

She offered a listening ear and a place to belong.

The final years of her life were spent between St. Xavier’s Convent in Ennis and Catherine McAuley House in Limerick where she died peacefully on

Tuesday 27th October last. May Maureen now be welcomed to the home of the Father and hear those words: “I was a stranger and you made me welcome.
