A Heartfelt Thank You Dear friends, As I prepare to take up my new appointment as Parish Priest of Drumragh (Omagh) on 30th October, I wish to record my deep gratitude to you all for your kindness, support and genero sity, both in the life of the parishes and to me personally, for the last seven years.

Throughout my seven years of ministry in Aghyaran and Ardstraw West & Castlederg, and three years in Drumquin, I have been supported by wond erful teams of people who have walked with me and, on so many occasions, somehow made the impos sible possible!

I feel very blessed to have been part of the faith story of the people of the three parishes and, i n thanking you for the very significant role you have played in inspiring an encouraging me in my own faith journey, I very much hope that, in some small way, my ministry among you has helped and encouraged you on your own journey of faith.

I could never have managed without the help I have had in the Parochial House, the Office, our churches, our halls, graveyards and our schools.

To our parish secretary, newsletter editor, sacristans, caretakers, school staff and volunteers: thank you.

You kept everything going, often with little recogn ition or outward expressions of gratitude.

And behind all of our liturgies there lies an ‘army’ of people – readers, eucharistic ministers, altar servers, musicians/choirs, collectors, counters, cleaners, groundsmen, caretakers – people who just show up faithfully and willingly, week in and week out.

Special thanks too to those who have given of their gifts, knowledge and time on parish committees including the various fundraising groups, parish tea group, children’s liturgy and, more recently, on our Leadership Teams. I am indebted also to the staff and children of our schools for their warm welcome at all times and their co-operation and willingness to share in the life of our churches and parishes.

I have been privileged to accompany many families and individuals on milestone occasions in their lives over the last seven years.

It is always wonderful to share with families in the happy, joyful occasions – the baptisms, weddings, Golden Weddings, First Communions, Confirmations and other a chievements.

Part of priestly ministry of course is journeying with families in their darkest hours – families facing illness, death and the loss of loved ones, sometimes in the most heartbreaking circumstances.

It has been a privilege for me to have walked with many families in sad, difficulty and challenging times in their lives.

Again, I have found the faith and courage shown by so many families at such times to be deeply humbling, and a s ource of strength and reassurance to me in my own faith.

An important part of any priest's ministry is visiting and bringing the Eucharist to those who are housebound or unable to come to church.

Visitation of the housebound in our parishes has been a very privileged part of my ministry; so too has visitation of our Nursing Homes.

We came through Covid together, and we've shared seven Christmases and Easters.

I won't forge t any of this! In recent years it has also been a joy to have Fr Thomas minister alongside me.

His generosity and helpfulness have been a great blessing.

Thank you, Fr Thomas, for all your support. thanks too to Fr Gilmore for his wise counsel, and to Fr Peter McCawille for the generous help he continues to give to us all.

I leave with a heavy heart, but also with memories of friendship and neighbourliness that, for me, make the Gospel come alive.

Last but not least, I want to say a word of welcome to my successo r, Fr Chris.

There are a good many projects underway or just about to begin, but Fr Chris is talented, capable and hardwor king, and I have no worries on that score.

With your help and encouragement, and with his leadership, I know the p eople of these three parishes will grow stronger in faith and keep the light of the Gospel burning brightly, a beacon of hope in a fragile world.

I hope Fr Chris will be as happy here as I have been, and I ask God's blessing on all that you do together.

Please keep me in your thoughts and prayers.

I'll always keep the people of this pastoral area in mine, asking that God will reward you for all your goodness and kindness.

With love and gratitude, Fr Paul

Ardmore Parish Newsletter Twenty-Ninth Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A Sunday, 18th October 2026 Gospel Reflection (Matthew 22:15–21) In today’s gospel, religious leaders try to trap Jesus with a question about paying taxes to Caesar.

Pointing to the coin’s image and inscription, He teaches us to fulfil our earthly responsibilities while giving our deepest allegiance to God.

Jesus reminds us that not every question deserves a fight.

What matters is whether our choices reflect goodness, truth, and love, drawing us closer to God, strengthening our character, and blessing others.

A life well lived is measured not by arguments won, but by faithfulness to God’s will.

The coin carried Caesar’s image, reminding us to respect legitimate laws, perform our duties honestly, support our families, and contribute positively to society.

Faith does not excuse us from responsible citizenship; it inspires us to fulfil our duties with sincerity and fairness.

The coin bore Caesar’s image, but our lives are called to reflect God’s goodness.

Though we cannot do so perfectly every day, we can choose to grow: let our words encourage, our hands help, and our hearts forgive.

We may give money to the world, time to our responsibilities, and attention to our ambitions, but we must give God what belongs to Him: our love, worship, gratitude, trust, and obedience.

Our relationship with God remains the foundation of a meaningful life.

We may carry coins in our pockets, but we carry God’s calling in our hearts.

Give the world your honest service, others your genuine love, and God your whole heart.

That is how an ordinary life becomes a beautiful offering to Him.

BISHOP MICHAEL ROUTER AT FATHER HEGARTY’S ROCK Only three weeks after his installation as Bishop of Derry, The Most Reverend Michael Router paid a pas- toral visit last Sunday to the parishes of Burt, Inch & Fahan and Desertegney & Lower Fahan (Buncrana).

We were delighted to have him amongst us on what was an unseasonably warm and sunny day, for what was a spectacularly wonderful occasion.

He walked with us on a Synodal walk from the Stone Jug and cele- brated Mass on Father Hegarty’s Rock , before returning then to Cockhill Parish Hall for tea and refreshments.

The turnout was splendid, the welcome warm, the sup- port immense and the faith tangible.

In the midst of a sometimes stiff Atlantic breeze he joked about not be-ing blown into the water, seemingly perched as he was on the edge of the Lough.

But from there he com- manded a great view of the gathering and the Statue of Friar Seamus Hegarty OP – and he offered a most memorable Mass recalling the courage and hope which Father Hegarty had in abundance, and asking God to grant us the same gifts for the challenges in our own time.

The music and singing were outstanding and the joy and contentment of those gathered there, of all ages, was clear to be seen.

After visiting Father Walsh in his home, the bishop returned to Derry with a spring in his step and firm in the knowledge that he will always be welcome in these parts.

It was a truly wonderful occasion made possible only by the devotion and help of huge teams of people: our stewards and volunteers, choirs and musicians, farmers and bakers, schools and Parents’ Associations (especially at St Oran’s NS ), and to all who came to celebrate.

To them all and to God himself, we must always be thankful.

Jesus reminds us that not every question deserves a fight.

What matters is whether our choices reflect goodness, truth, and love, drawing us closer to God, strengthening our character, and blessing others.

A life well lived is measured not by arguments won, but by faithfulness to God’s will.

The coin carried Caesar’s image, reminding us to respect legitimate laws, perform our duties honestly, support our families, and contribute positively to society.

Faith does not excuse us from responsible citizenship; it inspires us to fulfil our duties with sincerity and fairness.

The coin bore Caesar’s image, but our lives are called to reflect God’s goodness.

Though we cannot do so perfectly every day, we can choose to grow: let our words encourage, our hands help, and our hearts forgive.

We may give money to the world, time to our responsibilities, and attention to our ambitions, but we must give God what belongs to Him: our love, worship, gratitude, trust, and obedience.

Our relationship with God remains the foundation of a meaningful life.

We may carry coins in our pockets, but we carry God’s calling in our hearts.

Give the world your honest service, others your genuine love, and God your whole heart.

That is how an ordinary life becomes a beautiful offering to Him.

I feel very blessed to have been part of the faith story of the people of the three parishes and, i n thanking you for the very significant role you have played in inspiring an encouraging me in my own faith journey, I very much hope that, in some small way, my ministry among you has helped and encouraged you on your own journey of faith.

I could never have managed without the help I have had in the Parochial House, the Office, our churches, our halls, graveyards and our schools.

To our parish secretary, newsletter editor, sacristans, caretakers, school staff and volunteers: thank you.

Bishop Michael Router at Father Hegarty’s Rock Only three weeks after his installation as Bishop of Derry, The Most Rev erend Michael Router paid a pastoral visit last Sunday to the parishes of Burt, Inch & Fahan and Desertegney & Lower Fahan (Buncrana).

We were delighted to have him amongst us on what was an unse asonably warm and sunny day, for what was a spectacularly wonderful occasion.

He walked with us on a Synodal walk from the Stone Jug and celebrated Mass on Father Hegarty’s Rock, before returning then to Cockhill Parish Hall for tea and refreshments.

The turnout was splendid, the welcome warm, the support immense and faith tangible.

In the midst of a sometimes stiff Atlantic breeze he joked about not being blown into the water, seemingly perched as he was on the edge of the Lough.

But from there he commanded a great view of the gathering and the Statue of Friar Seamus Hegarty OP – and he offered a most memorable Mass recalling the courage and hope which Father Hegarty had in abundance, and asking God to grant us the same gifts for the challenges in our own time.

The music and si nging were outstanding and the joy and contentment of those gathered there, of all ages, was clear to be seen.

After visiting Father Walsh in his home, the bishop returned to Derry with a s pring in his step and firm in the knowledge that he will always be welcome in these parts.

It was a truly wonderful occasion made possible only by the devotion and help of huge teams of people: o ur stewards and volunteers, choirs and musicians, farmers and bakers, schools and Parents’ Associations (especially at St Oran’s NS), and to all who came to celebrate.

To them all and to God himself, we mus t always be thankful.

GIVING TO GOD OR GIVING TO CAESAR They must really have thought they had their victim sewn up!

If Jesus said he paid the Roman taxes, he recognised the Emperor, not God, as his Lord.

If he said he didn’t pay, he was a traitor to Rome.

Jesus turns the que stion back on them. First he makes them admit that they themselves rec ognise Rome as overlord by carrying a Roman coin, for the coin would carry the Emperor’s head.

Next he puts them a question: what do they consider is due to Caesar?

Finally he goes beyond their question, to interrogate their ultimate loyalty: in the last analysis, just what is due to God?

At a superficial l evel this seems a little verbal tussle, in which Jesus outwits his opponents.

But the story was remembered and passed on in the Christian community not because of Jesus’ cleverness, but because at a deeper level it is a question which Jesus puts to each of us: just where do our loyalties and priorities lie?

Money? Respect? Fame? A good holiday? Comfort?

Power? Jesus is not a dictator who imposes his will.

He just asks the que stion and leave us to give our own answer.

To those who question him he gives no easy answer, but always replies with another question.

Jesus reminds us that not every question deserves a fight.

What matters is whether our choices reflect goodness, truth, and love, drawing us closer to God, strengthening our character, and blessing others.

A life well lived is measured not by arguments won, but by faithfulness to God’s will.

The coin carried Caesar’s image, reminding us to respect legitimate laws, perform our duties honestly, support our families, and contribute positively to society.

Faith does not excuse us from responsible citizenship; it inspires us to fulfil our duties with sincerity and fairness.

The coin bore Caesar’s image, but our lives are called to reflect God’s goodness.

Though we cannot do so perfectly every day, we can choose to grow: let our words encourage, our hands help, and our hearts forgive.

We may give money to the world, time to our responsibilities, and attention to our ambitions, but we must give God what belongs to Him: our love, worship, gratitude, trust, and obedience.

Our relationship with God remains the foundation of a meaningful life.

We may carry coins in our pockets, but we carry God’s calling in our hearts.

Give the world your honest service, others your genuine love, and God your whole heart.

That is how an ordinary life becomes a beautiful offering to Him.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 21:15-21 In today’s Gospel Jesus tells us to “repay to Caesar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God.” His words invite us to reflect on our priorities, our responsibilities and the place God holds in our lives.

While we have duties to our families, communities and society, our relationship with God must always remain at the centre.

Giving to God what belongs to God means more than attending Mass or saying our prayers.

It means living our faith through love, kindness, forgiveness and compassion.

When we care for the vulnerable, support those in need and reach out to the lonely, we make God's love visible through our actions.

In the busyness of everyday life, we can sometimes forget to make time for God.

Today's Gospel reminds us that our possessions and achievements should never take his place in our hearts.

What matters most is how we use our gifts and blessings to serve God and others.

As we reflect on today's Gospel, let us ask ourselves: Am I putting God first?

Am I using my gifts for the good of others?

Do my words and actions reflect the love of Christ?

May we have the courage to put God first, the generosity to serve others and the humility to recognise his presence in everyone we meet.

Lord, help us to give you what truly belongs to you: our hearts, our lives and our love.

Guide us to serve others with kindness and generosity, and may everything we say and do bring glory to your holy name.

Amen. World Mission Sunday is the day set aside by the Catholic Church throughout the world to publicly renew its commitment to its universal mission, it’s calling to bring the Good News of Christ to the ends of the earth.

It is through the witness of a reconciled, fraternal and united community that the proclamation of the Gospel acquires its full communicative power.

As Christ’s disciples, we are all called and sent to be instruments of his compassion in the context of our broken, confused and anxiety riven world.

While all baptised members of the Church are called to bear witness to Christ and the Gospel of love, wherever they are, there is also, within the Church, a special calling to mission outside one’s own cultural or national setting – mission ad gentes.

Down through the centuries the Church’s mission to the nations was carried out by the members of missionary congregations and societies, ready and willing to embark upon a courageous outreach to peoples and cultures outside their homelands.

Irish missionaries and their supporters have made, and continue to make, an outstanding contribution to this missionary outreach.

Without the inspiration and leadership given by these women and men, the missionary impulse of the Church would have gradually diminished and died out, and the Church would have never realised its essential vocation to bring the Good News to the ends of the earth.
