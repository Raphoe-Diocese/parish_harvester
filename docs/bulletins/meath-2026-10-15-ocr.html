Do you keep saying “later” to God, while the busy things take up every hour? The wedding banquet used in todays Gospel points to the joy at the end of all things, where God and His people are one and grief is over. It is the best news there has ever been. Andnow watch what the invited guests do with it. They will not come. The king sends servants to call them, and they make ligh tofit. One goes off to his farm, another to his bu siness. They are busy with things to do, and the feast can wait, and so they dec line the one invitation that outlasts the grave. Th is the quiet tragedy, and it is ours more than we like to admit. God is rarely r efused by dramatic rejection. He is refused by dist raction. By good and ordinary things, the work, the errands, the screen, the thousand tasks that are not sins but that silently fill every hour the feast was meant to have. We do not say no to God. We say later. We say when things settle. So the king does something astonishing. He says, the feast is ready, but those invited were not worthy. Goouttothe roads, to the edges of town, and invite everyone you find, both bad and good, until the hall is full. The ones toobusy miss it. The ones with no claim at all, the poor , the overlooked, the sinners from the street corners, are carried in to the wedding. This is the mercy underneath the whole parable. When the comfortable decline, God does not cancel the feast. He widens th e door. The people who know they are hungry come ru nning, while the people who thought they had better things to do stay home with their better things. The feast is free, but it is not cheap. We are all invited exactly as we are, off the street, unwort hy, carrying our sins. However, we cannot come to the wedding of the Lamb and insist on staying exactly as we walked in. To a ccept the invitation is to accept being changed by it. The deepest hunger in us, the one no farm or business or busy hour can touch, is the hunger for this feast, for God Himself. Sothe invitation is in your hands today, as real as it was on Isaiah’s mountain. The feast that swallows up death is prepared. The only question is whether we will come, or send our regrets and go back to being busy. The table is set. The hall is filling. And still He sends His servants out to the roads to find us… Collections 3rd/ 4th October – Development Fund: €1,310 ; Plate: €905 ; October Offerings: €1,450 . Thank You Very Much! ST CARLO ACUTIS: On 12 October, we honour St Carlo Acutis, a young saint whose deep love for the Eucharist and commitment to prayer continue to inspire people worldwide. SAINT IGNATIUS OF ANTIOCH: On 17th October, the Church commemorates Saint Ignatius of Antioch, bishop, martyr, and faithful disciple of Christ. On his journey to martyrdom, he encouraged Christians to remain united in faith and love. His courageous witness inspires us to trust God, stand firm in truth, and follow Christ with conviction. SAINT LUKE THE EVANGELIST: On 18th October, the Church celebrates Saint Luke the Evangelist, physician, companion of Saint Paul, and author of the Gospel of Luke and the Acts of the Apostles.
