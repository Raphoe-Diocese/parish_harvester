All welcome. The Parish Of Ballyhale – Knocktopher In 1936 was the foundation of the Carmelite Convent of Kocktopher by James, Earl of Ormond and grandson of Edmund le Botiller.

The town of Knocktopher, which according to Lewis, “ appears to have enjoyed the privileges of a free borough by a kind of prescriptive right”, granted a weekly market and several fairs in 1365 by the King, at the request of the same Earl.

The Earl of Ormoond died in his castle of Knocktopher in 1382 and was buried in St. Canice’s Cathedr al, Kilkenny.

In 1487, Sir James Butler, father of Pierce Ruadh, Earl of Ormond, also died i n the castle of Knocktopher.

He is buried in the old Augustinian Abbey, Callan.

Pardons were gr anted to the provost and commonalty of Knocktopher for a fine of £4 on January 17th, 1571/72. Mass of the Sick and Anointing of the Sick The Sacrament of Anointing of the Sick brings spiritual grace, peace, stre ngth, comfort and healing.

It can restore spiritual, emotional and physical health, if it is God’s will.

Any Catholic or Christian in serious illness, advancing age or preparing for surgery can avail of it.

It can be repeated monthly or if an illness worsens.

The priest lays his hands on the person and anoints their forehead and the palms of their hands with blessed oil offering a special prayer of healing and grace.

In relation to this important Sacrament, starting in November, the last Thursday of the month in Ferrybank at 10.00am and the last Friday of the month at 10.00am in Slieverue, a special Mass of the Sick including anointing will be celebrated.

Amen. November The Month Of Holy Souls “It is a holy and wholesome thought to pray for the dead that they may be loosed form their sins.” (2 Maccabees 12:46).

Each November, the Church engages in a great crusade of prayer for our dead.

We pray for our dead because we believe there is a communion - an unbreakable link - between the living and the dead.

That link ensures us that we can help them and they can help us.

One saint said that the Holy Souls may win astounding favours for us and deliver us from evils, sickness and dangers of every kind.

Our prayer, especially the holy sacrifice of the Mass, is the greatest expression of our gratitude and love for those who have died.

In fact, the November Novena of Masses is the most powerful way to honour with great respect the memory of our dead.

Our prayers will enrich their eternal happiness and their concern for us will enhance our pilgrim lives.

You are encouraged to attend Mass, as often as you can, during the month of November for the Holy Souls.

November Reflection - Fr. Willie. As the days shorten and the earth rests, November calls us into a quiet space of memory and hope.

The crisp air and lengthening shadows invite us to pause from the restless pace of daily life and enter into the gentle of reflection.

Nature itself seems to bow its head in surrender, teaching us the quiet beauty of letting go and trusting in the promise of renewal.

In the stillness of these November days, we carry those who have gone before us not as distant figures of the past, but as living presences held forever in the tender mercy of God.

In that sacred hush, the veil between heaven and earth feels thin.

We remember their smiles, their voices, their quiet sacrifices, and the love they so generously poured into our lives.

Death has not severed our bond; rather, it has transformed it, anchoring our grief in the steadfast hope of eternal life.

As we look back with gratitude, we also look forward with faith, knowing that the God who holds our loved ones in His eternal embrace holds us in our present pilgrimage.

In this season of remembrance, may our hearts find comfort in the quiet assurance that love never ends, and that we walk toward a shared light that no darkness can ever extinguish.

Shed oes not hide from the earnest heart.

But she must be sought — and that seeking requires a soul that is awake.

St. Paul, in the second reading, speaks directly to every family that has lost someone they love: “to make sure that you do not grieve about them, like the other people who have no hope.” Grief is not forbidden - it is human and holy.

But Christian grief is never the final word.

Because Jesus rose from the dead, those who have “fallen asleep” in Him have not vanished.

They have gone ahead. The Gospel brings the same truth into sharp focus.

In the Parable of the Ten Bridesmaids, five keep their lamps burning and enter the wedding feast. Five do not.

The difference is not talent, wealth, or status - it is preparation.

The oil in those lamps, the Fathers of Church tell us, is the interior life of grace: charity, prayer, the sacraments, fidelity livedd ay after day.

It cannot be borrowed at the last moment.

It must be cultivated now. November reminds us, through the memory of those we have lost, that the Bridegroom is coming.

The question put before every heart today is simply this: Is still burning?

Blessed Fulton Sheen - An Irish Connection The Church has recently beatified a bishop with strong links to Ireland.

Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen (1895-1979), the celebrated American preacher, writer and pioneer of Catholic radio and television, was beatified in St Louis, Missouri, last September.

For Irish Catholics there is a special connection.

Fulton Sheen was the grandson of John and Mary Fulton from Croghan , Co. Roscommon, who emigrated to Illinois.

His Irish heritage remained important to him throughout his life.

Archbishop Sheen became one of the best-known Catholic voices of the twentieth century.

Through radio and his enormously popular television programme “Life Is Worth Living”, he brought the Christian message into millions of homes.

His preaching combined a deep love of Christ with devotion to the Eucharist and Our Lady.

His beatification is an invitation to rediscover his simple conviction that holiness is possible in ordinary life and that modern means of communication can be used to proclaim the Gospel.

For us in Ireland Blessed Fulton Sheen's Roscommon roots give us another reason to celebrate and to ask his prayers: Blessed Fulton Sheen, pray for us!
