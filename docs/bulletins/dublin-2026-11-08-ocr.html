Some are well-known saints whom the Church honours publicly; many others are ordinary people whose holiness was lived quietly in daily acts of faith, hope, and love.

The saints remind us that holiness is not reserved for a select few.

Through baptism, each of us is called to become a saint and to reflect God's love in our families, workplaces, schools, and communitics.

Their lives encourage us to persevere in prayer, trust in God's mercy, and remain faithful in times of joy and difficulty.

As we celebrate this feast, let us thank God for the saints who inspire us and remember with gratitude those holy people who have influenced our lives and helped us grow in faith.

May their example guide us on our own journey towards the fullness of life with God.

Reflection on the Readings this Sunday ‘Beloved: See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God …’ Today’s feast is an occasion for great thanksgiving, honouring all the saints, who offer us memories and models of holiness.

We, too, are called to holiness; to be saints.

We can try to do this by following the message and example of Jesus, as shown in the Gospel.

Jesus teaches the disciples God’s law in a new way, summed up in the Beatitudes, are a map with of eight different paths.

By following them, we align our lives with the heart of God and ‘holiness’. The First Reading gives us a vision of heaven.

Those who belong to the Lord are marked with a seal and kept safe, ‘an innumerable number from every nation, from all tribes and peoples.

In the Second Reading, John addresses us as ‘beloved’, reminding us that we are children of God.

Although today’s world may misunderstand God’s Way, we are promised eternal life when we will see God as he is.

The Psalmist sings of the created world and all dwelling in it.

Those who seek the Lord’s face with clean hands and a pure heart will receive his blessing.

On All Saints’ Day, the Church commemorates the communion of saints in a special way.

The commemoration of All Saints’ Day stems from a belief that there is a powerful spiritual bond between those in heaven, and the living.

All Saints’ Day celebrated the saints of the Church, both those that are well-known and those unknown.

The day has been celebrated in some capacity since at least the 4th century.

On the Commemoration of All the

Faithful Departed

or All Souls’ Day, we pray for all those who have died.

MONDAY 2ND NOVEMBER Our annual Day of Prayer for the souls of all the faithful departed.

Indulgences for the Faithful Departed: on All Souls’ Day, we can help our beloved dead by our prayers.

A plenary indulgence, applicable to the Holy Souls, is granted to those who visit any parish church or public oratory on 2nd November and there recite the Our Father and the Creed once.

Every day from 1st to 8th November inclusive, a plenary indulgence, again applicable to the Holy Souls, is granted to those who visit a cemetery and pray for the departed.

It would be lovely if even you go there and simply pray: “Eternal rest grant unto them O Lord and let perpetual light shine upon them and may they rest in peace.

May their souls and the souls of all the faithful departed, through the mercy of God rest in peace.

Monday 2nd November Mass at 10am in St Monica’s Church . Pastors Desk All Sain ts Today, November 1st, the feast of All Saints is a distinctive day in our Catholic list of feast days.

The Church has a very precise process, called canonisation through which it feels confident to say that somebody has lived a life in tune with the will of God and has now reached the kingdom of heaven.

However, the Church knows too that we can’t limit the number of people who have reached eternal happiness in continuation of their holy Christian lives.

They are people known to us – family, friends, colleagues and people like ourselves who have helped the thirsty, the hungry, those without clothes and shelter.

The love of neighbour that, after our death leads to God’s right hand.

These were the gestures that carried weight with Jesus.

The feast of All Saints is a day of great hope and inspiration for us.

As the Epistle to the Hebrews states “since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us rid ourselves of every burden and sin that clings to us and persevere in running the race that lies before us.”(Heb. 12:1) Mass attendance A holy elderly priest was once asked, “how many people attended the early morning Mass today”? He responded, “There were three old ladies, the janitor, several thousand archangels, a large number of seraphim, and several million of the triumphant saints of God”. But we always have room for one more!

We should always keep in mind the great cloud of witnesses that surround us at every Mass.

So, on this feast we remember the many good people gone before us whom we’ve met in life and without embarrassment pray to them to help us on our journey . May we join them one day in the communion of saints.
