Season of Creation 2026: 1 September to 4 October With the theme of ‘ Living Water ’ for this year’s Season of Creation, we pray: Holy God, whose Spirit hovered over the waters at the beginning, we gather as part of your community of all creation, thirsty for your grace. We give thanks for the gift of water, the veins of our common home, that sustains the cedar and the whale, the pasture and the city. We are grateful for the local waters that nourish our homelands and our fellow creatures. We give thanks that in your well-watered garden, every drop is a sacred testament to your l ove. Yet, we confess that we have treated this gift as a mere commodity. We have choked the seas with our waste and watched the warming tides rise in judgment. Melt our frozen hearts, OGod, andletthe waters of repentance flow. Lord of all Life, you promised that wherever the river flows, everything shall live. Stir inusa calling to care for the waters and to honour the dignity of the thirsty. May your Word be a spring within us, gushing for the healing of the nations. May Christ our Lord who is Living Water refresh usforthe work of restoration. And may the Holy Spirit carry us on currents of grace until justice rolls down like wate rs and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream. Amen.

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 18:21-35 In today’s Gospel, Peter asks Jesus how often he should forgive someone who sins against him. Peter suggests seven times, but Jesus goes beyond counting: forgiveness is not something we measure; itisawayof life. Jesus tells the parable of the unforgiving servant. A man owes his master an enormous debt, far beyond anything he could repay. Yet when he asks for mercy, the master forgives the whole debt. This reminds usofthe generosity of God’s mercy towards us. Butthe servant then refuses to forgive someone who owes him a much smaller amount. Having received mercy, he fails to show mercy himself. The message is challenging. We can receive God’s forgiveness while still holding on to anger, resentment or hurt. Jesus reminds us that the mercy we receive is meant to be shared. Forgiveness does not mean ignoring injustice or pretending that hurt does not matter. Serious wounds may take time, prayer, wise counsel and appropriate boundaries. But we are invited to place our hurts in God’s hands and not allow bitterness to have the f inal word. At every Mass, we encounter God’s mercy. Christ gives himself forusand calls us to extend that same love to others. This week, perhaps we can bring one difficult relationship before the Lord andaskforthe grace to take one step towards forgiveness. Forgiveness is not simply something God asks of us - it is something God first gives to us. Lord Jesus, you have forgiven us more than we can ever repay. Teach us to receive your mercy with grateful hearts and to share it with those who have hurt us. Give us the grace to forgive, the wisdom to seek justice where needed, and the humility to place every wound into your loving hands. Amen.
