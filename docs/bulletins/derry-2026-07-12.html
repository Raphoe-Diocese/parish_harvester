Parables speak to the heart and command an alertness to all that is said and all that is unsaid. We need to pay attention to detail, but must also be careful not to miss the hidden meaning in the parable experience. The parable does not end once we are finished hearing or reading it. It stays with us; disturbs us; forces us to ponder. Parables should awaken our senses. Today’s parable highlights the various ways that we do not allow God’s word to penetrate deep into our hearts and minds. Jesus tells the disciples; ‘The reason I speak to them in parable is that ‘seeing they do not perceive, and hearing they do not listen’. Jesus emphasises the enormous harvest that is waiting for those who really ‘open their hearts’. St Eugene's Cathedral (Templon) A Short Reflection on today's Gospel Matthew 13:1-23 In today’s Gospel, Jesus paints a familiar image of a farmer scattering seed with remarkable generosity. The seed falls on every kind of ground, reminding us that God offers His grace and His word to everyone without exception. The difference lies not in the quality of the seed, but in the condition of the soil that receives it. Jesus invites us to reflect honestly on our own hearts. At different times we may recognise ourselves in each type of soil. We can become hardened by disappointment, shallow in our commitment, or distracted by the pressures and attractions of daily life. Yet the Lord never stops sowing. He continues to speak to us through the Scriptures, the Sacraments, the Church, and the quiet moments of prayer. The good soil is the heart that remains open, patient, and willing to be transformed by God’s grace. Such a heart does not simply hear the word but lives it. The fruit it bears is seen in acts of kindness, forgiveness, generosity, and faithful discipleship. As we listen to God's word this week, may we ask the Holy Spirit to break up whatever has become hard within us, remove the thorns that hinder our growth, and deepen our roots in Christ. Then the seed of the Gospel will flourish, bearing an abundant harvest for the glory of God and the service of our neighbour.
