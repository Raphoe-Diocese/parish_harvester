November: Month of Remembrance Each year, our church in its wisdom, sets aside the month of November as an opportune time when we are asked to remember and pray for our departed loved ones.

It begins with our celebration of the Feast of All Saints on Sunday November 1st. This great feast is followed by All Souls Day on

Monday 2nd. Of all the feast days we celebrate over the course of a year, these are the ones that resonate with each one of us, when we remember our own departed loved ones, whom we pray are now enjoying eternal rest. When we remember and pray for our loved ones, particularly in November, their personalities and stories live on.

We all have our own individual ways of keeping their memories alive.

For some it is visiting a family graveside to say a prayer or place fresh flowers.

For others it will involve writing the names on a list of the dead and placing them in a box near the altar in each of our 4 churches.

They are then prayed for at each mass that is celebrated.

For others it may be lighting a candle placed in front of a photograph of their loved ones as they offer a silent prayer of thanksgiving.

In our parish church of St. Mary’s, we have placed our Remembrance Tree in the sanctuary.

It contains the names of the 47 people who have died in our parish over the past year.

The highlight of our remembrance for them will culminate with the celebration of our special mass of remembrance on

Friday 20th November at 8pm, in St. Mary’s church, when we invite their families to come to honour and pray for them.

REFLECTION FOR THE SOLEMNITY OF ALL SAINTS Today, the Church celebrates the SOLEMNITY OF ALL SAINTS - not only the famous saints we know by name, but also the countless men and women from every nation, language, and race who lived quiet, faithful lives and now enjoy the presence of God.

Think of our great-grandparents who may have lived very good lives but were not canonised as Saints by the Church.

The vision in the Book of Revelation in our first reading (Apocalypse 7: 2-4,9-14) reminds us that heaven is not empty; it is filled with a great multitude who have washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb.

In other words, holiness is not a dream reserved for a few special people.

It is the calling of every Christian. The second reading from (1 John 3:1-3) tells us something even more beautiful: “ We are already children of God .” This means holiness begins now.

It is not something we wait for until after death.

It starts in our daily choices, in the way we speak, forgive, work, pray, and relate with others.

To be a saint is simply to belong to God and to allow His grace to shape our lives.

Do you want to be a Saint? Don't wait till tomorrow, begin today to do good and strive towards holiness of life.

The Gospel (Matthew 5:1-12) on the Beatitudes gives us the path to attain this holiness.

Jesus says that the poor in spirit, the gentle, the merciful, the pure in heart, the peacemakers, and those who hunger for justice are blessed.

These are not just beautiful words; they are practical lessons for daily life.

Holiness i s lived when we choose honesty over lies, humility over pride, forgiveness over revenge, prayer over despair, and peace over conflict.

The saints teach us that holiness is possible in ordinary life.

It is possible in the home, in the workplace, in the classroom, in illness, in old age, and even in suffering.

The saints were not perfect people; they were people who kept returning to God.

They failed, repented, trusted, and persevered.

Today, let us ask for the grace to live like the saints: to be faithful in sm all things, generous in love, patient in trials, and hopeful in God’s promises.

May this feast renew our desire for heaven and remind us that the road to glory is walked one day, one decision, and one act of love at a time.

Thanks for your support. Reasons why we honour the saints: 1- The saints lived heroic lives of Faith.

St. Paul asks us to serve and honour such noble souls.

He advises Christians to welcome, serve, and honour those who have put their trust in Jesus.

The saints enjoy Heavenly bliss as a reward for their Faith in Jesus.

Hence, they deserve our veneration. 2- The saints are our role models.

They teach us by their lives that Christ's holy life of love, mercy and forgiveness can, with the grace of God, be lived by ordinary people from all walks of life and at all times. 3- The saints are our Heavenly mediators who intercede for us before Jesus, the only mediator between God and us. 4- The saints are the instruments that God uses to work miracles in the present.

On the feast of All Saints, the Church invites and challenges us to walk the walk of the Saints, not just talk the talk: "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the Kingdom of Heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in Heaven" (Mt 7:21). 2) The feast gives us an occasion to thank God for having invited so many of our ancestors to join the company of the Saints.

May our reflection on the heroic lives of the Saints, with our imitation of their lifestyle, enable us to hear from our Lord the words of grand welcome to eternal bliss: "Well done, good and faithful servant!

Enter into the joys of your Master" (Mt 25:21).

We need to pray to the Saints, both the canonized and the uncanonized, asking them to pray on our behalf that we may live our lives in faithfulness like theirs, and so receive the same reward they are enjoying.

Norms for Indulgences at the Commemoration of All Souls

From 12 noon on Nov 1st to midnight on Nov 2nd, all who have confessed, received Holy Communion and prayed for the Pope's intentions (one Our Father & one Hail Mary, can gain one plenary indulgence by visiting a church, and there reciting one Our Father & the Apostle's Creed.

This indulgence is applicable only to the souls of the departed.

Confession may be made any time within the week preceding or the week following Nov 1. The faithful who visit a cemetery and pray for the dead may gain a plenary indulgence applicable only to the Holy Souls by saying the above prayers once per day from Nov 1st to 8th.
