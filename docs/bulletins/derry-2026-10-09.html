Season of Creation: Our symbol for 2026 - Immersion in Living Water - is a symbol of renewal and birth: water flowing from the Temple as a sign of God’s caring and healing for us. We are called to step into the water, until weareso deep that we must depend on God’s grace to sustain us. Those who receive this water are invited to become sources of life for others, allowing God’s love to flow through them into the world. And thus, the water of God will become inus“a spring of water welling up to eternal life”, as Jesus promised (John 4:14). Forgetting: The tenants in today’s Gospel have forgotten this and are driven by a mistaken view of themselves, the land on which they live, and the Landlord who owns that land. They have taken possession of land that is not theirs toownandare living divided from the land. Therefore, the landlord decides to take the land from them and give it to more deserving tenants. This is a parable and not meant to be taken literally. The point is that when we hold a wrong view of ourselves, we will hold a wrong view of Creation, and we will not give to God what is due – and what is this? Our very selves in complete trust. Now, this isnottosaywe are not meant to work to produce and to feed ourselves. As the liturgy beautifully illustrates in the consecration - fruit of the earth, and work of human hands, when given back to Godis returned to us blessed. What it does mean is that we need to discover a true vision of ourselves and our brothers and sisters of Creation. As we have seen earlier the human fear that wedonot have enough or that someone might take what is ours leads to grasping; we want to own what is not ours to own and fail to return to God what is God’s and to share with the rest of creation. So, we exploit Sister Mother Earth for everything that we can get, and we build great storage barns for ourselves, and we ignore what Jesus told us about thewayin which nature, in a sense, trusts in the goodness of Godtobe clothed andfed. The opposite of this trusting leads us to ownership of what is not ours toown, anda division of the human family which leads to suffering and causes the very scarcity that we fear. A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 21:33-43 In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells the parable of the vineyard, reminding us that God lovingly entrusts gifts, responsibilities and opportunities to his people. The vineyard is fruitful, carefully prepared and full of promise, yet the tenants forget that it does not truly belong to them. They begin to act as though they are the owners rather than those who have been entrusted with its care. Wetoocanbe tempted to hold tightly to what God has placed in our hands - our time, talents, possessions, abilities and even our relationships. We can easily forget that these are gifts to be received with gratitude and used for the good of others. The Gospel invites us instead to become faithful stewards, recognising that everything we have is ultimately a gift from God and that we are called to help it flourish. At the heart of the parable is God’s patient and enduring love. Even when his messengers are rejected and his people turn away, God does not abandon them. He continues to call them back to himself, offering opportunities to change, to return and to begin again. In sending his Son, Jesus, God reveals just howfarheis willing togoto draw us into his love and restore us to friendship with him. As a parish community, may we receive all that God has entrusted to us with grateful hearts. Maywebe generous in sharing our gifts, faithful in our responsibilities, and attentive to the needs of those around us. Andmayour lives, like a well-tended vineyard, bear abundant fruit - fruit that gives glory to God and brings hope, love and blessing to others.
