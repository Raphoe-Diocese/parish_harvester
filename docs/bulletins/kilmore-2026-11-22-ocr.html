Reflection: Feast of ‘Jesus Christ, the King of the Universe’: This las t Sunday of the liturgical year is when we celebrate Jesus as Universal King.

This Sunday’s Gospel takes us to the foo t of the cross on Calvary.

At the end of his journey, over his head, his crime was posted: “Jesus Christ, King o f the Jews”. How many took the charge seriously?

Did anyone of sane mind really think he was a threat to Caesar or to public order?

The irony of that day was that he was a king, but not in the way that the wo rldly mind could understand.

Jesus as king was the complete reversal of ways usually followed by royalty: Instead he came to serve others and stand with the poor and marginalised and the lowliest of people.

The mission of Jesus was to establish the Kingdom of God in the world.

The qualities of the reign of God would be: A Kingdom of truth and life; a Kingdom of holiness and grace; a Kingdom of justice, love and peace.

So today we express in the ultimate triumph of the reign of God and renew our commitment to do our part in living up to the ideals of Jesus Christ. November Prayer: “Lord God, whose days are without end and whose mercies are beyond cou nting, keep us mindful that life is short and the hour of death unknown.

Let your Spirit guide our days here on earth in ways of holiness and justice; that we may serve you in union with the whole church, sure in faith, strong in hope and perfect in love.

And when our earthly dwelling is ended, lead us rejoicing into your kingdom where you live for ever and ever.
