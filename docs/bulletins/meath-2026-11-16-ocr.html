God’s Ways are Not Our Ways… This Sunday’s Gospel reading shows us what it means to be truly foolish.

We believe in Christ – pray – good! We put time into our Christian faith growth and we value o pportunities for more faith formation – very wise!

A nd we say that we trust God – wonderful!

But what happens when God doesn’t a nswer our prayers fast enough?

What if we need Chri st’s loving embrace or his protection from evil and yet we’re s uffering with no end in sight?

Where is Jesus then? Usually, God’s timing is not ours.

We think we have the right idea about how soon God should intervene when we ask for his help . Right now is a good time for it, right?

Of course! But perhaps not from God’s perspective.

When we despair over unanswered prayer, the reality behind it is that we’re emotionally unprepared for any timing that’s different from what we think it s hould be.

In today’s gospel, the foolish bridesmaids thought the groom would arrive before their lamps burned out.

Like us, they tho ught they understood the situation.

Do we? Furthermore, if God’s plans ar illogical – if they don’t make sense to us – we dis trust them.

And then we get ourselves into the mess of trying to handle things apart and separate from God.

However, a lot of t he time and especially in difficult times, we cannot trust our own logic or understanding of the situations we’re in.

Therefo re if we can fully trust in what it represents to be a Christian, the sooner we will find out that God’s ways are always awesomely much, much better than anything we can ever imagine!

From this our trust i n God is strengthened more and more!

Points to Ponder In today’s Gospel, Jesus draws from this revelation about the future the fu ture the practical moral that a Christian needs to be on the watch, living each day as if it were his last. The important thing is not to speculate about when these events will happen and what form they will take, but to live in such a way that they Įnd us in the state of grace.

Living each day as if it were your last is a powerful concept that can help us to live more fully and with greater purpose.

It reminds us that our time on this earth is limited and that we should make the most of every moment.

It also enco urages us to focus on what is truly important in life, such as our relationships with others and our relationsh ip with God.

Keeping watch is another important aspect of this passage.

It reminds us to be vigilant and to stay alert t6o the signs of the times.

We should be aware of the world around us and the events That are unfolding, so that we can respond appropriately.

This means being informed about what is happening in our communities, our nation, and the world, and taking action when necessary.

Today’s Gospel, then, is a powerful reminder to Christians to live each day as if it were their last and to keep watch for the signs of the times.

By doing so, we can live more fully and with greater purpose, and we can be prepared for w hatever the future may hold for us.

This special and personal evening prayer is an opportun ity to remember those who have died, give thanks for their lives and support one another in our shared experience of grief.

Each loss tells its own story: a parent, grandparent, spouse, child or dear friend.

Whether a life was long and full or heartbreakingly short, whether death was tragic ands udden or after a long illness, the absence of someone we love leaves an ache that words cannot fully express.

Our Annual Remembrance Mass brings into focus the year that has passed, particularly that difficult first year of grief, marked by so many firs ts the first birthday, the first Easter and the first Christmas, all occasions when we deeply miss our deceased loved ones.

Grief can leave us feeling like broken pieces, pieces of our lives scat tered and a sense that we may never be whole again.

Yet a mosaic is made beautiful through its many broken pieces, each one precious and necessary, brought together forming a colourful image.

Our Christian faith reminds us that, through Chri st’s Resurrection, what is broken can be made whole again.

Death is not the end; it is the promise of new life in God’s eternal love.

November, with its shorter days and fading light, speaks of loss a nd sadness, but beneath the bare earth seeds wait for the promise of spring.

So too, our faith invites us to hold on to hope, even in t he darkness, trusting in the promise of resurrection.

During the Mass, each candle lit will represent a beloved soul, with a special candle remembering those who died outside our parish.

May their light remind us that every life is precious and that our loved ones are held in the light of Christ, the Risen Lord.

As we share this Eucharist, may we find comfort in God’s presence, strength in one another and hope in the promise of eternal life.

Follow the apostolic visit of Pope Leo XIV to Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru on www.vaticannews.va and other Catholic media outlets The logo for Pope Leo XIV’s visit to Peru incorporates various elements that evoke faith, the history of the Church and its evangelizing mission in the country.

The heart framing the composition represents an invitation to open one’s heart to God, to one’s brothers and sisters, and to the future with hope.

Inside the heart is a map of Peru, an emblem of the entire nation in all its cultural, human and spiritual richness.

The dove of the Holy Spirit, placed above the national territory, recalls the presence of God who guides the Church, inspires reconciliation and renews the hope of the Peruvian people.

In the centre, Pope Leo XIV is depicted in the act of greeting and with the smile of the Good Shepherd, as a sign of his closeness and of his mission to confirm God’s holy people in the faith.

Surrounding him are some of the principal saints of the Peruvian tradition: St Rose of Lima, St Turibius, St Martin de Porres, St Francis Solano and St John Macias.

Taken as a whole, the logo conveys the image of a Church sustained by the Holy Spirit, enriched by the witness of her saints, and guided by the Successor of Peter — a Church called, through the Holy Father’s visit, to renew hope and strengthen unity in Peru.
