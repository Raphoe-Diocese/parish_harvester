SAINT OF THE WEEK Royal figures today attract attention for all the wrong reasons — think of Prince Harry, and his family.

This was not the case with Elizabeth of Hungary.

She was a princess, married at 14 and widowed at 20. After that, she used what money she had to build a hospital where she served the poor herself.

She once said, “Everything I own really belongs to the poor”, words you don’t hear from royalty today.

St Elizabeth of Hungary (feast day

Tuesday 15 November) is the patron saint of charity, bakers, beggars, brides, hospitals, nurses, widows and homeless people: pray for us.

FEAST OF THE WEEK

Wednesday 18 November is the 400th anniversary of the dedication of St Peter’s Basilica in Rome, which Pope Leo will honour with a special Mass.

That date was chosen because it was on that date in 326 that the first St Peter’s opened.

The first church was built on the site of the Circus of Nero where St Peter had been crucified, and the ancient cemetery where he was buried.

Peter is the rock on which our church is built.

Readings October 6th Gn 2:18-24. Ps 127, 5. Heb 2:9-11. Mk 10:2-16. Annual Mass for Bereaved November 20th Most of November we concentrate on remembering our dead.

The Holy Souls Novena, the visits to the graveyards are good but they make it impossible for people to avoid being revisited by the pain caused by the death of a loved one.

Here in Ballinhassig Parish we have had sad, sudden and untimely deaths this year and in the past few years.

Consequently we have many parishioners for whom November is a very sad and lonely month.

The thought of facing Christmas and beyond without the departed loved one always brings enormous sadness in need of some ray of hope to dispel the darkness of grief.

Conscious of this, the Annual Mass for the Bereaved will be held on Friday November 20th at 7.00pm in Ballyheada Church.

All those who suffered a bereavement in the past twelve months, family members, neighbours and friends are especially invited to attend this Special Mass.

It is hoped this Special Mass will bring a message of hope, comfort and consolation to all who live with the pain of loss, especially the death of a loved one.

As part of the ceremony everybody will be invited to bring a lighted candle, available at the door, to the altar on entering the church.

These individual candles will then symbolise a community of hope with all candles then lighting as one.

Everybody welcome on the night. We are also conscious that we may not be aware of all parishioners who suffered bereavements of family members who resided outside the parish.

The night, however, is not only for those bereaved in the past twelve months, it is a night for remembering and praying for all who are grieving.

We need to live be fore God and respond to him sincerely by using the talents he given us to the maximum.

The servants who invest their talents and make a re turn on them have understood the purpose of their lives and the time they have at their disposal.

Help Keep our Parishes and Churches Going and in Good Repair!

The parish income for our seven parishes covers the basic bills for eight churches - light, heat, insurance, salaries of employees, church requisites (bread, wine, candles, etc.) - but is slipping.

Our parish income is kept up by unusual elements: car parking, renting houses, the parishes' lotto.

The offertory collections and monthly maintenance collections do not cover all of our ordinary expenses.

When extra expenses occur - and our old buildings require lots of maintenance - our income is far from covering these costs.

In order to begin addressing this shortfall, and in order to avoid closing any churches, there are two proposals we the priests and finance committees would like to put before you which should not cause any additional burden for you.

Planned Giving to your parish reduces financial pressure to give and makes it sustainable.

Using the boxes of offertory envelopes reminds us to get our planned offering ready for Sunday and holyday Mass; people say getting a new box helps them think what regular offering they would like to give and would fit their budget; the envelopes are also returned from neighbouring parishes when our parishioners go there for Mass and drop their envelope into the offertory basket.

Instead, you might prefer cheques, Standing Orders, Pay Zone (via our website), and other electronic funds transfers; they are alternative easy ways to support the parish in a planned and non-pressured fashion.

If you wish to use the envelope system, next weekend (21st – 22nd November) we will provide a form for you to register.

We will then arrange for your box to be in the sacristy of your choice for you to collect before Christmas.

The boxes of envelopes are arranged to be used from 1st January to 31st December.

Income Tax Relief Scheme making your generosity go further For those who pay income tax, the state offers you the possibility of diverting some of your tax to the charity of your choice, provided you give at least €250 per year to that charity and have some record of the donation, e.g. through the envelope system, a cheque, payments from a standing order.

Participating in the Income Tax Relief Scheme in favour of your parish - the charity - does not cost you any more than what you have already donated, the state simply adds to your generosity at no additional cost to you.

An annual contribution from you to the parish of €250 can mean €100 extra for the parish from the tax you already paid to the state: at no extra cost to you.

This scheme is entirely confidential and gives the government no more information about your finances than it already has.

Details of your giving to the parish is known only by those who need to know - the person applying to the government for the tax back.

The priests do not get involved in dealing with your information.

If you wish to participate in the income tax relief scheme in favour of your parish, again next week we will have another form for you to sign up to the scheme and you can return it in complete confidence to any sacristy after any Mass.

Some members of the finance committees will be speaking on this at Masses next weekend, 21st/22nd November, and the two forms will be available for you at that time.

Sunday 15th November 2026 33rd Sunday in Ordinary Time ~ Year A Gospel Reflection A lot done… more to do!

This phrase, often used during election campaigns, has become part of our everyday vocabulary.

It also applies to each of us who calls ourselves a disciple of Christ. Jesus sets a very high bar through his example.

He is the supreme boundary-breaker, teaching us to overcome our fears and take risks for the sake of others and the Kingdom.

Today’s Gospel, the parable of the talents, challenges us to use our gifts for the sake of others.

The two servants who have multiplied their talents are praised by the master.

The third servant, however, chooses safety and refuses to reach for higher goals.

He takes the easy route: sitting comfortably and waiting.

True discipleship asks us to getup, get moving and be among the people.

It calls us to respond to the challenge that there is more to do: to strive for “more” by leaving our comfortable places and embracing a passion for justice and compassion for the poor.

WEEK OF WITNESS FOR PERSECUTED CHRISTIANS . 15-22 November 2026. 220 million Christians live in countries where they are directly exposed to persecution, while 413 million Christians live in countries where religious freedom is severely violated, that is 18%, or about one in five Christians.

Around the church and in the Parish Centre are fact sheets outlining some facts of the top countries where this persecution is real and the types of persecution being faced by Christians.

Red Wednesday 2026 will be observed on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, as part of a global initiative by Aid to the Church in Need (ACN) to highlight the persecution of Christians and promote religious freedom.
