Feast Days Monday 29th June: St. Peter and St. Paul were important apostles of Jesus Christ, with distinct backgrounds and roles. St. Peter, an ordinary man, one of the original twelve disciples, was chosen by Jesus and considered the ‘rock’ upon which the Church was to be built. St. Peter was martyred in Rome, likely during Nero’s persecution. St. Paul, initially a persecutor of Christians, underwent a dramatic conversion and became ‘Apostle to the Gentiles’, a zealous missionary spreading Christianity and a prolific writer whose scripts and letters are foundational to Christian theology. St. Paul was martyred in Rome, likely around the same time as St. Peter. Their joint feast signifies their remarkable commitment to spreading the Gospel; their grace and courage to bear witness and the wisdom to shape the early Church. Tuesday 30th June: The First Martyrs of the See of Rome When the city of Rome had been devastated by fire in the year 64, the Emperor Nero launched a persecution against the Christians, who were thrown to the wild beasts in the arena or soaked in tar and used as living torches. Their deaths are documented in the writings of the Roman historian Tacitus and in Pope St. Clement’s letter to the Corinthians.
