First Reading: Is 22: 19 -23. Shebna, master of the
royal palace, having climbed to become master of King
Hezekiah’s palace had become proud and self -
important. In the interest of good governance, he is
replaced by Eliakim. The metaphor of ‘the key of the
House of David on his shoulder’ is borrowed from an
ancient Egyptian custom. (What may be Shebna’s ac-
tual, elaborate tomb was recently discovered at Siloam
cemetery in Jerusalem.)
Second Reading: Rom. 11:33 -36. At the end of three
chapters setting out how pagans and Jews have been
part of God’s plan of salvation, Paul composes this
triumphant hymn to the wisdom of God. As Creator He
has an overview of all time and place. Who could ever
teach him? Who could ever be his counsellor?
Gospel: Mt. 16:13 -20. By a special divine grace Peter
declares timelessly that Jesus is “the Messiah and Son
of the living God." Jesus now gives him a hitherto un-
known personal name - ‘petros’ - meaning ‘rock’. The
new name emphasises his new role as the strengthener
of his brothers’ faith and foundation of unity for his
church. This is also a watershed moment. From this
time on, Jesus began to speak of his coming passion. GARDEN FETE 2026
