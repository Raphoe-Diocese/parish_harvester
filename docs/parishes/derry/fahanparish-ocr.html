For the nine Sundays before Christmas we will gather for one hour and the ceremo ny will comprise of the Rosary, a talk given by an invited speaker, the Novena prayer and petitions ( which can be placed in a box at the door of the church), a Eucharistic Healing Ceremony and fini shing with Benediction of the Most Blessed Sacrament.

Once again, the theme of this year’s Novena is “Throwing Open the Doors to Christ ” – taken from the homily of Pope St John Paul at the Opening Mass of his Pontificate on 22 October 1978. The first Guest speaker on the opening night will be Bishop Michael Router.

All are very welcome. Please come along, bring your families and make the most of this opportunity for prayer as we approach the season of Chri st’s birth. (The Holy Hour each Sunday evening in St Mary’s Oratory will cease for the moment, resuming after Christmas.) “November Remember ” Pastoral Initiative With the grim statistic of there having been 150 people killed on the country’s roads so far this year (12 more than the same period last year) – 45 of these people aged under 25 and 30 aged over 65 (of the 29 pedestrians killed, 10 were over 65) – we need to redouble our efforts as road users to do what we can to make our roads safer, especially as we enter the darker and colder months.

To this end, while November is a time when we remember our dead, this year, in an att empt to acknowledge that the life and death of each of us has its influence on others, we would like as many people as possible to take on a pledge to use the roads with care in November.

The de tails of that pledge are to be found on posters and our social media pages.

Also, next weekend, people ar asked to collect a prayer card for their car to be recited each day during the mo nth and a bottle of holy water to be sprinkled on their car and kept there.

Chief Superintendent Goretti Sheridan will address the Cockhill Novena on

There will be signage placed on the roads entering our two pari shes to remind us of this.
