Sunday 6 September 2026
Twenty-third Sunday in Ordinary Time
Shared responsibility for ourselves
and our neighbour
The readings for this week ask us and indeed challenge us to embrace our shared
responsibility for one another. In the first reading, God appoints Ezekiel as a
watchman for the house of Israel. This role which Ezekiel gains is not about
showing authority over others, but about a deep spiritual attentiveness to others.
God reminds us that we are complicit if we remain silent while a brother or sister
walks a path of self-destruction. True community requires us to care about the
spiritual well-being as well as the physical well-being of our neighbour.
This call to look out for one another is grounded entirely in Christian charity. As
Saint Paul reminds us in his letter to the Romans, our only standing debt is to love
one another. When we are called to offer guidance or accountability, it must never
flow from a place of pride, judgment, or self-righteousness. Love does no evil to a
neighbour, which means our corrections must be gentle, private, and always have
a motivation or desire for the other person’s ultimate good and healing.
In the Gospel of Matthew, Jesus provides a practical method for resolving conflict
and seeking reconciliation within the Church. Instead of resorting to destructive
gossip, Christ commands us to speak directly and privately to the person who has
offended us. The primary goal is always restoration or to "win over" our brother
or sister. In doing so, this protects the dignity of the individual while preserving
the harmony of the entire community.
Jesus seals this teaching with a beautiful promise of His divine presence: "Where
two or three are gathered in my name, there am I in the midst of them." This
reminds us that reconciliation is not merely a human effort, but a sacramental
reality. When we gather in humility to heal fractured relationships, Christ is
actively present among us, offering the grace needed to bind wounds and loosen
the bonds of sin.
