Twenty-second Sunday in Ordinary Time
Are your ways God’s ways?
In a major shift from last week, Peter learns how difficult it can be to accept
God’s way. The previous few lines on St. Matthew’s Gospel show Peter
recognising Jesus as the Christ, while now he cannot bear to hear that the
Lord must suffer, die, and rise again. To us as modern-day listeners, Jesus’
reply is abrupt: Peter is thinking “not as God does, but as human beings
do.” It is a reminder that faith is not simply agreeing with pleasant truths;
it is learning to trust God even when the road ahead includes sacrifice. Even
Peter took time to realise this.
Jeremiah understands this struggle. God’s word burns within him like fire,
yet speaking it brings mockery and rejection and many of us know
something of that tension. We want to follow Christ, but understandably
we also want comfort, approval, and an easier path. We may be tempted
to keep quiet, avoid difficult choices, or shape our lives around what the
world expects.
Paul’s letter to the Romans offers us a practical answer: “Do not conform
yourselves to this age but be transformed by the renewal of your mind.”
Christian discipleship begins in the daily offering of ourselves to God: our
time, work, relationships, worries, and hopes. This is not dramatic for most
of us. It is found in forgiving someone, serving without being noticed,
choosing honesty, praying when tired, or standing beside someone in our
lives who is struggling.
Jesus does not ask us to take up the cross because suffering is good in itself.
He asks it because love is costly, and true life is found when we give
ourselves away as anyone who is married could testify. This week, let us
ask for the courage to see with God’s eyes and to follow Christ in one
concrete choice. The cross we carry with him is never the end of the story;
it is the path that leads to resurrection and life.
