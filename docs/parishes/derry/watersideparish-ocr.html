Sunday 22nd November 2026 Thirty-Fourth Sunday in Ordinary Time The Solemnity of Christ the King marks the end of the Church’s liturgical year.

At a time when many people have scant regard for monarchs or kings, we need to ask ourselves what does it mean to honour Jesus Christ as king?

What kind of kingship are we celebrating?

Yes, Jesus is a king but, as he himself states clearly, his kingdom is ‘not of this world’ (cf.

Jn 18:36). He has no stately palace, no mighty army, no marble throne.

He resides among the needy and the vulnerable, the hungry, the naked, the thirsty, the stranger, the sick and those in prison.

The king we are called to worship and serve, as today’s gospel reading from Matthew makes clear, is a king who identifies himself with the most neglected of God’s children.

And the way he wants to be served is by practical care and concern for these brothers and sisters of ours: ‘I was hungry and you gave me food.

I was thirsty and you gave me drink; I was a stranger and you made me welcome; naked and you clothed me, sick and you visited me, in prison and you came to see me’(Mt 25:35-36).

The challenge of today’s gospel is to recognise and respond to Christ in the broken and wounded people of our world.

Christ is still suffering in those who are hungry for food and for justice; in the strangers longing for acceptance and a word of welcome; in the sick needing healing and care; and in the prisoners hoping for a friendly smile and a listening ear.

Looking out for and responding to such people is to recognise and attend to the broken body of Christ. Ardmore Cemetery The cemetery at Ardmore has been recently extended and in the coming weeks it will be used for burials.

It is a good opportunity to remind all of us that our cemeteries are sacred spaces where our loved one’s earthly remains are resting there and should be treated with respect and reverence.

Please remember that it is only a ‘right to bury’ that is obtained and not the purchase of a grave.

The title to the land remains with the parish name.

Once the new section comes into use, this area will be a lawn cemetery which will affect the way this area of the cemetery will be maintained: o Graves will be lawn without any surround o Artificial grass cannot be used o A headstone must have a space for flowers (such as on the plinth) to keep the grass area free to be cuto A month following a funeral, any flowers and wreaths must be removed o Please do not bring benches to the cemetery o Please do not plant trees in the cemetery o Any items such as pots, flowers which impede the maintenance of the cemetery will be removed
