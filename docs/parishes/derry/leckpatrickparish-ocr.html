clonleighparish@derrydiocese.org ; Website : www.clonleighparish.com Gospel Reflection (Matthew 25:31-46) In the gospel today, Jesus gives us a powerful and moving picture of the final judgment.

The Son of Man returns in glory and separates the sheep from the goats, not according to wealth, success, or even outward religious practices, but according to how they responded to those in need.

Jesus identifies Himself with the hungry, thirsty, the stranger, the sick, and those imprisoned, teaching us that whenever we show love and compassion to others, we are serving Him.

This Gospel reminds us that faith is meant to be lived.

It is easy to say that we love God, but Jesus invites us to make that love visible through acts of kindness, mercy, and generosity.

The sheep are praised because their care for others flowed naturally from hearts shaped by God's love.

The challenge for us is not simply to avoid doing wrong, but to actively seek opportunities to do good.

Every person in need is an opportunity to encounter Christ and to share His love with the world.

No act of kindness is ever too small in God's eyes.

A listening ear, an encouraging word, a helping hand, or a generous gift can become a sign of God's presence in someone's life.

Let us ask the Lord to give us eyes that see the suffering around us, hearts filled with compassion, and hands ready to serve.

As we follow the example of Jesus, who came not to be served but to serve, may we bring His love to those we meet each day.

And when we stand before Him, may we hear those joyful words we all long to hear: "Well done, good and faithful servant" (Matthew 25:21).

May the Lord’s grace guide us, His peace sustain us, and His blessing remain with us always.
