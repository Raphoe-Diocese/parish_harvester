Sunday 16th August 2026 – Twentieth Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A
The Gospel for this Sunday recounts Jesus’ encounter with a Canaanite woman whose daughter is tormented by a
demon (Matthew 15:21 -28) . Initially, Jesus appears dismissive, stating that He was sent only to the lost sheep of Israel
and even comparing her t o a dog unworthy of the children’s bread. Yet, the woman persists, humbly acknowledging her
need and demonstrating remarkable faith. Her courage and persistence lead Jesus to commend her faith and heal her
daughter
This passage challenges us to reflect on faith and humility. True faith is not about pretending to be strong; it is
about admitting our need for God and asking for help, even when answers seem delayed or social barriers make it
difficult. The woman’s exam ple teaches that persistence in pray er and trust in God’s mercy can overcome obstacles
and bring about transformation, both for ourselves and others.

PILGRIMAGE TO CYPRUS
The parish is considering a 7-night pilgrimage to Cyprus from
16–23 May 2027 , following in the footsteps of St Paul and
St Barnabas . The pilgrimage would include visits to many of
Cyprus’s most important Christian and historical sites, with the
opportunity to celebrate Mass in places closely associated with
the early Church.
The package includes direct flights from Belfast , full-board
accommodation in a four -star seafront hotel, and a guided
programme of pilgrimage visits throughout the week. The
estimated cost is £1,675 per person . At this stage, we are simply
seeking expressions of interest . If you think you may wish to join
the pilgrimage, please contact the parish office. Registering
your interest is not a commitment to travel .
