Fr Micheál McGavigan PP – Fr Roni Zacharias CC – Fr Stephen Ward CC Sunday 11th October 2026 – Twenty Eighth Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A In this Sunday's Gospel, Jesus compares the Kingdom of Heaven to a wedding feast. The king has everything prepared and sends out invitations, yet many people refuse to come. Some are simply too busy, while others reject the invitation altogether. Intheend, the king opens his doors to everyone he can find, and the banquet hall is filled. The Gospel invites us to reflect on the contrast between God's vision and the world we often experience. So much of modern life is marked by division, conflict and people retreating behind barriers of fear, anger and suspicion. Yet God's dream for humanity is not a barricade but a banquet: a place where people gather, where differences are overcome, and where everyone is welcomed. Each day we make a choice. We can add to the barriers that divide people or help build the table where people can meet, listen, forgive and belong. God's invitation remains open. The feast is ready. Will we accept it? Parishes of Glendermott & Strathfoyle Sunday 11th October 2026 Twenty-Eight Sunday in Ordinary Time Be clothed in …. Jesus’ favourite way of teaching was by telling stories or parables. In today’s gospel, he tells a story about a banquet prepared by a King for his son’s wedding. When everything is ready for the feast, the king sends his servants, not once but twice, to call the invited guests to come. When these refuse to come, for one reason or another, he finally asks his servants to go to the crossroads in the town and invite everyone they can find, good and bad alike, to come to the wedding. These come, and the ‘wedding hall is filled’ (Mt 22:10). Like most of the parables of Jesus, this is a story about the Kingdom of God. All are called to be part of the kingdom, but not everyone appreciates the invitation, or responds to it appropriately. Sadly, despite repeated invitations, they refuse to come. Instead, they seize the servants, mistreat and kill them. This refers to the hostile reception given to the prophets. All are invited, good and bad alike, until the wedding hall is filled. What Jesus is saying is that everyone is invited to the Kingdom of God. All kinds of people are called to be part of God’s new world. It does not belong to an elite group of specially chosen ones. It encompasses saints and sinners. The only requirement is a ‘wedding garment’ (Mt 22:11-12). But what is this wedding garment? St Paul gives us the answer: “You should be clothed in sincere compassion, in kindness and humility, gentleness and patience…. Over all these clothes, to keep them together and complete them, put on love.” (Colossions 3:12-15) World Mission Sunday - 18th October 2026 Next weekend the Church celebrates World Mission Sunday, the weekend which the Pope annually invites all Catholics throughout the world to support the Churches missionary life.
