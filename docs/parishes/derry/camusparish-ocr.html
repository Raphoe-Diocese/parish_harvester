Parishes of Camus, Clonleigh and Leckpatrick Twenty-Third Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A – 13th September 2026 Parish Clergy Fr Michael McCaughey, PP: (028) 71882274 / (028) 71882215 Fr Declan Boland (priest in residence) Email : leckpatrickparish@derrydiocese.org; Website : www.leckpatrickparish.com Email : strabaneparish@derrydiocese.org; Website: www.camusparish.com Email: clonleighparish@derrydiocese.org; Website : www.clonleighparish.com Thoughts on the Gospel (Matthew 18:21-35) The gospel today tells of Peter asking Jesus how many times he should forgive someone who wrongs him. Jesus responds with a parable: a servant forgiven of an immense debt refuses to forgive a fellow servant of a small debt. The lesson is clear: those who receive mercy must extend mercy. Forgiveness begins with humility, recognising our own frailty and the mercy we have received. Itisnot weakness, but strength rooted in grace. Humility opens our hearts to God’s mercy and enables us to extend that same mercy to others. Forgiveness also requires empathy. By seeing the humanity of those who hurt us, we can respond with compassion without excusing wrongdoing. In forgiving, we honour the forgiveness we ourselves have received and become stewards of grace. Ultimately, forgiveness reshapes our hearts, relationships, and communities, making us more like the Onewho forgives endlessly. The parable of the unforgiving servant is not just a warning; itisani nvitation. It calls us to live with a HEART shaped by humility, empathy, accountability, release, and transformation. Forgiveness is not easy, butitis essential. When we forgive, we reflect the very heart of God. Andin doing so, we discover freedom, peace, andthejoyofa life lived in mercy. Lord, help us forgive with humble hearts and share your mercy with others. Amen.
