POINTS TO PONDER – 25 October 2026 To love the Lord with all our heart – is this even possible?

A common assumption is that love must just come, that it grows organically, and that it cannot be forced.

That’s infatuation, not true love. Any kind of love – for a parent, spouse, child, or friend – will reach a point during which it is no longer easy.

For love to be true, it must be a free choice and commitment. ‘All your heart, soul, and mind’ sounds rather extreme, even rather dangerous.

The world says that loving anyone totally is a risky proposition – what i f the one we love rejects us or lets us down, leaving us with nothing?

And the world is even more terrified to love God totally, who cannot even be seen.

This makes sense; after all, to love totally is to trust that we will find fulfillment precisely by not looking for it ourselves, but by looking to fulfill someone else.

That old, almost clichéd phrase, ‘love grows when i t is given away,’ is actually spot-on in this regard.

True love takes a leap of faith, which is what frightens nonbelievers.

Many people are okay with going to church every now and then or volunteering a charity from time to time (and posting it on Instagram, of course).

Loving like that does not require us to make any kind of leap, any kind of definitive change, any commitment.

But loving God with everything means that all we do is for love of him.

We trust that he will take care of us. That is heroic; that is what it means to be a saint THOUGHT FOR THE DAY ‘Remember that nothing is small in the eyes of God.

Ardmore Parish | 25 October 2026 CULMORE PARISH Newsletter THIRTIETH SUNDAY IN ORDINARY TIME 25 OCTOBER 2026 YEAR A Gospel Reflection | Matthew 22:34–40 In today’s Gospel, Jesus teaches that the greatest c ommandment is to love God wholeheartedly and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

These two responsibilities are inseparable and form the foundation of Christian life.

Loving God involves more than religious practices suc h as prayer, worship, and Scripture reading.

It means trusting Him in difficult times, remaining faithful through challeng es, choosing what is right, and allowing Him to guide every aspect of our lives.

Genuine love for God is shown not only in words but also in daily actions.

Our love for God becomes visible in how we treat othe rs.

We cannot truly love God while harming, judging , or refusing to forgive those around us.

Instead, we are called to show compa ssion, kindness, patience, and mercy.

Even simple acts such as listening, encouraging, forgiving, and helping those in need become powerful expressions of God’s love.

Jesus also calls us to recognise the dignity and worth of every person, including those who differ from us or disappoint us.

True love does not ignore wrongdoing, but it speaks the truth with respect and sets boundaries without hatred.

The central message of this Gospel is that love must be at the heart of both our relationship with God and our relationship with others.

Faith is not measured by religious knowledge or activities alone, but by whether it transforms us into more loving people.

When love guides our thoughts, words, and actions, our lives become a living witness to God’s presence in the world.

Prayer for the Week May the Lord bless us with hearts open to love.

May He help us to see Christ in one a nother and to bring kindness, hope, and compassion wherever we go.
