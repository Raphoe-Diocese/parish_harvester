Sunday 16th August 2026
Twentieth Sunday in Ordinary Time
“Wom an,you have great faith”
The Canaanite woman in the Gospel appears almost to be the opposite
of Peter from last week whom Jesus accused of having little faith. As an
outsider, she comes to him with a mother’s urgent prayer: “Lord, help
me.” At first her plea seems to meet silence and resistance. Yet she does
not walk away: she remains humble, courageous, and persistent,
trusting that even the smallest share in God’s mercy is enough to bring
healing.
Her faith it is not loud in a demanding entitled way. She displays a strong
faith in the sense of refusing to give up on Jesus. She, an outsider,
recognises him as Lord and places before him the suffering of someone
she loves. Many of us will know something of that prayer: the prayer
made beside a hospital bed, for a family member in difficulty, for a child
who has lost their way, or for a burden that we carry that seems slow to
lift.
Jesus’ final words to her are full of hope: “Woman, you have great faith.”
The Gospel reminds us that God’s mercy is wider than our boundaries.
The first reading speaks of God’s house as “a house of prayer for all
peoples,” and the Gospel shows that promise beginning to unfold in the
life of Christ. No one who turns to the Lord with faith is beyond his
compassion.
This Gospel is both comforting and challenging. It comforts us by
teaching us to persevere in prayer, even when answers seem delayed. It
challenges us to become a community where those who feel distant,
overlooked, or outside the usual circle can still encounter welcome,
dignity, and mercy. This week, let us pray with the Canaanite woman’s
simple words: “Lord, help me.” And let us ask for the grace to see others
with the generous heart of Christ.Waterside (& Strathfoyle amalgamated)
