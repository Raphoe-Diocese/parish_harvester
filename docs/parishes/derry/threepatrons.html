Sunday 25th October 2026 – Thirtieth Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A In this Sunday’s Gospel, Jesus gives us the two great commandments: love God and love our neighbour.

But there are two words we can easily overlook: “Love your neighbour as yourself.” Jesus does not tell us to put ourselves first, but neither does he tell us that we do not matter.

Christian love means learning to see ourselves as God sees us, with honesty, patience and mercy.

We all have faults, regrets and things we would change, but none of these takes away our dignity or God’s love for us.

Perhaps learning to accept God’s mercy for ourselves also helps us to become more merciful towards others.

We do not have to demand perfection from ourselves, and neither should we demand it from those around us.

Speak to yourself with the same kindness you would offer to somebody you love.

You too carry the image of God. WELCOME TO GOD’S FAMILY Eoghan David O’Carroll; Grace Anna McCauley; Myles Eamon Doherty.

Sunday 25th October 2026 Thirtieth Sunday in Ordinary Time The Commandment of Love is the greatest of the commandments, the mother of all the virtues, and the pulsating heart of the Christian message.

We are created in the image of a God who is Love; so, we cannot live without love In today’s gospel reading from Matthew, one of the Pharisees, a lawyer, asks Jesus to identify ‘the greatest commandment of the Law’(Mt 22:36).

In response, Jesus quotes the great ‘shema’ prayer recited daily by all devout Jews (cf.

Deut 6:4-5). This prayer exhorts us to love God with our whole heart, mind and soul.

Jesus immediately adds a second commandment, familiar to his audience namely, ‘love your neighbour as yourself’ (Mt 22:39).

Sadly, the word ‘love’ is so overused in our world today that it has lost much of its meaning.

We speak of love of country, love of sport, love of food, love between friends, love of parents, romantic love, etc As Pope Leo teaches, ‘True love is not merely a fleeting, briefly inflamed emotion.

It is more. It is a deep commitment to the other, a decision, a journey and a covenant for life All genuine love is manifested in action; it is practical and outgoing.

In the words of St Augustine: ‘It has the hands to help others.

It has the feet to hasten to the poor and needy.

It has the eyes to see misery and want. It has the ears to hear the sighs and sorrows of people.

Such love is demanding. It summons us to leave our comfort zones, to give our time, our energy, our talents, and, indeed, our very selves to others FEAST OF ALL SAINTS Mass Arrangements for the Feast of All Saints-
