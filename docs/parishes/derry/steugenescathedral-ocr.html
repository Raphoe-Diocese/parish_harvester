A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 6:13-20
In today’s Gospel, Jesus asks his disciples a question that goes to the heart of our faith:
“Who do you say that I am?” Peter answers with courage and conviction: “You are the
Christ, the Son of the living God.”
It is a question Jesus asks each one of us too. We may know about Jesus through Scripture,
prayer and the life of the Church, but Jesus invites us to something deeper than simply
knowing about him. He asks us to recognise him personally as the Son of God a nd to place
our lives in his hands.
Peter’s answer is a moment of great faith, yet Jesus also reminds him that this faith is a gift
from God. The Church itself is built upon this faith, and Christ promises that the powers of
evil will not overcome it. These words are a source of hope for us, especially when our own
faith feels weak or when the Church faces difficulties.
Our response to Jesus cannot remain only words. To call him “the Christ” means allowing
his presence to shape the way we live: how we treat others, how readily we forgive, how we
respond to those in need, and how faithfully we carry our own crosses.
Perhaps then, this Gospel invites us to pause and answer Jesus’ question for ourselves: Who
is Jesus for me? May we have the grace to recognise him more clearly, trust him more
deeply, and follow him more faithfully each day.

Today, Sunday 23rd August, we celebrate with joy the Solemnity of our patron,
Saint Eugene, whose name and witness have a special place in the life of S aint Eugene’s
Cathedral. Saint Eugene served the Church with courage and faith, remaining faithful to
Christ in challenging times. His life reminds us that holiness is not simply about great deeds,
but about faithfully following Christ in the circumstances in which God has placed us.
As we celebrate our patronal feast, we give thanks for the faith handed down to us and for
the living community that gathers in this Cathedral. S aint Eugene’s witness encourages us
to remain steadfast in our faith, generous in our service of others, and united as a parish
family.
May S aint Eugene pray for our Cathedral community and for all who enter through its doors.
May his example inspire us to proclaim Christ with courage and to live our faith with joy.
St Eugene, pray for us .

St Monica and St Augustine : On 28th August, we celebrate St Monica, whose faithful
prayers for her son Augustine never ceased, even when he had wandered far from the
Christian faith. Her patience, trust and perseverance are an encouragement to all who pray
for their loved ones. On 29th August, we celebrate St Augustine, Bishop and Doctor of the
Church. Through God’s grace and his mother’s prayers, Augustine experienced a profound
conversion and became one of the Church’s greatest teachers.
Their lives remind us that no one is beyond God’s mercy and that persistent prayer can bear
beautiful fruit. May St Monica teach us to trust God patiently, and St Augustine inspire us
always to seek the truth and rest our hearts in God. Thank you
