A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 18:21-35
In today’s Gospel, Peter asks Jesus how often he should forgive someone who sins against
him. Peter suggests seven times, but Jesus goes beyond counting: forgiveness is not
something we measure; it is a way of life.
Jesus tells the parable of the unforgiving servant. A man owes his master an enormous debt,
far beyond anything he could repay. Yet when he asks for mercy, the master forgives the
whole debt. This reminds us of the generosity of God’s mercy towards us. But the servant
then refuses to forgive someone who owes him a much smaller amount. Having received
mercy, he fails to show mercy himself.
The message is challenging. We can receive God’s forgiveness while still holding on to
anger, resentment or hurt. Jesus reminds us that the mercy we receive is meant to be shared.
Forgiveness does not mean ignoring injustice or pretending that hurt does not matter.
Serious wounds may take time, prayer, wise counsel and appropriate boundaries. But we are
invited to place our hurts in God’s hands and not allow bitterness to have the f inal word.
At every Mass, we encounter God’s mercy. Christ gives himself for us and calls us to extend
that same love to others.
This week, perhaps we can bring one difficult relationship before the Lord and ask for the
grace to take one step towards forgiveness. Forgiveness is not simply something God asks
of us - it is something God first gives to us.
Lord Jesus, you have forgiven us more than we can ever repay. Teach us to receive your
mercy with grateful hearts and to share it with those who have hurt us. Give us the grace to
forgive, the wisdom to seek justice where needed, and the humility to place every wound
into your loving hands. Amen.
