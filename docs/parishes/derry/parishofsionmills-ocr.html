The Soil of Our Lives
This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower . It is one of the most
familiar stories in the Gospels. A farmer scatters seed. Some falls on the
path, some on rocky ground, some among thorns, and some on rich soil.
Only the rich soil produces a harvest.
Anyone who has ever grown anything in soil knows that you can have the
best seed in the world, but if the ground isn't ready , nothing will come of
it. The work is in the preparation. The digging, the clearing, the weeding.
It is slow and unspectacular , and nobody sees it. But it makes all the
difference.
Isaiah reminds us this weekend that God's W ord never returns to Him
empty . Like the rain and the snow that water the earth, His word always
accomplishes what it was sent to do. The seed is never the problem. God
is endlessly generous. He scatters His grace everywhere, on all of us,
without measure and without favourites. The question the parable puts to
each of us is a simple one: what kind of ground is He landing on?
Every one of us has been planted with gifts. Gifts of faith, of kindness, of
patience, of prayer . Some of them are lying dormant because the soil of
our lives has become hardened, or crowded, or shallow . The worries of
the week, the busyness, the old hurts we haven't let go of — these are
the thorns and the stones. They don't destroy the seed. They just stop it
growing.
The good news is that soil can always be worked: A little time in prayer
each day . An honest look at what is choking the W ord in us. A return to
the Sacraments. Small, quiet acts of clearing the ground! When we do
that, our roots go down deeper into God, and we find a steadiness that
the heat of life cannot wither .
St Paul tells us the whole of creation is groaning, waiting to be set free.
That freedom begins in small plots of ground, that is in hearts like yours
and mine that let the W ord take root and grow .

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 5:21-28
Today’s Gospel contains an exchange between Jesus and a Canaanite woman in which Jesus
appears to exercise racism and elitism to a degree which shocks us to the core. But is this
what is really taking place! Is Jesus a victim of inherited prejudice and a n unquestioning
acceptance of Jewish supremacy? Or is he demonstrating effectively what happens when
we fail to reach out in love to all mankind: when we fail to realise that it is God’s will and
the will of Jesus, His beloved Son, for us to gro w in unive rsal love and care as opposed to
self-love, racism and the false notion of selected supremacy! What happens is that a child
remain s suffering in torment while a loving mother is forced to watch on in helpless anguish.
Jesus always see s into the truth which lives in the heart. He sees int o the heart of the
Canaanite woman. He sees her love for her tormented child. He sees her willingness to
suffer insult for the sake of her child. And he sees her unflinching faith in him as her child’s
only hope.
Jesus, may we grow and mature daily in universal love and care, away from ‘self -love and
elitism’ in the knowledge that it is your will that we do so.

a care for justice (and) act with integrity…” as they preach the message of God’s salvation
for everyone, the Good News that the Church is “a house of prayer for all the peoples”.
