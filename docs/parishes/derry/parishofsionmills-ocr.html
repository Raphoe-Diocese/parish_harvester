26th Sunday in Ordinary Time 27 September 2026 Words and Deeds For the second Sunday running, we find ourselves in the vineyard. This week a father asks his two sons togoand work. One says no, then thinks better ofitand goes. The other says, "Yes, sir,"and never turns up. Jesus asks a simple question. Which of them did the father's will? It was theonewho actually went. What we say matters. What we do matters more. Every choice has consequences, reaching further than we will ever know, into our families, our neighbourhoods, and eternity. Ezekiel puts it plainly. The direction of a life counts. A good person can drift. A sinner can turn. God looks not so much at where we have been as at where we are heading. The cancel culture of today’s world writes people off at the first mistake: one wrong word andyouare finished. Christ offers something far better: forgiveness, a real chance to learn, to know better andtodo better. He offers freedom instead of shame, light instead of darkness, a way forward, a turn around, and eternal life. Thesonwho said nowasnot finished. He changed his mind, and his change of mind changed everything. Our choices are never purely private. A kind word lifts a household. A broken promise causes chaos and lets a whole family down. And when we cancel someone, we all lose a brother or sister. Saint Paul urges us to have the mind of Christ, who emptied himself in service. His choice to love totheendhas consequences for every oneofus. So this week, look atthe‘yes’you once gave. At baptism. At confirmation. Ask: Have we gone into the vineyard? And who might we need to stop writing off? Sion Mills http://www.parishofsionmills.com
