23rd Sunday in Ordinary Time
6 September 2026
"You have won back your brother"
God appoints Ezekiel a watchman, a guardian, this Sunday. When
danger comes, he must speak, and, if he stays silent, the harm is on his
head. That duty did not end with the prophets. It belongs to every one of
us, the baptised.
But notice how Jesus asks it to be done. Not a public shaming. Not
plastered across social media first. Go quietly, he says, just the two of
you. Our world has forgotten this. It is quick to condemn, quick to cancel,
quick to cast people out for their failures. The Gospel does the opposite:
it corrects in order to win the person back. Cancel culture writes people
off. Christ never does.
That is correction properly understood. Correction rooted in the one debt
St Paul says we can never pay off: the debt of love. Love that does not
depend on the other person deserving it. Love that speaks the truth
gently, patiently, without a hint of superiority, given unconditionally and
always keeps the door open.
And yes, speaking that truth today can cost us. We may be laughed at,
called outdated, told to keep our faith to ourselves. But speak anyway.
Kindly, humbly, but speak. Silence is not kindness; it is abandonment. So
many we love have not rejected the faith, they have simply drifted, the
fire has gone low. A word of love may be the very thing that rekindles it.
This week, be a gentle guardian. A phone call. A visit. "I miss seeing you
at Mass" — said and meant. "I'm praying for you" — said and done.
We are never sent out alone. Christ goes with us — his love poured into
our hearts. Your duty now is to go, and pour it out.
Fr Sean
