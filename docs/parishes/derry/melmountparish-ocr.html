The Soil of Our Lives
This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower. It is one of the most
familiar stories in the Gospels. A farmer scatters seed. Some falls on the
path, some on rocky ground, some among thorns, and some on rich soil.
Only the rich soil produces a harvest.
Anyone who has ever grown anything in soil knows that you can have the
best seed in the world, but if the ground isn't ready, nothing will come of
it. The work is in the preparation. The digging, the clearing, the weeding.
It is slow and unspectacular, and nobody sees it. But it makes all the
Isaiah reminds us this weekend that God's Word never returns to Him
empty. Like the rain and the snow that water the earth, His word always
accomplishes what it was sent to do. The seed is never the problem. God
is endlessly generous. He scatters His grace everywhere, on all of us,
without measure and without favourites. The question the parable puts to
each of us is a simple one: what kind of ground is He landing on?
Every one of us has been planted with gifts. Gifts of faith, of kindness, of
patience, of prayer. Some of them are lying dormant because the soil of
our lives has become hardened, or crowded, or shallow. The worries of
the week, the busyness, the old hurts we haven't let go of — these are
the thorns and the stones. They don't destroy the seed. They just stop it
The good news is that soil can always be worked: A little time in prayer
each day. An honest look at what is choking the Word in us. A return to
