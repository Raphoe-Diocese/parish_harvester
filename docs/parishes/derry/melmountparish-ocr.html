Welcome Back
After the summer break, it is good to be back with our weekly reflection
and the Gospel this Sunday is a great place to rest art.
Last week we heard Peter's great confession: "You are the Christ." This
week, only moments later in the same conversation, the same Peter gets
it badly wrong. Jesus speaks of the cross, and Pete r takes him aside to
talk him out of it. "Get behind me, Satan," Jesus replies. Peter, the rock,
has become a stumbling block!
There is great comfort in that. Peter is not a badd ie here; he is a man who
loves Jesus and cannot bear the thought of him suff ering. His mistake is
one we all make: wanting God on our terms, wanting faith without cost,
wanting the crown without the cross.
Jesus does not reject Peter. He simply tells him to get back where a
disciple belongs: behind the Master, following, not leading.
Jeremiah knew the same struggle. In the first readi ng he complains that
God has seduced him, that being a prophet has broug ht him nothing but
insult and derision. He decides to say no more and then finds a fire
burning in his heart that he cannot hold in. Faith is like that. It costs, and
yet we cannot walk away from it, because it is life itself.
"Anyone who loses his life for my sake will find it ." As a new school and
parish year begins, that is the question the Gospel puts to each of us:
where am I spending my life? On things that will pa ss, or on the One who
lasts?ndMelmount (& Camus) https://www.melmountparish.com
