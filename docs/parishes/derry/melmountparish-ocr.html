Let It Go!
Peter thinks forgiving seven times is generous. Jes us says seventy-
seven. Which means: stop counting!
Then Jesus tells a story about a servant who owed a fortune he could
never repay. He begged, and the master wiped the wh ole thing out.
Minutes later that same servant found a man who owe d him a few
months' wages and took him by the throat.
Look at him closely. He has just been handed the gi ft of a lifetime and he
cannot see it. He is so buried in his own materiali sm, his own rights, his
own small world, that the master's mercy has not to uched him at all. He
walked out of that room exactly the man he walked i n. Forgiven, but not
free.
That is the danger for every one of us. We can rece ive God's mercy again
and again, in confession, at the altar, and still w alk out clutching the old
grudge. The neighbour. The sibling. The house. The land. The job
promotion. The one who let us down. The First Readi ng puts it bluntly:
resentment is a thing we nurse, and it eats the one holding it. It narrows
our vision until, like that servant, we cannot see past the debt.
Jesus ends the parable with one condition: we must forgive from the
heart. Not through gritted teeth. Not a cold truce that keeps the old score
sheet handy in a drawer. Forgiveness is not pretend ing the wrong did not
matter, but it is a real letting go, deep down from where the grudge lives.
And the most powerful thing is that the moment we l et it go, we are the
one set free. The weight lifts. We can breathe.Melmount (& Camus) https://www.melmountparish.com
