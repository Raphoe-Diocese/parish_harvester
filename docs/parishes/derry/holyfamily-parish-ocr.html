Peter has just confessed that Jesus is the Christ, yet he ca nnot accept that the Messiah
must suffer and die. He wants the glory of Christ without the Cross. Jesus tells him,
“Get behind me,” calling Peter back to his proper place: not direc ting Jesus, but
following him. When Jesus asks us to deny ourselves, he is not asking us to hat e
ourselves or believe we are worthless. Rather, he asks us to surrender the false self
that must always succeed, remain in control and avoid weakness. Taking up the cross
means accepting the cost of loving faithfully, trusting that Christ wal ks before us.
The Gospel contains a paradox: when we cling to life on our own terms, we lose it;
when we entrust ourselves to Christ, we discover life. The question i s not simply
whether Jesus accompanies my plans, but whether I am willing to follow whe re he
leads.
