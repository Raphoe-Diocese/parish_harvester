30th Sunday in Ordinary Time 25th October 2026 One Question, One Answer: Two Sides, Same Coin Another week, another trap.

Last Sunday they came with a coin. This week a lawyer comes with a question: which commandm ent is the greatest?

Jesus does not hesitate. Love the Lord your God With all your heart, all your soul, all your mind.

Then, though nobody asked , he adds a second and says it is like the first: love your neighbour as yourself.

On these two hang the whole Law and the Prophets.

Think back to that coin. Every coin has two sides, heads and tails, but nobody has ever spent one side of a coin.

It is one piece of metal or it is nothing.

That is what Jesus is saying about love.

Love of God is one side. Love of neighbour is the other.

Try to separate the m and you are left holding something worthless.

You cannot love the Go d you have never seen while ignoring the neighbour you see every day . And you cannot truly love the neighbour without the God whose image that neighbour bears.

That is the link. Last week Jesus asked whose image was on the coin.

We said that we bear God’s image. So does the stran ger, the widow, the orphan, the man whose cloak is held in pledge.

The first reading is blunt about it: give the cloak back by sunset, because it is all he has to sleep in.

God’s compassion lands in practical places, in coins and cloaks.

St Paul tells the Thessalonians that their faith is known everywhere.

Not because of their preaching, but because people sawhow they lived.

Their faith had two sides, but it was never two-faced.

Like the coin, it was solid and the same.
