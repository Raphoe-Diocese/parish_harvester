The Soil of Our Lives
This Sunday Jesus tells us the parable of the sower . It is one of the most
familiar stories in the Gospels. A farmer scatters seed. Some falls on the
path, some on rocky ground, some among thorns, and some on rich soil.
Only the rich soil produces a harvest.
Anyone who has ever grown anything in soil knows that you can have the
best seed in the world, but if the ground isn't ready , nothing will come of
it. The work is in the preparation. The digging, the clearing, the weeding.
It is slow and unspectacular , and nobody sees it. But it makes all the
difference.
Isaiah reminds us this weekend that God's W ord never returns to Him
empty . Like the rain and the snow that water the earth, His word always
accomplishes what it was sent to do. The seed is never the problem. God
is endlessly generous. He scatters His grace everywhere, on all of us,
without measure and without favourites. The question the parable puts to
each of us is a simple one: what kind of ground is He landing on?
Every one of us has been planted with gifts. Gifts of faith, of kindness, of
patience, of prayer . Some of them are lying dormant because the soil of
our lives has become hardened, or crowded, or shallow . The worries of
the week, the busyness, the old hurts we haven't let go of — these are
the thorns and the stones. They don't destroy the seed. They just stop it
growing.
The good news is that soil can always be worked: A little time in prayer
each day . An honest look at what is choking the W ord in us. A return to
the Sacraments. Small, quiet acts of clearing the ground! When we do
that, our roots go down deeper into God, and we find a steadiness that
the heat of life cannot wither .
St Paul tells us the whole of creation is groaning, waiting to be set free.
That freedom begins in small plots of ground, that is in hearts like yours
and mine that let the W ord take root and grow .

A short reflection on today’s Gospel Matthew 1 5:21-28
Today’s Gospel contains an exchange between Jesus and a Canaanite woman in which Jesus
appears to exercise racism and elitism to a degree which shocks us to the core. But is this
what is really taking place! Is Jesus a victim of inherited prejudice and a n unquestioning
acceptance of Jewish supremacy? Or is he demonstrating effectively what happens when
we fail to reach out in love to all mankind: when we fail to realise that it is God’s will and
the will of Jesus, His beloved Son, for us to gro w in unive rsal love and care as opposed to
self-love, racism and the false notion of selected supremacy! What happens is that a child
remain s suffering in torment while a loving mother is forced to watch on in helpless anguish.
Jesus always see s into the truth which lives in the heart. He sees int o the heart of the
Canaanite woman. He sees her love for her tormented child. He sees her willingness to
suffer insult for the sake of her child. And he sees her unflinching faith in him as her child’s
only hope.
Jesus, may we grow and mature daily in universal love and care, away from ‘self -love and
elitism’ in the knowledge that it is your will that we do so.

a care for justice (and) act with integrity…” as they preach the message of God’s salvation
for everyone, the Good News that the Church is “a house of prayer for all the peoples”.

Thoughts on the Gospel
Matthew 14:13 –21 – The Feeding of the Five Thousand
The miracle of the loaves and fishes begins with compassion . Before Jesus fed the
crowd, He first recognised their suffering and their need. His heart was moved by those
who were tired, hungry, and searching for hope.
In our own busy lives, God gently calls us to notice those around us who may be lonely,
discouraged, forgotten, or struggling. A simple word of encouragement, a listening ear,
or a small act of kindness may become someone else's miracle.
The disciples looked only at what they didn't have . Faced with thousands of hungry
people, they saw only five loaves and two fish. Jesus, however, asked them to offer what
they already possessed.
God rarely asks us for what we do not have. Instead, He asks us to place before Him our
time, our talents, our compassion, and our willingness to serve. However small these
gifts may seem, He is able to multiply them beyond anything we could imagine.
Before distributing the food, Jesus lifted everything to His Father in prayer. This reminds
us that surrender is not a sign of weakness or defeat. Rather, it is an act of trust. We all
carry burdens that feel too heavy or situations that seem impossible, y et when we place
them into God's hands, what appears insufficient can become more than enough.
Finally, the twelve baskets of leftovers remind us of God's overwhelming generosity. His
blessings are never intended to stop with us but to flow through us. When we forgive
generously, encourage freely, and share what we have with others, God's grace
continues to multiply in countless lives.
Prayer
Lord, fill our hearts with compassion. Use the little we have to accomplish great
things for Your Kingdom. Teach us to trust You completely, to surrender our
limitations into Your hands, and to share Your blessings generously with all whom
we meet. Amen.

Sunday 16th August 2026 – Twentieth Sunday in Ordinary Time – Year A
The Gospel for this Sunday recounts Jesus’ encounter with a Canaanite woman whose daughter is tormented by a
demon (Matthew 15:21 -28) . Initially, Jesus appears dismissive, stating that He was sent only to the lost sheep of Israel
and even comparing her t o a dog unworthy of the children’s bread. Yet, the woman persists, humbly acknowledging her
need and demonstrating remarkable faith. Her courage and persistence lead Jesus to commend her faith and heal her
daughter
This passage challenges us to reflect on faith and humility. True faith is not about pretending to be strong; it is
about admitting our need for God and asking for help, even when answers seem delayed or social barriers make it
difficult. The woman’s exam ple teaches that persistence in pray er and trust in God’s mercy can overcome obstacles
and bring about transformation, both for ourselves and others.

PILGRIMAGE TO CYPRUS
The parish is considering a 7-night pilgrimage to Cyprus from
16–23 May 2027 , following in the footsteps of St Paul and
St Barnabas . The pilgrimage would include visits to many of
Cyprus’s most important Christian and historical sites, with the
opportunity to celebrate Mass in places closely associated with
the early Church.
The package includes direct flights from Belfast , full-board
accommodation in a four -star seafront hotel, and a guided
programme of pilgrimage visits throughout the week. The
estimated cost is £1,675 per person . At this stage, we are simply
seeking expressions of interest . If you think you may wish to join
the pilgrimage, please contact the parish office. Registering
your interest is not a commitment to travel .
