Sunday 25th The greatest and first commandment; you must love you the Lord our God with all your heart, soul and mind.

The second resembles it: you must love your neighbour as yourself.

Tuesday 27th St Otteran, is usually identified with Odhran who preceded Columba to Iona.

His death is recorded in 548 and his grave was greatly revered in Iona.

He was chosen by the Vikings as patron of Waterford in 1096 and later patron of the diocese.

Wednesday 28th St Simon and St Jude, apostles.

Nothing is known of Simon except that he was born in Cana and was called the Zealot.

Jude, also known as Thaddeus, was the apostle who a sked the Lord at the Last Supper why he showed himself only to his disciples and not to the world.

Thursday 29th St Colman , bishop. From Kilmacduagh, Co Clare.

Founded two churches in Aran, on Inis Mhór, before returning to Kilmacduagh to make a further foundation there.

Saturday 31st Blessed Dominic Collins (1566-1602) was born in Youghal, Co. Cork.

After a military career he joined the Jesuits as a Brother.

He was captured in 1602 by the English who tried to make him abjure his faith.

He was martyred in Youghal on 31 October 1602.

30th Sunday in Ordinary Time 25th October 2026 One Question, One Answer: Two Sides, Same Coin Another week, another trap.

Last Sunday they came with a coin. This week a lawyer comes with a question: which commandment is the greatest?

Jesus does not hesitate. Love the Lord your God with all your heart, all your soul, all your mind.

Then, though nobody asked, he adds a second and says it is like the first: love your neighbour as yourself.

On these two hang the whole Law and the Prophets.

Think back to that coin. Every coin has two sides, heads and tails, but nobody has ever spent one side of a coin.

It is one piece of metal or it is nothing.

That is what Jesus is saying about love.

Love of God is one side. Love of neighbour is the other.

Try to separate them and you are left holding something worthless.

You cannot love the God you have never seen while ignoring the neighbour you see every day.

And you cannot truly love the neighbour without the God whose image that neighbour bears.

That is the link. Last week Jesus asked whose image was on the coin.

We said that we bear God’s image. So does the stranger, the widow, the orphan, the man whose cloak is held in pledge.

The first reading is blunt about it: give the cloak back by sunset, because it is all he has to sleep in.

God’s compassion lands in practical places, in coins and cloaks.

St Paul tells the Thessalonians that their faith is known everywhere.

Not because of their preaching, but because people sawhow they lived.

Their faith had two sides, but it was never two-faced.

Like the coin, it was solid and the same.

Loving God is what we do in Church. Loving neighbour is how the world knows we mean it.

Two sides. Same coin. Spend it well. Fr Sean Announcements Sion Mills http://www.parishofsionmills.com
