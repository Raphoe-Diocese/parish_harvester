Sunday 1st November 7pm All welcome Readers’ Rota 2027 – An Invitation to Take Part.

A new Readers’ Rota for 2027 is currently being prepared, and we warmly invite anyone who would like to become a reader, or who is already involved in this ministry, to let Fr Gabriel know.

We would especially love to welcome our young people and junior readers to this important ministry.

Junior readers will be accompanied by an adult reader, with each proclaiming a reading at Mass.

They will meet in the sacristy before Mass to prepare together.

We warmly encourage our young people to consider taking up this ministry and playing a more active part in the life of our parish.

Guidance, support and training will be provided, and each reader will have their own Missal to help them practise and prepare.

No one needs to feel apprehensive about beginning; with preparation and encouragement, confidence will grow.

Bishop Kevin Doran has recommended that readings at Mass be proclaimed from the Lectionary at the ambo.

To help ensure that everything runs smoothly, readers are asked to arrive in good time before Mass and check the Lectionary to make sure it is open at the correct page.

Good preparation is essential and helps every reader proclaim the Word of God clearly, reverently and confidently.

To our young people and their parents: we encourage you to consider this wonderful opportunity to become more involved in our parish community.

With the support of parents and the wider parish family, our young people can grow in confidence, deepen their faith and discover the joy of sharing God’s Word with others.

If you would like to join the Readers’ Rota for 2027, please speak to Fr Gabriel.

Everyone is welcome, and every willing heart is valued.

Our policy regarding children in our parish churches?

If your kids are loud, please still come to church.

Page 12

If you're running late because getting everyone out the door was a battle, please still come to church.

If your toddler won't sit still, please still come to church.

If your baby needs to eat, please still come to church.

If your child asks questions at the least convenient time, please still come to church.

If someone cries, drops a toy, needs a snack, or has to make their third trip to the bathroom, please still come to church.

If your child doesn't know all the words, all the prayers, or exactly when to sit and stand, please still come to church If you feel like you spent the entire mass managing your children and heard approximately 12% of the sermon, please still come to church.

If Sunday morning feels messy instead of peaceful, please still come to church If you're worried about, if you're worried your children are distracting the people around you, please still come to church.

Because children do not learn how to be part of the church by staying home they can sit quietly.

They learn by being here. They learn the singing by hearing us sing.

They learn the prayers by hearing us pray.

They learn to worship by watching us worship.

They learn that church is home because we make room for them here.

The wiggles, whispers, dropped crayons, crying babies, and little voices are not interruptions to the life of the church.

They are evidence that the church is alive.

So bring the babies, bring the toddlers, bring the big kids, come late, sit at the back, sit in the front, step out where you need to. and come right back in.

Just come.
