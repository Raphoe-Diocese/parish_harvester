The Illusion of the Ego When we look at human existence through the eyes of the world, we are taught to measure worth by what we accumulate, what we conquer, and how high we can elevate ourselves. But the gospel calls us to a different standard. In the economy of salvation, the ultimate triumph of a soul is not the building up of the self, but the quiet, courageous surrender of the ego to God. Sacred Scripture reminds us in the Gospel of Matthew, "Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted." The human ego is a restless entity. It constantly demands to be right, to be noticed, to be validated, and to be first. Saint John the Baptist captured the essence of the managed ego when he said of Jesus, "He must increase, but I must decrease." When we clear away the heavy clutter of pride, we create a void —not an empty emptiness, but a space that can finally be filled by Christ’s light. The souls who truly impact us in this life are rarely the loudest or the most self-absorbed. They are the ones who make room for others because they have first made room for God. They remind us that true dignity doesn't come from a puffed-up sense of importance, but from a surrendered heart .
