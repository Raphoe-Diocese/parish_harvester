Mon - Wed. 9.00am - 12. 30pm Pope Pius XI introduced the feast of Christ the King in 1925. This was a period of great tension ands tress in Europe; many nations were still badly divided and people were living their lives in fear.

Throughout Europe totalitarianism and fascism had taken hold and their encouragement for intolerance, racism and persecution brought su ffering and fear to many.

In Russia communism had taken hold and restricted people's freedom in varied ways.

The feast of Christ the King that for believers there is a real gospel value, an allegiance higher than any other, an allegiance which prompts us to question all the ideologies or loyalties that would claim us.

Christ can only be truly called King as the cruci fied one.

That the group gathered around him could truly be the children of the kingdom undermines our assumptions about who is worthy and who is to be excluded.

None of those who jeer at him or reject him are placed outside his concern or shephe rding care.

The promise to the good thief is a symb ol of Christ exercising his kingly power to deliver his people from those who would deprive them of their freedom - just as prime duty for a political king is to ensure the security of his people.

The qualities central to the kingdom of Christ are Love, Peace, Justice and Forgiveness.

The central proclamation of Christianity is the victory of the Love of God.

What does it mean in today's world to talk about the reign of Christ, when so many ideologies and things seem to reign?

Certainly, weapons seem to rule; power seems to rule at times; terrorism seems to rule in many areas; violence rules in other quarters.

For some, economics with its twin advertising and marketing rules; for others an addiction may rule the roost.
