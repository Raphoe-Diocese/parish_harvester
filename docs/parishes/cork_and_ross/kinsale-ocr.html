WORLD MISSION SUNDAY – 18TH OCTOBER 2026 World Mission Sunday, which is celebrated on the 2nd last Sunday of October each year, is the one Sunday of the year when thee ntire global Church comes together in support of mission.

It is the Holy Father’s annual appeal for spiritual and financial support so that the life giving work of overseas mission and missionaries can continue.

Mission Sund ay collections will take place in every single parish where the Church is pr esent.

It is a moment of universal solidarity when each member of the Church family, regardless of location or background, plays their part in supporting each other.

All offerings and donations made on World Mission Sunday become part of the Holy Father’s Universal Solidarity Fund.

This fund is a lifeline for struggling missionaries and the communities they serve across Africa, Asia and Latin America, where over 1,100 mission dioceses a re found.

These dioceses are often in remote areas devastated by and natural disasters or where suppressed communities are just opening up to the life-saving message of Jesus Christ. As these dioce ses form and grow, so do their needs.

The Mission Sunday collection offers young dioceses the financial and spiritual assistance they need to hel p their men, women and children survive and thrive.

The needs of overseas dioceses vary greatly.

These needs can include the purchase of a motorbike so a priest can celebrate Mass in remote villages, or the building of a church so that people can gath er and celebrate in safety.

In essence, Mission Sunday: provides Church-centred infrastructure by helping c ommunities build churches, schools and clinics; prepares future leaders and carers of the Church by supporting the training of sisters, priests, religious brothers and catechists; supports missionary programmes that protect and care for children’s well-being by offering safe shelter, healthcare, educ ation and hope for the future.

His response is both simple and profound: “Give back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God.” Jesus acknowledges our responsibilities as citizens and members of society.

We have duties towards the communities in which we live and work.

Yet He reminds us that there is a deeper loyalty that comes before all others.

While coins may bear the image of Caesar, each human person bears the image of God.

The Gospel challenges us to reflect on what belongs to God in our own lives.

Our time, talents, relationships, compassion, and faith are gifts entrusted to us by the Lord.

As we fulfil our earthly responsibilities, we are also called to offer ourselves wholeheartedly to God, allowing His values of justice, truth, mercy, and love to shape everything we do.

May we live as faithful citizens of the world, while never forgetting that we belong first to God.

Deacon Eric Thank You to Fr Kevin When Fr Kevin announced in May that he had been appointed by Archbishop Farrell to a new ministry in Wicklow, our church was about to close for essential refurbishment works.

As a result, there was little opportunity for parishioners to express their thanks for his seven and a half years of devoted service to our parish.

The Parish Pastoral Council recently discussed how we might show our appreciation for Fr Kevin and agreed to organise a parish collection towards a gift from the community he served so faithfully.

Special envelopes are available at the back of the church and may be placed in the collection boxes or returned to the Parish Office.

We also hope to welcome Fr Kevin back to Monkstown in the coming weeks for a gathering of parishioners, giving us an opportunity to thank him personally and wish him every blessing in his new ministry.

A Sad Farewell to Bruno Many of you will remember Bruno, Fr Kevin's Bernese Mountain Dog, who was a familiar sight around the parish.

After more than eleven and a half years as Fr Kevin's faithful companion, Bruno has sadly died.

Anyone who has loved and cared for a dog will understand how deep that bond can be.

We know that Bruno's loss will be keenly felt and we remember Fr Kevin in our prayers at this sad time.
