October – Month of the Rosary Pope Leo has invited Catholics to pray the Rosary for peace during October. “I would like to make an appeal that is particularly close to my heart, here in this place,” Pope Leo said during his Angelus address in Lourdes, at the conclusion of the outdoor Mass he celebrated there. “It was here the Immaculate Virgin appeared to Saint Bernadette with the Rosary and, asit were, guided her in prayer.” “For this reason, today I invite all the faithful and all Christian communities to pray the Rosary throughout the coming month of October, with the particular intention that wars may cease and that peace may be established among peoples in conflict,” the Pope continued. “Let us accompany this prayer with concrete acts of penance and mercy, committing ourselves always to fostering dialogue and reconciliation.” The Rosary is recited at 9.40am before the 10.00am Mass. In response to the Pope’s invitation, itmaybe an idea to find some time during the month of October to pray the Rosary as a family, even just one decade. The Illusion ofthe Ego When we look at human existence through the eyes of the world, we are taught to measure worth by what we accumulate, what we conquer, and how high we can elevate ourselves. But the gospel calls ustoa different standard. In the economy of salvation, the ultimate triumph of a soul isnotthe building upofthe self, but the quiet, courageous surrender oftheegoto God. Sacred Scripture reminds usinthe Gospel of Matthew, "Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted." The human egoisa restless entity. It constantly demands to be right, to be noticed, to be validated, and to be first. Saint John the Baptist captured the essence of the managed ego when he said of Jesus, "He must increase, but I must decrease." When we clear away the heavy clutter of pride, we create a void —not an empty emptiness, but a space that can finally be filled by Christ’s light. The souls who truly impact us in this life are rarely the loudest or the most self-absorbed. They are the ones who make room for others because they have first made room for God. They remind us that true dignity doesn't come from a puffed-up sense of importance, but from a surrendered heart .
