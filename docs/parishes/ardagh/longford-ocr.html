THE MISSION OF THE CHURCH, OUR COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY Today, we celebrate World Mission Sunday.

We set aside this day every year to think, talk and reflect about the mission which Jesus entrusted to his disciples and to us in Mathew’s Gospel.

Pope Francis once reflected that "today, every land is a ‘mission land’ and every dimension of human life is mission territory". This marks a major shift in how the Church views its role in the modern world.

Historically, "the missions" meant sending missionaries from stablished Christian nations to far-off countries.

Today, the Pope emphasises that the global landscape has completely transformed.

Several factors must have caused this; the collapse of Christendom, geographical and human boundaries.

On the other hand, Pope Leo chose the official theme for the centenary celebration of World Mission Sunday: “One in Christ, United in Mission”. Pope Leo emphasized that through baptism; every single person becomes a member of the Body of Christ and holds a unique responsibility to cooperate as one family to spread the Gospel.

He noted that evangelisation is fundamentally "always a work of love" There are two ways to be a missionary: we can give to mission by going and we can go to mission by giving.

Dear brothers and sisters let us be actively involved in mission.

Your Mission Sunday donation envelopes, for the promotion of mission in the church, should be returned as soon as possible.

Pope Leo dedicates his October prayer intention to mental health ministry, calling on the Church to listen without judging and accompany those who suffer without excluding them. “We bring before you O Lord, our brothers and sisters who struggle with challenges to their mental health, who suffer in solitude with anxiety, depression, or any other mental illness,” he prays.

Pope Leo asks forgiveness for the times when Christians have looked the other way, contributing to stigma and discrimination against people with mental illnesses.

He prays that the Church may become “a safe space where everyone has a place – a community that accompanies and never excludes, that listens without judging and draws near without fear.” The Pope also remembers those who provide care, including professionals, family members and volunteers, asking God to give them strength and compassion. “Send your Spirit upon us, that we may learn to welcome and integrate care for mental health into the life and ministry of our communities and of the whole Church,” he concludes, “and thus become a living sign of your mercy, which never abandons and always restores.”

Simple explanation of Jesus' message today

1) it is the duty of Christians to pay for the services and the privileges that the state provides -- like Hospitals, paved roads, police and fire departments, schools, and Social welfare amongst other necessities. 2) We must respect the laws of our country in order to live in peace. 3) When the state oversteps the mark and puts itself in the place of God, Christians are, as a last resort, absolved from obedience. "We must give to Caesar the things that are Caesar's and To God the things that are God's.

If there is a clash between God's law and man's we as'Christians must put Him first. 2) We need to “give to God what is God’s?” How?

Since everything is God's, we must give ourselves to Him 100%, not just 10% on Sundays.

We should be generous in fulfilling our Sunday obligations, and we should find time every day for prayer and worship in the family, for the reading of the Bible, and for the proper training of our children in Faith and morals.

St. Augustine teaches that when we truly succeed in “giving to God what is God's,” we are “doing justice to God.” This requires that we return to God, with dividends, that which God has entrusted to us, remembering that we are mere managers or stewards of God's gifts.

The common theme of today's readings is the nature of our obligations to God and to our country.

The readings show us how, with God's help, we can be ideal citizens of both earth and Heaven.

In the Gospel, Jesus escapes from the trap in the question, “Is it lawful to pay the census tax to Caesar or not?” by stating, “Repay to Caesar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God.” By this answer, Jesus reminds His questioners that if they are that concerned and careful about paying taxes to the state, they should be much more concerned and careful about their service to God and their obligations to Him as their Creator and Lord.

In other words God requires worship from us.

It is all we can give Him in the end. That's all I had to give Him when lying on my back in Hospital and not allowed to turn left or right in case of doing damage to the healing process.
