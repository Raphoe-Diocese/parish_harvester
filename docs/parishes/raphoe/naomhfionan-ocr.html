Thursday adoration includes the Divine Mercy 5:30pm and concludes with Rosary 8:30pm All Masses are available live on: www.naomhĮonan.com & Parish Radio 106.5 FM Reflection Today’s parable is not about financial or material gain.

It is concerned with properties of much greater value, properties that God has entrusted to all of us: life, love, freedom, beauty, truth, and other individual gifts.

These properties do not belong to us. They are entrusted to us by God, and we are accountable for them.

The word ‘entrusted’ is the key to understanding this parable.

God has confidence in us. He trusts us to look after the goods he has placed in our care.

We might say that God has dreams for us far greater than any earthly father or mother have for their children.

While the word talent, as used in today’s gospel, refers to a weight or unit of currency common in the world of Jesus’ time, most people today use it to refer to a person’s particular gifts or abilities of mind or body.

Interpreted in this sense, the parable may help us to reflect on the particular gifts or abilities God has given us, and which he is challenging us to use in the service of his reign.

We should ask ourselves how well we are using these gifts i n the service of our community, parish, and the wider society.

If I were to meet Jesus t oday and he were to ask me: How did you use the talents I gave you?

Who benefited and howdid they benefit from them?

Could I say, with confidence, that I have used them to the best of my ability to promote God’s reign in the world?

If I can honestly say that I have used them to serve others rather than myself, then I have every right to expect to hear the reply of the Master to the productive servants: ‘Well done, good and faithful servant; you have shown you can be faithful in small things; come and join in your master’s happiness’ (Mt 25:21).
