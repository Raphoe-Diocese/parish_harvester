Thursday adoration includes the Divine Mercy 5:30pm and concludes with Rosary 8:30pm All Masses are available live on: www.naomhĮonan.com & Parish Radio 106.5 FM Reflection A frequent biblical image for God’s homecoming is that of a wedding feast. This is an image that recurs in many of the parables of Jesus, and we come across it in today’s gospel reading from Matthew, which recounts the parable of the wise and the foolish bridesmaids.

A bit of background may help us to understand this parable.

According to Jewish custom, the role of bridesmaids was to accompany and help the bride to prepare for the wedding.

Bridesmaids would often wait with the bride in her home until the groom arrived and then joyfully escort the bridal couple with lighted lamps to the bridegroom’s home, where the marriage ceremony and subsequent feast would take place.

And the feast would last for several days.

In the parable of Jesus, there are ten bridesmaids.

Five of them are foolish and five are wise.

Because of a lengthy delay in the bridegroom’s arrival, the five foolish bridesmaids run out of oil and rush to town to purchase more.

In their absence, the bridegroom arrives and the wise bridesmaids, who had brought extra oil with them, are ready to accompany the groom and his bride to the wedding feast. When the foolish bridesmaids eventually arrive at the wedding hall and beg to be admitted, they are turned away with the words, ‘Truly I tells you, I do not know you’(Mt 25:12).

The meaning of the parable seems fairly clear.

It is telling us to be always ready to welcome the Lord into our lives whenever he comes.

Jesus ends the parable with the warning, ‘Keep awake, therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour’ (Mt 25:13).

We should always be ready to meet the Lord when he calls us.
