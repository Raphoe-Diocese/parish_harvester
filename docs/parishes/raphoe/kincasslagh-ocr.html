WEEKLY NEWSLETTER 18th October 2026 – Twenty-Ninth Sunday of Ordinary Time Step by Step th rough the Mass The Liturgy of the Eucharist (Part 12) When the priest says “Pray Brothers and Sisters” he is not saying “Let us pray” as he does elsewhere.

Here the word “Pray” is a command. It is a call to attention of sorts.

And that is why the people respond by standing up at this point – an act which biblically and liturgically denotes taking a posture of readiness to do some great and godly task.

The task in hand is the uniting of our lives with the life and death of Jesus made present in this Mass.

Could there be a more important and godly task than this?

This great and godly task is not simply passively awaiting the priest, (who has sacred power the laity do not have), to bring about the change in the bread and wine which becomes the Body, Blood, Soul and Divinity of Jesus.

The lay faithful are encouraged, urged even, to unite themselves with self-offering of Jesus on Calvary, to make the pursuit of spiritual union with him an active endeavour in this prayer; and exercise their baptismally rooted Commo n Priesthood – a real power to offer right worship, praise and sacrifice to God the Father through Christ Jesus – as they will hear at every Mass: “Through Him, With Him, and In Him” – Him being the Lord Jesus.

Baptism unites us to Christ Jesus – it makes us Christian; makes us sons and daughters of the Father, and Co-Heirs with Christ. In a sense, it makes our souls capable of uniting our sacrifice of praise and the offering of our lives (and the many ways we serve) to the life and sacrificial service of Jesus on the Cross.

The Second Vatican Council taught on this aspect of the Common Priesthood of all the baptised: “The baptized, by regeneration and the anointing of the Holy Spirit, are consecrated as a spiritual house and a holy priesth ood, in order that through all those works which are those of the Christian they may offer spiritual sacrifices and proclaim the power of Him who has called them out of darkness into His marvellous light… The ministerial priest, by the sacred power he enjoys, teaches and rules the priestly people; acting in the person of Christ, he makes present the Eucharistic Sacrifice, and offers it to God in the name of all the people.

But the faithful, in virtue of their royal priesthood, join in the offering of the Eucharist…Taking part in the Eucharisti c Sacrifice, which is the fount and apex of the whole Christian life, they offer the Divine Victim to God, and offer themselves along with it.
