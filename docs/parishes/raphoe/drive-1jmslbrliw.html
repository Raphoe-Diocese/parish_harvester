Eucharistic Adoration. In John 14:9 Jesus said to his disciples, “For a long time I have been with youall, yetyoudo not know me”. The greatest waytogetto know Jesus – apart from Holy Mass – is by spending time with him in Adoration. “Come tomeallyouwho labour or are heavily burdened and I will give you rest”. Jesus invites us to come into his presence. How awesome is that? Howdidthe practice of adoration begin? Back in the 12th century the Catholic faithful wished to have more time after Holy Communion to pray in the Presence of Jesus. Their wish was granted when Thomas Aquinas (Saint Thomas Aquinas), introduced Eucharistic Adoration. This powerful form of prayer spread throughout the world to the present day. The consecrated Host is placed in the monstrance on the altar. People may pray, read, sit in silent contemplation or just rest, in the True Presence of Jesus. “Heart speaks to heart.” (St. Teresa of Calcutta) New members of our parish community have signed up and committed to an hour of adoration each week. That is great news, but more are always needed to keep this devotion viable. Many people drop intothe church on a casual basis during the hours of adoration. That is also very highly commended.
