It’s Easy To See What Alcohol Is Doing To The Drinker
But Can You See What It Is Doing To You?
These questions can help you identify if someone else ’s drinking is affecting you.
If your answer is yes to any of the following, Al -Anon Family Groups can help.
• Do you worry about how much someone else drinks?
• Do you tell lies to cover up for someone else ’s drinking?
• Are you afraid to upset someone for fear that it will set off a drinking bout?
• Do you feel that if the drinker loves you, he would stop drinking to please you?
• Do you feel like a failure because you can ’t stop thee drinking?
• Do you make threats such as “If you don ’t stop drinking, I ’ll leave you “?
• Do you search for hidden alcohol?
• Do you feel angry, confused or depressed most of the time?

Liturgical ‘Sign of Peace ’ The Sign of Peace remains an integral part of
the celebration of the Eucharist and has been reintroduced as a normal
practice across the Diocese. The customary expression of the Sign of Peace
in Ireland is a handshake. However, other non -contact forms of the sign of
peace such as a nod, a bow or a smile are also legitimate expressions of the
Sign of Peace. No offence should be taken if someone prefers to express a
non-contact Sign of Peace. The Diocese encourages all to continue to
exercise good hygienic practices, and if you exhibit flu -like symptoms, you
are encouraged to remain at home.

A Brief history of St Malachy’s Church, Kilclief
The first Church dedicated to St Malachy was erected
in 1785 when the Parish Priest was Fr John
Fitzsimons. It replaced several small Chapels in the
area. Mass was also held in houses and fields. It cost
£200 and was paid for by subscription. Over the alta r
there was a painting of the Crucifixion. The Church
was capable of seating 280 people and there was a
sundial on the walls.
A school was held in the Chapel then.
A gallery was added to increase its capacity in 1830.
The ruins of the original Church are still accessible
through the Cemetery.
In 1925 a porch was built but was later replaced by a geometrical tower which
housed the bell which was blessed by Fr Crossin on 15th August 1952 and was
rung for the first time. Fr Crossin christened the new bell “Joseph”.
Unfortunately the bell cracked and a new bell was fitted and blessed on 10th May
In 1968/69 as assessment was carried out on the Church which stated the roof
and walls were in an advanced state of decay and it was decided the Church
should be replaced. The site for the new Church was given to the Parish by Mrs
Cultra of Ballywooden. Th e last Mass was celebrated in the old Church on 5th
January 1969.
Mass was first celebrated in the new Church on
4th April 1971 .
The Church was dedicated to St Malachy ,
blessed and officially opened by the Most Rev
Dr Philbin on 16th May 1971.
