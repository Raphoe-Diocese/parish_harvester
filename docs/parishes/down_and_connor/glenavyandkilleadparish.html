She was a woman like many. She would
fight for her child, so she took on Jesus,
the best -known religious figure around.
Like someone taking on the minister for
health or children over a medical card for
a chronically ill child and not letting them
off the hook. Jesus was testing her.
He saw faith, but he wanted everyone
else to see it. So, he told her she didn't fit
into the local religious sphere because she was foreigner, like an asylum
seeker or a refugee. She knew she fitted into the heart of Jesus simply
because she was the mother of a sick child and believed he could help.
Even if he would cure everyone, maybe he would come back to her at
the end. Jesus went further - he cured her child and praised her faith.
She is another of the unnamed people of the gospel. Like the woman of
Samaria, the rich young man, the thief on Calvary - she is herself, and
she is all of us. She is everyone, man and woman, of faith.
Faith in a surprising God who, in Jesus, his Son, never says no to
goodness. Faith that keeps us energetic and alive, not tired, in soul as
well as body.
Remember your feeling of gratitude for the children of your family.
Lord, I believe. Strengthen my faith.

Liturgical ‘Sign of Peace ’ The Sign of Peace remains an integral part of
the celebration of the Eucharist and has been reintroduced as a normal
practice across the Diocese. The customary expression of the Sign of Peace
in Ireland is a handshake. However, other non -contact forms of the sign of
peace such as a nod, a bow or a smile are also legitimate expressions of the
Sign of Peace. No offence should be taken if someone prefers to express a
non-contact Sign of Peace. The Diocese encourages all to continue to
exercise good hygienic practices, and if you exhibit flu -like symptoms, you
are encouraged to remain at home.
