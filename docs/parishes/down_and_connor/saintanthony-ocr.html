A Brief history of St Patrick’s Church, Cargagh
Page 34
St Patrick’s Church was one of the five Old Mass Houses
reported in 1731 , originally built of mud, packed with stone
and with a mud floor. It was thatched with reeds and sprigs
from the bog.
It was described as “a square building with no vestry
attached ”. The Priest put his robes on behind a screen and
when it rained the roof leaked and made puddles on the
earthen floor. People sat on wooden forms.
The building was replaced with stone in 17 54. The
outside porch at the roadside was built sometime later
and a gallery erected after that as the congregation gre w.
The gallery and three windows in the west side of the
Church were made by James McConville of Drumroe.
The seats were made by Tommy Curran and the earthen
floor was replaced with wood.
A new altar was donated by Michael Little in 1902 when
Rev O’Kane was PP . The altar was made by Joe & James
Murnin of Cloughy. In 1947 some renovations were made
to Cargagh – the ceiling was raised, a new sacristy
replaced the old lean -to with its fire and tall red brick
chimney . The old confession box was removed to provide
extra seating. The builder was William Taggart of Saul.
The stained glass window in the altar gable wall was brought
from Dublin and depicts the Crucifixion as centrepiece.
