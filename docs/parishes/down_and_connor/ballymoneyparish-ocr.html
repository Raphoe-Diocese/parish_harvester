THREE WARNINGS FROM JESUS In the Gospel this Sunday, the Lord tells a parable about what sounds like a very strange wedding.

In fact, this story is an accurate description of how marriages took place in the Holy Land at that time.

But thatÕs not why Jesus tells this tale.

He wants us to give us three warnings we need to know if weÕre serious about going to heaven when we die.

Firstly, we do not know the day or the hour when Jesus will come for us.

ThatÕs because God doesnÕt want us to be scared of the future but to focus on being the best people we can be right now.

Secondly, when our time comes we will be held accountable.

It will be too late to change then! Thirdly, there are some things that others canÕt supply for us - like our faith, love, or a good character.

We are personally responsible for the way we live our lives, and one day we will be judged for it too.

Why November is the month to recall the Four Last Things ÒThe month of November has always been a time of the year to re ßect on the Four Last Things: death, judgement, Heaven, and Hell.

It may not be a topic we would want to meditate on, but it is a topic that the Church repeatedly brings to mind during this traditionally dark and gloomy month in the Northern Hemisphere.

Many priests will begin to preach on this topic during the month of November, dedicating each Sunday to one of the Four Last Things.

Whydid the Church choose November as a month to focus on such a theological theme?

Here are a few reasons why the Catholic Church chose November for this topic. 1. In the Northern Hemisphere, November is a time of the year when many people watch as trees lose their leaves, and when many plants diedue to the lack of sunlight.

It is a time when the natural world "dies" and lays dormant until spring. 2. November begins with the feast of All Saints and the commemoration of All Souls, starting the month out with a very obvious re ßection on death and our Þnal destination.

These celebrations often continue throughout the rest of the month with various "All Saints" day feasts for various religious orders. 3. An indulgence, applicable only to the souls in Purgatory, is granted to the faithful who devoutly visit a cemetery and pray for the departed in November.

The indulgence is plenary each day from the 1st to the 8th of November; on other days of the year it is partial.

Death is continually brought-up in the mind of Catholics. 4. The Roman Catholic liturgical year ends in November after the Solemnity of Christ the King.

The year ends immediately before the beginning of Advent, which ushers in a new liturgical year.

For these reasons, November is a perfect time to recall the Four Last Things.
