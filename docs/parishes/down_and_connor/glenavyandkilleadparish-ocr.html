Dear Sisters and Brothers in Christ, Each year the Season of Creation invites us to pause, reflect and renew our commitment to caring for God’s creation, our common home. This year's theme, “Living Water", inspired by Ezekiel 47:1-12, offers us a powerful image of hope, healing and renewal. In the prophet's vision, water flows forth bringing life wherever it goes, transforming what is barren into places of abundance and growth. In his Message for the World Day of Prayer for the Care of Creation, Pope Leo XIV reminds us that "environmental justice can no longer be regarded as an abstract concept or a distant goal. Itisan urgent need; itisa matter of justice, social, economic and human." This challenge invites ustosee care for creation notasan optional concern butasan essential expression of our Christian faith and our love of neighbour. As the Season of Creation began this year on the 1 of September, Pope Leo Offered another profound invitation. He encouraged us to place our daily lives into "silent mode" for a while, setting aside the constant noise and distractions that surround us, so that we might hear "the melodious voice of creation, through which God, the Author of all things, speaks to us." We need moments of stillness in which we can rediscover our place within God creation, hear thecryofthe earth andthecryofthe poor, and recognise Once more the beauty and harmony that God has entrusted to our care. Pope Leo reminds us that such contemplation is the beginning of an authentic ecological conversion, one that transforms thewaywe relate to the world around us. I am particularly struck by the theme of "Hope from Faith and Action" contained within the Season of Creation resources - At a time when many people feel overwhelmed by environmental concerns, we are reminded that Christian hope is rooted in faith expressed through action. As the Season of Creation materials beautifully express it, "Every act to care for creation becomes part of a global stream of hope. May this Season of Creation deepen our gratitude for the gift of creation, strengthen our commitment to environmental justice, and inspire us to become faithful stewards of the earth entrusted to our care. Liturgical ‘Sign of Peace ’ The Sign of Peace remains an integral part of the celebration of the Eucharist and has been reintroduced as a normal practice across the Diocese. The customary expression of the Sign of Peace in Ireland is a handshake. However, other non-contact forms of the sign of peace such asanod, abowora smile are also legitimate expressions of the Sign of Peace. No offence should be taken if someone prefers to express a non-contact Sign of Peace.
