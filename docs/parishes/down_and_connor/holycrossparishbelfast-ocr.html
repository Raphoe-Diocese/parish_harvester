Today is World Mission Sunday . The Opening Prayer of the Mass today which is taken from the Mass for the Evangelisation of Peoples states “O God you have willed that your Church be the sacrament of salvation for all nations… ” The Church exists for mission, just as a fire exists for burning.

Today’s Gospel (Matthew 22:15-21)) Jesus calls us to “to give back to God what belongs to God” (Matthew 22:21).

What belongs to God? He created all things-everything belongs to Him.

What carries God’s face as the coin carried Caesar’s face?

What does God expect us to give? No t merely endless prayers - even though that is good!

But what God wants us to give is a well-organised human life for all.

As long as there are starving children and refugees, poor people who have no means to be educated, where there is war, famine, and indifference to the plight of our planet - then our tax to God has not yet been paid.

Paul’s first letter to the people of Thessalonica (1:1-5) is today’s second reading.

This letter is among the earliest of all Christian writings and the two letters that Paul wrote to these new Christians give us an invaluable insight into the young and joyful faith of this emerging Christian community.

Thessalonica, which is modern day Salonika in Greece, was part of Macedonia, the land of Alexander the Great who conquered the known world in his time.

This town was the main link between Rome and the East, and the coming of Christianity to Thessalonica was crucial in making Christianity a world religion.

We are told of Paul’s visit to that town in Acts 17:1-10. We know that he preached in their synagogue on three weekends and was hustled out of the town by some who disagreed with his teaching about Jesus.

However Paul, who now was in Corinth, was worried about his new converts and sent his fellow worker Timothy back to them to see how they were getting on.

Timothy returned to tell Paul that all was well with his new fledging Christian converts.

Paul was delighted with the news that their faith in Jesus not only survived but was flourishing in his absence.

He wrote them this letter. In the first reading from Isaiah 45:4-6 we hear how God called Cyrus, King of Persia (559-530 BC) who conquered Babylon to allow his people to go back home from exile to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple.

God’s power is at work even in the unbeliever.

Today the task of spreading the Good News about Jesus is still the Church’s calling.

We are called to bring people to the awareness of all that God is doing for them in His Son, Jesus Christ. Collections are taking place today in every single parish across Europe to help support this work.

The needs of the Church in mission dioceses, of which there are over 1,100 continues to grow.

These dioceses are often in remote areas devastated by war and natural disasters.

Our offerings help their men, women and children survive and ultimately flourish through the action and work of missionaries.

This is our moment of universal solidarity, when each member of our Church family, regardless of location or background can play their part in supporting each other.

This collection is a lifeline, not only to missionaries, but also the future of the Church.

There are envelopes at the back of the church that can be used to place your contribution for the support of the oversea missions.

We are all conscious that we are all living in financially stringent times.

In the light of that reality your generosity is remarkable.

Thank you. Father Charles Cross World Mission
